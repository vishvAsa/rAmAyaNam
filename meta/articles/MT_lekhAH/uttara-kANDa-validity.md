## vana-parva narration
This question is often raised. While Uttarakāṇḍa is definitely part of the Rāmāyaṇa of Vālmīki as understood by tradition, in philological terms it is clearly a later accretion. This can be inferred by a comparison with the earliest "para-rāmāyaṇa", i.e., the Rāmopākhyāna narrated by the Bhārgava Mārkaṇḍeya to the Pāṇḍavas in the Vana-parvan of the Mahābhārata. This is an archaic paralog of the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa as it closely follows it in form and language despite the condensation and some major differences (e.g., Lakṣmaṇa kills Kumbhakarṇa). The Rāmopākhyāna ends thus:

> tathā bharata-śatrughnau  
sametau guruṇā tadā |  
vaidehyā darśanenobhau  
praharṣaṃ samavāpatuḥ ||  
tasmai tad bharato rājyam  
āgatāyābhisatkṛtam |  
nyāsaṃ niryātayām āsa  
yuktaḥ paramayā mudā ||
tatas taṃ vaiṣṇave śūraṃ  
nakṣatre ’bhimate ’hani |
vasiṣṭho vāmadevaś ca  
sahitāv abhyaṣiñcatām ||  
>
> so ’bhiṣiktaḥ kapi-śreṣṭhaṃ  
sugrīvaṃ sasuhṛjjanam |  
vibhīṣaṇaṃ ca paulastyam  
anvajānād gṛhān prati ||  
abhyarcya vividhai ratnaiḥ  
prītiyuktau mudā yutau |  
samādhāyeti kartavyaṃ  
duḥkhena visasarja ha ||  
>
> puṣpakaṃ ca vimānaṃ tat  
pūjayitvā sa rāghavaḥ |  
prādād vaiśravaṇāyaiva  
prītyā sa raghunandanaḥ ||  
>
> tato devarṣi-sahitaḥ  
saritaṃ gomatīm anu |  
daśāśvamedhān ājahre  
jārūthyān sa nirargalān ||

> Then Bharata and Śatrughna, united with their guru at that time, both attained immense joy upon beholding Bharata, with supreme joy, handed back and surrendered that kingdom to him who had returned and been duly honored, as if restoring a sacred deposit.
> 
> Then, on an auspicious day under the lunar mansion of Viṣṇu (Śravaṇa), Vasiṣṭha and Vāmadeva together lustrated (=crowned) that hero.
> 
> He [Rāma], having been crowned, took leave of and dismissed for their homes the best of monkeys, Sugrīva along with his friends, and the Paulastya Vibhīṣaṇa.
> 
> Having honored them with various gems, filled with affection and joy, having settled all duties, he sent them off sorrowfully.
> 
> And that descendant of Raghu, having worshipped that Puṣpaka airplane, affectionately gave it back to Vaiśravaṇa.
> 
> Then, accompanied by the divine sages along the Gomatī river, he performed ten uninterrupted horse sacrifices of the Jārūthya type.

So there is clearly no Uttarakāṇḍa here. 

## Completion
Nevertheless, parts of the Uttarakāṇḍa contain early circum-Rāmāyaṇa myths that were clearly gathered to give the reader context: e.g., about the origin of rākṣasas of Laṅkā, Hanūmat and the earlier battles between Viṣṇu and the rākṣasas. 

## uttara-bhāga
It also has a genuine uttara-bhāga, in the form of the Lavanāsura war and the succession to the Ikṣvāku domain by eight princes born of the four brothers. 

## sItA disappearence
We believe that, like agni-parīkṣā of Sītā, the disappearance of Sītā into the earth is also symbolic: the plowed land that is separated from Indra returns to a chthonic+++(=underworldic)+++ state. 

This core of the Uttarakāṇḍa served as the locus for the accretion of other myths.
