Book V : Sundara Kanda - Book Of Beauty

Realizing that the woman he saw at Ravana’s house was not Seetha, Hanuma begins to search again for Seetha.

भागसूचना
  1. वह सीता नहीं है—ऐसा निश्चय होनेपर हनुमान् जी का पुनः अन्तःपुरमें और उसकी पानभूमिमें सीताका पता लगाना, उनके मनमें धर्मलोपकी आशंका और स्वतः उसका निवारण होना
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Dismissing his mistaken impression that Mandodarī is Sītā, Hanumān reflects on Jānakī’s unwavering fidelity to Rāma and resumes searching the banquet hall among Rāvaṇa’s slumbering consorts.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अवधूय च तां बुद्धिं बभूवावस्थितस्तदा ।
जगाम चापरां चिन्तां सीतां प्रति महाकपिः ॥ १ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अवधूय च तां बुद्धिं बभूवावस्थितस्तदा ।
जगाम चापरां चिन्तां सीतां प्रति महाकपिः ॥ १ ॥

मूलम् - VR

अवधूय च ताम् बुद्धिम् बभूव अवस्थितः तदा । जगाम च अपराम् चिन्ताम् सीताम् प्रति महा कपिः ॥ ५-११-१

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

फिर उस समय इस विचारको छोड़कर महाकपि हनुमान् जी अपनी स्वाभाविक स्थितिमें स्थित हुए और वे सीताजीके विषयमें दूसरे प्रकारकी चिन्ता करने लगे ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

सीतादेविय पातिव्रत्यवे मॊदलाद महागुणगळन्नु स्मरिसिद हनुमन्तनु रावणन अन्तःपुरदल्लि मलगिरुववळु सीतॆयल्लवॆन्दु निश्चयिसि स्थिरतॆयन्नु हॊन्दि, सीतादेवियु सिक्कदिरुव बग्गॆ पुनः योचिसतॊडगिदनु.॥1॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

1. mahaakapiH = the great Hanuma; tadaa = then; avadhuuya = removing; taam buddhim = that thought; bhabhuuva = became avasthitaH = with the right mind; jagaama = and went; aparaam chintaam = (with)another thought; siitaam prati = about Seetha.

अनुवादः

The great Hanuma then removing that thought became with the right mind and had another thought about Seetha.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Shaking off that notion (tāṃ buddhiṃ [that Mandodarī was Sītā]), the great monkey then became steady (avasthitaḥ) [in his discernment] and entered upon another line of reflection (aparāṃ cintāṃ) concerning Sītā.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

न रामेण वियुक्ता सा स्वप्तुमर्हति भामिनी ।
न भोक्तुं नाप्यलङ्कर्तुं न पानमुपसेवितुम् ॥ २ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

न रामेण वियुक्ता सा स्वप्तुमर्हति भामिनी ।
न भोक्तुं नाप्यलङ्कर्तुं न पानमुपसेवितुम् ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

(उन्होंने सोचा—) ‘भामिनी सीता श्रीरामचन्द्रजीसे बिछुड़ गयी हैं । इस दशामें वे न तो सो सकती हैं, न भोजन कर सकती हैं, न शृंगार एवं अलंकार धारण कर सकती हैं, फिर मदिरापानका सेवन तो किसी प्रकार भी नहीं कर सकतीं ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

श्रीरामनिन्द बेर्पट्टिरुव सीतादेवियु निश्चिन्तळागि मलगिरलारळु. आ स्थितियल्लि अन्न-पानादिगळ कडॆगॆ मनस्सागदु. अलङ्कार माडिकॊळ्ळलारळु.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Separated from Rāma (rāmeṇa viyuktā), that noble lady (bhāminī) could never bear to sleep, nor to eat, nor even to adorn herself (alaṃkartuṃ), nor to indulge in drink (pānam upasevituṃ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

नान्यं नरमुपस्थातुं सुराणामपि चेश्वरम् ।
न हि रामसमः कश्चिद् विद्यते त्रिदशेष्वपि ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

नान्यं नरमुपस्थातुं सुराणामपि चेश्वरम् ।
न हि रामसमः कश्चिद् विद्यते त्रिदशेष्वपि ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘वे किसी दूसरे पुरुषके पास, वह देवताओंका भी ईश्वर क्यों न हो, नहीं जा सकतीं । देवताओंमें भी कोई ऐसा नहीं है जो श्रीरामचन्द्रजीकी समानता कर सके ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अन्य मनुष्यन अथवा देवतॆगळ ऒडॆयनाद इन्द्रन बळियल्लियू, अवळु स्वस्थ चित्तळागि कुळितुकॊळ्ळलारळु; यार बळिगू होगलारळु. एकॆन्दरॆ श्रीरामनिगॆ सदृशनादवनु देवतॆगळल्लियू यारु इल्ल.॥3॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

2;3. saa bhaaminii = that Seetha; na arhate = is not suitable; svaptum = to sleep ; raameNa viyuktaa = separated from Rama; na bhoktum = will not eat; na apyalamkartum api = will not decorate also; na = not suited; upasevitum = to drink; paanam = a beverage; upasthaatum = to reach; anyam naram = another man; suraaNaam eshvaram api = even though he were Indra; hi = because; na vidyate hi = there is indeed no; kashchit = one; raamasamaH = equalling Rama; tridasheshhvapi = even among gods; iyam anyaa = this is another woman; iti = thus; nishchitya = deciding; saH = that Hanuma; chachaara = paced; paanabhuumau = in that banqueting hall.

अनुवादः

That Seetha is not suitable to sleep separated from Rama, will not eat, will not decorate also, not suited to drink a beverage, to reach another man even though if he were Indra because there is indeed no one equaling Rama even among gods. This is another woman - thus deciding that Hanuma paced in that banqueting hall.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Nor could she attend upon (upasthātuṃ) another man—even were he the lord of the gods (surāṇām īśvaram)—for there is none equal to Rāma (rāmasamaḥ) even among the immortals (tridaśeṣv api).”

पानभूमौ हरिश्रेष्ठः सीतासन्दर्शनोत्सुकः ॥ ४ ॥

क्रीडितेन अपराः क्लान्ता गीतेन च तथा पराः ॥ ५-११-४ नृत्तेन च अपराः क्लान्ताः पान विप्रहताः तथा ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘अतः अवश्य ही यह सीता नहीं, कोई दूसरी स्त्री है ।’ ऐसा निश्चय करके वे कपिश्रेष्ठ सीताजीके दर्शनके लिये उत्सुक हो पुनः वहाँकी मधुशालामें विचरने लगे ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आदुदरिन्द इल्लि मलगिरुववळु ‘‘सीतादेवियन्तू अल्लवे अल्ल’’ ऎन्दु निश्चयिसिकॊण्डु आ कपिवरनु सीता सन्दर्शनक्कागि उत्साहदिन्द अन्तःपुरदल्लि हागू पानगृहगळल्लि पुनः हुडुकतॊडगिदनु.॥4॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

4. aparaaH = some women; klaantaaH = were tired; kriiDitena = from erotic dalliance; tathaa = and then; aparaaH = some other women; giitena = (were tired) from singing; aparaaH = some others; klaantaaH = were weary; nR^ittena = from dance; tathaa = and; paanaviprahataaH = were unconscious from consumption of liquor.

अनुवादः

Some women were tired from erotic dalliance and then some other women were tired from singing; some others were weary from dance and were unconscious from consumption of liquor.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Ascertaining (niścitya), “This woman is someone else (anyeyam),” that foremost of monkeys (hariśreṣṭhaḥ), eager to behold Sītā (sītāsaṃdarśanotsukaḥ), roamed once more through that banqueting hall (pānabhūmau).

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

वहाँ कोई स्त्रियाँ क्रीड़ा करनेसे थकी हुई थीं तो कोई गीत गानेसे । दूसरी नृत्य करके थक गयी थीं और कितनी ही स्त्रियाँ अधिक मद्यपान करके अचेत हो रही थीं ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आ पानभूमियल्लि कॆलवु स्त्रीयरु क्रीडाविहारदिन्द बळलि मलगिद्दरु. कॆलवरु हाडि-हाडि आयासगॊण्डु मलगिद्दरु. इन्नू कॆलवरु नृत्यमाडि दणिदु मलगिद्दरु. मत्तू कॆलवरु अतियाद मधुपानदिन्द प्रज्ञाशून्यरागि पवडिसिद्दरु.॥5॥

Some women were stationed on tabors, on Mrudangas, on seats, some other women rested on chief carpets.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

[There he saw] some women exhausted by sporting (krīḍitena klāntāḥ), others likewise by singing (gītena), still others wearied by dancing (nṛtyena), and others overcome by drink (pānaviprahatāḥ).

बहुत-सी स्त्रियाँ ढोल, मृदंग और चेलिका नामक वाद्योंपर अपने अंगोंको टेककर सो गयी थीं तथा दूसरी महिलाएँ अच्छे-अच्छे बिछौनोंपर सोयी हुई थीं ॥ ६ ॥

कॆलवरु डोलु-मृदङ्ग वाद्यगळन्नु नुडिसुवुदरल्लि आसक्तरागि निद्दॆहोगिद्दरु. कॆलवरु श्रेष्ठवाद हंसतूलिका तल्पगळल्लि मलगिद्दरु.॥6॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

And [he beheld] other women resting (saṃsthitāḥ) upon muraja drums (murajeṣu), mṛdaṅga tabors (mṛdaṅgeṣu), and celikā instruments (celikāsu), as well as reclining (saṃviṣṭāḥ) upon choice carpets (āstaraṇamukhyeṣu).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Returning from the banquet floor toward Rāvaṇa’s couch, Hanumān beholds a thousand accomplished women slumbering after amorous sport around the demon king, who shines like a bull or royal tusker.

वानरयूथपति हनुमान् जी ने उस पानभूमिको ऐसी सहस्रों रमणियोंसे संयुक्त देखा, जो भाँति-भाँतिके आभूषणोंसे विभूषित, रूप-लावण्यकी चर्चा करनेवाली, गीतके समुचित अभिप्रायको अपनी वाणीद्वारा प्रकट करनेवाली, देश और कालको समझनेवाली, उचित बात बोलनेवाली और रति-क्रीड़ामें अधिक भाग लेनेवाली थीं ॥ ७-८ ॥

अन्तःपुरद समीपदल्लिये इद्द पानगृहगळल्लियू साविरारु अङ्गनॆयरु इद्दरु. अवरॆल्लरू सुन्दरवाद हागू श्रेष्ठवाद आभरणगळिन्द समलङ्कृतरागिद्दरु. तम्म रूप सौन्दर्यगळन्नु बगॆ-बगॆयागि वर्णिसि हेळुव स्वभाव दवरागिद्दरु. अन्तॆये रावणन रूपवन्नु, वीर्य-पराक्रमगळन्नु नाना रीतियिन्द बण्णिसुत्तिद्दरु. तत्कालोचितवाद गीतॆगळन्नु हाडि अवुगळ अर्थवन्नु हेळुत्तिद्दरु. ऎल्लरू देश-कालोचितवागि मातनाडुव कुशलतॆयिन्द कूडिद्दरु.॥7-8॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

अनुवादः

The warrior among Vanaras saw a thousand of women decorated by jewellery with a nature of talking about beauty, conversing about the correct meaning of songs being sung, behaving according to time and place, wont to speak appropriate words, indulged in sleep after sexual dalliance.

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

5.11.1 (The Recovery of Discernment)

On 5.11.1, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) glosses तां बुद्धिं as Hanumān’s momentary suspicion regarding Sītā (सीतासंभावनया प्रवृत्तां) and explains अवस्थितः as regaining steadiness of mind (स्थिरतास्वभावे प्रतिष्ठितः). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) similarly defines तां बुद्धिं as इयं सीतेत्येतादृशीं बुद्धिम् (the notion that she is Sītā'), while Govindarāja (_Bhūṣaṇa_) glosses अवस्थितो बभूव as स्वस्थितो बभूव (he became self-possessed’).

5.11.2–4 (Sītā’s Incomparable Fidelity)

Śivasahāya explains उपस्थातुं (5.11.3a) as sitting near another man with a calm mind (स्वस्थचित्ततया समीपे आसितुं), while Rāma Varmā renders it अङ्गीकर्तुं (to accept'). Because Govindarāja omits the second half of 5.11.4 and reads पानभूमौ in 5.11.4a, he glosses पानभूमौ as ततोऽन्यत्रेत्यर्थः (somewhere other than Rāvaṇa’s bed’), whereas Rāma Varmā takes it specifically as the drinking pavilion (पानशालायां).

5.11.5–6 (Slumbering Consorts in the Banquet Hall)

Govindarāja connects verses 5 and 6 into a single syntactic sentence (एकं वाक्यम्) by supplying the verb ददर्श (ददर्शेत्यध्याह्रियते), notes that क्रीडितेन is an impersonal passive participle used as an action noun (भावे निष्ठा), and glosses विप्रहताः as क्लान्ताः and संस्थिताः as sleeping while using the drums as pillows (उपधानीकृत्य शयिताः). Rāma Varmā identifies चेलिका as a specific musical instrument (वाद्यविशेषः).

5.11.7–8 (Accomplishments of the Royal Consorts)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construe verses 7 and 8 as a single sentence (श्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयि) qualifying the banquet hall (पानभूमिं ददर्श), where Rāma Varmā notes that the women’s talk of beauty and fitting speech occur unconsciously in their sleep (निद्रोद्भ्रमवशादेव बोध्यम्). Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa), who reads रताभिरतसंसुप्तं in 8b and omits verses 9–10, takes Rāvaṇa himself (रावणमिति शेषः) as the direct object of ददर्श.

5.11.8b (Govindarāja’s Erotic Analysis)

Govindarāja distinguishes the two members of his compound रताभिरतसंसुप्तं: रत denotes external amorous play (बाह्यरतिरुच्यते), while अभिरत denotes intimate union (करणबन्धादिकमुच्यते), forming an elliptical compound of the śākapārthivādi class (शाकपार्थिवादित्वाद्विभक्तिलोपि समासः) meaning `asleep from the fatigue of amorous sport’ (क्रीडाखेदेन सुप्तमित्यर्थः).

5.11.9–12 (Textual Status of 9–10 and the Two Similes)

Both Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya explicitly record that verses 9–10 (अन्यत्रापीत्यादिश्लोकद्वयं) are read out of negligence by modern scribes (नव्याः प्रमादात् पठन्ति); for those who retain them, Rāma Varmā explains the scene as Hanumān returning from the outer banquet hall to Rāvaṇa’s inner sleeping chamber (पुनरभ्यन्तरे शयनस्थाने आगत्यान्वेषणम्), where Rāvaṇa shines among his consorts like a bull among kine or a tusker surrounded (परिकीर्णो परिवृतः) by cow-elephants.

5.11.13–15 (Return to the Banquet Hall and Metonymy)

Explaining why the banquet hall (पानभूमिं) is mentioned again after 5.11.4, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) note that Hanumān had first viewed only the hall and its sleeping women, and now inspects the specific viands laid out within it (तत्रस्थपदार्थकथनाय). Govindarāja remarks that animal names like मयूरान् refer metonymically to their dressed meats (मयूरादिशब्दाः मयूरादिपिशिते लक्षणाः) placed in heaps (भागशः पिण्डशः), while Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) glosses अभक्षितान् as untouched (अवशिष्टानित्यर्थः).

5.11.16 (Three Identifications of Vādhrīṇasa)

All three commentators cite legal and lexical authorities to identify वाध्रीणस: Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya quote the Viṣṇudharmottara (कृष्णग्रीवो रक्तशिराः श्वेतपक्षो विहंगमः) for a bird species, a Smṛti text (त्रिपिबं त्विन्द्रियक्षीणं… वाध्रीणसं च ते प्राहुर्याज्ञिकाः) for an old goat whose tongue and two ears touch water while drinking (जिह्वाकर्णौ च पानसमये जलं स्पृशन्ति), and Halāyudha (वाध्रीणसः खड्गमृगः) for a rhinoceros; Govindarāja adds Amara’s glosses on शल्यान् (श्वाविदः, porcupines) and सौवर्चल (रुचकाख्यो लवणविशेषः).

5.11.17–18 (Culinary Glosses on Rāga and Khāṇḍava)

All three gloss एकशल्यान् as single-boned fish (मत्स्यविशेषान्) and कृतनिष्ठितान् as thoroughly cooked (पर्याप्तपक्वान्). For रागखाण्डवैः (Govindarāja रागषाडवैः), all three quote the culinary maxim सितामध्वादिमधुरो द्राक्षादाडिमजो रसः । विरलश्चेत्कृतो रागः सान्द्रश्चेत्खाण्डवः स्मृतः: sweetened grape or pomegranate juice is called rāga when liquid and khāṇḍava (or ṣāḍava) when thick, seasoned with sour and salt (अम्ललवणोत्तंसैः) to dispel sluggishness of the tongue (जिह्वाजाड्यपरिहारकत्वं).

5.11.19–21ab (The Banquet Hall Ablaze Without Fire)

Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) links 19–20ab as one sentence (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी) describing how the floor, strewn with cast-off ornaments (अपविद्धैः विस्रस्तैः), scattered drinking vessels, fruits, and floral offerings (पुष्पबलिः), nourishes heightened beauty (अधिकां श्रियं पुष्यति). In 20cd–21ab, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) explains that the banquet hall appears ablaze even without fire (वह्निं विनापि प्रदीप्तेवोपलक्ष्यते) because its well-fitted (सुश्लिष्टैः सुकृतसंधिबन्धैः) couches and seats are wrought of gold and gems (सुवर्णरत्नमयशयनासनैः).

5.11.21cd–22 (Dressed Meats and Natural vs. Brewed Liquors)

Rāma Varmā glosses बहुप्रकारैः as meats of diverse quadrupeds and birds (चतुष्पादादिभेदैः), वरसंस्कारसंस्कृतैः as seasoned with choice culinary ingredients (श्रेष्ठसंस्कारकद्रव्ययोगेन—Śivasahāya specifies camphor and musk, कर्पूरादिभिः), and कुशलसंयुक्तैः as cooked by expert chefs (निपुणपाचकपक्वैः). He distinguishes दिव्याः सुराः—natural wines springing spontaneously from kadamba and other trees (वृक्षेभ्यः कदम्बादिभ्यः स्वतः प्रादुर्भूताः; Govindarāja: अमृतमथनोद्भूतवारुणीजातीयाः)—from कृतसुराः, liquors brewed by distillers (शौण्डिकैरुत्पादिताः).

5.11.23–24 (The Four Classes of Āsava Wines)

All three commentators classify the four brewed wines (कृतसुरावान्तरभेदाः): शर्करासव (distilled from cane sugar), माध्वीक (from honey, मधुजनितः, or grapes, द्राक्षोपादानकः), पुष्पासव (from madhūka blossoms, मधूकपुष्पादिजः), and फलासव (from dates and other fruits, खर्जूरफलादिजः), each scented with fragrant powders (वासचूर्णैः सुगन्धद्रव्यचूर्णैर्मृष्टाः अधिवासिताः).

5.11.25–26 (Vessels of Silver, Gold, and Gems)

Concluding his note on हिरण्मयैः (24c), Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) cites the lexical rule कृताकृतं हेमरूप्यं हिरण्यमभिधीयते to interpret हिरण्मयैः as wrought of silver (रजतमयैः), avoiding tautology with जाम्बूनदमयैः (golden') in 25a. On 5.11.25–26, Rāma Varmā (_Tilaka_) and Śivasahāya (_Śiromaṇi_) construe पानश्रेष्ठां भूमिं as the floor where the finest drinks stand in silver and golden jars (राजतादिषु कुम्भेषु पानानि श्रेष्ठानि यस्यां तां भूमिं), while सीधोः (of rum’) governs भाजनानि (`vessels’) in verse 27.

5.11.27–28 (Goblets Untouched, Half-Drained, and Apportioned)

All three commentators note that पानानि in 28ab denotes drinking vessels (पानपात्राणि / पानभाजनानि) by metonymy (नैव प्रपीतानि पूर्णाम्रसवान्येव). Reading क्वचित्पानं विभागशः in 28d, Rāma Varmā explains that the liquors were apportioned separately for specific guests (इदममुकमिदममुकमिति विभज्य ददर्शेत्यर्थः).

5.11.29–30 (Empty Couches and Slumbering Embraces)

Rāma Varmā explains why the women’s own couches stood empty (शून्यानि पतिशून्यानि): bereft of their lord, they had gathered on shared couches and fallen asleep embracing one another (अत एव परस्परं समाश्लिष्येति). In verse 30, a woman overcome by sleep (निद्राबलपराजिता) approaches another’s couch (तच्छयनस्थानं प्राप्य), snatches her companion’s garment (बलाद्गृहीत्वा), and sleeps embracing either the garment (प्रावृत्य, Tilaka) or the companion herself (तामालिङ्ग्य, Śiromaṇi).

5.11.31–33ab (Breathing Zephyrs and the Puṣpaka Breeze)

All three commentators gloss गात्रजं (31b) idiomatically as resting upon the limbs (गात्रस्थम्), while the garments and garlands barely flutter (अत्यर्थं न स्पन्दते) in the soft breath-wind of the sleeping women. On 32–34ab, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) notes that Hanumān now steps out of the banquet hall onto the Puṣpaka terrace (पानभूमितोऽपि बहिरागत्य तत्र स्थिते पुष्पके), or else that the banquet hall itself lies within Puṣpaka (पानभूम्यादिकमपि पुष्पकस्थमेवेत्यन्ये).

5.11.33cd (Rasānām vs. Snānānām)

Both Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) read रसानां चन्दनानां (`of culinary juices and sandal-pastes’), while Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya record the reading स्नानानां चन्दनानाम्, explaining it as an elliptical compound for bath-unguents and sandal-woods worthy of the bath (स्नानार्हचन्दनानामित्यर्थः). All three gloss मूर्च्छितः as thickened or pervasive (प्रवृद्धः / व्याप्तः).

5.11.34cd–36 (The Simile of Slumbering Night-Lotuses)

Govindarāja notes that in श्यामावदाताः the initial a- of अवदाताः (`fair, white’) is elided according to Bhāguri’s rule (भागुरिमतेनाकारलोपः शुक्ला इत्यर्थः). Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya link 35–36ab as a single sentence (श्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयि): overcome by sleep and heightened passion (मदनेन तद्धेतुकातिरत्या विमूर्च्छितं कान्तं), the women’s beauty resembled lotus-clusters folded in slumber at night (रात्रौ संकुचितानां पद्मिनीनामिव).

5.11.37–39 (Moral Scruple and the Sight of Another’s Consorts)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) traces Hanumān’s sudden moral alarm (धर्मसाध्वसशङ्कितः धर्मलोपनिमित्तं यत्साध्वसं भयं तेन शङ्कितः) to his recollection of the scriptural injunction नेक्षेत नग्नां परस्त्रियम् (`One must not look upon another’s wife undressed’), since in sleep their garments had slipped (प्रसुप्तस्य अनेन नग्नप्रायत्वं सूचितम्). On 39cd (परदारपरिग्रहः), Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) takes it as Rāvaṇa’s harem (परदाररूपान्तःपुरस्य), whereas Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) also interpret it as Rāvaṇa himself, the sinful abductor of another’s wife (सीतापहारकः अयं रावणो मया दृष्टः).

5.11.40–41 (Resolution of Scruple Through Purity of Mind)

Rāma Varmā glosses निश्चितैकान्तचित्तस्य as one whose mind rests on a settled philosophical conclusion (निश्चिते प्रमाणप्रतिपन्ने एकान्ते सिद्धान्ते चित्तं यस्य), while Śivasahāya explains it as a mind unwaveringly resolved that fulfilling his master’s mission entails no fault (स्वामिकार्यस्यासाध्यत्वाच्चैतद्दर्शनमपि न दोषावहमिति). All three gloss विश्वस्ताः (41b) as sleeping without fear of strangers (इतरालोकनशङ्कारहिताः) and वैकृत्यं as passion or mental perturbation (विकारः).

5.11.42 (The Mind as Arbiter of Merit and Sin)

Expounding the ethical maxim मनो हि हेतुः, Rāma Varmā remarks that sense-activity leads to sin only when impelled by a mind tainted by passion or hatred (मनःक्षोभजतदुभयरागद्वेषवत इन्द्रियप्रवृत्तिर्हि पापाय), whereas in matters of merit and sin (पुण्यपापव्यवस्थायां) one’s own unperturbed conscience is the witness (स्वचित्तमेव साक्षीति भावः).

5.11.43–45 (Searching Within the Same Species)

Anticipating the objection that even a self-controlled sage should avoid the proximity of sleeping women (ननु वशीकृतेन्द्रियाणामपि स्त्रीसन्निधावागमनमनुचितम्, Śiromaṇi), Hanumān invokes the pragmatic necessity (अनन्यथासिद्धत्व, Tilaka) of his royal commission: a lost creature must be sought among its own species (सजातीयेष्वेव मार्गणं प्रामाणिकम्), where योनिः denotes class (योनिः जातिः सजातीय इत्यर्थः, Bhūṣaṇa), for a lost woman cannot be sought among does (मृगीषु). Rāma Varmā adds that this necessity also absolves him of the fault of having looked upon Rāvaṇa (एतेन रावणदर्शनजोऽपि दोषः परिहृतः).

5.11.44cd (Impersonal Neuter Construction)

Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) notes the impersonal neuter syntax (सामान्ये नपुंसकम्) of न शक्यं प्रमदा नष्टा मृगीषु परिमार्गितुम्, contrasting with the personal feminine agreement शक्या वैदेही in 43ab.

5.11.46–48 (Departure from the Banquet Hall)

Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) points out the epic ātmanepada form अपश्यत for standard अपश्यत् (अपश्यत अपश्यत्). In 47cd–48, Rāma Varmā explains अपक्रम्य as stepping out of the inner apartment into the outer court (रावणान्तःपुरबहिःकक्षात्), while Govindarāja, reading तद्विचेतुं in 48d, construes तत् as referring to the remaining sections of Rāvaṇa’s palace complex (तत् रावणान्तःपुरम्).

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

फिर तो श्रीमान् पवनकुमारने उस पानभूमिको छोड़कर अन्य सब स्थानोंमें उन्हें बड़े यत्नका आश्रय लेकर खोजना आरम्भ किया ॥ ४८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

वीरनाद हनुमन्तनु अल्लॆल्लू सीतादेवियन्नु काणदॆ, इतर श्रेष्ठराद स्त्रीयरन्ने अनुलक्षिसुत्ता निराशनागि अल्लिन्द हॊरडलनुवादनु. सर्व शुभ लक्षणगळिन्द कूडिद्द मारुतियु आ पान भूमियिन्द निर्गमिसि, तन्न प्रयत्नदल्लि कृतनिश्चयवुळ्ळवनु पुनः आ रावणान्तःपुरदल्लि सीतादेवियन्नु हुडुकुत्ता सागिदनु.॥48-49॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये सुन्दरकाण्डे एकादशः सर्गः ॥ ११ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके सुन्दरकाण्डमें ग्यारहवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ ११ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

महर्षिवाल्मीकि विरचित आदिकाव्यवाद श्रीमद्रामायणद सुन्दरकाण्डदल्लि हन्नॊन्दनॆय सर्गवु मुगियितु.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Leaving behind that banqueting hall (āpānabhūmim utsṛjya), the glorious son of Marut (śrīmān mārutiḥ), resorting once more to strenuous effort on every side (bhūyaḥ sarvataḥ yatnam āśritaḥ), set about searching for her (tāṃ vicetuṃ pracakrame).
