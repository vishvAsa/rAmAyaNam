Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy - Intro

Book II : Ayodhya Kanda - Book Of Ayodhya

In this chapter, Sumantra enters the royal court and observes all the arrangements made for the coronation of Sri Rama. Ordered by Dasaratha to fetch Sri Rama, he enters the mighty building of Sri Rama.

भागसूचना
  1. सुमन्त्रका राजाकी आज्ञासे श्रीरामको बुलानेके लिये उनके महलमें जाना
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

At daybreak under the Puṣya asterism and Rāma’s natal Cancer ascendant, Veda-versed Brahmins, ministers, and guild chiefs assemble with golden pitchers, a tiger-skin chariot, and sacred waters.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ते तु तां रजनीमुष्य ब्राह्मणा वेदपारगाः ।
उपतस्थुरुपस्थानं सह राजपुरोहिताः ॥ १ ॥

मूलम्

उपतस्थुरुपस्थानं सह राजपुरोहिताः ॥ १ ॥

मूलम् - VR

ते तु ताम् रजनीम् उष्य ब्राह्मणा वेद पारगाः । उपतस्थुर् उपस्थानम् सह राज पुरोहिताः ॥ २-१५-१

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

वे वेदोंके पारङ्गत ब्राह्मण तथा राजपुरोहित वह रात बिताकर प्रातःकाल (राजाकी प्रेरणाके अनुसार) राजद्वारपर उपस्थित हुए थे ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

राजन प्रेरणॆयन्तॆ अनेक वेदपारङ्गत ब्राह्मणरु, पुरोहितरु बॆळगागुत्तले राजद्वारदल्लि बन्दु सेरिद्दरु.॥1॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

1. te = these; brahmaNaaH = brahmanas; vedapaaragaaH = skilled in vedas; ushhya = stayed; taam rajiniim = for whole night; upatasthuH = and arrived; upasthaanam = at nearby place; saha raaja purohitaaH = along with royal priest; Vasista.

अनुवादः

Those brahmans skilled in Vedas stayed there all the night and arrived at a nearby place along with the royal priest, Vashishta.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having passed that night (tāṃ rajanīm uṣya), those Brahmins who had mastered the Vedas (veda-pāragāḥ), together with the royal family priests (saha-rāja-purohitāḥ), attended the royal assembly hall (upatasthur upasthānam).

अमात्या बलमुख्याश्च मुख्या ये निगमस्य च ।
राघवस्याभिषेकार्थे प्रीयमाणाः सुसङ्गताः ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

मन्त्री, सेनाके मुख्य-मुख्य अधिकारी और बड़े-बड़े सेठ-साहूकार श्रीरामचन्द्रजीके अभिषेकके लिये बड़ी प्रसन्नताके साथ वहाँ एकत्र हुए थे ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मन्त्रिगळु, मुख्य-मुख्य सैन्याधिकारिगळु, दॊड्ड-दॊड्ड वर्तकरु श्रीरामन पट्टाभिषेकक्कागि बहळ सन्तोषदिन्द अल्लि नॆरॆदिद्दरु.॥2॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

2. amaatyaaH = ministers; balamukhyaasheha = army chiefs; * ye* = which; mukyaaH = prominent leaders; nigamasya = of the city (those people); samgataaH = assembled; priyamaaNaaH = welcoming; abhishhekaarthe = cause of Rama’s coronation.

अनुवादः

Ministers, army chiefs, prominent leaders of the city assembled there, welcoming the cause of Rama’s coronation.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

The ministers (amātyāḥ), the commanders of the army (bala-mukhyāś ca), and the foremost leaders of the merchant guilds (mukhyā ye nigamasya ca) gathered together in high delight (prīyamāṇāḥ susaṃgatāḥ) for the sake of Rāghava’s consecration.

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

निर्मल सूर्योदय होनेपर दिनमें जब पुष्यनक्षत्रका योग आया तथा श्रीरामके जन्मका कर्क लग्न उपस्थित हुआ, उस समय श्रेष्ठ ब्राह्मणोंने श्रीरामके अभिषेकके लिये सारी सामग्री एकत्र करके उसे जँचाकर रख दिया । जलसे भरे हुए सोनेके कलश, भलीभाँति सजाया हुआ भद्रपीठ, चमकीले व्याघ्रचर्मसे अच्छी तरह आवृत रथ, गङ्गा-यमुनाके पवित्र सङ्गमसे लाया हुआ जल—ये सब वस्तुएँ एकत्र कर ली गयी थीं ॥ ३—५ ॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

3. raamasya = Rama’s; abhishhekaaya = coronation; upakalpitam = arranged by; dvijendraiH = the best of brahmanas; udite = after rising; suurye = of sun; vimale = clearly; abhyaagate = on arrival of ; pushhyecha = pushyami star; praapte = and after arrival of; raamasya = Rama’s; janma = birth; sthite = position; karkatakalagne = of auspicious time of cancer.

अनुवादः

The best of brahmanas were prepared to do Rama’s coronation in day-time after the sun rises clearly at the arrival of Pushya star and on arrival of Rama’s auspicious birth time of Cancer.

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

निर्मल सूर्योदयवागि पुष्य नक्षत्रद योग हागू श्रीरामन जन्म कर्काटक लग्न प्राप्तवादाग श्रेष्ठ ब्राह्मणरु श्रीरामन पट्टाभिषेकक्कागि ऎल्ल सामग्रिगळन्नु जॊतॆ सेरिसि, सिद्धवागि इरिसिद्दरु. पुण्यजलदिन्द तुम्बिद स्वर्णकलशगळु, चॆन्नागि अलङ्करिसिद भद्रपीठ, हॊळॆयुत्तिरुव व्याघ्र चर्मवन्नु हॊदिसिद दिव्यरथ, गङ्गा-यमुना सङ्गमदिन्द तन्द पवित्रजल, हीगॆ ऎल्ल वस्तुगळन्नु तन्दु अणिगॊळिसिद्दरु.॥3-5॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

4. kaaJNchanaa = golden; jala kumbhaasheha = water pots; * svalaNkR^itam* = well decorated; bhadrapiitham = throne; rathashcha = and the chariot; aastiirNaH = covered by; bhaaasvataa = shining; vyaaghracharmaNaa = tiger-skin.

अनुवादः

Golden water-pots, well-decorated throne and chariot covered well by shining tiger-skin were impressive.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

3: When the spotless sun had risen (udite vimale sūrye), when the day joined with the Puṣya asterism had arrived (puṣye cābhyāgate ’hani), and when the Cancer ascendant (lagne karkaṭake)—in which Rāma’s nativity stood (janma rāmasya ca sthite)—had come round,

4: everything required for Rāma’s consecration was arrayed by the foremost of the twice-born (dvijendrair upakalpitam): golden water-pitchers (kāñcanā jala-kumbhāś ca) and a splendidly adorned auspicious throne (bhadra-pīṭhaṃ svalaṃkṛtam),

5: and a chariot duly spread over (rathaś ca samyag āstīrṇaḥ) with a gleaming tiger-skin (bhāsvatā vyāghra-carmaṇā), together with water brought from the sacred confluence of the Gaṅgā and the Yamunā (gaṅgā-yamunayoḥ puṇyāt saṃgamāt).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Waters from all oceans, honey, curds, clarified butter, parched grain, eight auspicious maidens, a rutting elephant, golden and silver pitchers, a white yak-tail fan, and the royal parasol stand ready.

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

2.15.1–2 (upatasthur upasthānam & Assembly at Dawn)

Opening Sarga 15, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) notes that the poet describes the assembly at the palace gate (राजद्वारि मिलितानां जनानाम्) as Sumantra proceeds outwardly as before (यथापूर्वं प्रवृत्तिं वदन्). Having passed the night (उष्योषित्वा), the Brahmins reach the उपस्थानम्—the royal gate (राजद्वारम्, Tilaka, Śiromaṇi) or hall (सदः, Bhūṣaṇa). On निगमस्य, Tilaka and Śiromaṇi read guilds (वणिक्समूहः), whereas Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) cites the Vaijayantī (निगमो निश्चये वेदे पुरे पथि वणिक्पथे) for the city (पुरस्य).

2.15.3–4 (karkaṭake lagne & the Golden Bhadrapīṭha)

As the sun rises on Puṣya and Rāma’s natal Karkaṭa ascendant arrives (जन्मकालस्थे कर्कटलग्ने च प्राप्ते, Tilaka), Brahmins array the consecration articles (अभिषेकार्थद्रव्यजातम्, Bhūṣaṇa). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explains neuter उपकल्पितम् collectively (सामान्यविवक्षया नपुंसकत्वैकत्वे) and भद्रपीठम् as an auspicious throne (भद्रलक्षणचिह्नितपीठविशेषः).

2.15.5–6 (prāgvahāḥ & Sacred Waters)

Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) glosses प्राग्वाहाः as east-flowing rivers (गोदावरीकावेर्यादयः), ऊर्ध्ववाहाः as springs or western rivers (प्रत्यग्वाहाः), and तिर्यग्वाहाः as north-south streams (गण्डकीशोणभद्रादयः), while Tilaka takes क्षीरिणः (क्षीरमिव जलम्) as milk-sweet.

2.15.7–8 (kṣaudraṃ dadhi & Pitchers Covered with Milky Shoots)

Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss क्षौद्रम् as honey (मधु), दर्भाः as kuśa grass, and सुमनसः as flowers (पुष्पाणि). On क्षीरिभिः, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śiromaṇi specify milky-tree shoots (क्षीरिवृक्षपल्लवैः), Bhūṣaṇa naming aśvattha and udumbara (अश्वत्थोदुम्बरादिभिः) and noting that specifying full pitchers here after v. 4 involves no redundancy (अत्र तद्विशेष इति न पुनरुक्तिः).

2.15.9–10 (candrāṃśuvikacaprakhyam & the Royal Chowrie)

Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) distinguishes day-lotuses from water-lilies in पद्मोत्पलयुताः (कमलजातिविशेषत्वान्न पौनरुक्त्यम्). Construing v. 9cd with v. 10ab, Śiromaṇi and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explain चन्द्रांशुविकचप्रख्यम् as gleaming with expanded lustre like moonbeams (चन्द्रांशव इव विकचाः विस्तृताः प्रख्या कान्तिर्यस्य) and वालव्यजनम् as the royal chowrie (चामरम्).

2.15.11–12 (abhiṣekapurassaram & Musical Instruments)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) gloss अभिषेकपुरःसरम् as foremost of the consecration articles (अभिषेकसामग्रीषु मुख्यः, Tilaka), while Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) reads अभिषेकपुरस्कृतम् and glosses वादित्राणि as the fourfold instruments headed by the vīṇā (वीणादिचतुर्विधवाद्यानि).

2.15.13–15 (tathājātīyam ādāya & Waiting for the Monarch)

Joining vv. 12–14, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi), and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explain that the Brahmins and tributary kings (महीपतयः, Bhūṣaṇa; ब्राह्मणादयः, Tilaka), bringing consecration articles (तथाजातीयं वस्तुजातम्) to the gate by royal command (राजवचनात्), wonder who will announce them (को नु राज्ञो नः प्रतिवेदयेत्) now that the sun has risen and Rāma’s consecration stands ready (सज्जः = संपन्नसाधनः, Bhūṣaṇa).

2.15.16–17 (rāmaṃ… prasthito hy aham & Sumantra’s Courtesy)

All three supply आनेतुम् after रामम् (राममानेतुमिति शेषः). Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) note that although hastening out, Sumantra pauses because the lords are revered by Daśaratha and Rāma (पूज्या राज्ञो भवन्तश्च), promising to inquire after the king’s sleep (सुखशयनं पृच्छन्तीति विज्ञापयामि, Tilaka) at their word (आयुष्मतां वचनात्).

2.15.18 (anāgamanakāraṇam & purāṇavit)

Sumantra also asks why the awakened king has not come forth (बहिरनागमनकारणं किम्, Tilaka). On पुराणवित्, Tilaka glosses conversant with ancient lore (चिरकालकथाभिज्ञः), whereas Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explains knowing court protocol toward vassals (सामन्तराजादिवर्तनरूपपुरातनवृत्तान्तज्ञः).

2.15.19–20 (sadā saktam & Standing Behind the Curtain)

Sumantra enters the chamber described as सदा सक्तम्—glossed by Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) as ever unbarred to him (अवारितद्वारम्, Tilaka; सर्वदा अनिवारितम्, Bhūṣaṇa), while Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) adds frequented by attendants or celebrated by poets (स्वसेवितमनिवारितम्… कविभिर्निबद्धम्). All three gloss शयनीयम् as the bedchamber (शयनगृहम्) and तिरस्करणिमन्तरा as standing with the curtain (जवनिकाम्) between them (एतेन तावत्पर्यन्तं गमनं सूचितम्, Tilaka).

2.15.21–22 (āśīrbhir guṇayuktābhiḥ & Bardic Praise)

Why does Sumantra praise Daśaratha (राघवम्) again? Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explain that courtly custom requires a sūta to preface any petition with panegyric (राज्ञो विज्ञापनात्पूर्वं स्तुतेः कुलधर्मत्वात्, Bhūṣaṇa; राजविज्ञापनस्य स्तुतिपूर्वकत्वौचित्यात्, Tilaka), invoking Soma, Sūrya, Śiva, Kubera (वैश्रवण), Varuṇa, Agni, and Indra.

2.15.23–24 (kuru kāryam anantaram & the Waiting Estates)

Sumantra bids the king perform the immediate consecration duty (अनन्तरं कार्यमभिषेकरूपम्, Śiromaṇi), reporting that Brahmins, army chiefs, and merchants (नैगमा वणिजः, Tilaka) await his audience (दर्शनं तेऽभिकांक्षन्ते).

2.15.25–27 (yad asy abhihito mayā & Kataka’s Critique)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) glosses प्रतिबुद्ध्य as recognizing Sumantra behind the curtain by his voice (जवनिकान्तर्हितमपि सुमन्त्रं ध्वनिना ज्ञात्वा). Under the variant अभिहितोऽनया (“bidden by her”), Tilaka and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explain ममाज्ञा inclusively (उपलक्षणं मया चेत्यर्थः), whereas Kataka holds that Kaikeyī’s unopposed order counts as the king’s; Tilaka refutes Kataka’s deletion of Sarga 14’s closing verses by pointing to पुनः (“once again”) in v. 27 (इति राजा इति श्लोके पुनःपदासंगतिः).

2.15.28–29 (manyamānaḥ priyaṃ mahat & Joy on the Highway)

Unaware of the king’s secret grief (हार्दाभिप्रायज्ञानात्, Tilaka), Sumantra deems the summons a great joy (अभिषेकं प्रियं मन्यमानः, Śiromaṇi). Both Tilaka and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) distinguish हृष्टः as horripilation (पुलकितगात्रः, Tilaka) and प्रमुदितः as mental rapture (आनन्दचित्तः).

2.15.30 (rāmādhikaraṇāḥ kathāḥ & Pleonastic hṛṣṭavat)

Sumantra hears talk centered on Rāma (रामाधिकरणाः); all three commentators flag हृष्टवत् as an ārṣa pleonastic suffix वति (स्वार्थे वतिरार्षः, Tilaka, Śiromaṇi, Bhūṣaṇa).

2.15.31–32 (mahākapāṭapihitam & Golden-Pinnacled Architecture)

Because it is early morning, the great outer door-leaves remain shut while only the small wicket-gate is open (प्रातःसमयत्वान्महाकपाटेन पिहितम् स्वल्पद्वारस्यैवोद्घाटितत्वात्, Tilaka, Śiromaṇi), whereas Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) reads महाकपाटविहितम् (“built with great doors”) and cites the Amarakośa for वितर्दिः as a raised pavilion (स्याद्वितर्दिस्तु वेदिका) and तोरणम् as an outer portal (तोरणोऽस्त्री बहिर्द्वारम्). On काञ्चनप्रतिमैकाग्रम्, Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa explain that golden statues adorn its prominent roof-peaks (शिखराग्राणि).

2.15.33–34 (varamālyānām & the Fragrance of Mount Dardura)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) gloss वरमाल्यानां मणिभिः as great central gems set within golden flower-garlands (स्वर्णपुष्पमालानामन्तरालवर्तिभिः महादीप्तिमद्भिर्मणिभिः). All three commentators identify दर्दुरः (दार्दुरम्) as the sandalwood-producing mountain bordering Malaya (मलयसंनिकृष्टचन्दनगिरिः).

2.15.35–36 (sukṛtehāmṛgākīrṇam & Ornamental Reliefs)

Citing the Amarakośa (कोकस्त्वीहामृगो वृकः), all three commentators explain सुकृतेहामृगाकीर्णम् as adorned with golden or sculpted figures of wolves along the pillars and walls (द्वारभित्तिस्तम्भादिषु सुष्ठु रचितैरीहामृगैर्वृकैराकीर्णम्, Bhūṣaṇa) and भक्तिभिः as decorative relief-carvings (पद्मचित्रादिभिरुत्कीर्णम्, Tilaka).

2.15.37–39 (meruśṛṅgasamam & Provincials, Dwarfs, and Kirātas)

Defending the repetition of रामवेश्म ददर्श ह after v. 31, Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) remarks that restating the verb for new epithet clusters is Vālmīki’s signature style (भगवतो वाल्मीकेरियं हि रीतिः), while Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) notes that मेरुगुहासमम् (v. 32) described the interior and मेरुशृङ्गसमम् (v. 37) its height. All three supply उपायनानि with उपादाय (“bringing gifts”), and under कुब्जकैरातकावृतम्, Bhūṣaṇa cites Halāyudha for कैरातकम् as diminutive mountaineers (किरातानां स्वल्पशरीरकाणां समूहः).

2.15.40–41 (varūthinā & the Vaṃśastha Shift)

Shifting to Vaṃśastha, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) gloss राजकुलम् as the royal highway (राजमार्गम्) and वरूथिना via the Amarakośa (रथगुप्तिर्वरूथो ना, Bhūṣaṇa) as a fender-guarded chariot, while समाकुलोल्बणम् means loud with deer and peacocks (प्रचुरतरखगमाधिकस्वनवत्, Tilaka).

2.15.42 (kakṣyās & the Kapiñjalādhikaraṇa Rule)

Passing through the plural courtyards (कक्ष्याः)—fixed at three by the Mīmāṃsā kapiñjalādhikaraṇa maxim (कपिञ्जलाधिकरणन्यायेन, Śiromaṇi)—Sumantra reaches the inner apartment (शुद्धान्तम् = अन्तःपुरम्, Tilaka, Bhūṣaṇa).

2.15.43–45 (abhimaṅgalārthāḥ & Crowds in Koṭis and Parārdhas)

In Upajāti, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi), and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explain that even beyond the three courts (कक्ष्यात्रयात्परं गृहम्, Śiromaṇi) Sumantra hears the benedictions (अभिमङ्गलार्थाः) of citizens busy with Rāma’s consecration. On v. 45, Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa note that कोट्या (10^7) and परार्धैः (10^17) signify the vast multitude (अनेनातिसंमर्दो दर्शितः, Tilaka), while Śiromaṇi sees an implied wonder that Rāma’s gate, like Puṣpaka, accommodates all (द्वारस्य आश्चर्यशक्तिमत्त्वं ध्वनितम्).

2.15.46–47 (śatruñjayaṃ nāgam & Passing the Ministers)

Sumantra spots Rāma’s riding-elephant (रामोपवाह्यम्) named शत्रुञ्जयम् (तद्गुणकं तन्नामानं च, Tilaka), resembling a cloud-topped mountain (महामेघाधिरूढपर्वतसदृशम्), in rut (प्रभिन्नो गर्जितो मत्तः, Amarakośa, Bhūṣaṇa), and defying the goad (अत्यङ्कुशम्), before clearing a path (व्यपोह्य) past the ministers (वल्लभान्).

2.15.48 (makaro yathārṇavam & Seven-Storied Vimānas)

In Vaṃśastha, Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa cite the lexicon (विमानोऽस्त्री देवयाने सप्तभूमौ च सद्मनि) to gloss महाविमान- as celestial cars or seven-storied palaces, which Sumantra enters unchallenged (अवार्यमाणः) like a makara entering the ocean.

स तत्र शुश्राव च हर्षयुक्ता । रामाभिषेकार्थकृता जनानां । नरेंद्रसूनोरभिमंगळार्थाः । सर्वस्य लोकस्य गिरः प्रहृष्टः ॥ २-१५-४४

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

श्रीरामका वह भवन इन्द्रसदनकी शोभाको तिरस्कृत कर रहा था । मृगों और पक्षियोंसे सेवित होनेके कारण उसकी रमणीयता और भी बढ़ गयी थी । सुमन्त्रने उस भवनको देखा । वह अपनी प्रभासे प्रकाशित होनेवाले मेरुगिरिके ऊँचे शिखरकी भाँति सुशोभित हो रहा था ॥ ४४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रामन अरमनॆयु देवेन्द्रन अरमनॆगॆ सदृशवागित्तु. सुमनोहरवागि ध्वनिमाडुव सुन्दरवाद मृग-पक्षिगळिगॆ अदु आश्रयवागित्तु. मेरुपर्वतद शिखरदन्तॆ ऎत्तरवागिद्द आ अनुपम सौन्दर्यदिन्द विराजिसुत्तिद्द रामभवनवन्नु सुमन्त्रनु नोडिदनु.॥44॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

44. tatra = then; saH = he; shushraava = heard; * prahR^ishhTaaH* = delightful; giraH = words; lokasya = of people; raamaabhishhekaartha kR^itaan = about things done for Rama’s coronation; harshayuktaaH = filled with joy; janaanaam = of people; abhimangalaarthaaH = for the sake of prosperity; narendra suunoH = of Rama.

अनुवादः

There, Sumantra heard delightful people talking about Rama’s coronation and the rites to be performed for the sake of Rama’s prosperity.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

And Sumantra beheld Rāma’s lovely mansion (veśma rāmasya ramyam), rivaling the abode of Mahendra (mahendra-sadma-pratimam), frequented by deer and birds (mṛga-pakṣi-juṣṭam), and blazing with splendour like a lofty peak of Mount Meru (meror iva śṛṅgam uccam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

उपस्थितैरञ्जलिकारिभिश्च
सोपायनैर्जानपदैर्जनैश्च ।
कोट्या परार्धैश्च विमुक्तयानैः
समाकुलं द्वारपदं ददर्श ॥ ४५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 उपस्थितैरञ्जलिकारिभिश्च
1 सोपायनैर्जानपदैर्जनैश्च ।
1 कोट्या परार्धैश्च विमुक्तयानैः
1 समाकुलं द्वारपदं ददर्श ॥ ४५ ॥

मूलम् - VR

महेंद्रसद्मप्रतिमं तु वेश्म । रामस्य रम्यं मृगपक्षिजुष्टं । ददर्श मेरोरिव शृंगमुच्चं । विभ्राजमानं प्रभया सुमम्त्रः ॥ २-१५-४५

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस भवनके द्वारपर पहुँचकर सुमन्त्रने देखा—श्रीरामकी वन्दनाके लिये हाथ जोड़े उपस्थित हुए जनपदवासी मनुष्य अपनी सवारियोंसे उतरकर हाथोंमें भाँति-भाँतिके उपहार लिये करोड़ों और परार्धोंकी संख्यामें खड़े थे, जिससे वहाँ बड़ी भारी भीड़ लग गयी थी ॥ ४५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रामभवनद बागिलिगॆ होगि सुमन्त्रनु नोडुत्तानॆ-श्रीरामनन्नु वन्दिसलु कैमुगिदु उपस्थितराद पुरजनरु तम्म वाहनगळिन्द इळिदु बगॆ-बगॆय उडुगॊरॆगळन्नु हिडिदुकॊण्डु कोटि-कोटि सङ्ख्यॆयल्लि नॆरॆदु भारी गद्दलवे उण्टागित्तु.॥45॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

45. sumantraH = Sumantra; dadarsha = saw; ramya = beautiful; raamasya veshma = Rama’s palace; mahendrasadmapratimam = equalling the mansion of Mahendra; mR^igapakShijuShTam = filled wih antelopes (and) birds; shR^ingam eva = like top; uchcha = of high; meroH = Meru mountain; vibhraajamaanam = shining; prabhayaa = with radiance.

अनुवादः

Rama’s palace was as high as top of the Meru mountain, shining with radiance. The mansion was filled with birds and antelopes. It was like Indra’s mansion. Sumantra saw such a beautiful house of Rama.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

He beheld the gateway approach (dvāra-padam) thronged by tens of millions and hundreds of trillions (koṭyā parārdhaiś ca) of country-folk and citizens who had unyoked their vehicles (vimukta-yānaiḥ) and stood waiting with folded hands bearing tribute-offerings (upasthitair añjali-kāribhiś ca sopāyanaiḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततो महामेघमहीधराभं
प्रभिन्नमत्यङ्कुशमत्यसह्यम् ।
रामोपवाह्यं रुचिरं ददर्श
शत्रुञ्जयं नागमुदग्रकायम् ॥ ४६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 ततो महामेघमहीधराभं
1 प्रभिन्नमत्यङ्कुशमत्यसह्यम् ।
1 रामोपवाह्यं रुचिरं ददर्श
1 शत्रुञ्जयं नागमुदग्रकायम् ॥ ४६ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

तदनन्तर उन्होंने श्रीरामकी सवारीमें आनेवाले सुन्दर शत्रुञ्जय नामक विशालकाय गजराजको देखा, जो महान् मेघसे युक्त पर्वतके समान प्रतीत होता था । उसके गण्डस्थलसे मदकी धारा बह रही थी । वह अंकुशसे काबूमें आनेवाला नहीं था । उसका वेग शत्रुओंके लिये अत्यन्त असह्य था । उसका जैसा नाम था, वैसा ही गुण भी था ॥ ४६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

महामेघदिन्द कूडिद पर्वतदन्तॆ इद्द, मदिसिद, अङ्कुश प्रहारवन्नु सहिसद, सुन्दरवाद, शत्रुसैन्यवन्नु भेदिसुव सामर्थ्यवुळ्ळ महाकाय शत्रुञ्जय ऎम्ब श्रीरामन पट्टदानॆयन्नु सुमन्त्रनु अल्लि नोडिदनु.॥46॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

46. dadarsha = saw; dvaarapadam = area of entrance; samaakulam = filled with; kotyaa = crores; parardhaishcha = and millions of; jaanapadaiH = of rural folk; janaishcha = and people; upasthitaaH = who approached; anjalikaaribhisheha = with folded hands; sopaayanaiH = with gifts; vimuktayaanaiH = who left their respective vehicles.

अनुवादः

He saw crores and millions of rural folk and other people with folded hands approaching the area leaving their respective vehicles.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then he beheld the handsome, colossal-bodied elephant named Śatruñjaya (śatruñjayaṃ nāgam udagra-kāyam), Rāma’s royal riding-mount (rāmopavāhyam), resembling a mighty cloud resting upon a mountain (mahā-megha-mahīdharābham), streaming with rut (prabhinnam), defying the goad (atyaṅkuśam), and utterly irresistible (atyasahyam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स्वलङ्कृतान् साश्वरथान् सकुञ्जरा-
नमात्यमुख्यांश्च ददर्श वल्लभान् ।
व्यपोह्य सूतः सहितान् समन्ततः
समृद्धमन्तःपुरमाविवेश ह ॥ ४७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स्वलङ्कृतान् साश्वरथान् सकुञ्जरा-
1 नमात्यमुख्यांश्च ददर्श वल्लभान् ।
1 व्यपोह्य सूतः सहितान् समन्ततः
1 समृद्धमन्तःपुरमाविवेश ह ॥ ४७ ॥

मूलम् - VR

ततो महामेघमहीधराभं । प्रभिन्नमत्यङ्कुशमत्यसह्यम् । रामोपवाह्याम् रुचिरम् ददर्श । शत्रुम्जयं नागमुदग्रकायम् ॥ २-१५-४७

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उन्होंने वहाँ राजाके परम प्रिय मुख्य-मुख्य मन्त्रियोंको भी एक साथ उपस्थित देखा, जो सुन्दर वस्त्राभूषणोंसे विभूषित थे और घोड़े, रथ तथा हाथियोंके साथ वहाँ आये थे । सुमन्त्रने उन सबको एक ओर हटाकर स्वयं श्रीरामके समृद्धिशाली अन्तःपुरमें प्रवेश किया ॥ ४७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

सुन्दर वस्त्राभूषणगळिन्द अलङ्कृतरागि, रथ, कुदुरॆ, आनॆ मॊदलादवुगळॊन्दिगॆ अल्लिगॆ बन्दिरुव राजन परमप्रिय मुख्य-मुख्य मन्त्रिगळू अल्लि उपस्थितरागिरुवुदन्नु नोडिदनु. सुमन्त्रनु अवरॆल्लरन्नु बदिगॆ सरिसि श्रीरामन समृद्धिशालि अन्तःपुरवन्नु प्रवेशिसिदनु.॥47॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

47. tataH = there after; dadarsha = saw; ruchiram = beautiful; naagam = elephant; shatrunjayam = called satrunjayam; udagrakaayam = with highly elevated; raamopavaahyam = Rama’s royal vehichle; mahaamegha mahiidharaabham = like great cloud and mountain;prabhinnam = intoxicated; atyaN^kusham = uncontrollabel; asahyam = intolerable.

अनुवादः

There he saw a beautiful elephant called Satrunjaya with highly elevated body and Rama’s royal conveyance. It was like a great cloud and a mountain. It was intoxicated, uncontrollable and intolerable.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

The charioteer also beheld the king’s beloved chief ministers (amātya-mukhyāṃś ca dadarśa vallabhān), splendidly attired and accompanied by horses, chariots, and elephants (sāśva-rathān sakuñjarān); clearing his way past them where they stood assembled on every side (vyapohya sūtaḥ sahitān samantataḥ), he entered the opulent inner apartment (samṛddham antaḥpuram āviveśa ha).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततोऽद्रिकूटाचलमेघसन्निभं
महाविमानोपमवेश्मसंयुतम् ।
अवार्यमाणः प्रविवेश सारथिः
प्रभूतरत्नं मकरो यथार्णवम् ॥ ४८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततोऽद्रिकूटाचलमेघसन्निभं
महाविमानोपमवेश्मसंयुतम् ।
अवार्यमाणः प्रविवेश सारथिः
प्रभूतरत्नं मकरो यथार्णवम् ॥ ४८ ॥

मूलम् - VR

स्वलंकृतान् सास्वरथान् सकुम्जरा । नमात्यमुखयांश्च ददर्श वल्लभान् । व्यपोह्य सूतः सहितान्समंततः । समृद्धमंतःपुर माविवेश ह ॥ २-१५-४८

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

जैसे मगर प्रचुर रत्नोंसे भरे हुए समुद्रमें बेरोक-टोक प्रवेश करता है, उसी प्रकार सारथि सुमन्त्रने पर्वत-शिखरपर आरूढ़ हुए अविचल मेघके समान शोभायमान महान् विमानके सदृश सुन्दर गृहोंसे संयुक्त तथा प्रचुर रत्न-भण्डारसे भरपूर उस महलमें बिना किसी रोक-टोकके प्रवेश किया ॥ ४८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

हेरळवाद रत्नगळिन्द तुम्बिद्द समुद्रवन्नु यावुदे अड्डि आतङ्कगळिल्लदॆ मॊसळॆयु प्रवेशिसुवन्तॆये सारथि सुमन्त्रनु पर्वत शिखरद मेलॆ आरूढवाद मेघदन्तॆ शोभिसुव, महाविमानदन्तॆ सुन्दर गृहगळिन्द कूडिद हेरळ रत्न भण्डारदिन्द तुम्बिद आ भवनवन्नु यावुदे अड्डि आतङ्कगळिल्लदॆ प्रवेशिसिदनु.॥48॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

48. dadarsha = saw; amaatya mukhyaamshaha = the ministers in chief; vallabhaan = who were beloved to the king; svalankR^itaan = who were well-adorned; saashvarathaan = with horse carriages sakunjaraan = the people; gathered; tataH = on all sides; aviveshaH = entered; samR^iddham = rich; antaHpuram = inner apartment.

अनुवादः

The ministers in chief who were beloved to the king, were well-adorned and came there on horse-carriages and elephants. Sumantra passed over the people gathered there on all sides and entered the rich inner apartment.

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्येऽयोध्याकाण्डे पञ्चदशः सर्गः ॥ १५ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके अयोध्याकाण्डमें पंद्रहवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥१५ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद अयोध्याकाण्डदल्लि हदिनैदनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु.॥15॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then, unrestrained by any warder (avāryamāṇaḥ), the charioteer entered that mansion—which resembled a motionless cloud resting upon a mountain peak (adri-kūṭācala-megha-sannibham), was joined with seven-storied palaces rivaling great celestial cars (mahā-vimānopama-veśma-saṃyutam), and abounded in jewels—even as a makara sea-monster enters the jewel-teeming ocean (prabhūta-ratnaṃ makaro yathārṇavam).
