Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy - Intro

Book II : Ayodhya Kanda - Book Of Ayodhya

In this chapter, Sumantra arrives at Rama’s palace to fetch him to royal court. Rama starts to the royal court to meet his father. On the way Rama observes the jubiliant city.

भागसूचना
  1. सुमन्त्रका श्रीरामके महलमें पहुँचकर महाराजका संदेश सुनाना और श्रीरामका सीतासे अनुमति ले लक्ष्मणके साथ रथपर बैठकर गाजेबाजेके साथ मार्गमें स्त्री-पुरुषोंकी बातें सुनते हुए जाना
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Passing the crowded outer gate into a secluded court guarded by spear-bearing youths and aged saffron-robed chamberlains, Sumantra asks that his arrival be announced to Rāma.

स तदन्तःपुरद्वारं समतीत्य जनाकुलम् ।
प्रविविक्तां ततः कक्ष्यामाससाद पुराणवित् ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

पुरातन वृत्तान्तोंके ज्ञाता सूत सुमन्त्र मनुष्योंकी भीड़से भरे हुए उस अन्तःपुरके द्वारको लाँघकर महलकी एकान्तकक्षामें जा पहुँचे, जहाँ भीड़ बिलकुल नहीं थी ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

हिन्दिन वृत्तान्तवन्नु बल्ल सुमन्त्रनु जनरिन्द निबिडवागिद्द अरमनॆय हॊराङ्गणवन्नु दाटि जनबाहुळ्यविल्लद अरमनॆय ऒळाङ्गणवन्नु प्रवेशिसिदनु.॥1॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having passed beyond that gate of the inner apartment which was thronged with people (tad antaḥpura-dvāraṃ samatītya janākulam), Sumantra, knower of ancient lore (purāṇavit), then reached a secluded courtyard (praviviktāṃ tataḥ kakṣyām),

अप्रमादिभिरेकाग्रैः स्वानुरक्तैरधिष्ठिताम् ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

वहाँ श्रीरामके चरणोंमें अनुराग रखनेवाले एकाग्रचित्त एवं सावधान युवक प्रास और धनुष आदि लिये डटे हुए थे । उनके कानोंमें शुद्ध सुवर्णके बने हुए कुण्डल झलमला रहे थे ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

श्रीरामन अरमनॆय ऒळाङ्गणदल्लि प्रास, धनुस्सुगळन्नु, परिशुद्धवाद कर्णकुण्डलगळन्नु धरिसिद्द, रक्षणॆयल्ले एकाग्र चित्तरागिद्द, जागरूकरागिद्द, श्रीरामनल्ले हॆच्चु अनुरक्तरागिद्द युवकरु निन्तिद्दरु.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

which was guarded by youths bearing darts and bows (prāsa-kārmuka-bibhradbhiḥ), wearing burnished earrings (mṛṣṭa-kuṇḍalaiḥ), ever vigilant (apramādibhiḥ), single-minded (ekāgraiḥ), and deeply devoted to their lord (svānuraktaiḥ).

ददर्श विष्ठितान् द्वारि स्त्र्यध्यक्षान् सुसमाहितान् ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस ड्यौढ़ीमें सुमन्त्रको गेरुआ वस्त्र पहने और हाथमें छड़ी लिये वस्त्राभूषणोंसे अलंकृत बहुत-से वृद्ध पुरुष बड़ी सावधानीके साथ द्वारपर बैठे दिखायी दिये, जो अन्तःपुरकी स्त्रियोंके अध्यक्ष (संरक्षक) थे ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

काषाय वस्त्रगळन्नु धरिसि बॆत्तगळन्नु हिडिदु वस्त्राभूषणगळिन्द अलङ्कृतराद अनेक वृद्धराद अन्तःपुरद अधिकारिगळु द्वारदल्लि कुळितिरुवुदन्नु सुमन्त्रनु नोडिदनु.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

There at the doorway he beheld aged men attired in saffron-red garments (kāṣāyiṇo vṛddhān), holding rattan canes in their hands (vetra-pāṇīn), splendidly adorned (svalaṃkṛtān), attentive, and appointed as superintendents over the women’s apartments (stry-adhyakṣān susamāhitān).

ते समीक्ष्य समायान्तं रामप्रियचिकीर्षवः ।
सहसोत्पतिताः सर्वे ह्यासनेभ्यः ससम्भ्रमाः ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

सुमन्त्रको आते देख श्रीरामका प्रिय करनेकी इच्छावाले वे सभी पुरुष सहसा वेगपूर्वक आसनोंसे उठकर खड़े हो गये ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

सुमन्त्रनु बन्दिरुवुदन्नु नोडि सम्भ्रमयुक्तराद श्रीरामन प्रियवन्नु माडुव आ ऎल्ल पुरुषरु सट्टनॆ आसनगळिन्द ऎद्दु निन्तरु.॥4॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

अनुवादः

All of them, the well-wishers of Rama, saw that Sumantra arriving and raised from their seats hurriedly at once.

Beholding him approaching (samīkṣya samāyāntam), and desiring to do what was pleasing to Rāma (rāma-priya-cikīrṣavaḥ), all of them sprang up at once from their seats in eager haste (sahasotpatitāḥ sarve hy āsanebhyaḥ sasambhramāḥ).

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

राजसेवामें अत्यन्त कुशल तथा विनीत हृदयवाले सूतपुत्र सुमन्त्रने उनसे कहा—‘आपलोग श्रीरामचन्द्रजीसे शीघ्र जाकर कहें, कि सुमन्त्र दरवाजेपर खड़े हैं’ ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

राजसेवॆयल्लि अत्यन्त कुशल, विनीत हृदयवुळ्ळ सूतपुत्र सुमन्त्रनु द्वारपालकरल्लि ‘सुमन्त्रनु द्वारदल्लि निन्तिरुवनु ऎन्दु श्रीरामचन्द्रनिगॆ बेगने तिळिसिरि’ ऎन्दु हेळिदनु.॥5॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

To them the humble-minded and courtly son of a charioteer (vinītātmā sūta-putraḥ pradakṣiṇaḥ) spoke: “Quickly announce to Rāma that Sumantra stands at the door (sumantro dvāri tiṣṭhati)!”

ते राममुपसङ्गम्य भर्तुः प्रियचिकीर्षवः ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

स्वामीका प्रिय करनेकी इच्छावाले वे सब सेवक श्रीरामचन्द्रजीके पास जा पहुँचे । उस समय श्रीराम अपनी धर्मपत्नी सीताके साथ विराजमान थे । उन सेवकोंने शीघ्र ही उन्हें सुमन्त्रका संदेश सुना दिया ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

ऒडॆयन प्रिय माडुव आ ऎल्ल सेवकरु श्रीरामचन्द्रन बळिगॆ होदरु. आग श्रीरामनु धर्मपत्नियाद सीतॆयॊन्दिगॆ विराजिसुत्तिद्दनु. आ सेवकरु शीघ्रवागि समन्त्रन सन्देशवन्नु रामनिगॆ तिळिसिदरु.॥6॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

2.16.1–2 (praviviktāṃ kakṣyām & the Vigilant Youths)

Opening Sarga 16, Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) notes the transition from Sumantra’s arrival at Rāma’s palace to Rāma’s procession toward Daśaratha. Passing the crowded outer portal, Sumantra reaches the secluded inner court (प्रविविक्तां जनबाहुल्यरहिताम्, Śiromaṇi). On प्रासकार्मुकबिभ्रद्भिः, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explain the compound through an objective genitive (प्रासकार्मुकाणां बिभ्रद्भिः… कर्मणः संबन्धसामान्यत्वेन विवक्षणात् षष्ठी).

2.16.3–4 (kāṣāyiṇo vṛddhān & the Aged Chamberlains)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) gloss काषायिणः as wearing safflower-dyed red garments (कौसुम्भादिकषायरक्तवस्त्रधारिणः), while Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) vividly describes them as ninety-five-year-old chamberlains (पञ्चनवतिवर्षवयस्कान्) whose adornment (स्वलंकृतान्) comes from the sandal, musk, and saffron transferred when Rāma and Sītā embrace them during morning bows.

2.16.5–6 (pradakṣiṇaḥ & Immediate Announcement)

All three commentators gloss प्रदक्षिणः as skilled in courtly service (सेवानिपुणः, Tilaka, Bhūṣaṇa, citing Amarakośa दक्षिणः सरलोदारौ). Supplying इति after तिष्ठति (इतिकरणं द्रष्टव्यम्, Bhūṣaṇa), the chamberlains report Sumantra’s presence to Rāma seated with Sītā (सहभार्याय).

2.16.7–8 (abhyantaram pituḥ & the Golden Couch)

Knowing Sumantra to be his father’s confidential counselor (पितुरभ्यन्तरमत्यन्तमन्तरङ्गम्, Tilaka, Śiromaṇi, Bhūṣaṇa), Rāma summons him straight into Sītā’s private apartment (तत्रैव सीतावस्थितिदेश एव, Tilaka). Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) likens the golden couch (सौवर्णे पर्यङ्के) to a golden mountain setting off Rāma’s dark rain-cloud hue (काञ्चनगिरिः कालमेघमिव रामं भासयति).

2.16.9–10 (varāharudhirābheṇa & the Moon with Citrā)

All three commentators note the proverbial deep crimson hue of boar’s blood (वराहरुधिरमतिरक्तवर्णमिति प्रसिद्धम्, Tilaka, Śiromaṇi, Bhūṣaṇa), Bhūṣaṇa explaining it as saffron-blended sandalwood (कुङ्कुममिश्रितया) cooled and perfumed by Sītā’s lotus hands. Standing beside him with a chowrie, Sītā shines like the star Citrā conjoined with the full moon (चैत्रपौर्णमास्यां चित्राख्यतारकयोपेतम्, Bhūṣaṇa; तदभिधतारया उपेतं शशिनम्, Śiromaṇi).

2.16.11–12 (vinītavat & vihāraśayanāsane)

On विहारशयनासने, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) offers either a seat serving for recreation and rest or a singular collective Dvandva (समाहारद्वन्द्वः, Tilaka; एकवद्भावः, Bhūṣaṇa) with the variant सुखं पृष्ट्वा (“inquiring after Rāma’s walk, sleep, and sitting”).

2.16.13–14 (kausalyā suprajā rāma & Accepting the Summons)

Echoing Viśvāmitra’s famous dawn invocation, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi), and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) supply त्वया सुपुत्रेण with कौसल्या सुप्रजाः (“through you, Kausalyā is blessed with a noble son”), Bhūṣaṇa also noting the Bahuvrīhi suffix -असिच्. Honoring Sumantra by accepting his message (सुमन्त्रवचोऽङ्गीचकार, Tilaka), Rāma turns to Sītā.

2.16.15–16 (madantare & Kaikeyī’s Perceived Benevolence)

Citing the Amarakośa (अन्तरमवकाशावधिपरिधानान्तर्धिभेदतादर्थ्ये), Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) glosses मदन्तरे as “for my sake” (मदर्थे). Hearing that Daśaratha is with Kaikeyī, Rāma infers that clever Kaikeyī (सुदक्षिणा = समर्था, Tilaka; चतुरा, Bhūṣaṇa), knowing the aged king’s wish not to delay until Bharata’s return (भरतासन्निधौ रामाभिषेकस्त्वत्संमतो न वेति, Tilaka, Bhūṣaṇa), is urging him to proceed at once.

2.16.17–18 (arthakāmakaram & Preventing Sītā’s Doubt)

Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) observes that v. 17 forestalls any suspicion in Sītā’s mind (सीताशङ्कां वारयति): Rāma calls Kaikeyī his mother (जननी) devoted to his welfare (अर्थकामा मे), and rejoices that sending Sumantra (अर्थकामकरम्) proves their auspicious intent.

2.16.19–21 (abhidadhyuṣī & Escorting Rāma)

Matching the friendly messenger to the assembly (यादृशी मत्परा… परिषत्, Tilaka), Rāma bids Sītā sport with her companions (परिवारेण सखीसमूहेन). Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explains पतिसंमानिता as Rāma placing his own garland upon Sītā’s neck (स्वकण्ठादुन्मुच्य दत्तस्वमालिका), while Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) flag अभिदध्युषी (अभिध्यायन्ती) as an ārṣa participle.

2.16.22–23 (rājasūyābhiṣecanam & the Antelope Skin)

Sītā prays that after the regency consecration Daśaratha may crown Rāma with the Rājasūya (राजसूयं महाराज्याभिषेचनम्, Tilaka) as Brahmā (लोककृत्) crowned Indra, wishing to serve him while he wears the sacrificial antelope skin (वराजिनधरम्) and carries the blackbuck horn (कण्डयनार्थं हस्ते कृष्णमृगस्य शृङ्गम्, Śiromaṇi, Bhūṣaṇa).

2.16.24 (Tilaka’s Esoteric Double Meaning / Arthāntara)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) shows how vv. 16–24 carry a hidden prophetic meaning (एकमर्थान्तरमपि गर्भितम्) so that Sītā’s words never fail (सत्यसंकल्पाया भगवत्याः): Kaikeyī, prompted by the Inner Controller (अन्तर्यामिप्रेरणया), urges Rāma’s forest exile (रावणवधफलकवनगमनार्थम्), and Sītā will serve Rāma clad in deer-skin (वराजिनधरम्) in the forest (वने राज्यम्)!

2.16.25–26 (siṃho giriguhāśayaḥ & Lakṣmaṇa at the First Door)

Emerging with Sumantra like a mountain-cave lion (गिरिगुहाशयः सिंह इव), Rāma sees Lakṣmaṇa standing at the first doorway (प्रथमद्वारि, Bhūṣaṇa) with hollowed hands joined in humble salutation (प्रह्वाञ्जलिपुटम् = प्रह्वत्वसूचकाञ्जलिपुटवन्तम्, Tilaka; explained via possessive अच् by Govindarāja, Bhūṣaṇa).

2.16.27–28 (madhyamakakṣyāyām & the Tiger-Skin Chariot)

In the middle court Rāma greets his friends and suitors (अर्थिनः स्वदर्शनस्वाभिषेकार्थिनः, Tilaka, Bhūṣaṇa). On वैयाघ्रम्, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) cite the Amarakośa (द्वैपं वैयाघ्रमाजिनम्) for “covered with tiger-skin” (व्याघ्रचर्मपरिवृतम्), whereas Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) suggests appearing as if wrought of tiger’s ivory (व्याघ्रदन्तनिर्मितत्वेन प्रतीयमानम्).

2.16.29–30 (kareṇuśiśukalpaiḥ & the Thousand-Eyed Simile)

All three commentators gloss असंबाधम् as spacious (विपुलम्, Tilaka) and करेणुशिशुकल्पैः as steeds sturdy and massive as elephant calves (करभसदृशैः, Tilaka, Bhūṣaṇa). Comparing Rāma to thousand-eyed Indra (सहस्राक्षः), Tilaka remarks that through his jewelled ornaments reflecting his form, Rāma too appears thousand-eyed!

2.16.31–32 (mahābhrād iva candramāḥ & Lakṣmaṇa’s Service)

Citing the Amarakośa (पर्जन्यो रसदब्देन्द्रौ), Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) and Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) gloss पर्जन्यः as a roaring cloud (मेघ इव), while Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) adds that if पर्जन्यः means Indra, सः refers directly to Rāma. Under the reading महेन्द्रादिव चन्द्रमाः, Śiromaṇi and Bhūṣaṇa explain that Rāma emerges from his palace like the moon rising over Mount Mahendra (महेन्द्राख्यपर्वतविशेषात्). In v. 32c, Tilaka records both चित्रचामर- (“wielding a jewel-handled chowrie”) and चन्द्रचामर- (“moon-white chowrie”), whereas Bhūṣaṇa reads छत्रचामरपाणिः (“holding the parasol in one hand and the chowrie in the other”).

2.16.33–34 (halahalāśabdaḥ & the Genitive Absolute)

All three commentators explain हलहला as an onomatopoeic rendering of the crowd’s joyous roar (कलकलादिशब्दः, Bhūṣaṇa; अनुकरणम्, Tilaka) and construe तस्य निष्क्रममाणस्य as either a genitive absolute (“as Rāma was coming forth”) or modifying जनौघस्य.

2.16.35–36 (āśaṃsavaḥ & the Vanguard)

Before Rāma march sandal-anointed warriors bearing swords and bows, uttering blessings (आशंसवः = भद्रमिति शेषः, Tilaka; रामश्रेय आशंसमानाः, Bhūṣaṇa).

2.16.37–38 (harmyavātāyanasthābhiḥ & rāmapiprīṣayā)

As lion-roars echo along the avenue, women at the palace lattice-windows (हर्म्यवातायनस्थाभिः = प्रासादगवाक्षस्थाभिः, Śiromaṇi, Bhūṣaṇa) shower Rāma with blossoms. All three gloss the desiderative noun पिप्रीषा as the desire to please (प्रीणनेच्छा, Tilaka, Bhūṣaṇa; रामतुष्टीच्छया, Śiromaṇi), and cite वदि अभिवादनस्तुत्योः to gloss ववन्दिरे as “they praised” (अस्तुवन्).

2.16.39–40 (siddhayātram & Praise of Kausalyā and Sītā)

Women on the terraces and on the street (हर्म्यस्थाः क्षितिस्थाश्च) hail Kausalyā’s joy at seeing Rāma’s procession achieve its fruit (सिद्धयात्रम् = सफलगमनम्, Tilaka, Bhūṣaṇa) and attain his ancestral kingdom (पित्र्यं राज्यम्). They also deem Sītā the foremost of all women (सर्वसीमन्तिनीभ्यः सीमन्तिनीवराम्).

2.16.41–42 (rohiṇīva śaśāṅkena & Sītā’s Austerity)

Explaining why Sītā excels all women (तच्छ्लोकार्थहेतुत्वेनाह, Bhūṣaṇa), they exclaim that she practiced great austerity (महत्तपः सुचरितम्) to win union with Rāma like Rohiṇī with the moon. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) notes that प्रासादशृङ्गेषु synecdochically includes the women on the ground (क्षितेरप्युपलक्षकः).

2.16.43–45 (ātmādhikārā vācaḥ & the Citizens’ Acclaim)

In Upajāti and Indravajrā, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi), and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss आत्माधिकाराः as speeches centered upon Rāma (आत्मानमधिकृत्य प्रवृत्ताः). Combining present and future in श्रियं गच्छति… गमिष्यन्, Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa explain that Rāma goes to his father’s palace to receive vast royal fortune (श्रियं प्राप्तुं गच्छति), while the citizens rejoice that under his rule (मनुजाधिपेऽस्मिन्) none will ever see sorrow (अप्रियं प्रियाभावं दुःखमनिष्टं च न पश्येत्).

2.16.46 (svastikasūtamāgadhaiḥ & the Three Classes of Panegyrists)

All three distinguish the heraldic classes in स्वस्तिकसूतमागधैः: स्वस्तिकाः are bards crying “Victory!” (जयजयेति मङ्गलं प्रयुञ्जाना बन्दिनः, Tilaka, Bhūṣaṇa); सूताः are Paurāṇika eulogists (ब्राह्मण्यां क्षत्रियाज्जाताः, Bhūṣaṇa); and मागधाः are genealogists (वंशपाठकाः, Tilaka, Śiromaṇi; मागधः क्षत्रियाविशोः, Amarakośa, Bhūṣaṇa).

2.16.47 (paripūrṇacatvaram & the Grand Highway)

Closing in Vaṃśastha, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) glosses चत्वराः as crossroads (चतुष्पथाः), पण्यसंचयम् as heaps of market wares (आपणद्रव्याणां संचयः), and महापथम् as the spotless royal highway (राजमार्गम्).

स राघवस्त्रत्र तदा प्रलापान् । शुश्राव लोकस्य समागतस्य । आत्माधिकारा विविधाश्च वाचः । प्रहृष्टरूपस्य पुरे जनस्य ॥ २-१६-४३

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस समय अयोध्यामें आये हुए दूर-दूरके लोग अत्यन्त हर्षसे भरकर वहाँ श्रीरामचन्द्रजीके विषयमें जो वार्तालाप और तरह-तरहकी बातें करते थे, अपने विषयमें कही गयी उन सभी बातोंको श्रीरघुनाथजी सुनते जा रहे थे ॥ ४३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग अयोध्यॆगॆ बन्दिरुव दूर-दूर देशद जनरु अत्यन्त हर्षगॊण्डु श्रीरामचन्द्रन विषयदल्लि बगॆ-बगॆयागि वार्तालापमाडुत्तिद्द ऎल्ल मातुगळन्नु श्रीरामनु केळुत्ता होगुत्तिद्दनु.॥43॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

43. tadaa = then; saH = that; raaghavaH = Rama;sushraava = heard; pralapaan = words; lokasya = of people; samaagatasya; gathered; tatra = there; vaachaH cha = and also words; vividhaaH = variously; aatmaadhikaaraaH = pertaining to himself; pure janasya = by citizens; prahR^ishhTaruupasya = who were much delighted.

अनुवादः

Then, Rama heard the words of people gathered there and also the words variously pertaining to himself, by citizens.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

There at that time Rāghava heard the joyful discourses of the assembled populace (pralāpān śuśrāva lokasya samāgatasya), and the manifold words concerning himself (ātmādhikārā vividhāś ca vācaḥ) uttered by the exultant people in the city (prahṛṣṭa-rūpasya pure janasya):

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

एष श्रियं गच्छति राघवोऽद्य
राजप्रसादाद् विपुलां गमिष्यन् ।
एते वयं सर्वसमृद्धकामा
येषामयं नो भविता प्रशास्ता ॥ ४४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

एष श्रियं गच्छति राघवोऽद्य
राजप्रसादाद् विपुलां गमिष्यन् ।
एते वयं सर्वसमृद्धकामा
येषामयं नो भविता प्रशास्ता ॥ ४४ ॥

मूलम् - VR

एष श्रियं गच्छति राघवोऽद्य। राजप्रसादातद् विपुलं गमिष्यन् । एते वयम् सर्वसमृद्धकामा। योषामयं नो भविता प्रशास्ता ॥ २-१६-४४

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

वे कहते थे—‘इस समय ये श्रीरामचन्द्रजी महाराज दशरथकी कृपासे बहुत बड़ी सम्पत्तिके अधिकारी होने जा रहे हैं । अब हम सब लोगोंकी समस्त कामनाएँ पूर्ण हो जायँगी, क्योंकि ये श्रीराम हमारे शासक होंगे ॥ ४४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

ईग ई श्रीरामचन्द्रनु महाराजा दशरथन कृपॆयिन्द बहळ दॊड्ड सम्पत्तिन अधिकारियागुवनु. नम्म ऎल्ल जनर समस्त कामनॆगळु पूर्णवागुववु, एकॆन्दरॆ ई श्रीरामनु नमगॆ राजनागुवनु ऎन्दु हेळुत्तिद्दरु.॥44॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

44. raaja prasaadaat = by king’s propitiousness; eshhaH = this; raaghavaH = Rama; gamishhyan = who will obtain; vipulaam = the earth; adya = now; gachchhati = will gain; shriyam = wealth; yeshhaam naH = for which of us; ayam = he; bahvitaa = will become; prashastaa = ruler; ete vayam = such of us; sarvasamR^idhdhakaamaa = will have fulfilled all desires.

अनुवादः

“By the king’s propitiousness, this Rama today is going to obtain this earth and the wealthy kingdom. All our desires are going to be fulfilled because Rama will become the ruler.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Yonder goes Rāghava today, about to attain vast royal fortune through the king’s grace (rāja-prasādād vipulāṃ śriyaṃ gamiṣyan)! Truly we are all folk whose every desire is fulfilled (ete vayaṃ sarva-samṛddha-kāmāḥ), for whom this prince shall be our ruler (yeṣām ayaṃ no bhavitā praśāstā)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

लाभो जनस्यास्य यदेष सर्वं
प्रपत्स्यते राष्ट्रमिदं चिराय ।
न ह्यप्रियं किञ्चन जातु कश्चित्
पश्येन्न दुःखं मनुजाधिपेऽस्मिन् ॥ ४५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

लाभो जनस्यास्य यदेष सर्वं
प्रपत्स्यते राष्ट्रमिदं चिराय ।
न ह्यप्रियं किञ्चन जातु कश्चित्
1 पश्येन्न दुःखं मनुजाधिपेऽस्मिन् ॥ ४५ ॥

मूलम् - VR

लाभो जनस्यास्य यदेष सर्वं। प्रपत्स्यते राष्ट्रमिदं चिराय । न ह्यप्रियं किंचन जातु कश्चित् । पश्येन्न कुःखं मनुजादिपेऽस्मिन् ॥ २-१६-४५

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

यदि यह सारा राज्य चिरकालके लिये इनके हाथमें आ जाय तो इस जगत् की समस्त जनताके लिये यह महान् लाभ होगा । इनके राजा होनेपर कभी किसीका अप्रिय नहीं होगा और किसीको कोई दुःख भी नहीं देखना पड़ेगा’ ॥ ४५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

ई इडी राज्यवु चिरकाल श्रीरामन कैगॆ बन्दरॆ ई जगत्तिन समस्त जनरिगॆ महालाभवागुवुदु. इवनु राजनादरॆ ऎन्दिगू यारिगू अप्रियवागलारदु. यारू दुःखितनू इरलारनु ऎन्दु अवरॆल्लरू मातनाडिकॊळ्ळुत्तिद्दरु.॥45॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

45. yat = since when; eshhaH = this Rama; pravatsyate = will get; sarvam idam raashhTram = this entire state; chiraaya = for a long time(since then); laabhaH = benefit; asya janasya = for these people; asmin mamjaadhipe = when this Rama becomes king; kashchit = anyone; jaatu = at anytime; na pashyet hi = will not see kichana = anything; apriyam = unkindly; na = nor; duHkham = grief.

अनुवादः

“If Rama rules this state for a long time, it will be a great benefit to the people. If he becomes a king none can see dislike and sorrow at any time.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

It is the supreme gain of this people (lābho janasyāsya) that this prince shall rule over this entire realm for a long time to come (yad eṣa sarvaṃ prapatsyate rāṣṭram idaṃ cirāya); for while he is lord of men, no one shall ever behold anything unwelcome or any sorrow (na hy apriyaṃ kiṃcana jātu kaścit paśyen na duḥkham)!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स घोषवद्भिश्च हयैः सनागैः
पुरःसरैः स्वस्तिकसूतमागधैः ।
महीयमानः प्रवरैश्च वादकै-
रभिष्टुतो वैश्रवणो यथा ययौ ॥ ४६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स घोषवद्भिश्च हयैः सनागैः
पुरःसरैः स्वस्तिकसूतमागधैः ।
महीयमानः प्रवरैश्च वादकै-
रभिष्टुतो वैश्रवणो यथा ययौ ॥ ४६ ॥

न घोषवद्भिश्च हयैः सनागैः
पुरःसरैः स्वस्तिकसूतमागधैः ।
महीयमानः प्रवरैश्च वादकै-
राभिष्टुतो वैश्रवणो यथाययौ ॥

मूलम् - VR

स घोषवद्भिश्च हयैः सनागैः । पुरस्सरैः स्वस्तिकसूतमागधैः । महीयमानः प्रवरैश्च वादकैः । अभिष्टुतो वैश्रवणो यथा ययौ॥ २-१६-४६

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

हिनहिनाते हुए घोड़ों, चिग्घाड़ते हुए हाथियों, जय-जयकार करते हुए आगे-आगे चलनेवाले वन्दियों, स्तुतिपाठ करनेवाले सूतों, वंशकी विरुदावलि बखाननेवाले मागधों तथा सर्वश्रेष्ठ गुणगायकोंके तुमुल घोषके बीच उन वन्दी आदिसे पूजित एवं प्रशंसित होते हुए श्रीरामचन्द्रजी कुबेरके समान चल रहे थे ॥ ४६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

कुदुरॆगळ हेषारव, आनॆगळ चीत्कार, जय-जयकार माडुत्ता मुन्दुगडॆ होगुत्तिद्द वन्दिगळ जयध्वनि, सूतर स्तुतिपाठगळ, मागधरु माडुत्तिद्द वंशद बिरुदावळि, गायकर तुमुल ध्वनिगळॊन्दिगॆ श्रीरामचन्द्रनु कुबेरनन्तॆ सागुत्तिद्दनु.॥46॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

46. suutamaagadhaiH = bards and panegyrists; svastika = invoking blessings; purassaraiH = moving in front; mahiiyamaanaH = praising with great respect; abhishhTutaH = eulogised; pravaraiH vaadyakaiH = by the most excellent instrumentalists; saH = that Rama; vaishraavaNotatha = like Kubera the god of weatlh; yayau = went; hayaiH = with horses; sanaagaiH = together with elephants; ghoshhavadbhiH = having resounds.

अनुवादः

While bards and panegyrists were moving in front praising with great respect, invoking blessings and eulogised by the most excellent instrumentalists, Rama, like Kubera the god of wealth, went with resounding horses and elephants.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Honored by neighing and bell-decked steeds and elephants marching in front (sa ghoṣavadbhiś ca hayaiḥ sanāgaiḥ puraḥsaraiḥ), and by panegyrists crying ‘Victory!’, charioteer-bards, and genealogists (svastika-sūta-māgadhaiḥ), and praised by foremost musicians (pravaraiś ca vādakair abhiṣṭutaḥ), he advanced like Vaiśravaṇa Kubera (vaiśravaṇo yathā yayau).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

करेणुमातङ्गरथाश्वसङ्कुलं
महाजनौघैः परिपूर्णचत्वरम् ।
प्रभूतरत्नं बहुपण्यसञ्चयं
ददर्श रामो विमलं महापथम् ॥ ४७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

करेणुमातङ्गरथाश्वसङ्कुलं
महाजनौघैः परिपूर्णचत्वरम् ।
प्रभूतरत्नं बहुपण्यसञ्चयं
ददर्श रामो विमलं महापथम् ॥ ४७ ॥

मूलम् - VR

करेणुमात्ङ्गरथाश्वसंकुलं । महाजनौघैः परिपूर्णचत्वरम् । प्रभूतरत्नं बहुपण्यसंचयं । ददर्श रामो विमलं महापथम् ॥ २-१६-४७

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

यात्रा करते हुए श्रीरामने उस विशाल राजमार्गको देखा, जो हथिनियों, मतवाले हाथियों, रथों और घोड़ोंसे खचाखच भरा हुआ था । उसके प्रत्येक चौराहेपर मनुष्योंकी भारी भीड़ इकट्ठी हो रही थी । उसके दोनों पार्श्वभागोंमें प्रचुर रत्नोंसे भरी हुई दुकानें थीं तथा विक्रयके योग्य और भी बहुत-से द्रव्योंके ढेर वहाँ दिखायी देते थे । वह राजमार्ग बहुत साफ-सुथरा था ॥ ४७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रामनु सागुत्तिद्द आ विशाल राजमार्गवु हॆण्णानॆगळिन्द, मत्त गजगळिन्द, रथाश्वगळिन्द निबिडवागि तुम्बिहोगित्तु, प्रतियॊन्दु कूडुरस्तॆयल्लि भारी सङ्ख्यॆयल्लि जनरु नॆरॆदिद्दरु. इक्कॆलगळल्लि विपुल रत्नगळिन्द तुम्बिद अङ्गडिगळिद्दु हागू इतर माराटद वस्तुगळु राशि-राशियागि काणुत्तिद्दवु. आ स्वच्छ-सुन्दरवाद राजबीदियल्लि श्रीरामनु होगुत्तिद्दनु.॥47॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

47. raamaH = Rama; dadarsha = saw; mahaapatham; great royal road; vimalam = which was spotless; prabhuuta ratnam = having abundant diamonds; bhaupaNya sanchayam = havin abundant diamonds; bahupaNya sanchayam = having many vendible commodities; kareNu maataN^ga rathaashva sankulam = full of female and male elephants; chariots and horses; paripuurNa chatvaram = the road junctions were filled; janaughaiH = with multitude of people.

अनुवादः

Rama saw the great royal road which was spotlessly clean, having diamonds and various vendible commodities. It was full of male and female elephants, horses and chariots. There was abundance of people gathered at road junctions.

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्येऽयोध्याकाण्डे षोडशः सर्गः ॥ १६ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके अयोध्याकाण्डमें सोलहवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ १६ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद अयोध्याकाण्डदल्लि हदिनारनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु ॥16॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Rāma beheld the spotless grand highway (dadarśa rāmo vimalaṃ mahā-patham), thronged with female elephants, mighty tuskers, chariots, and steeds (kareṇu-mātaṅga-rathāśva-saṃkulam), its crossroads filled to overflowing with vast multitudes of people (mahā-janaughaiḥ paripūrṇa-catvaram), abounding in jewels and heaped with rich merchandise of every kind (prabhūta-ratnaṃ bahu-paṇya-saṃcayam).
