Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy - Intro

Book II : Ayodhya Kanda - Book Of Ayodhya

This chapter describes Lakshmana’s anger and Rama consoling Lakshmana.

भागसूचना
  1. लक्ष्मणका रोष, उनका श्रीरामको बलपूर्वक राज्यपर अधिकार कर लेनेके लिये प्रेरित करना तथा श्रीरामका पिताकी आज्ञाके पालनको ही धर्म बताकर माता और लक्ष्मणको समझाना
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Addressing the weeping Kausalyā, the distressed Lakṣmaṇa denounces the infatuated and aged king for banishing the faultless, gentle Rāma at a woman’s bidding without cause.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तथा तु विलपन्तीं तां कौसल्यां राममातरम् ।
उवाच लक्ष्मणो दीनस्तत्कालसदृशं वचः ॥ १ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तथा तु विलपन्तीं तां कौसल्यां राममातरम् ।
उवाच लक्ष्मणो दीनस्तत्कालसदृशं वचः ॥ १ ॥

मूलम् - VR

तथा तु विलपन्तीम् ताम् कौसल्याम् राम मातरम् । उवाच लक्ष्मणो दीनः तत् काल सदृशम् वचः ॥ २-२१-१

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

इस प्रकार विलाप करती हुई श्रीराम माता कौसल्यासे अत्यन्त दुःखी हुए लक्ष्मणने उस समयके योग्य बात कही— ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

ई प्रकार विलपिसुत्तिरुव श्रीराममातॆ कौसल्यॆय बळि अत्यन्त दुःखितनाद लक्ष्मणनु आ समयक्कॆ सरियागि इन्तॆन्दनु.॥1॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

1. lakshhmaNaH = Lakshmana; diinaH = became dejected; uvaacha = spoke; vachaH = the words; tatkaala sadR^isham = suitable for that time; taam kausalyyaam = to that kausalya; rama maataram = Rama’s mother; tathaa = thus; vilapantiim = weaping.

अनुवादः

Lakshmana became dejected and spoke these words, properly befitting for that time, to that Rama’s mother Kausalya who was thus weeping.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then to Kausalyā, the mother of Rāma, who was lamenting thus, the distressed Lakṣmaṇa spoke words suited to that moment (tatkālasadṛśam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

न रोचते ममाप्येतदार्ये यद् राघवो वनम् ।
त्यक्त्वा राज्यश्रियं गच्छेत् स्त्रिया वाक्यवशङ्गतः ॥ २ ॥
विपरीतश्च वृद्धश्च विषयैश्च प्रधर्षितः ।
नृपः किमिव न ब्रूयाच्चोद्यमानः समन्मथः ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

न रोचते ममाप्येतदार्ये यद् राघवो वनम् ।
त्यक्त्वा राज्यश्रियं गच्छेत् स्त्रिया वाक्यवशङ्गतः ॥ २ ॥
विपरीतश्च वृद्धश्च विषयैश्च प्रधर्षितः ।
नृपः किमिव न ब्रूयाच्चोद्यमानः समन्मथः ॥ ३ ॥

मूलम् - VR

न रोचते मम अपि एतत् आर्ये यद् राघवो वनम् । त्यक्त्वा राज्य श्रियम् गच्चेत् स्त्रिया वाक्य वशम् गतः ॥ २-२१-२ विपरीतः च वृद्धः च विषयैः च प्रधर्षितः । नृपः किम् इव न ब्रूयाच् चोद्यमानः समन्मथः ॥ २-२१-३

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘बड़ी माँ! मुझे भी यह अच्छा नहीं लगता कि श्रीराम राज्यलक्ष्मीका परित्याग करके वनमें जायँ । महाराज तो इस समय स्त्रीकी बातमें आ गये हैं, इसलिये उनकी प्रकृति विपरीत हो गयी है । एक तो वे बूढ़े हैं, दूसरे विषयोंने उन्हें वशमें कर लिया है; अतः कामदेवके वशीभूत हुए वे नरेश कैकेयी-जैसी स्त्रीकी प्रेरणासे क्या नहीं कह सकते हैं? ॥ २-३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

दॊड्डम्म! श्रीरामनु राज्यलक्ष्मीयन्नु त्यजिसि काडिगॆ होगुवुदु ननगू ऒळ्ळॆयदॆनिसुवुदिल्ल. महाराजरादरो ईग स्त्रीय मातिगॆ मरुळागि अवर प्रकृति विपरीतवागिदॆ. ऒन्दु अवरु मुदुकरागिद्दारॆ, इन्नॊन्दु विषयगळिगॆ वशीभूतवागिरुवरु. आद्दरिन्द मन्मथनिगॆ वशीभूतरागि आ राजरु कैकॆयन्तह स्त्रीय प्रेरणॆयिन्द एनु तानॆ माडलाररु.॥2-3॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

2. “aarye = Oh; the venerable lady! atat = this; na rochate = is not liking; mamaapi = to me also; itiyat = as in the manner this; raaghavaH = Rama; vaakyaarasham gataH = being influenced by the words; striyaaH = of a woman; gachchhet = go; vanam = to forest; tyaktvaa = leaving; raajya sriiyam = prosperous kingdom.”

अनुवादः

“Oh, the honoured lady ! I also do not like Rama to be influenced by the words of a woman and go to the forest, leaving the prosperous kingdom.”

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

3. “nR^ipaH = king; vipariitasheha = with perverted mind; * vR^idhdhhashcha* = old aged; pradharshhitaH = who is outraged; vishhayaiH = by sensual enjoyments; samanimadhaH = who is possessed of passion; na bruuyaat = can he not talk; kimiva = of anything; chodyamaanaH = prompted (by Kaikeyi).

अनुवादः

“The king with perverted mind, of old age, one who is outraged by sensual enjoyments and possessed of passion can talk any thing, prompted by Kaikeyi.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

2: “It does not please me either, O noble lady (ārye), that Rāghava, falling under the sway of a woman's words, should abandon the royal fortune and depart to the forest!”

3: “Perverted in judgment (viparītaḥ), aged, overpowered by sensual pleasures, and infatuated with passion (samanmathaḥ)—what indeed would the king not utter when goaded on by her?”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

नास्यापराधं पश्यामि नापि दोषं तथाविधम् ।
येन निर्वास्यते राष्ट्राद् वनवासाय राघवः ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

नास्यापराधं पश्यामि नापि दोषं तथाविधम् ।
येन निर्वास्यते राष्ट्राद् वनवासाय राघवः ॥ ४ ॥

मूलम् - VR

न अस्य अपराधम् पश्यामि न अपि दोषम् तथा विधम् । येन निर्वास्यते राष्ट्रात् वन वासाय राघवः ॥ २-२१-४

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘मैं श्रीरघुनाथजीका ऐसा कोई अपराध या दोष नहीं देखता, जिससे इन्हें राज्यसे निकाला जाय और वनमें रहनेके लिये विवश किया जाय ॥४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

श्रीरघुनाथनल्लि राज्यदिन्द हॊरगट्टि वनवास माडुवन्तह यावुदे अपराध अथवा दोषवन्नु नानु नोडुवुदिल्ल.॥4॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

4. na pashyaami = I can neither see; aparaadham = offence; na pashyaami = nor can I see; doshshamapi = even fault; asya = in him; tathaa vidham = that can; yena = by any means; nirvaasyate = expel; raaghavaH = Rama; raashhTraat = from state ; vanavaasaaya = to forest.”

अनुवादः

“I can not see any offence or cause for blame in Rama that can expel him from the state to the forest.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“I see no offense (aparādham) in him, nor any blemish (doṣam) of such a kind for which Rāghava should be banished from the kingdom to dwell in the forest.”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

न तं पश्याम्यहं लोके परोक्षमपि यो नरः ।
स्वमित्रोऽपि निरस्तोऽपि योऽस्य दोषमुदाहरेत् ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

न तं पश्याम्यहं लोके परोक्षमपि यो नरः ।
स्वमित्रोऽपि निरस्तोऽपि योऽस्य दोषमुदाहरेत् ॥ ५ ॥

न तं पश्यामहं लोके परोक्षमपि यो नरः ।
स्वमित्रोऽपि निरस्तोऽपि योऽस्य दोषमुदाहरेत् ॥

मूलम् - VR

न तम् पश्याम्य् अहम् लोके परोक्षम् अपि यो नरः । स्वमित्रोऽपि निरस्तोऽपि योऽस्यदोषमुदाहरेत् ॥ २-२१-५

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘मैं संसारमें एक मनुष्यको भी ऐसा नहीं देखता, जो अत्यन्त शत्रु एवं तिरस्कृत होनेपर भी परोक्षमें भी इनका कोई दोष बता सके ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

जगत्तिनल्लि यावुदे मनुष्यनु अत्यन्त शत्रु हागू तिरस्कृतनागिद्दरू परोक्षदल्लियू श्रीरामनल्लि यावुदे दोषवन्नु तोरिसुवुदन्नु नानु नोडिल्ल.॥5॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

5. aham = I; na pashyaami = have not seen; loke = in this world; tam = such a person; udaaharet = who talks; dosham = ill; * asya* = of Rama; parokshhamapi = even indirectly; yaH naraH = that person who is; svamitro api = even an enemy; nirasto api = or an expellee."

अनुवादः

I have not seen in this world any person, whether an enemy or an expellee, speaking ill of Rama indirectly."

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“I behold no man in the world who, even behind his back—even were he a bitter enemy (svamitro 'pi) cast out by him—could utter a single fault of his.”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

देवकल्पमृजुं दान्तं रिपूणामपि वत्सलम् ।
अवेक्षमाणः को धर्मं त्यजेत् पुत्रमकारणात् ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

देवकल्पमृजुं दान्तं रिपूणामपि वत्सलम् ।
अवेक्षमाणः को धर्मं त्यजेत् पुत्रमकारणात् ॥ ६ ॥

मूलम् - VR

देव कल्पम् ऋजुम् दान्तम् रिपूणाम् अपि वत्सलम् । अवेक्षमाणः को धर्मम् त्यजेत् पुत्रम् अकारणात् ॥ २-२१-६

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘धर्मपर दृष्टि रखनेवाला कौन ऐसा राजा होगा, जो देवताके समान शुद्ध, सरल, जितेन्द्रिय और शत्रुओंपर भी स्नेह रखनेवाले (श्रीराम-जैसे) पुत्रका अकारण परित्याग करेगा? ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

देवतॆगळन्तॆ शुद्धनू, सरळनू, जितेन्द्रियनू, शत्रुगळ मेलॆयू स्नेहविडुववनू (श्रीराम नन्तह) आद पुत्रनन्नु धर्मद मेलॆ दृष्टियिट्टिरुव याव राजनु ताने त्यजिसबल्लनु.॥6॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

6. “kaH = who; avekshhamaaNaH = observing; dharmam = ethicalness; akaaraNaat = causelessly; tyajet = get rid of; putram = son; devakalpam = who is equal to god; R^ijum = who is honest; daantam = who is self-restrained; vatsalam = who is affectionate; R^ipuuNaamapi = even towards enemies?”

अनुवादः

“Can anybody observing ethicalness, causelessly get rid of a son who is equal to god who is honest, who is self-restrained and who is affectionate even towards adversaries?”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“What man having regard for dharma would without cause abandon a son who is like a god (devakalpam), upright, self-controlled, and affectionate even toward his foes?”

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Urging Rāma to seize the sovereignty before the news spreads, Lakṣmaṇa vows to stand bow in hand like Death himself, slay Bharata’s partisans, and bind or slay their hostile father.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तदिदं वचनं राज्ञः पुनर्बाल्यमुपेयुषः ।
पुत्रः को हृदये कुर्याद् राजवृत्तमनुस्मरन् ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तदिदं वचनं राज्ञः पुनर्बाल्यमुपेयुषः ।
पुत्रः को हृदये कुर्याद् राजवृत्तमनुस्मरन् ॥ ७ ॥

मूलम् - VR

तत् इदम् वचनम् राज्ञः पुनर् बाल्यम् उपेयुषः । पुत्रः को हृदये कुर्यात् राज व्ऱ्त्तम् अनुस्मरन् ॥ २-२१-७

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘जो पुनः बालभाव (विवेकशून्यता) को प्राप्त हो गये हैं, ऐसे राजाके इस वचनको राजनीतिका ध्यान रखनेवाला कौन पुत्र अपने हृदयमें स्थान दे सकता है? ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

वृद्धाप्यदिन्द बालभाववन्नु हॊन्दिरुव विवेकशून्यनाद ई राजन विवेकरहितवाद मातगळन्नु राजनीतियन्नु पूर्णवागि तिळिदिरुव याव राजपुत्रनु ताने मनस्सिगॆ तन्दुकॊळ्ळुत्तानॆ.॥7॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

7. “kaH = which son; anusmaran = who remembers; raja vR^ittam = royal usage; kuryaat = act; hR^idaye = with heart; tat idam vachanam = such of these words; raajJNaH = of king; upeyushhaH = who got; baalyam = childhood punaH = again?”

अनुवादः

“Which son, knowing royal usage, can agree to the words of this king who is behaving as though he got childhood again?”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“What son remembering the conduct of kings (rājavṛttam) would take to heart such words of a king who has lapsed into second childhood (punarbālyam upeyuṣaḥ)?”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

यावदेव न जानाति कश्चिदर्थमिमं नरः ।
तावदेव मया सार्धमात्मस्थं कुरु शासनम् ॥ ८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

यावदेव न जानाति कश्चिदर्थमिमं नरः ।
तावदेव मया सार्धमात्मस्थं कुरु शासनम् ॥ ८ ॥

मूलम् - VR

यावद् एव न जानाति कश्चित् अर्थम् इमम् नरः । तावद् एव मया साधम् आत्मस्थम् कुरु शासनम् ॥२-२१-८

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘रघुनन्दन! जबतक कोई भी मनुष्य आपके वनवासकी बातको नहीं जानता है, तबतक ही, आप मेरी सहायतासे इस राज्यके शासनकी बागडोर अपने हाथमें ले लीजिये ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रघुनन्दन! निन्न वनवासद मातन्नु याव मनुष्यनू तिळियदिरुवाग नीनु नन्न सहायदिन्द ई राज्यद शास्त्रनवन्नु कैगॆत्तिको.॥8॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

8. “yaavadeva = even till; kashchit = any; naraH = person; na jaanaati = does not know; ivam artham = this matter; taava deva = in the mean time; kuru = make; shaasanam = dominion; aatmastham = your own; * mayaa sa ardham* = together with me.”

अनुवादः

“Even before others get to know about this matter, make this dominion your own, with my help.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Before any man learns of this matter, take the sovereignty under your own control (ātmasthaṃ kuru śāsanam) together with me!”

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

2.21.1–3 (tatkālasadṛśam and viparītaḥ)

Nāgeśa Bhaṭṭa (Tilaka) explains that Lakṣmaṇa’s words are “suited to that moment” (तत्कालसदृशं रामवियोगहेतुककौसल्यादुःखप्रशमनादिकालोचितम्) solely to console Kausalyā, rather than expressing settled defiance toward his father (केवलं कौसल्याशोकशान्त्यर्थं न तु सद्धृदयम्). In v. 3, Daśaratha is विपरीतः (“inverted in nature,” प्राप्तप्रकृतिवैपरीत्यः [Tilaka]; विपरीतवयोधर्मा [Bhūṣaṇa]) because old age (वृद्धत्वम्) and subjugation to sense-objects (विषयप्रधर्षितत्वम्) have left him infatuated (समन्मथः) and goaded on by Kaikeyī (चोद्यमानः स्त्रिया).

2.21.4–5 (aparādham vs. doṣam and svamitraḥ)

Distinguishing the two terms in v. 4, Tilaka glosses अपराधम् as an offense against the father (पितृविषयकम्) and दोषम् as a sin against the subjects (प्रजावधादिपातकम्), whereas Govindarāja takes अपराधम् as treason (राजद्रोहम्) and दोषम् as a grave sin (महापातकादिकम्). On स्वमित्रोऽपि निरस्तोऽपि (v. 5c), both commentators resolve स्वमित्रः as सुतराम् अमित्रः (“an utter enemy”) who, even when cast out (निरस्तः) for a crime, cannot allege a single fault in Rāma even behind his back (परोक्षमपि).

2.21.6 (devakalpam and ripūṇām api vatsalam)

Govindarāja derives देवकल्पम् (“nearly equal to a god, ever-pure”) by the suffix -कल्पप्, glosses ऋजुम् as upright in thought, word, and deed (करणत्रयार्जवयुक्तम्), and joined with Tilaka notes that Rāma remains affectionate even toward hostile persons like Kaikeyī (रिपूणां कैकेय्यादीनां विषयेऽपि वत्सलं स्निग्धम्).

2.21.7–9 (punarbālyam and ātmasthaṃ śāsanam)

On पुनर्बाल्यमुपेयुषः (v. 7b), Tilaka and Govindarāja explain “second childhood” (बालभावम्) as unreflective impulsiveness and slavery to passion (अविमृष्यकारित्वं कामपरवशत्वम्), while राजवृत्तम् denotes traditional statecraft (राजनीतिम्). In v. 8d, शासनम् means royal rule (शस्यत इति शासनं राज्यम् [Bhūṣaṇa]), which Rāma should bring under his own control through consecration (अभिषेकेणात्मस्थं स्वाधीनं कुरु); Tilaka also records the variant शोभनम्. In v. 9, अधिकं कर्तुम् means obstructing the consecration (अभिषेकविघ्नम् [Tilaka]) or surpassing Rāma’s valor (तव पौरुषाधिकं पौरुषं कर्तुम् [Bhūṣaṇa]).

2.21.10–11 (pakṣyaḥ and mṛdur hi paribhūyate)

In v. 11, Tilaka and Śiromaṇi identify Bharata’s partisan (पक्ष्यः) with his maternal uncle Yudhājit and others (मातुलादिः), while Govindarāja glosses it as his allied troop (सहायभूतो वर्गः). Since a mild man is despised (मृदुः शान्तस्वभावः परिभूयते), Lakṣmaṇa argues that gentleness is unsuited to the present crisis.

2.21.12 (vadhyatāṃ badhyatām api)

If Daśaratha becomes an enemy (अमित्रभूतः शत्रुपक्षसहायभूतः), Tilaka reads वध्यतां बध्यतामपि (“let him be bound or even slain”), remarking that at the twilight of the Tretā and Dvāpara ages (त्रेताद्वापरयोः संधावयमवतारः) a slight trace of adharma enters Lakṣmaṇa’s fiery speech, whereas Śiromaṇi reads doubled बध्यतां बध्यताम् (वीप्सादिरुक्तम्, “let him be firmly bound”).

2.21.13 (guror apy avaliptasya)

To justify restraining a father, Lakṣmaṇa cites a celebrated canonical maxim (स्वोक्तार्थे धर्मशास्त्रं प्रमाणयति [Bhūṣaṇa]): even an elder or preceptor (गुरोरपि) who becomes arrogant (अवलिप्तस्य गर्वितस्य), fails to discern right action from wrong (कार्याकार्यमजानतः), and takes to a lawless path (उत्पथम् अमर्यादाम्) must be subjected to punishment or restraint (कार्यं भवति शासनं दण्डः [Śiromaṇi]). Hence, adds Tilaka, because such restraint has scriptural sanction (शास्त्रसंमतत्वात्), no sin attaches to it.

2.21.14–15 (balam vs. hetum)

In v. 14, Govindarāja and Śiromaṇi distinguish बलम् as military or royal power (सैन्यादिहेतुकपराक्रमम् / राजत्वप्रयुक्तबलम्) and हेतुम् as the legal pretext of the boons (वरदानरूपहेतुम्); Tilaka explains उपस्थितम् as the sovereignty that has devolved upon Rāma by right of primogeniture (ज्येष्ठत्वेन न्यायतस्तवैव प्राप्तमिदं राज्यम्).

2.21.16–18 (the fourfold Kṣatriya oath and dīptam agnim)

Addressing Kausalyā (देवि), Lakṣmaṇa swears by his truthfulness (सत्येन), his bow (धनुषा, the classic Kṣatriya oath: प्रसिद्धश्च क्षत्रियाणां धनुषः शपथः [Śiromaṇi]), his charitable gifts (दत्तेन दानेन / वापीकूपादिनिर्माणजनितसुकृतेन), and his sacrifices (इष्टेन यागादिजनितादृष्टेन). In v. 17, whether Rāma enters a blazing fire out of wrath (दीप्तमग्निं क्रोधम् [Tilaka]) or the forest by his father’s command, Lakṣmaṇa vows to enter there first.

2.21.19–21 (sthitam bālye and yadi rocate)

In v. 19 (omitted in Govindarāja’s text, as noted in the apparatus: श्लोकोऽयं गो॰ नास्ति), Tilaka explains that Daśaratha is pitiful (कृपणम्) because his mind is obsessed with Kaikeyī (कैकेय्यासक्तमानसम्), and despised in old age (वृद्धभावेन गर्हितम्) for remaining in amorous folly (बाल्ये कामुकत्वे स्थितम्). In v. 21, when Kausalyā tells Rāma to do what follows next “if it pleases you” (यदि रोचते), Tilaka and Govindarāja observe that out of fear of her supremely righteous son’s displeasure (परमधार्मिकराममातृत्वादाग्रहं विहाय) she already senses that Lakṣmaṇa’s violent proposal does not please Rāma (स्वानभिमतत्वं लक्ष्मणोक्तस्य ध्वनितम्).

2.21.22–23 (śuśrūṣa mām ihasthaḥ)

Anticipating that Rāma will reject seizing the throne by force, Kausalyā urges him at least to remain at home (इहस्थः) rather than obey a co-wife’s unrighteous demand (अधर्म्यं वचः). Govindarāja cites the smṛti maxim गुर्वर्थेभ्यो माता गरीयसी (“the mother is weightier in reverence than other elders”) to ground her command शुश्रूष माम्.

2.21.24 (the exemplar Kāśyapa)

Both Tilaka and Govindarāja cite the Purāṇic narrative of Kāśyapa (कश्यपपुत्रेष्वेकः), who without leaving his own home (स्वगृहे नियतो वसन्) practiced the supreme austerity of serving his mother (मातृशुश्रूषारूपमहातपसा) and thereby attained heaven and the rank of Prajāpati (त्रिदिवं गतः मातृसेवया प्राजापत्यपदं प्राप्तः).

2.21.25–27 (gauraveṇa and prāyam ihāsiṣye)

Invoking the principle that a mother surpasses a father a hundredfold in reverence (पितुर्दशगुणं माता गौरवेणातिरिच्यते [Tilaka]), Kausalyā withholds her permission (नानुजानामि आज्ञां न करोमि [Bhūṣaṇa]) and begs that if Rāma must go to the forest, he take her with him to eat even grass (त्वया सह मम श्रेयस्तृणानामपि भक्षणम्). Otherwise, she vows to undertake a fast unto death (प्रायम् अनशनदीक्षाम् [Tilaka, Bhūṣaṇa]).

2.21.28 (the Ocean and Pippalāda’s kṛtyā)

All three commentators explain the Purāṇic allusion in ब्रह्महत्यामिवाधर्मात् समुद्रः सरितां पतिः: in a former age, because the Ocean caused grief to his mother (मातृदुःखजननरूपाधर्मात्), or wronged the sage Pippalāda, he was assailed by a fiery female spirit (पिप्पलादब्रह्मर्षिकृतां कृत्यां प्राप्य) and suffered torment equal to the hell reserved for Brāhmaṇa-slayers (ब्रह्महत्याप्राप्यनरकविशेषान् प्राप्तवान्).

2.21.29–30 (priority of the father’s command)

Explaining why Rāma says he lacks the power to transgress his father’s word (नास्ति शक्तिः पितुर्वाक्यं समतिक्रमितुं मम), Tilaka and Govindarāja note that since executing both contradictory commands is impossible (युगपदनुष्ठानासंभवात्), the father’s command prevails because it was received first (पितृवचसः प्रथमप्राप्तत्वेन प्राबल्यात्).

2.21.34–35 (aklībam)

Tilaka and Govindarāja gloss अक्लीबम् as free of faintheartedness or hesitation (कातररहितम् / अकातरम् अक्लिष्टम्).

2.21.36 (reconciling the primacy of mother and father)

On पूर्वैरयमभिप्रेतो गतो मार्गः, Govindarāja resolves the apparent conflict between the maxims exalting the mother (पितुर्दशगुणं माता गौरवेणातिरिच्यते) and those exalting the father: the mother’s preeminence applies to general reverence and personal service (तत्तु शुश्रूषामात्रे), whereas in executing an explicit command (वचनकरणे) the father alone is the supreme authority (पितुरेव नियन्तृत्वात्).

2.21.37–39 (na kaścin nāma hīyate)

Closing his reply to Kausalyā, Rāma affirms that no one who obeys his father’s word ever suffers spiritual loss (न कश्चिन्नाम हीयते धर्मेणेति शेषः [Tilaka, Śiromaṇi]). Addressing Lakṣmaṇa next (लक्ष्मणं पुनरब्रवीत्), he first praises Lakṣmaṇa’s supreme affection (मयि स्नेहमनुत्तमम्), valor, and unassailable energy (तेजश्च सुदुरासदम्) to soften his rebuke.

2.21.40 (satyasya ca śamasya ca)

Explaining why Kausalyā’s grief is so overwhelming (मम मातुर्महद् दुःखमतुलम्) and why Lakṣmaṇa seconds her, Govindarāja and Tilaka note that neither has grasped the deeper secret of truthfulness and inner restraint (सत्यस्य शमस्य अभिप्रायं रहस्यमविज्ञाय): even though Rāma understands that secret, asks Govindarāja, why does Lakṣmaṇa speak against dharma (त्वं किमधर्म्यं वदसि)?

2.21.41–42 (dharme satyaṃ pratiṣṭhitam and saṃśrutya)

Unfolding the secret of truth (धर्मतत्त्वमाह [Bhūṣaṇa]), Rāma declares that dharma is the supreme end in the world (लोके पुरुषार्थेषु धर्मः परमः), that truth is rooted in and culminates in dharma (धर्मे सत्यं प्रतिष्ठितं धर्मैकपर्यवसायि सत्यम्), and that having solemnly promised (संश्रुत्य प्रतिज्ञाय) to obey a father, mother, or Brāhmaṇa, one standing in dharma must never make that pledge empty (वृथा मोघं न कर्तव्यम्).

2.21.43–44 (refuting secular kṣatradharma and lokāyata policy)

Answering Kausalyā’s objection that the command was spoken by her co-wife (v. 22), Rāma replies that Kaikeyī urged him solely on the authority of his father’s word (पितुर्हि वचनात् कैकेय्याहं प्रचोदितः). On v. 44, Govindarāja offers a celebrated philosophical synthesis: Rāma bids Lakṣmaṇa abandon this ignoble resolve (अनार्यां दुष्टाम्) rooted in ruthless Kṣatriya realpolitik (रोदनाद्यसहिष्णुक्षत्रधर्माश्रिताम्, citing Mahābhārata: क्षत्रधर्मो महारौद्रः शस्त्राजीव इति स्मृतः). In this passage, observes Govindarāja, Vālmīki stages the refutation of the materialistic Lokāyata doctrine that political expediency alone divorced from dharma secures success (तत्र लक्ष्मणमुखेन लोकायतं प्रवर्तितं तन्निरासेन रामेण धर्मः स्थाप्यत इति रहस्यम्).

2.21.45–46 (śāpitāsi mama prāṇaiḥ)

Joining his palms, Rāma binds Kausalyā by an oath on his own life (शापितासि शपथं प्रापितासि गमननिरोधं मा कुर्विति शेषः [Tilaka]) to cease obstructing his departure and perform his parting benedictions (स्वस्त्ययनानि).

2.21.47–48 (the simile of Yayāti)

All three commentators explain ययातिरिव राजर्षिः: just as King Yayāti fell from heaven to earth when his merit was exhausted and ascended to heaven again (दिवं हित्वा भूमौ पतितः पुनर्दिवं यथा गतः), so Rāma will return to Ayodhyā once his vow is fulfilled (तीर्णप्रतिज्ञः समापितप्रतिज्ञः).

2.21.49–50 (pitur niyoge sthātavyam and sambhārān)

Extending the obligation beyond himself (न केवलं ममैवायं भारः किंतु युष्माकमपि [Bhūṣaṇa]), Rāma declares that Kausalyā, himself, Sītā, Lakṣmaṇa, and Sumitrā must all abide by the king’s command. On v. 50a, Tilaka and Śiromaṇi take सम्भारान् as the consecration materials (अभिषेकसम्भारान्, which Śiromaṇi’s reading संहृत्य means “putting away”), whereas Govindarāja takes सम्भृत्य सम्भारान् as gathering the auspicious materials needed for parting benedictions (पूजाद्रव्याणि).

2.21.51–53 (avyagram aviklavam and svadhayāmṛtena)

Govindarāja cites Amara (व्यग्रो व्याकुल आकुलः) to gloss अव्यग्रम् as unagitated (अनाकुलम्) and अविक्लवम् as resolute (अविषण्णं दृढनिश्चयप्रतिपादकम्). Reviving from her swoon (मृतेव संज्ञां प्रतिलभ्य मूर्च्छितेति यावत्), Kausalyā protests in v. 53 that without Rāma she cares nothing for relatives or the next world (लोकेनेतरबन्धुजनेन [Tilaka]; परलोकेन [Bhūṣaṇa]), obsequial offerings (स्वधया पितृपूजनेन), or immortality (अमृतेन).

2.21.54 (the torch-assailed elephant)

Under Tilaka’s reading ध्वान्तमभिप्रविष्टः, Rāma’s inner anguish blazes like an elephant in darkness tormented by men with firebrands (उल्काभिरलातैरपोह्यमानः); under Govindarāja’s reading अध्वानमनुप्रविष्टः, just as an elephant that has entered the road stands firm despite torches, so Rāma stands firm on the path of dharma (स्वमार्ग एव स्थितोऽभूत्).

2.21.55–56 (Vālmīki’s praise of Rāma’s incomparable dharmaniṣṭhā)

Commenting on यथा स एवार्हति तत्र वक्तुम् (v. 55d), both Tilaka and Govindarāja highlight the poet’s figure of self-comparison (अनन्वयालङ्कारः): in such a supreme moral crisis (तत्र तस्मिन् धर्मसङ्कटे अतिकृच्छ्रावस्थायाम्), no other person existed who was so single-mindedly devoted to dharma (एतादृशधर्मैकनिष्णातपुरुषान्तरस्याभावात् स एवार्हतीति वाल्मीकिः स्तौति). In v. 56d, Tilaka notes that अभ्यर्दयसि / अभ्यर्दसि carries an implicit causative sense (अन्तर्भावितण्यर्थता, “you pain me”) and records the variant अभ्यर्थयसि.

2.21.57 (the triad of puruṣārthas and the simile of the three-virtued wife)

Expanding v. 41 (धर्मो हि परमो लोके), Tilaka and Govindarāja explain that when the merit of past dharma ripens (धर्मफलोदयेषु), all three human aims (धर्मार्थकामाः) flow from dharma alone, just as a single wife who is obedient (वश्या अनुकूलवती) fulfills religious duty (धर्मसाधनम्), being beloved (अभिमता प्रिया) fulfills desire (कामसाधनम्), and bearing noble sons (सपुत्रा / सुपुत्रा) secures wealth and the worlds hereafter (अर्थसाधनम्).

2.21.58–59 (choosing dharma alone when the three diverge)

Where all three aims cannot be combined in a single act (यस्मिन् कर्मणि सर्वे धर्मादयोऽसंनिविष्टाः [Tilaka, Bhūṣaṇa]), one must undertake that from which dharma arises (धर्मो यतः स्यात् तदुपक्रमेत), because one bent on wealth detached from dharma (अर्थपरो धर्मविरुद्धकेवलार्थपरः) is hated, and sensuality (कामात्मता)—exemplified in Daśaratha himself (राज्ञ इव [Tilaka])—is condemned. Since Daśaratha unites three titles of authority—preceptor (गुरुः), king (राजा), and aged father (पिता च वृद्धः)—only a cruel man (नृशंसः) would disobey his command, whether issued from anger, joy, or desire.

2.21.60–61 (why Kausalyā cannot accompany Rāma to the forest)

Refuting Kausalyā’s plea to take her along (vv. 26, 52), Rāma points out that Daśaratha is her husband, refuge, and dharma (देव्याश्च भर्ता स गतिश्च धर्मः): for a wife, dwelling with her husband—not with her son—is her duty (तेन सह वास एवास्या धर्मः न तु मया सह वासः [Tilaka]). While that righteous king lives and stands firm on his own path of truth (स्वे पथि सत्यमार्गे वर्तमाने), only a widow (विधवा स्वामिरहिता नारीव) would leave home to follow her son into the forest (विधवाया एव पुत्रेण सह वनगमनमुचितम् [Bhūṣaṇa]).

2.21.62–63 (plural naḥ and spurning an unrighteous crown)

On कुरुष्व नः स्वस्त्ययनानि (“perform benedictions for us,” v. 62b), Govindarāja explains that since Sītā does not yet know of the exile and Lakṣmaṇa has just vowed to precede Rāma into the forest (v. 17), the plural pronoun नः stands for the dual (आवयोः, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa). In v. 63, Rāma refuses to turn his back on glorious fame (महोदयं महाफलं यशः पृष्ठतः कर्तुं नालम्) or choose a paltry earth (अवरां तुच्छप्रयोजनभूतां महीम्) by unrighteousness for the sake of a fleeting life (अदीर्घकाले चञ्चले जीविते).

2.21.64 (hṛdi pradakṣiṇam)

Having taught Lakṣmaṇa the inner philosophy of dharma (दर्शनं स्वसंमतं धर्मरहस्यमनुशास्य [Tilaka]), Rāma circumambulates Kausalyā mentally in his heart (तां हृदि जननीं प्रदक्षिणं चकार), because, notes Śiromaṇi, an overt physical circumambulation at that moment would have signaled immediate separation (प्रत्यक्षप्रदक्षिणस्य वियोगज्ञापकत्वात् तन्न कृतम्).

“Oh, mother the queen! Permit me to go to forest. As Yayati returned to heaven by the power of truth, give me your power of blessings so that I can return here after completion of exile.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“While that righteous king lives—and especially while he abides upon his own path of truth (sve pathi vartamāne)—how could the queen depart hence with me, like some other woman who is a widow (vidhaveva nārī)?”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

सा मा ऽनुमन्यस्व वनं व्रजन्तं
कुरुष्व नः स्वस्त्य्-अयनानि देवि ।
यथा समाप्ते पुनर् आव्रजेयं
यथा हि सत्येन पुनर् ययातिः ॥ ६२ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 सा मानुमन्यस्व वनं व्रजन्तं
1 कुरुष्व नः स्वस्त्ययनानि देवि ।
1 यथा समाप्ते पुनराव्रजेयं
1 यथा हि सत्येन पुनर्ययातिः ॥ ६२ ॥

मूलम् - VR

यशो ह्यहम् केवलराज्यकारणात् । न पृष्ठतः कर्तुमलम् महोदयम् । अदीर्घकाले न तु देवि जीविते । वृणेऽवरामद्य महीमधर्मतः ॥ २-२१-६२

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘अतः देवि! तुम मुझे वनमें जानेकी आज्ञा दो और हमारे मङ्गलके लिये स्वस्तिवाचन कराओ, जिससे वनवासकी अवधि समाप्त होनेपर मैं फिर तुम्हारी सेवामें आ जाऊँ । जैसे राजा ययाति सत्यके प्रभावसे फिर स्वर्गमें लौट आये थे ॥ ६२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आद्दरिन्द देवि! ननगॆ काडिगॆ होगलु नीनु आज्ञॆयन्नु कॊडु हागू नम्म श्रेयस्सिगागि स्वस्तिवाचन माडिसु, अदरिन्द वनवासद अवधि मुगिदाग राजा ययातियु सत्यद प्रभावदिन्द पुनः स्वर्गदिन्द बन्दन्तॆ नानु पुनः निन्न सेवॆगागि हाजरागुवॆनु.॥62॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

62. aham = I; na alam = cannot; vR^ishhTataH kartum = keep back; yashaH = reputation; mahodayam = and glory ; kevalaraajyakaaraNaat = for the sake of kingdom alone; devii = oh; queen! jiivite = In this life; adiirghakaale = which is not long; navR^iNe = I cannot long for; adya = now; avaraam = inferior; mahiim = earth; adharmataH = unrighteously.

अनुवादः

“I cannot keep back reputation and glory for the sake of kingdom alone. This cannot unrighteously long for this inferior kingdom.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Therefore grant me leave as I go to the forest, O queen, and perform the auspicious benedictions for us (kuruṣva naḥ svastyayanāni), so that when my vow is completed I may return again, even as Yayāti returned to heaven through truth!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

यशो ह्य् अहं केवल-राज्य-कारणान्
न पृष्ठतः कर्तुम् अलं महोदयम् ।
अ-दीर्घ-काले न तु देवि जीविते
वृणे ऽवराम् अद्य महीम् अ-धर्मतः ॥ ६३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

यशो ह्यहं केवलराज्यकारणा-
न्न पृष्ठतः कर्तुमलं महोदयम् ।
अदीर्घकालेन तु देवि जीविते
वृणेऽवरामद्य महीमधर्मतः ॥ ६३ ॥

यशो ह्यहं केवलराज्यकारणा-
न्न पृष्टतः कर्तुमलं महोदयम् ।
अदीर्घकालेन तु देवि जीविते
वृणेऽवरामद्य महीमधर्मतः ॥

मूलम् - VR

प्रसादयन् नर वृषभः स मातरम् । पराक्रमाज्जिगमिषुरेव दोम्डकान् । अथ अनुजम् भ्ऱ्शम् अनुशास्य दर्शनम् । चकार ताम् ह्ऱ्दि जननीम् प्रदक्षिणम् ॥ २-२१-६३

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘केवल धर्महीन राज्यके लिये मैं महान् फलदायक धर्मपालनरूप सुयशको पीछे नहीं ढकेल सकता । मा! जीवन अधिक कालतक रहनेवाला नहीं है; इसके लिये मैं आज अधर्मपूर्वक इस तुच्छ पृथ्वीका राज्य लेना नहीं चाहता’ ॥ ६३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

केवल धर्महीन राज्यक्कागि नानु महा फलदायक धर्मपालनरूपी सुयशवन्नु कळॆदुकॊळ्ळलु बयसुवुदिल्ल. अम्मा! जीवनवु हॆच्चुकाल इरुवन्तहुदल्ल; अदक्कागि नानु इन्दु अधर्मपूर्वक ई तुच्छ पृथिय राज्यवन्नु पडॆयलु बयसुवुदिल्ल.॥63॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

63. saH = that Rama; navR^ishhabhaH = the best of men; jigimishhureva = desired to go; daNdakaan = to Dandaka forest ; paraakramaat = with prowess; * prasaadayan* = consoling; maataram = mother; atha = and afterwards; anushaasya = advised; amjam = brother; bhR^isham = intensely; darshanam = with good thoughts; pradakshhiNam chakaara = made circumbulatory salutation; taamjanamiim = to his mother; hR^idi = heartily.

अनुवादः

Rama, the best of men, decided to go to Dandaka forest fearlessly with his prowess, consoled his mother, advised his brother by good thoughts and made hearty circumbulatory salutation to his mother.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“For the sake of mere kingship (kevalarājyakāraṇāt), I cannot cast behind my back (pṛṣṭhataḥ kartum) renown that brings supreme exaltation (mahodayam); in a life of brief duration, O queen, I do not choose this mean earth today through unrighteousness!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

प्रसादयन्नरवृषभः स मातरं
पराक्रमाज्जिगमिषुरेव दण्डकान् ।
अथानुजं भृशमनुशास्य दर्शनं
चकार तां हृदि जननीं प्रदक्षिणम् ॥ ६४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

प्रसादयन्नरवृषभः स मातरं
पराक्रमाज्जिगमिषुरेव दण्डकान् ।
अथानुजं भृशमनुशास्य दर्शनं
चकार तां हृदि जननीं प्रदक्षिणम् ॥ ६४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

इस प्रकार नरश्रेष्ठ श्रीरामचन्द्रजीने धैर्यपूर्वक दण्डकारण्यमें जानेकी इच्छासे माताको प्रसन्न करनेका प्रयत्न किया तथा अपने छोटे भाई लक्ष्मणको भी अपने विचारके अनुसार भलीभाँति धर्मका रहस्य समझाकर मन-ही-मन माताकी परिक्रमा करनेका संकल्प किया ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

ई प्रकार नरश्रेष्ठ श्रीरामचन्द्रनु धैर्यदिन्द दण्डकारण्यक्कॆ होगुव इच्छॆयिन्द तायियन्नु सन्तोषगॊळिसलु प्रयत्निसिदनु हागू तम्मनाद लक्ष्मणनन्नु तन्न विचारक्कनुसार चॆन्नागि धर्मद रहस्यवन्नु तिळिसि, तायियन्नु मनस्सिनल्ले प्रदक्षिणॆ माडिदनु.॥64॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्येऽयोध्याकाण्डे एकविंशः सर्गः ॥ २१ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके अयोध्याकाण्डमें इक्कीसवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ २१ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद अयोध्याकाण्डदल्लि इप्पत्तॊन्दनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु.॥21॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thus conciliating his mother, that bull among men—firmly resolved to go to the Daṇḍaka forest through his heroic energy—earnestly instructed his younger brother in the truth of dharma (darśanam) and circumambulated his mother in his heart (cakāra tāṃ hṛdi jananīṃ pradakṣiṇam).
