Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy - Intro

Book II : Ayodhya Kanda - Book Of Ayodhya

Rama gives instruction to Bharata as regards the duties of a king and the polity under an ideal monarchy under the pretext of enquiring about the welfare of his father and others.

भागसूचना
  1. श्रीरामका भरतको कुशल-प्रश्नके बहाने राजनीतिका उपदेश करना
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Beholding Bharata fallen on the ground in matted locks and bark-cloth like the sun at the end of a yuga, Rāma lifts him onto his lap, smells his head, and anxiously asks why he has come to the forest while King Daśaratha lives.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

जटिलं चीरवसनं प्राञ्जलिं पतितं भुवि ।
ददर्श रामो दुर्दर्शं युगान्ते भास्करं यथा ॥ १ ॥
कथञ्चिदभिविज्ञाय विवर्णवदनं कृशम् ।
भ्रातरं भरतं रामः परिजग्राह पाणिना ॥ २ ॥
आघ्राय रामस्तं मूर्ध्नि परिष्वज्य च राघवम् ।
अङ्के भरतमारोप्य पर्यपृच्छत सादरम् ॥ ३ ॥

अङ्के भरतमारोप्य पर्यपृच्छत सादरम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

जटा और चीर-वस्त्र धारण किये भरत हाथ जोड़कर पृथ्वीपर पड़े थे, मानो प्रलयकालमें सूर्यदेव धरतीपर गिर गये हों । उनको उस अवस्थामें देखना किसी भी स्नेही सुहृद्के लिये अत्यन्त कठिन था । श्रीरामने उन्हें देखा और जैसे-तैसे किसी तरह पहचाना । उनका मुख उदास हो गया था । वे बहुत दुर्बल हो गये थे । श्रीरामने भाई भरतको अपने हाथसे पकड़कर उठाया और उनका मस्तक सूँघकर उन्हें हृदयसे लगा लिया । इसके बाद रघुकुलभूषण भरतको गोदमें बिठाकर श्रीरामने बड़े आदरसे पूछा— ॥ १—३ ॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

1. raamaH = Rama; dadarsha = saw; (Bharata); jaTilam = with matted locks; chiiravasanam = wearing bark-robes; praaN^jalim = and with joined palms; patitam = lying; bhuvi = on the ground; durdarsham = incapable of being seen; bhaaskaram yathaa = as the sun; yugaante = at the end of the world-period.

अनुवादः

Rama saw Bharata, with matted locks, wearing bark-robes and, with joined palms, lying on the ground, incapable of being seen as the sun at the time of the dissolution of the world.

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

जटाधारियागिद्द, नारुमडियन्नुट्टिद्द, युगान्तद सूर्यनोपादियल्लि नोडलु अशक्यनागिद्दु, कैमुगिदुकॊण्डे नॆलद मेलॆ बिद्दिरुव भरतनन्नु श्रीरामनु नोडिदनु. अवनिगॆ भरतन गुरुते सिगलिल्ल. अवनु अष्टु कृशवागिद्दु, कुन्दिद मुखभावदिन्द कूडिद्द, अत्यन्त बडकलाद इवनु नन्न तम्म भरतने ऎम्बुदन्नु हेगो तिळिदु अवनन्नु तन्नॆरडु तोळुगळिन्द हिडिदॆत्तिदनु. बळिक श्रीरामनु भरतन नॆत्तियन्नु आघ्राणिसि गाढवागि आलिङ्गिसिकॊण्डु तॊडॆय मेलॆ कुळ्ळिरिसिकॊण्डु आदरपूर्वकवागि प्रश्निसिदनु.॥1-3॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

2. raamaH = Rama; katham chit = with some difficulty; abhiJ^Naaya = recognized; bharatam = Bharata; vivarNa vadanam = whose face became pale; kR^isham = who was emaciated; parijagraaha = and took; bhraataram = his brother; baahunaa = by the arm.

अनुवादः

Rama recognized with some difficulty his brother Bharata, whose face became pale and who was emaciated, and took him by the arm.

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

3. aaghraaya = smelling; muurdhni = the crown of his head; aaropya = placing him; an^ke = on his lap; raamaH = Rama; raaghavaH = born in Raghu dynasty; pariSvajya cha = embraced; tam bharatam = that Bharata; paryapR^ichchhat = and enquired (as follows); samaahitaH = with an absorbent mind.

अनुवादः

Smelling the crown of his head, placing him on his lap, Rama who was born in Raghu dynasty embraced Bharata and with an absorbent mind, enquired of him as follows:

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1: Rāma beheld him fallen upon the ground with palms joined in supplication, wearing matted locks and garments of bark (jaṭilaṃ cīra-vasanaṃ prāñjaliṃ patitaṃ bhuvi dadarśa rāmo), hard to gaze upon like the sun at the dissolution of a world-age (durdarśaṃ yugānte bhāskaraṃ yathā).

2: Recognizing only with difficulty his brother Bharata, whose face was pale and body emaciated (kathañcid abhivijñāya vivarṇa-vadanaṃ kṛśam bhrātaraṃ bharatam), Rāma embraced him with his arm (rāmaḥ parijagrāha bāhunā).

3: Smelling him upon the crown of his head and embracing him (āghrāya rāmas taṃ mūrdhni pariṣvajya ca rāghavaḥ), Rāma placed Bharata upon his lap and questioned him with composed mind (aṅke bharatam āropya paryapṛcchat samāhitaḥ):

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

क्व नु तेऽभूत् पिता तात यदरण्यं त्वमागतः ।
न हि त्वं जीवतस्तस्य वनमागन्तुमर्हसि ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

क्व नु तेऽभूत् पिता तात यदरण्यं त्वमागतः ।
न हि त्वं जीवतस्तस्य वनमागन्तुमर्हसि ॥ ४ ॥

मूलम् - VR

क्व नु ते अभूत् पिता तात यद् अरण्यम् त्वम् आगतः । न हि त्वम् जीवतः तस्य वनम् आगन्तुम् अर्हसि ॥ २-१००-४

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘तात! पिताजी कहाँ थे कि तुम इस वनमें आये हो? उनके जीते-जी तो तुम वनमें नहीं आ सकते थे ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मगु! नीनु वनक्कॆ बन्दिरुवाग, तन्दॆयवरु ऎल्लिद्दरु? अवरु जीविसिरुवाग नीनु काडिगॆ बरुवुदु सरियल्ल.॥4॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

4. taata = my darling!; kvam = where; abhuut = is; te pitaa = your father; yat tvam = (that) you; aagataH = have come; araNyam = to the forest?; tasya = He; jiivitaH = being alive; tvam = you; na arhasi hi = ought not have; aagatum = to come; vanam = to the forest.

अनुवादः

“My darling! Where is our father, that you have come to the forest? He, being alive, you ought not have to come to the forest.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Where, dear brother, was your father that you have come to the wilderness (kva nu te ’bhūt pitā tāta yad araṇyaṃ tvam āgataḥ)? For surely you ought not to come to the forest while he is alive (na hi tvaṃ jīvatas tasya vanam āgantum arhasi).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

चिरस्य बत पश्यामि दूराद् भरतमागतम् ।
दुष्प्रतीकमरण्येऽस्मिन् किं तात वनमागतः ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

चिरस्य बत पश्यामि दूराद् भरतमागतम् ।
दुष्प्रतीकमरण्येऽस्मिन् किं तात वनमागतः ॥ ५ ॥

मूलम् - VR

चिरस्य बत पश्यामि दूराद् भरतम् आगतम् । दुष्प्रतीकम् अरण्ये अस्मिन् किम् तात वनम् आगतः ॥ २-१००-५

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘मैं दीर्घकालके बाद दूरसे (नानाके घरसे) आये हुए भरतको आज इस वनमें देख रहा हूँ; परंतु इनका शरीर बहुत दुर्बल हो गया है । तात! तुम क्यों वनमें आये हो? ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

ऎष्टो दिनगळ बळिक दूरद मावन मनॆयिन्द बन्दिरुव भरतनन्नु नानु इन्दु नोडुत्तिद्देनॆ. आदरॆ इवन शरीर बहळ दुर्बलवागिदॆ. अय्या! नीनु काडिगॆ एकॆ बन्दॆ.॥5॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

5. pashyaami = I see; bharatam = Bharata; chirasya = after a long time; aagatam = who comes; duuraat = from a far-off distance; duSpratiikam = wearing a mournful face; asmin araNye = into this forest; bata = Alas!; taata = My darling!; kim = why; aagataH = have you come; vanam = to the forest?.

अनुवादः

“I see you after a long time, coming from a far-off distance, wearing a mournful face into this forest Alas! Why have you come to the forest, my darling?”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Alas! After a long time I behold Bharata come from afar (cirasya bata paśyāmi dūrād bharatam āgatam), worn and altered in form in this wilderness; why, dear brother, have you come to the forest (duṣpratīkam araṇye ’smin kiṃ tāta vanam āgataḥ)?

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

कच्चिन्नु धरते तात राजा यत् त्वमिहागतः ।
कच्चिन्न दीनः सहसा राजा लोकान्तरं गतः ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

कच्चिन्नु धरते तात राजा यत् त्वमिहागतः ।
1 कच्चिन्न दीनः सहसा राजा लोकान्तरं गतः ॥ ६ ॥

मूलम् - VR

कच्चिद्धारयए तात राजा यत्त्वमिहागतः । कच्चिन्न दीनः सहसा राजा लोकान्तरं गतः ॥ २-१००-६

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘भाई! महाराज जीवित हैं न? कहीं ऐसा तो नहीं हुआ कि वे अत्यन्त दुःखी होकर सहसा परलोकवासी हो गये हों और इसीलिये तुम्हें स्वयं यहाँ आना पड़ा हो? ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

तम्म! महाराजरु जीवन्तरागिरुवरे? अवरु अत्यन्त दुःखितरागि कूडले परलोक वासिगळागिल्लवल्ल? हागू अदक्कागि निनगॆ काडिगॆ बरबेकायिते.॥6॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

6. taata = my dear brother!; kachchiddhaarayate raajaa = Is the king alive? yat tvam = (that) you; aagataH = have come; iha = here?; diinaH raajaa = (I hope) the miserable king; na gataH kachchit = has not indeed departed; lokaantaram = to the other world; sahasaa = all of a sudden.

अनुवादः

“My dear brother! Is the king alive, that you have come here? I hope the miserable king has not indeed departed to the other world, all of a sudden.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Does the king still sustain his life-breath, dear brother, since you have come here (kaccid dhārayate tāta rājā yat tvam ihāgataḥ)? I trust the grieving king has not suddenly departed to the other world (kaccin na dīnaḥ sahasā rājā lokāntaraṃ gataḥ)!

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Rāma asks whether Bharata’s ancestral kingdom remains secure, whether King Daśaratha, the royal preceptor Vasiṣṭha, and Queens Kausalyā, Sumitrā, and Kaikeyī are well, and whether the family chaplain duly tends and announces the sacred fires.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

कच्चित् सौम्य न ते राज्यं भ्रष्टं बालस्य शाश्वतम् ।
कच्चिच्छुश्रूषसे तात पितुः सत्यपराक्रम ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 कच्चित् सौम्य न ते राज्यं भ्रष्टं बालस्य शाश्वतम् ।
कच्चिच्छुश्रूषसे तात पितुः सत्यपराक्रम ॥ ७ ॥

1 कच्चित्सौम्य न ते राज्यं भ्रष्टं बालस्य शाश्वतम् ।
कच्चिच्छ्रुश्रूषसे तात पितुः सत्यपराक्रम ॥

मूलम् - VR

कच्चित्सौम्य नते राज्यं भ्रष्ठं बालस्य शाश्वतम् । कच्चिछुश्रूषसे तात पितरं सत्यविक्रमम् ॥ २-१००-७

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘सौम्य! तुम अभी बालक हो, इसलिये परम्परासे चला आता हुआ तुम्हारा राज्य नष्ट तो नहीं हो गया? सत्यपराक्रमी तात भरत! तुम पिताजीकी सेवा-शुश्रूषा तो करते हो न? ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

सौम्य! नीनु इन्नु बालकनागिरुवॆ, अदक्कागि परम्परॆयिन्द नडॆदुबन्दिरुव निन्न राज्यवु भ्रष्टवागिल्लवल्ल? सत्यपराक्रमि भरतने! नीनु तन्दॆय सेवा-शुश्रूषॆयन्नु माडुत्तिरुवॆयल्ल.॥7॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

7. saumya = O; gentle brother!; (I hope); shaashvatam = the permanent; raajyam = kingdom; na bhraSTam kachchit = has in no way suffered; te = from your; baalasya = youthful inexperience; taata = My darling!; shushruuSate kachchit = Are you rendering service; pitaram = to our father; satyavikramam = who is truly valiant?

अनुवादः

“O, gentle brother! I hope the eternal kingdom has in a no way suffered from your youthful experience. My darling! Are you rendering service to our father, who is truly valiant?”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

I trust, gentle brother, that your everlasting kingdom has not slipped away from you while you are yet young (kaccit saumya na te rājyaṃ bhraṣṭaṃ bālasya śāśvatam)! Do you dutifully serve our father of true valor, dear brother (kaccic chuśrūṣase tāta pitaraṃ satya-vikramam)?

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

कच्चिद् दशरथो राजा कुशली सत्यसङ्गरः ।
राजसूयाश्वमेधानामाहर्ता धर्मनिश्चितः ॥ ८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

कच्चिद् दशरथो राजा कुशली सत्यसङ्गरः ।
राजसूयाश्वमेधानामाहर्ता धर्मनिश्चितः ॥ ८ ॥

मूलम् - VR

कच्चिद् दशरथो राजा कुशली सत्य सम्गरः । राज सूय अश्व मेधानाम् आहर्ता धर्म निश्चयः ॥ २-१००-८

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘जो धर्मपर अटल रहनेवाले हैं तथा जिन्होंने राजसूय एवं अश्वमेध-यज्ञोंका अनुष्ठान किया है, वे सत्यप्रतिज्ञ महाराज दशरथ सकुशल तो हैं न? ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

धर्मदल्लि अचलनागिद्द, राजसूय, अश्वमेध यज्ञगळ अनुष्ठान माडिद, सत्यप्रतिज्ञराद दशरथ महाराजरु क्षेमवागि इरुवरल्ल.॥8॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

Hunting, gambling, sleeping during the day, lustfulness, inebriation, pride, calumny, lounging about idly or aimlessly, diversions such as singing and dancing. (2). Five kinds of fortifications: By moat, high bank, trees thickly planted, a space destitute of grain or provisions, the turning of waters. (3) Four expedients:- Making peace, liberality, sowing dissension, chastisement. (4) Seven limbs of state king, ministers, friends, treasure, territory, forts and an army. (5) Three objects of human pursuit: Religious merit, material wealth and sensuous enjoyment or the three kinds of power (viz. energy, power of dominion, power of counsel) (6) Three branches of learning: the three Vedas, the knowledge relating to agriculture, commerce and other vocational pursuits and political science. (7) Six strategic expedients: Coming to terms with the enemy, waging war against him, marching against him, biding one’s time to seek a favourable opportunity, causing dissension in the enemy’s ranks, seeking protection of a powerful ally. (8) Adversity brought about by divine agencies: Fire, water in the shape of excessive rains or floods, epidemic or endemic diseases, famine and pestulence, earthquakes and Tsunamis. (9) Adversity brought about by human agencies: officials, thieves, enemies, king’s favourites and king himself, when acutated by greed. (10)Twenty types of monarchs (who are not worth-negotiating with):1. a king who is yet a child. 2. Aged. 3. Who has been ailing for a long time. 4. who has been ostracised by his own kith and kin. 5. ho is characterized by a cowardly attitude. 6. who is surrounded by cowards. 7. who is greedy. 8. has greedy associated. 9. who has estranged his ministers and others. 10. who confers with fickle-minded persons 11. who speaks ill of divine begins and brahmins; 12. who is extremely indulged in sensuous pleasures and luxuries; 13. who is ill-fated; 14. a fatalist (who believes that all things are pre-determined or subject to fate); 15. who is afflicted by famine and; 16. by military reverses; 17. who (mostly) remains away from home; 18. who has numerous enemies; 19. who is in the clutches of adverse times and; 20. who is not devoted to truth and piety.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

68: Understanding in their true nature the group of ten, the group of five, the group of four, and the seven-fold constituent group (daśa-pañca-catur-vargān sapta-vargaṃ ca tattvataḥ), as well as the group of eight, the group of three, and the three sciences, O Rāghava (aṣṭa-vargaṃ tri-vargaṃ ca vidyās tisraś ca rāghava),

69: the conquest of the senses, the six-fold royal policy, divine and human calamities (indriyāṇāṃ jayaṃ buddhvā ṣāḍguṇyaṃ daiva-mānuṣam), the winning over of disaffected enemy subjects, the twenty-fold group of kings, and the circle of constituent states (kṛtyaṃ viṃśati-vargaṃ ca tathā prakṛti-maṇḍalam),

70: the ordering of military marches and battle arrays, and the two-rooted policies of peace and war (yātrā-daṇḍa-vidhānaṃ ca dvi-yonī sandhi-vigrahau)—do you duly observe and practice all these, O prince of great wisdom (kaccid etān mahā-prājña yathāvad anumanyase)?
