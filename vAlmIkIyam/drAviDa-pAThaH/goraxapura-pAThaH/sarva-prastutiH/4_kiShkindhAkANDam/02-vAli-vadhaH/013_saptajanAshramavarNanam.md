<details><summary>Desiraju Hanumanta Rao - Intro</summary>

**Book IV : Kishkindha Kanda - The Empire of Holy Monkeys**

After encouraging Sugreeva to challenge Vali for a second time duly wearing a flower garland as a mark
of identification between the two brothers, Rama and others proceed to Kishkindha. On their way they
chance to see a divine hermitage called sapta jana aashrama 'Hermitage
of Seven Sages.' On worshipping it they proceed to Kishkindha.
</details>

<details><summary>टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**13.1–3 (The Order of March and Sugrīva's Compact Neck)**

Commenting on **13.1–3**, **Śiromaṇi** construes the opening three verses as a single syntactic unit (श्लोकत्रयमेकान्वयि) describing Rāma's advance with his gold-adorned bow. On **13.3c** (संहतग्रीवः), **Tilaka** and **Bhūṣaṇa** explain the epithet either as naturally thick-necked (निबिडकण्ठ इत्यर्थः) or as having his neck bound with the _gajapuṣpī_ creeper (गजपुष्पीबन्धनेन वा).

kandaraaNi = mandira aakaara parvata vivaraaNi = *house-like mountain cavities;

<details><summary>टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**13.6 (Four Distinct Types of Mountain Cavities)**

Glossing the four terms for caves in **13.6** to rule out tautology (इति न पौनरुक्त्यं, **Śiromaṇi**), **Tilaka**, **Śiromaṇi**, and **Bhūṣaṇa** distinguish कन्दराणि as house-shaped mountain caverns (मन्दिराकारपर्वतविवराणि), निर्दराणि as split rock-fissures (स्फुटितशैलरन्ध्रविशेषान्), गुहाः as natural self-formed hollows (देवखातबिलानि), and दरीः as vast or fearsome gorges (महारन्ध्राणि; भयंकरगुहाः).
</details>

*7. maarge* = on way; *taTaakaan* = lakes; *vaiduurya vimalaiH toyaiH* = cat's
eye-like [gem-like,] without, slime, waters; *padmaiH ca aakosha kuDmalaiH* = lotuses, to their

**अनुवादः**

On their way they observed lakes that are glistening forth with gem-like waters without slime, and with
lotus buds in budding stage. [4-13-7]

**टिप्पनी**

The word *aakoshakuDmalaiH* has other renderings. One is as above, saying the lotuses in lakes
are still in the budding stage and they have not started to bloom. The other is that they are neither
large not tiny in size. Another is that the stem is having all buds from below to its end, if the compound
is separated as *aa kosha kuDmalaiH* .

**टिप्पनी**

Secondly the usage of *vaiduurya vimala udaka* is an often-repeated expression, which may not
be construed that the waters are just like cats-eye or Lapis Lazule, the precocious gem in *nava
ratna* / nine-gem combination. In fine composition it is *smaraNa alankaara* , a metaphorical
memorabilia. If it is said that the water is like *vaiduurya* or cat's-eye or Lapis, it reminds
us of all other eight gems including a diamond. As such the waters are crystalline and pellucid in their
look like priceless gems, for water is the invaluable gem-like gift of nature and flowing waters are
the priceless possessions for livelihood as well.

**टिप्पनी**

spell will be rhythmic. The first foot of verse nine is one *samaasa* / one compound only. Both
the unbroken compound and segregated word order are given below for reading pleasure.

**13.13–14 (The Cloud-Dark Grove Ringed by Plantains)**

On **13.13c** (द्रुमषण्डवनं), **Tilaka** and **Śiromaṇi** gloss षण्डः as a dense cluster of trees (द्रुमसमूहनिबिडं काननं), citing the _Medinī_ lexicon, while **Bhūṣaṇa** notes that specifying `tree-groves' excludes mere creeper-thickets (लतागुल्मव्यावृत्तिः).
