Desiraju Hanumanta Rao - Intro

Book IV : Kishkindha Kanda - The Empire of Holy Monkeys

Rama to imbue confidence in Sugreeva hurtles his arrow that penetrates seven sala trees and also the subterranean planes of earth and returns into his quiver. Sugreeva obliging the prowess of Rama submits himself to Rama, who encourages Sugreeva to challenge Vali for a duel. Accordingly a duel ensues wherein Vali assaults Sugreeva and chases him off. Battered and belittled, Sugreeva implores upon Rama why he did not come to his rescue. Rama apologetically states that he could not make out difference between the two brothers for they are alike. Rama asks Sugreeva to wear a garland and again challenge Vali, so with that identification of garland he could aim his arrow at the other dueller. Again they all advance towards Kishkindha for the duel with Vali.

Hearing Sugrīva’s eloquent appeal, mighty Rāma releases a single gold-decked arrow that pierces seven sāla trees, the mountain plateau, and the seven subterranean realms before returning to his quiver.

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

1. mahaatejaa raamaH = great-resplendent, Rama; sugriivasya su bhaaSitam = of Sugreeva, well, spoken; etat ca vacanam shrutvaa = them, also, words, on hearing; pratyaya artham = credence, to inculcate; jagraaha kaarmukam = took up, bow.

अनुवादः

On hearing the well-spoken words of Sugreeva that expressed doubt about Rama’s valour, that great resplendent Rama took-up bow to inculcate credence in Sugreeva. [4-12-1]

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

And having heard this eloquent (subhāṣitam) speech of Sugrīva, Rāma of mighty splendor (mahātejāḥ) took up his bow (kārmukam) in order to instill confidence (pratyayārtham).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

12.1–3 (The Piercing of the Seven Sālas and Subterranean Worlds)

Commenting on 12.1c (प्रत्ययार्थं), Śiromaṇi explains Rāma took up his bow strictly to instill confidence in Sugrīva (सुग्रीवविश्वासार्थं). On 12.3, Tilaka and Śiromaṇi note that although aimed at a single tree (एकसालोद्देशेनैव क्षिप्तोऽपि), the gold-feathered arrow (स्वर्णपुङ्खालंकृतः, Bhūṣaṇa) pierced all seven sālas, their mountain plateau (तदाश्रयगिरिप्रस्थमपि), and the seven subterranean realms (सप्तभूमिरसातलं विवेशेत्यर्थः).

Rama, the bestower of dignity to the sufferers, took up his dreadful bow, aimed at a sala tree, and darted single arrow, by which all the directions reverberated with the twang of his bowstring. [4-12-2]

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having grasped his dreadful bow and a single arrow (śaram ekam), that bestower of honor (mānadaḥ) aimed at a sāla tree and shot it, filling the directions with reverberations (ravaiḥ).

Loosed by that mighty warrior, that arrow adorned with gold (svarṇapariṣkṛtaḥ) pierced the seven sāla trees, the mountain plateau (giriprastham), and the seven subterranean realms (saptabhūmim).

4. saayakaH tu mahaa javaH = arrow, but, with great, briskness; muhuurtena saalaan bhittvaa = in a moment, sala trees, on drilling; niS patya ca = out, coming, also - came up from earth; punaH tuurNam tam eva pravivesha ha = again, swiftly, that, only [that quiver of Rama,], entered, indeed.

Thus that arrow which briskly drilled all the sala trees, came up in a moment from under the earth, and again swiftly re-entered the quiver of Rama. [4-12-4]

टिप्पनी

That arrow not only drilled one tree as asked by Sugreeva, but all the seven, and also a mountain from peak to core, and even entered the seventh earthly plane, that which is beneath the sixth plane of the earth, where atala, vitala, sutala, paataala, rasaatal, talaatala are the names of six strata of earth.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having pierced the sāla trees in an instant (muhūrtena), that shaft of tremendous speed (mahājavaḥ) emerged once more (niṣpatya ca punaḥ) and re-entered that very same quiver (tūṇam tam eva).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

12.4–5 (The Sentient Arrow’s Return and Sugrīva’s Wonder)

On 12.4c–d (निष्पत्य च पुनस्तूणं), Tilaka remarks that the arrow traveled to Rasātala, slew the subterranean demons (तत्रत्यासुरान्हत्वा), and re-entered its original quiver (पूर्वाधारं तूणम्), demonstrating that Rāma’s arrows are presided over by sentient deities (चेतनपराक्रमदेवताधिष्ठितत्वं सूचयति). Bhūṣaṇa attributes the inanimate shaft’s return to mantra-power (मन्त्रप्रभावेनाचेतनस्यापि पुनरागमनम्), while on 12.5 noting Sugrīva saw only the cloven trees.

अनुवादः

On seeing them the seven sala trees that are profoundly fissured, that best monkey Sugreeva went into a stunning astonishment at the rapidity of Rama’s arrow. [4-12-5]

Beholding those seven sāla trees cloven through (nirbhinnān) by the velocity of Rāma's arrow (śaravegena), the bull among Vānaras (vānarapuṅgavaḥ) entered into supreme wonder (vismayam paramam).

साथ ही उन्हें मन-ही-मन बड़ी प्रसन्नता हुई । सुग्रीवने हाथ जोड़कर धरतीपर माथा टेक दिया और श्रीरघुनाथजीको साष्टाङ्ग प्रणाम किया । प्रणामके लिये झुकते समय उनके कण्ठहारादि भूषण लटकते हुए दिखायी देते थे ॥ ६ ॥

जॊतॆगॆ मनस्सिनल्लि सन्तोषवू आयितु. सुग्रीवनु कैमुगिदु, नॆलक्कॆ मस्तकवन्नु चाचि श्रीरामनिगॆ साष्टाङ्ग नमस्कार माडिदनु. नमस्कारक्कागि बागिदाग अवन कण्ठहारादि भूषणगळु तूगुत्तिरुवुदु काणुत्तित्तु.॥6॥

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

6. parama priitaH saH sugriivaH = highly, gladdened, he, that Sugreeva; raaghavaaya kR^ita anjaliH = towards Raghava, makin palm fold; pra lambiikR^ita bhuuSaNaH = while his ornaments, made to dangling [on his crouching]; muurdhnaa nyapatat bhuumau = with head touching, fallen, on ground.

अनुवादः

Highly gladdened Sugreeva made palm-fold in that astonishment, kneeled while his ornaments dangled, and prostrated before Raghava with his head touching the ground. [4-12-6]

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Supremely delighted (paramaprītaḥ), Sugrīva folded his hands in reverence (kṛtāñjaliḥ) and prostrated himself on the ground with his head (mūrdhnā) before Rāghava, his ornaments hanging loosely down (pralambīkṛtabhūṣaṇaḥ).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

12.6 (The Etiquette of Prostration Before the Lord)

Glossing 12.6b (प्रलम्बीकृतभूषणः), Tilaka and Śiromaṇi explain that as Sugrīva fell flat in prostration (साष्टाङ्गप्रणामवशाल्लम्बमानावस्थापनोःकण्ठादिवर्तिसकलभूषणः), his forest-garlands and neck-ornaments hung loosely down. Bhūṣaṇa adds that this indicates his belly did not touch the ground (उदरास्पर्श उक्तः), thereby teaching the proper mode of bowing before the Lord (भगवद्विषये प्रणामप्रकारः शिक्षितो भवति).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Overjoyed by Rāma’s peerless feat of archery, Sugrīva praises his power to vanquish even Indra’s gods, implores him to slay Vālī today, and receives Rāma’s warm embrace.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Resplendent in bodily form (vibhrājamāno vapuṣā) and composed by Rāma's words (rāmavākyasamāhitaḥ), he went together with Rāma (jagāma saha rāmeṇa) and reached Kiṣkindhā once more (kiṣkindhām punar āpa saḥ).
