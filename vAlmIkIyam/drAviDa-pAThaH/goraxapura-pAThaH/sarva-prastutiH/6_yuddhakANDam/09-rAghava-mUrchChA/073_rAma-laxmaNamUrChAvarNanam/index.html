Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy - Intro

Book VI : Yuddha Kanda - Book Of War

Seeing Ravana, his father looking lamented after hearing the death of Ravana’s sons and brothers, Indrajit promises to destroy Rama and Lakshmana. He sets out, for the battle, accompanied by his army. After reaching the battle-field, Indrajit performed a sacrificial ritual there, duly making an oblation to the fire. After performing the sacrificial rite, Indrajit goes himself invisible into the sky. Indrajit then showers multitude of arrows towards the monkeys and the monkeys lose their consciousness. Indrajit tears asunder principal monkey-warriors by his maces and arrows. Then, he rains a multitude of arrows of Rama and Lakshmana. Rama says to Lakshmana that he along with Lakshmana can act as thought they fell unconscious, so that Indrajit can return to Lanka, boasting of his laurels of victory. Grievously hurt on the battle field by the missiles of Indrajit, Rama and Lakshmana along with the whole army of monkeys fell unconscious.

भागसूचना
  1. इन्द्रजित् के ब्रह्मास्त्रसे वानरसेनासहित श्रीराम और लक्ष्मणका मूर्च्छित होना
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Learning from the surviving Rākṣasa warriors that Atikāya, Triśiras, and Devāntaka have fallen, Rāvaṇa sinks into grief until Indrajit vows to slay Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa that very day.

ततो हतान् राक्षसपुङ्गवांस्तान्
देवान्तकादित्रिशिरोऽतिकायान् ।
रक्षोगणास्तत्र हतावशिष्टा-
स्ते रावणाय त्वरिताः शशंसुः ॥ १ ॥

मूलम्

देवान्तकादित्रिशिरोऽतिकायान् ।

ततो हतान्राक्षसपुङ्गवांस्तान् । देवान्तकादित्रिशिरोऽतिकायान् । रक्षोगणास्तत्र हतावशिष्टास् । ते रावणाय त्वरितं शशंसुः ॥ ६-७३-१

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

संग्रामभूमिमें जो निशाचर मरनेसे बच गये थे, उन्होंने तुरंत रावणके पास जाकर उसे देवान्तक, त्रिशिरा और अतिकाय आदि राक्षसपुङ्गवोंके मारे जानेका समाचार सुनाया ॥ १ ॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

1. tataH = then; te = those; hataavashiShTaaH = surviving; rakShogaNaaH = troops of demons; tatra = there; tvaritaaH = hastily; shashamsuH = told; raavaNaaya = Ravana; taan = that; devaantanaadi trishirotikaayaan = Devantaka; Trishira; Atikaya and so on; raakShasapuN^gavaan = the excellent demons; hataan = were killed.

अनुवादः

Then, those surviving troops of demons hastily told Ravana that Devantaka, Trishira, Atikaya and other foremost of demons had been killed.

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रणाङ्गणदल्लि अळिदुळिद राक्षसरु रावणन बळिगॆ बन्दु देवान्तक, त्रिशिरा, अतिकाय मॊदलाद राक्षस श्रेष्ठरु हतरादरॆम्ब वार्तॆयन्नु राजनिगॆ तिळिसिदरु.॥.॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then those hosts of Rākṣasas (rakṣogaṇāḥ) who survived the slaughter there hastened and reported to Rāvaṇa that those bulls among Rākṣasas—headed by Devāntaka, together with Triśiras and Atikāya—had been slain.

ततो हतांस्तान् सहसा निशम्य
राजा महाबाष्पपरिप्लुताक्षः ।
पुत्रक्षयं भ्रातृवधं च घोरं
विचिन्त्य राजा विपुलं प्रदध्यौ ॥ २ ॥

मूलम्

ततो हतांस्तान् सहसा निशम्य
राजा महाबाष्पपरिप्लुताक्षः ।
पुत्रक्षयं भ्रातृवधं च घोरं
विचिन्त्य राजा विपुलं प्रदध्यौ ॥ २ ॥

ततो हतांस्तान्सहसा निशम्य । राजा मुमोहाश्रुपरिप्लुताक्षः। पुत्रक्षयं भ्रातृवधं च घोरं । विचिन्त्य राजा विपुलं प्रदध्यौ ॥ ६-७३-२

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उनके वधकी बात सुनकर राजा रावणके नेत्रोंमें सहसा आँसुओंकी बाढ़ आ गयी । पुत्रों और भाइयोंके भयानक वधकी बात सोचकर उसको बड़ी चिन्ता हुई ॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Hearing all at once that they had been slain, the king, his eyes flooded with heavy tears (mahābāṣpapariplutākṣaḥ), pondered the destruction of his sons and the dreadful slaying of his brother [Kumbhakarṇa], and the king sank into deep reflection.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततस्तु राजानमुदीक्ष्य दीनं
शोकार्णवे सम्परिपुप्लुवानम् ।
रथर्षभो राक्षसराजसूनु-
स्तमिन्द्रजिद् वाक्यमिदं बभाषे ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततस्तु राजानमुदीक्ष्य दीनं
शोकार्णवे सम्परिपुप्लुवानम् ।
रथर्षभो राक्षसराजसूनु-
स्तमिन्द्रजिद् वाक्यमिदं बभाषे ॥ ३ ॥

मूलम् - VR

ततस्तु राजानमुदीक्ष्य दीनं । शोकार्णवे सम्परिपुप्लुवानम्। अथर्षभो राक्षसराजसूनुर् । अथेन्द्रजिद्वाक्यमिदं बभाषे ॥ ६-७३-३

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

राजा रावणको शोकके समुद्रमें निमग्न एवं दीन हुआ देख रथियोंमें श्रेष्ठ राक्षसराजकुमार इन्द्रजित् ने यह बात कही— ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

शोक समुद्रदल्लि मुळुगि दीननाद रावणनन्नु नोडि रथिगळल्लि श्रेष्ठनाद राक्षस राजकुमार इन्द्रजितनु हीगॆ हेळिदनु.॥.॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then, beholding the wretched king floundering in an ocean of grief (śokārṇave samparipupluvānam), Indrajit, the son of the Rākṣasa king and foremost of chariot-warriors (ratharṣabhaḥ), spoke these words to him:

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

न तात मोहं परिगन्तुमर्हसे
यत्रेन्द्रजिज्जीवति नैर्ऋतेश ।
नेन्द्रारिबाणाभिहतो हि कश्चित्
प्राणान् समर्थः समरेऽभिपातुम् ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

न तात मोहं परिगन्तुमर्हसे
यत्रेन्द्रजिज्जीवति नैर्ऋतेश ।
नेन्द्रारिबाणाभिहतो हि कश्चित्
प्राणान् समर्थः समरेऽभिपातुम् ॥ ४ ॥

न तात मोहं प्रतिगन्तु मर्हसे
यत्रेन्द्र जिज्जीवति नैर्ऋतेश ।
नेन्द्रारिबाणाभिहतो हि कश्चित्
प्राणान्समर्थः समरेऽभिपातुम् ॥

मूलम् - VR

न तात मोहं प्रतिगन्तुमर्हसि । यत्रेन्द्रजिज्जीवति राक्षसेन्द्र। नेन्द्रारिबाणाभिहतो हि कश् चित् । प्राणान्समर्थः समरेऽभिधर्तुम् ॥ ६-७३-४

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘तात! राक्षसराज! जबतक इन्द्रजित् जीवित है तबतक आप चिन्ता और मोहमें न पड़िये । इस इन्द्रशत्रुके बाणोंसे घायल होकर कोई भी समराङ्गणमें अपने प्राणोंकी रक्षा नहीं कर सकता ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

तन्दॆये! इन्द्रजितनु बदुकिरुववरॆगॆ नीवु चिन्तॆ, शोक माडबेडि. ई इन्द्रशत्रुविन बाणगळिन्द गायगॊण्डु यारू समराङ्गणदल्लि बदुकिरलारनु.॥4॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

4. taata = O father; nairR^itesha = the king od demons!; na arhase = you ought not; parigantum = to get; moham = emberassment; yatra indrajit jiivati = when Indrajit is alive; kashchit = anyone; indraari baaNaabhihataH = who is struck by Indrajit’s arrows; samarthaH hi abhipaatum = cannot indeed protect; praaNaan = his lives.

अनुवादः

“O father, the king of demons! You ought not to get embarrassed, while Indrajit is alive. Anyone who is struck by Indrajit’s arrows cannot indeed protect his life.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“O father (tāta), O lord of the Nairṛtas (nairṛteśa), you ought not to yield to delusion while Indrajit lives; for no one struck by the arrows of Indra's foe (indrāribāṇābhihataḥ) is able to preserve his life-breaths in battle.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

पश्याद्य रामं सह लक्ष्मणेन
मद‍्बाणनिर्भिन्नविकीर्णदेहम् ।
गतायुषं भूमितले शयानं
शितैः शरैराचितसर्वगात्रम् ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

पश्याद्य रामं सह लक्ष्मणेन
मद‍्बाणनिर्भिन्नविकीर्णदेहम् ।
गतायुषं भूमितले शयानं
शितैः शरैराचितसर्वगात्रम् ॥ ५ ॥

मूलम् - VR

पश्याद्य रामं सहलक्ष्मणेन । मद्बाणनिर्भिन्नविकीर्णदेहम्। गतायुषं भूमितले शयानं । शरैः शितैराचितसर्वगात्रम् ॥ ६-७३-५

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘देखिये, आज मैं राम और लक्ष्मणके शरीरको बाणोंसे छिन्न-भिन्न करके उनके सारे अङ्गोंको तीखे सायकोंसे भर देता हूँ, और वे दोनों भाई गतायु होकर सदाके लिये धरतीपर सो जाते हैं ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

इन्दु नानु राम-लक्ष्मणर शरीरगळन्नु बाणगळिन्द छिन्नभिन्नगॊळिसि अवर सर्वाङ्गवन्नु सायकगळिन्द तुम्बिबिडुवॆनु. मत्तॆ आ इब्बरू सहोदररु असुनीगुवुदन्नु नीनु नोडु.॥5॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

5. adya = today; pashya = see; raamam = Rama; lakShmaNena saha = along with Lakshmana; madbaaNa nirbhinna vikiirNa deham = with their bodies undistinguished; scattered; gataayuSham = and dead; shayaanam = lying down; bhuumitale = on the floor; aachita sarva gaatram = with their limbs diffused all over.

अनुवादः

“Today, you will see Rama along with Lakshmana, with their bodies undistinguished, scattered and dead, lying down on the floor, with their limbs diffused all over.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Behold today Rāma, together with Lakṣmaṇa, his body cleft and scattered by my shafts (madbāṇanirbhinnavikīrṇadeham), bereft of life (gatāyuṣam), lying upon the surface of the earth with all his limbs bristling with sharp arrows.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

इमां प्रतिज्ञां शृणु शक्रशत्रोः
सुनिश्चितां पौरुषदैवयुक्ताम् ।
अद्यैव रामं सह लक्ष्मणेन
सन्तर्पयिष्यामि शरैरमोघैः ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

इमां प्रतिज्ञां शृणु शक्रशत्रोः
सुनिश्चितां पौरुषदैवयुक्ताम् ।
अद्यैव रामं सह लक्ष्मणेन
सन्तर्पयिष्यामि शरैरमोघैः ॥ ६ ॥

इमां प्रतिज्ञां शृणु शक्रशत्रोः
सुनिश्चितां पौरुष दैवयुक्ताम् ।
अद्य्वॆव रामं सह लक्ष्मणेन
सन्तर्पयिष्यामि शरैरमोघैः ॥

मूलम् - VR

इमां प्रतिज्ञां शृणु शक्रशत्रोः । सुनिश्चितां पौरुषदैवयुक्ताम्। अद्यैव रामं सहलक्ष्मणेन । सन्तापयिष्यामि शरैरमोघैः ॥ ६-७३-६

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘आप मुझ इन्द्रशत्रुकी इस सुनिश्चित प्रतिज्ञाको, जो मेरे पुरुषार्थसे और दैवबल (ब्रह्माजीकी कृपा)-से भी सिद्ध होनेवाली है, सुन लीजिये—मैं आज ही लक्ष्मणसहित रामको अपने अमोघ बाणोंसे पूर्णतः तृप्त करूँगा—उनकी युद्धविषयक पिपासाको बुझा दूँगा ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

नन्न पुरुषार्थदिन्द मत्तु ब्रह्मदेवर कृपॆयिन्द सिद्ध वागुव इन्द्रशत्रुवाद नन्न सुनिश्चित प्रतिज्ञॆयन्नु केळिरि-‘‘नानु इन्दे लक्ष्मण सहित रामनन्न नन्न अमोघ बाणगळिन्द पूर्णवागि तृप्तिपडिसुवॆनु. अवन युद्ध पिपासॆयन्नु तीरिसुवॆनु.॥6॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

6. shR^iNu = Hear; shakrashatoH = of Indrajit’s; pratiJNaam = promise; sunishchitaan = which is very firm; pauruShadaivayuktaam = belonging to valour and coming from gods; adyaiva = now itself; samtarpayiShyaami = I shall overfill; raamam = Rama; lakShmaNena saha = together with Lakshmana; amoghaiH sharaughaiH = with unfailing flood of arrows.

अनुवादः

“Listen to Indrajit’s promise, which is very firm, backed by valour and divine blessing. Now itself, I will overwhelm Rama together with Lakshmana unfailing flood of arrows.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Hear this firmly resolved vow (pratijñām) of Śakra's foe, backed by manly valor and divine favor (pauruṣadaivayuktām): this very day I shall satiate Rāma, together with Lakṣmaṇa, with my unfailing shafts!

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Likening his impending feat to Viṣṇu’s stride at Bali’s sacrifice, Indrajit takes leave of Rāvaṇa and mounts his mule-drawn chariot, followed by Rākṣasa warriors on fierce mounts.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अद्येन्द्रवैवस्वतविष्णुरुद्र-
साध्याश्च वैश्वानरचन्द्रसूर्याः ।
द्रक्ष्यन्ति मे विक्रममप्रमेयं
विष्णोरिवोग्रं बलियज्ञवाटे ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अद्येन्द्रवैवस्वतविष्णुरुद्र-
साध्याश्च वैश्वानरचन्द्रसूर्याः ।
द्रक्ष्यन्ति मे विक्रममप्रमेयं
विष्णोरिवोग्रं बलियज्ञवाटे ॥ ७ ॥

मूलम् - VR

अद्येन्द्रवैवस्वतविष्णुमित्र । साध्याश्विवैश्वानरचन्द्रसूर्याः। द्रक्ष्यन्ति मे विक्रममप्रमेयं । विष्णोरिवोग्रं बलियज्ञवाटे ॥ ६-७३-७

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

6.73.1–2 (Rāvaṇa’s Renewed Grief)

Commenting on ततो हतांस्तान्, Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explains why Rāvaṇa grieves anew after earlier reports of Kumbhakarṇa’s and his sons’ deaths: previously the slaughter was heard only in general terms, whereas now the surviving Rākṣasas recount the catastrophe in full detail (पूर्वं कुम्भकर्णादिवधः सामान्येन श्रुतः संप्रत्येतैः सविशेषं श्रुत इत्याह तत इति). Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) unpacks the compound देवान्तकादित्रिशिरोऽतिकायान् as grouping those headed by Devāntaka together with Triśiras and Atikāya (देवान्तकादयश्च त्रिशिरोऽतिकायौ च तान्), while Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) glosses विपुलं प्रदध्यौ as pondered deeply (विपुलमत्यन्तं प्रदध्यौ विचारयामास).

6.73.3–4 (Indrajit’s Reassurance)

Both Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa derive संपरिपुप्लुवानम् as a perfect participle of √प्लु, which Tilaka glosses as plunged into an ocean of sorrow (शोकार्णवमग्नमित्यर्थः) and रथर्षभः as foremost of chariot-warriors (रथस्थानां श्रेष्ठः). On यत्रेन्द्रजिज्जीवति, Tilaka takes यत्र causally (यस्मादिन्द्रजिज्जीवति), whereas Śiromaṇi and Bhūṣaṇa take it temporally (यदा). All three identify इन्द्रारिः (“Indra’s foe”) as Indrajit himself.

6.73.5–6 (Valor and Divine Boons)

On पौरुषदैवयुक्ताम्, Tilaka and Śiromaṇi gloss पौरुषेण as innate martial valor (सहजशौर्यबलेन) and दैवेन as the grace of divine boons (भगवद्वरदानानुग्रहेण), while Bhūṣaṇa similarly explains them as personal effort and divine boons (भगवद्दत्तवरेण). Śiromaṇi glosses सन्तर्पयिष्यामि as “I shall fill completely” (पूरयिष्यामि) with unfailing arrows.

6.73.7–8 (Bali’s Sacrificial Enclosure and the Chariot)

Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explains बलियज्ञवाटे as Bali’s sacrificial hall (यागशालायां), where the gods headed by Indra will witness Indrajit’s immeasurable prowess just as they beheld Viṣṇu’s fierce Trivikrama stride (विष्णोरुग्रमप्रमेयं विक्रममिव). On खरश्रेष्ठसमाधियुक्तम्, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śiromaṇi derive समाधयः instrumentally (समाधीयते युद्धमेभिरिति समाधयो धनुःखड्गादयः) to mean weapons and war-gear such as bows and swords alongside the finest mules (खरश्रेष्ठैः), whereas Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) glosses it as yoked with excellent mules and firm concentration (खरैः समाधिना समाधानेन च युक्तम्).

6.73.9 (The Chariot of Hari)

Commenting on हरिरथोपमम्, Tilaka takes हरिरथः to mean the chariot of Indra (इन्द्ररथः), whereas Bhūṣaṇa interprets it as the chariot of the Sun (सूर्यरथः), citing the Amarakośa verse listing the senses of hari (यमानिलेन्द्रचन्द्रार्कविष्णुसिंहांशुवाजिषु). On यत्र युद्धम्, Bhūṣaṇa supplies the future passive participle कर्तव्यम् (“where the battle was to be conducted”).

6.73.10–12 (The Rākṣasa Retinue)

Tilaka notes that संहर्षमाणाः is an epic (ārṣa) form, which Śiromaṇi glosses as संहृष्यमाणाः (“rejoicing”). Both Śiromaṇi and Bhūṣaṇa observe that verses 11–13 form a single syntactic unit (श्लोकत्रयमेकान्वयि) governed by अनुजग्मुः from verse 10.

6.73.13–14 (Martial Array and Drumbeats)

Concluding the three-verse syntactic chain (श्लोकत्रयमेकान्वयि) carried over from verses 11–12, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explains that the Rākṣasa warriors bearing prāsa lances, swords, axes, maces, and bhuśuṇḍis followed Indrajit (अनुजग्मुरित्यनुकृष्यते). On verse 14, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śiromaṇi gloss आजिम् as battle (युद्धम्, सङ्ग्रामम्), toward which Indrajit sped accompanied by resonant conchs and kettle-drums.

6.73.15–16 (The Moon-White Parasol and Golden Chowries)

Both Tilaka and Śiromaṇi analyze शङ्खशशिवर्णेन as an elliptical comparison (शङ्खस्य शशिनश्च वर्ण इव वर्णो यस्य तत्), comparing Indrajit beneath his white royal parasol to the firmament (नभ इव) illumined by the full moon (प्रतिपूर्णेन चन्द्रमसा). On हैमैः modifying the yak-tail fans (चामरमुख्यैः), Tilaka glosses it as equipped with golden handles (हिरण्मयदण्डैः), while Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa), reading अवीज्यत and हेमविभूषितैः, explains it as fitted with golden ribs (हेमशलाकायुक्तैः).

6.73.17–18 (Rāvaṇa’s Benediction)

As the apparatus notes record (इदं सार्धं गो. पाठे), Govindarāja’s text preserves Rāvaṇa’s parting address praising Indrajit as an unrivalled chariot-warrior (अप्रतिरथः) who vanquished Indra and will effortlessly slay the mortal Rāghava.

6.73.19–21b (Arrival at Nikumbhilā)

Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) remarks that Laṅkā shone through Indrajit of peerless prowess just as the sky shines with the sun (अर्केण द्यौरिव रराज). Commenting on युद्धभूमिम्, both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śiromaṇi explain that it denotes not the open battlefield but the sacred precinct of Nikumbhilā where the homa securing victory in battle is performed (युद्धजयसंपादकहोमसाधनभूमिम् । निकुम्भिलास्थानमित्यर्थः). On रथं प्रति, Śiromaṇi glosses it as in all four directions around his chariot (रथस्य समीपे समन्ततश्चतुर्दिक्षु), while Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) takes it as facing the chariot (रथमुद्दिश्य).

6.73.21c–23b (The Abhicāra Oblation)

Tilaka glosses हुतभोक्तारम् as Agni (अग्निम्) and विधिवत् as according to the ritual procedure prescribed in its magic manual (तत्कल्पोक्तप्रकारः). On हविर्लाजसत्कारैर्माल्यगन्धपुरस्कृतैः, Tilaka and Śiromaṇi explain that Indrajit first honored the fire with garlands, unguents, and parched grain before offering the animal oblation (हविर्वक्ष्यमाणच्छागरूपं जुहुवे), whereas Bhūṣaṇa, reading हविर्लाजसंस्कारैः, interprets it as consecrated oblations and parched rice (संस्कृतहविर्लाजैः).

6.73.23c–24 (Sorcerous Implements)

Indrajit’s dark abhicāra rite substitutes arrow-blades (शरपत्राणि) for darbha grass, bibhītaka sticks for sacrificial fuel, red garments, and a black-iron ladle (स्रुवं कार्ष्णायसं).

6.73.25–27b (The Black Goat and Agni’s Epiphany)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explain that after strewing the fire with arrow-blades in place of kāśa grass—as prescribed in destructive abhicāra rites (अभिचारकर्मणि तेषामेव विनियोगात्)—Indrajit seized a live black goat by the throat to offer it whole into the fire (सजीवस्य होमार्धमिति शेषः). From the smokeless fire kindled at once (सकृदेव समिद्धस्य) arose tokens of victory (विजयं यान्यदर्शयन्): Agni himself rose up (स्वयमुत्थितः, glossed by Govindarāja’s Bhūṣaṇa as पुरुषरूपेणोत्थितः, whereas on his own reading स्वयमास्थितः he glosses स्वयमास्थावान्) with clockwise-spiraling flames (प्रदक्षिणावर्तशिखः) to accept the oblation.

6.73.27c–28 (Invoking the Brahmāstra)

Tilaka explains आहारयामास ब्राह्मम् as invoking the Brahmāstra and charging his bow and chariot with its mantra (सिद्धब्रह्मास्त्रमन्त्रेणाभ्यमन्त्रयत्). While the presiding deity of the missile was summoned (तदस्त्रमन्त्रदैवते क्रियमाणावाहने), the sky with sun, planets, and stars trembled (वितत्रास नभःस्थलम्).

6.73.29–30 (Vanishing into the Sky)

All three commentators note the ārṣa elision of the initial vowel of आत्मानम् in खेऽन्तर्दधेऽत्मानम् (आत्मशब्दाकारलोप आर्षः), and Bhūṣaṇa observes that अन्तर्दधे carries an implicit causative sense (अन्तर्भावितण्यर्थोऽयम्, “he rendered himself invisible”).

6.73.31–33 (Indrajit’s Exhortation to the Rākṣasas)

On verse 32, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi), and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) all supply a verb of speaking (आहेति शेषः / उवाचेति शेषः) to govern Indrajit’s direct command हृष्टा भवन्तो युध्यन्तु. Commenting on the two occurrences of ततः in verse 33, Bhūṣaṇa explains the first causally (“on account of that speech of Rāvaṇa’s son,” तस्माद् रावणिवचनात्) and the second temporally (द्वितीयस्ततःशब्दः पश्चाच्छब्दार्थः), while Śiromaṇi glosses अभ्यवर्षन् as covered (आच्छादयन्).

6.73.34 (How the Invisible Warrior is Surrounded)

Both Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa address how Indrajit can be described as surrounded by Rākṣasas (रक्षोभिः संवृतः) after vanishing into the sky in verse 29: they explain that he hovered in the region of the sky immediately above the middle of the Rākṣasa host (राक्षससैन्योपरि प्रत्यासन्नाकाशदेशे स्थितः; Bhūṣaṇa: रक्षोगणमध्यत उपरिस्थित इत्यर्थः । अन्यथा खेऽन्तर्दध इति पूर्वोक्तेन विरुध्येत). Bhūṣaṇa glosses नालीकः as a broad-headed arrow (विशालाग्रः शरः).

6.73.35–36 (Aiming at the Unseen Foe)

On अभ्यवर्षन्त सहसा रावणिम्, Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa explain that since Indrajit was invisible, the Vānaras hurled their trees and boulders toward the sky-sector inferred from his army’s position or the trajectory of his incoming missiles (रावणिमुक्तायुधागमनप्रदेशम्).

6.73.37–39 (Slaughter of the Vānara Ranks)

Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explains नव पञ्च च सप्त च as piercing groups of nine, five, and seven monkeys successively with a single shaft (एकेन शरेण नवपञ्चसप्तसङ्ख्याकान् हरीन् बिभेद). On मथितसंकल्पाः in verse 39, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) glosses it as having their determination to fight broken (संहृतयुद्धसंकल्पाः), Śiromaṇi as having their desire for battle uprooted (निकोचितयुद्धेच्छाः), and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) as having their hopes destroyed (नाशितमनोरथाः).

6.73.40–41 (Charging the Arrow-Sun and Recoiling)

Śiromaṇi unpacks the metaphor बाणगभस्तिभिः as rays consisting of arrows (बाणरूपकिरणैः) with which Indrajit scorched the battlefield like the sun (प्रतपन्तमादित्यमिवेन्द्रजितं). Tilaka notes that the Vānara chiefs charged toward the sky-region conjectured to hold him (तदधिष्ठितमित्युत्प्रेक्षितदिग्भागेनेति शेषः). On verse 41, Śiromaṇi traces the sequence of effects: their bodies cleft (भिन्ना विदीर्णा देहा येषाम्) and drenched in blood, the anguished and bewildered monkeys scattered in every direction (इतस्ततश्चरन्ति स्म).

6.73.42 (Heroic Stand for Rāma)

Tilaka glosses त्यक्तजीविताः as having renounced the wish to live (त्यक्तजीवितेच्छाः) and सशिलायुधाः as bearing boulders for weapons (शिलारूपायुधैः सहिताः).

6.73.43–45 (Warding Off the Boulder-Shower)

Standing their ground (समवस्थिताः), the Vānara warriors hurled a lethal rain of trees, crags, and boulders toward the unseen Indrajit, which the battle-conquering (समितिंजयः) Rākṣasa prince effortlessly swept away (व्यपोहत) before cleaving their divisions with fiery, serpent-like shafts (पावकसंकाशैः शरैराशीविषोपमैः).

6.73.46–48 (Syntax of the Catalogue of Wounded Chiefs)

Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) points out that the three and a half verses beginning with अष्टादशशरैस्तीक्ष्णैः (verses 46–49b) constitute a single connected sentence (अष्टादशेत्यादिसार्धश्लोकत्रयमेकान्वयम्). Stationed invisibly at a distance (दूरादवस्थितम्), Indrajit methodically targets the Vānara high command: piercing Gandhamādana with eighteen keen arrows, Nala with nine, Mainda with seven vitals-rending shafts (मर्मविदारणैः), and Gaja with five viśikha arrows.

6.73.48 (Jāmbavān, Nīla, Sugrīva, and Aṅgada)

Continuing the same syntactic unit, Indrajit strikes Jāmbavān with ten shafts and the field-marshal Nīla with thirty (नीलं त्रिंशद्भिरेव च), while the accusatives सुग्रीवमृषभं चैव सोऽङ्गदं द्विविदं तथा are governed by निष्प्राणानकरोत् तदा in the opening half of verse 49.

6.73.49–50 (Boon-Endowed Shafts and Doomsday Fire)

Concluding the three-and-a-half-verse sentence (अष्टादशेत्यादिसार्धश्लोकत्रयमेकान्वयम्) identified by Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa), verse 49a–b supplies the predicate निष्प्राणानकरोत् तदा governing Sugrīva, Ṛṣabha, Aṅgada, and Dvivida from verse 48. Bhūṣaṇa glosses दत्तवरैः as arrows empowered by divine boons (वरदत्तैः), with which Indrajit left the Vānara leaders breathless and harassed (अर्दयामास) the remaining chiefs like the swollen fire of cosmic dissolution (कालाग्निरिव मूर्च्छितः).

6.73.51–53 (The Blood-Soaked Vānara Host)

Verses 51c–52 form a connected unit: beholding the Vānara army bewildered (आकुलां), afflicted by his net of shafts (शरजालेन पीडिताम्, where Bhūṣaṇa reads मोहिताम्), and drenched in blood (क्षतजोक्षिताम्), Rāvaṇa’s son rejoiced with supreme satisfaction (परया प्रीत्या) and crushed their ranks on all sides.

6.73.54 (Advancing Beyond His Own Lines)

Commenting on स्वसैन्यमुत्सृज्य, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) explains that Indrajit departed from the sky-space directly above his own Rākṣasa troops (स्वसैन्योपरिभागाकाशमुत्सृज्य) to plunge invisibly (अदृश्यमानः) into the Vānara divisions like a dark storm-cloud (नीलाम्बुधरः).

6.73.55–56 (Slaughter by an Unseen Assailant)

Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) glosses मायाहताः as bewildered by the enemy’s deceitful illusion (मायया शत्रुनिर्मितच्छलेन हता मोहिताः), causing the mountain-huge monkeys to topple like peaks struck by Indra’s vajra. On verse 56, Śiromaṇi notes that the Vānaras perceived only (केवलं) the incoming sharp-tipped shafts without glimpsing Indrajit himself, whom Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa), reading मायानिगूढं and आवृतं, describes as veiled and unmanifest (अत एव आवृतम् अप्रकाशम्).

6.73.57–58 (Fiery Pikes, Swords, and Battle-Axes)

While Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śiromaṇi read विदारयामास (“he tore the Vānara chiefs apart”), Bhūṣaṇa reads विषादयामास (“he cast them into dejection”). On verse 58, Śiromaṇi explains that the pikes, swords, and axes shone like brandished firebrands (व्याविद्धो यो दीप्तानलः तस्य सन्निभानि) because they were wreathed in sparking flames (सविस्फुलिङ्गा विस्फुलिङ्गसहिताः उज्ज्वलपावका येषु तानि).

6.73.59–60 (Crimson Kiṃśuka Blossoms)

Tilaka and Śiromaṇi supply आसन् or बभूवुः in verse 59, comparing the blood-streaked Vānara commanders to flowering red kiṃśuka trees, whereas Bhūṣaṇa construes verses 59–60 as a single sentence (श्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयम्).

6.73.61 (Blinded Warriors Clinging Together)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) construes शरैः with नेत्रेषु ताडिताः (केचिन्नेत्रेषु ताडिताः शरैरित्यन्वयः): as the monkeys peered up into the sky to locate their unseen attacker, arrows pierced their eyes. On विविशुरन्योन्यं, Tilaka explains that the blinded Vānaras clasped one another’s bodies (अन्योन्यगात्रं समाश्लिष्य तस्थुः), while Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) glosses it as toppling upon one another for support (अन्योन्योपर्यालम्बनार्थं पेतुरित्यर्थः) before collapsing on the ground.

6.73.62–65 (The Four-Verse Catalogue of Twenty-One Chiefs)

Both Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) and Bhūṣaṇa note that verses 62–65 form a single four-verse syntactic unit (श्लोकचतुष्टयमेकान्वयि) governed by the finite verb विव्याध in verse 65c. The accusative list enumerates twenty-one tigers among monkeys (हरिशार्दूलान्) headed by Hanūmān, Sugrīva, Aṅgada, Gandhamādana, Jāmbavān, and Suṣeṇa.

6.73.65 (Mantra-Empowered Missiles)

Śiromaṇi glosses मन्त्रसंहितैः as consecrated with incantations (अभिमन्त्रितैः), qualifying the lances (प्रासैः), pikes (शूलैः), and keen arrows with which Indrajit pierced the entire Vānara leadership.

6.73.66–67 (Rāma’s Serene Disregard of the Volley)

Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) notes that गदाभिः in verse 66 stands by synecdoche (upalakṣaṇa) for all the previously mentioned lances and pikes (पूर्वोक्तप्रासादीनामुपलक्षणम्). On verse 67, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) and Bhūṣaṇa explain that Rāma treated the torrent of arrows like trifling raindrops (बाणवर्षं तृणीकृत्येत्यर्थः). Bhūṣaṇa highlights परमाद्भुतश्रीः and समीक्षमाणः: retaining his wondrous composure, Rāma glanced significantly at Lakṣmaṇa to convey that countering the Brahmāstra by force would be improper (ब्रह्मास्त्रप्रतिक्रियां कर्तुमयुक्तमिङ्गितैरभिवीक्षमाणः).

6.73.68–69 (The Unseen Foe and Martial Dharma)

All three commentators gloss नष्टदेहः as invisible-bodied (अदृष्टदेहः) because his form is hidden (अन्तर्हितभीमकायः). Śiromaṇi observes that Rāma considers striking a covert trickster—like striking a fleeing foe—contrary to kṣatriya rectitude, and endures the blow solely to uphold the truth of Brahmā’s boon (स्वधर्मसत्यपालनायैवेति ध्वनितम्).

6.73.70 (Reverence for Self-Born Brahmā)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) explains why Rāma refrains from slaying the invisible Indrajit with a sound-guided missile (शब्दवेध्यादिदिव्यास्त्रैः): just as Hanūmān honored Brahmā’s noose earlier, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa must revere the Self-born Creator (योऽस्य सकलसंसारस्य प्रभवः स्रष्टा) by enduring the volley through meditation on the Lord (भगवद्ध्यानबलेन सहस्व).

6.73.71–72 (Rāma’s Strategic Submission to the Boon)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) gloss पतिताग्र्यशूरम् as the Vānara host whose finest warriors lie felled (पतिता अग्र्यशूराः प्रशस्तवीरा यस्मिन् तत्). Explaining why passive endurance is the surest way to terminate the Brahmāstra assault (एतत्सहनस्यैवैतदुपरमोपायत्वम्), Tilaka notes that when Indrajit beholds the two brothers fallen motionless (पतितौ विसंज्ञौ) with every outward sign of ardor or anger suppressed (अप्रकाशितहर्षरोषविकारौ; Govindarāja’s Bhūṣaṇa on गतरोषहर्षौ: मृताविति यावत्, “as good as dead”), he will believe he has achieved victory and will withdraw into Laṅkā (अमरारिवासं लङ्कां).

6.73.72d (Surface Glory Versus Root Victory)

Reading रणाग्रलक्ष्मीम्, Bhūṣaṇa offers a penetrating insight into the word अग्र (“tip, surface”): Indrajit can seize only superficial battle-glory, whereas the root fortune of war (रणमूललक्ष्मीर्न ग्रहीतुं शक्येति सूच्यते) remains beyond his grasp because he cannot destroy the root (मूलघातः कर्तुं न शक्यत इत्युच्यते).

6.73.73–74 (Indrajit’s Triumphal Return)

Tilaka glosses विशस्तौ as afflicted (पीडितौ) and Śiromaṇi as seemingly slain (हिंसितसदृशौ बभूवतुः), whereupon Indrajit roared in triumph and returned to Laṅkā.

अनुवादः

Thus causing the army of monkeys along with Rama and Lakshmana to become despondent in battle, that Indrajit, getting eulogized by the demons, quickly reached the city of Lanka which was being protected by Ravana’s arms. Then, Indrajit cheerfully informed all that had happened, to his father.
