Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy - Intro

Book VI : Yuddha Kanda - Book Of War

Seetha was shocked, fell unconscious at the sight of Rama’s head and bow. She regained consciousness and asked Ravana to kill her also, so that she would join her husband in heaven. Meanwhile, Ravana is abruptly called away by an emissary and after consultation with his ministers, prepares his army for action against the forces of Rama.

भागसूचना
  1. श्रीरामके मारे जानेका विश्वास करके सीताका विलाप तथा रावणका सभामें जाकर मन्त्रियोंके सलाहसे युद्धविषयक उद्योग करना
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Recognizing Rāma’s eyes, hair, crest-jewel, and bow, and recalling Hanūmān’s report of his alliance with Sugrīva, Sītā reproaches Kaikeyī in anguish and collapses like a felled plantain tree.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

सा सीता तच्छिरो दृष्ट्वा तच्च कार्मुकमुत्तमम् ।
सुग्रीवप्रतिसंसर्गमाख्यातं च हनूमता ॥ १ ॥
नयने मुखवर्णं च भर्तुस्तत्सदृशं मुखम् ।
केशान् केशान्तदेशं च तं च चूडामणिं शुभम् ॥ २ ॥
एतैः सर्वैरभिज्ञानैरभिज्ञाय सुदुःखिता ।
विजगर्हेऽत्र कैकेयीं क्रोशन्ती कुररी यथा ॥ ३ ॥

मूलम् - VR

सा सीता तच्चिरो दृष्ट्वा तच् च कार्मुकम् उत्तमम् । । सुग्रीव प्रतिसंसर्गम् आख्यातम् च हनूमता ॥ ६-३२-१ नयने मुख वर्णम् च भर्तुस् तत् सदृशम् मुखम् । केशान् केश अन्त देशम् च तम् च चूडा मणिम् शुभम् ॥ ६-३२-२ एतैह् सर्वैर् अभिज्नानैर् अभिज्नाय सुदुह्खिता । विजगर्हे अथ कैकेयीम् क्रोशन्ती कुररी यथा ॥ ६-३२-३

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

सीताजीने उस मस्तक और उस उत्तम धनुषको देखकर तथा हनुमान् जी की कही हुई सुग्रीवके साथ मैत्री-सम्बन्ध होनेकी बात याद करके अपने पतिके-जैसे ही नेत्र, मुखका वर्ण, मुखाकृति, केश, ललाट और उस सुन्दर चूडामणिको लक्ष्य किया । इन सब चिह्नोंसे पतिको पहचानकर वे बहुत दुखी हुईं और कुररीकी भाँति रो-रोकर कैकेयीकी निन्दा करने लगीं— ॥ १-३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आ श्रीरामन मस्तकवन्नु, धनुष्यवन्नु नोडि, हनुमन्तनु हेळिद सुग्रीवनॊन्दिगॆ आद मित्रतॆय मातन्नु नॆनॆदु, तन्न पतियन्तॆये नेत्र, मुखवर्ण, मुखाकृति, कूदलु, हणॆ हागू आ सुन्दर चूडामणियन्नु गमनिसिदळु. इवॆल्ल चिह्नॆगळिन्द पतियन्नु गुरुतिसि अवळु बहळ दुःखितळागि कुकुर पक्षियन्तॆ अळुत्ता कैकेयियन्नु निन्दिसतॊडगिदळु.॥1-3॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

1; 2; 3. dR^ishhTva = seeing; tat = that; shiraH = head; tat = that; uttamam = excellent; kaarmukamcha = bow; sugriivapratisamsargam = (hearing about Rama’s) friendly union with Sugreeva; aakhyaatam = as appraised; hanuumataa = by Hanuman; nayane = the eyes; mukhavarNamcha = facial complexion; sadR^isham = resembling; bhartuH = (those of) her husband; tat = that; mukham = face; kesham = the hair; keshaanta deshamcha = the expanse of his fore-head; tam = that; shubham = beautiful; chuuDaa maNimcha = jewel worn on the top of his head; abhijN^aaya = and recognizing; sarvaiH = all; etaiH = these; abhijN^aanaiH = marks of proof; saa siitaa = that Sita; suduHkhitaa = was very much afflicted with sorrow ; kroshantii = crying; kurarii yathaa = like an osprey; vijagarhecha = and abused; kaikeyiim = Kaikeyi(as follows):

अनुवादः

Seetha saw the illusory head and bow. She heard Ravana narrating about Rama’s friendly relationship with Sugreeva, as earlier apprised by Hanuman. Recognizing that head as that of Rama, with a proof resembling her husband’s eyes, facial complexion, hair, expanse of his forehead and the beautiful jewel worn on the top of his head, she was very much afflicted with sorrow, cried like an osprey and abused Kaikeyi who was the originator of the present calamity (as follows):

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1: Beholding that head and that excellent bow (kārmukam uttamam), and recalling the alliance with Sugrīva (sugrīva-pratisaṃsargam) that had been recounted by Hanūmān,

2: as well as those eyes, the complexion of the face (mukha-varṇam), the visage so like her husband's (bhartus tat-sadṛśam), the hair, the hairline along the brow (keśānta-deśam), and that auspicious crest-jewel (cūḍā-maṇim)—

3: recognizing [him] by all these tokens of identity (abhijñānaiḥ), Sītā, stricken with sore grief (su-duḥkhitā) and crying out like a female osprey (kurarī yathā), reproached (vijagarhe) Kaikeyī right there:

सकामा भव कैकेयि हतोऽयं कुलनन्दनः ।
कुलमुत्सादितं सर्वं त्वया कलहशीलया ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘कैकेयि! अब तुम सफलमनोरथ हो जाओ, रघुकुलको आनन्दित करनेवाले ये मेरे पतिदेव मारे गये । तुम स्वभावसे ही कलहकारिणी हो । तुमने समस्त रघुकुलका संहार कर डाला ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

कैकेयि! ईग नीनु सफल मनोरथळागु. नीनु समस्त रघुकुलवन्नु संहरिसिवॆ.॥4॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

4. kaikeyii = O;Kaikeyi ;bhava = = be; sakaamaa = an accomplisher of your desires; ayam = this Rama; kula nandanaH = causing joy to the family;hato = was killed; sarvam = the entire;kulam = race; utsaaditam = has been destroyed; tvayaa = by you; kalaha shiilayaa = of quarrelsome nature.

अनुवादः

Kaikeyi! Be a fulfiller of your craving! This Rama, causing a joy to the family, has been slain. The entire race has been destroyed by a woman of squabbling nature.

“Be fulfilled in your desire (sa-kāmā bhava), O Kaikeyī! Slain is this delighter of the dynasty (kula-nandanaḥ); the entire royal line has been uprooted (utsāditam) by you, who delight in strife (kalaha-śīlayā)!

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘आर्य श्रीरामने कैकेयीका कौन-सा अपराध किया था, जिससे उसने इन्हें चीरवस्त्र देकर मेरे साथ वनमें भेज दिया था’ ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

कैकॆयु श्रीरामनिगॆ नारुमडियन्नु उडिसि नन्नॊन्दिगॆ काडिगॆ कळिसलु, आर्यनाद श्रीरामनु आकॆय याव अपराध माडिद्दनु.॥5॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

5. kim = what; vipriyam = harm; kR^itam nu = was done; kaikeyyaaH = to Kaikeyi; aaryeNa = by the venerable; raameNa = Rama?; * yat* = why; prasthaapito = was he sent on exile; vanam = to the forest; mayaa = along with me; dattvaa = by giving; ciira vasanam = clothes made of bark?

अनुवादः

“You gave clothes made of bark to the venerable Rama and sent him on exile to the forest along with me. What harm has he done to you?”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

What offense (vipriyam), pray, was ever done to Kaikeyī by the noble (āryeṇa) Rāma, that he was banished (pravrājitaḥ) to the forest together with me after being given garments of bark (cīra-vasanam)?”

Thus speaking, Seetha the miserable girl was trembling and fell on the ground like a plantain tree that was cut off. Thus speaking, Seetha the miserable girl was trembling and fell on the ground like a plantain tree that was cut off.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Reviving from her swoon and drawing near the phantom head, Sītā laments her widowhood, Kausalyā’s bereavement like a cow robbed of its calf, and the falsified prophecies of Rāma’s longevity.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Regaining her breath (samāśvasya) after a moment and recovering consciousness (cetanām), that large-eyed lady (āyatekṣaṇā) drew near (samupāsthāya) that head and lamented aloud:

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

8. mahaabaaho = O; the long armed! Anuvrata = the follower; viira vratam = of a strong will; haa = alas! hataa asmi = I have been killed; * gataa asmi* = I have become a witness; te = to your; imaam = this; pashcima avasthaam = last fate; kR^itaa = I have been made; vidhavaa = a widow.

अनुवादः

“O, the long armed Rama! The pursuer of a strong will! Alas, you have been killed! I have become a witness to your last fate. I have been made a widow.”

“Alas, I am undone (hatāsmi), O mighty-armed one, faithful to the warrior's vow (vīra-vratam anuvrata)! I have come to see this final state (paścimāvasthām) of yours, made a widow (vidhavā kṛtā)!

रावणानुगमिष्यामि गतिं भर्तुर्महात्मनः ॥ ३२ ॥

मूलम् - VR

शिरसा मे शिरसः च अस्य कायम् कायेन योजय । रावण अनुगमिष्यामि गतिम् भर्तुर् महात्मनः ॥ ६-३२-३२

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘रावण! मेरे सिरसे पतिके सिरका और मेरे शरीरसे उनके शरीरका संयोग करा दो । इस प्रकार मैं अपने महात्मा पतिकी गतिका ही अनुसरण करूँगी’ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रावणने! नन्न तलॆयन्नु पतिय तलॆयॊन्दिगॆ, नन्न शरीरवन्नु पतिय शरीरदॊन्दिगॆ संयोग माडिसु. हीगॆ नानु नन्न महात्मा पतिय गतियन्नु अनुसरिसुवॆनु.॥32॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

32. raavaNa = O; Ravana! yojaya = join; me shirasaa = my head; asya = with this Rama’s; shiraH = head; kaayam = body; kaayena = with the body; anugamishhyaami = I shall go along ; gatim = the path; mahaatmanaH = of my high soled; bhartuH = Lord.

अनुवादः

“O, Ravana! Join my head with his head and my body with his body. I shall go along the path of my magnanimous Lord.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Join (yojaya) my head with his head, and my body with his body (kāyaṃ kāyena); O Rāvaṇa, I shall follow (anugamiṣyāmi) the path (gatim) of my great-souled husband!”

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

6.32.1–3 (Tokens of Recognition and the Osprey’s Cry)

Govindarāja and Śivasahāya construe the first three verses as a single syntactic sentence (श्लोकत्रयमेकं वाक्यम्). Seeing the phantom head, the bow, the hairline along the forehead (केशान्तं ललाटम्), and the crest-jewel, and recalling the alliance between Rāma and Sugrīva previously reported by Hanūmān (सुग्रीवप्रतिसंसर्गं रामसुग्रीवयोः संबन्धं)—which lends credence to Rāvaṇa’s tale of the monkey army’s slaughter—Sītā identifies the tokens (एतैः सर्वैरभिज्ञानैः चिह्नैः अभिज्ञाय रामलोकान्तरगमनं ज्ञात्वा) and wails like an osprey (कुररीव क्रोशन्ती विलपन्ती). Śivasahāya curiously takes `Kaikeyī’ to include her maid Mantharā (कैकेयीं कैकेयीदासीं मन्थरां विजगर्हे).

6.32.4–5 (The Bitter Apostrophe to Kaikeyī)

Addressing the absent queen in bitter irony, Sītā cries Be satisfied!' (सकामा भव सम्पूर्णकामा भवेत्यर्थः). **Govindarāja** explains the biting implication: though Kaikeyī plotted Rāma's exile to secure the throne for Bharata, the death of Rāma, the very life-thread of the race, destroys Bharata and the whole dynasty as well (कुलतन्तुभूतरामहननेन तदेकपराः सर्वे भरतादयो हता एव), leaving strife itself as her only fruit (कलहशीलया कलहस्वभावया कलहप्रयोजनयेति यावत्). In 6.32.5, **Govindarāja** glosses _āryeṇa_ as dear to all’ (आर्येण सर्वप्रियकारिणेत्यर्थः), while Rāma Varmā supplies saha with mayā (चीरवसनं दत्त्वा मया सह प्रव्राजित इत्यन्वयः).

6.32.6 (Sītā’s Collapse)

Overcome by trembling, the pitiable princess crashes to the ground like a cut plantain stalk. Govindarāja glosses tapasvinī as worthy of pity' (तपस्विनी शोचनीया), explains went to the earth’ (जगतीं भूमिं जगाम) as fell unconscious in a swoon', and interprets _bālā_ (young girl’) as `simple-minded, unschooled in detecting demonic illusions’ (बाला मुग्धबुद्धिरित्यर्थः).

6.32.7–8 (Recovering Consciousness and the Warrior’s Vow)

On 6.32.7, Govindarāja explains that after catching her breath (समाश्वस्य उच्छ्वासं प्राप्य) and recovering awareness (चेतनां ज्ञानम्), Sītā opened wide her tear-filled eyes (आयतेक्षणा अश्रुभिः प्लुतत्वेन शिरोवलोकनार्थं विस्तृतलोचनेत्यर्थः). Rāma Varmā glosses samupāsthāya as placing the head beside her (समुपास्थाय स्वसमीपे स्थापयित्वा) and records the variant समुपाघ्राय (kissing/smelling the head'). In 8, **Govindarāja** defines Rāma's warrior-vow as never returning without slaying the foe (वीरव्रतं शत्रुमविनाश्य न निवर्तिष्ये इति संकल्पम्), and invokes the maxim गत्यर्था ज्ञानार्था इति न्यायात् to gloss _gatāsmi_ as I have witnessed’ (गतास्मि दृष्टवत्यस्मि).

6.32.9–10 (The Paradox of the Chaste Wife’s Widowhood)

Commenting on 6.32.9, Govindarāja and Śivasahāya note the popular maxim that a husband’s premature death stems from a fault in the wife (भर्तुर्मरणं भार्यादोषनिमित्तकं भवति); yet Sītā protests that since she has lived with spotless chastity (साधुवृत्तायाः सम्यग्जीवनवत्याः ममाग्रतस्त्वं गतोऽसि), his passing cannot be due to any unfaithfulness of hers (मद्वैगुण्यहेतुकं न भवतीति ज्ञापितम्). Rāma Varmā alternatively takes vaiguṇyam simply as `the supreme calamity’ (महान् अनर्थः उच्यते), avoiding the implication that all widows are unchaste. Reading दुःखाद्दुःखम् in 10a, Govindarāja traces her sequence of sorrows from forest exile to abduction (वनप्रस्थानं प्रथमं दुःखं तस्मादपि दुःखं रावणेनापहरणं) and now her rescuer’s death.

6.32.11–12 (Kausalyā’s Bereavement and Falsified Prophecies)

In 6.32.11, Sītā grieves for her mother-in-law: like a fond mother-cow robbed of its calf, Kausalyā is left childless (विवत्सा नष्टवत्सा) by her slain son (सा मम श्वश्रूः कौसल्या वत्सला धेनुर्यथा धेनुरिव त्वया हतेन पुत्रेण वत्सेनेव विवत्सा कृता). In 12, Rāma Varmā identifies the daivajñāḥ as Vasiṣṭha and the royal seers (दैवज्ञैः वसिष्ठादिभिरिति शेषः), while Śivasahāya glosses them as astrologers (दैवज्ञैर्ज्योतिर्विद्भिस्ते आयुर्दीर्घमुद्दिष्टं) whose prediction of Rāma’s long life now seems proven false (यतोऽल्पायुरसि अतस्तेषां वचनं मिथ्या).

6.32.13–14 (The Inscrutable Power of Time over Policy)

Retracting her doubt about the seers’ veracity, Sītā reflects on the power of Fate: Rāma Varmā, Śivasahāya, and Govindarāja explain that Time (kālaḥ), the primal cause of all beings (कालो भूतानां प्रभवः प्रभवाय हेतुस्तस्मादयमेनं प्राणिनं तथा पचति), ripens every creature toward its destined karmic fruit (कालः परमात्मा यथा येन प्रकारेण एनं पचनविषयभूतम् पचति) by first eclipsing even a wise man’s foresight (अतः प्राज्ञोऽपि त्वमप्राज्ञो जात इति भावः). Otherwise, asks Govindarāja on 6.32.14, how could Rāma, so skilled in warding off disasters, fall victim to an unforeseen night-attack (हि यस्माद्व्यसनानां वर्जने प्रतिरोधे कुशलोऽसि अतः अदृष्टम् अचिन्तितं सौप्तिकत्वेनादृष्टं वा मृत्युम् कस्मादापन्नः)?

6.32.15–16 (Sītā as Kālarātrī and the Earth as Rival Bride)

On 6.32.15, Govindarāja explains both readings: with ममाच्छिद्य, the ruthless Night of Doom (kālarātryā) tore Rāma away from Sītā; with मया कालरात्र्येत्यन्वयः (favored by Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya), Sītā identifies herself in a metaphor (मया कालरात्र्येति व्यस्तरूपकम्) as the cruel Kālarātrī whose embrace brought about his destruction (रौद्रया अतिनृशंसया अत्यन्तनिर्दयया कालरात्र्या रूपया मया करणभूतया संपरिष्वज्य आच्छिद्य बलात्कृत्य तथा हत इति). In 16, lying on the ground, he embraces the Earth like a rival bride (मां विहाय प्रियां नारीमिव पृथिवीमालिङ्ग्य शेषे); Govindarāja notes that the vocative puruṣarṣabha hints that such faithlessness is unbecoming of a courteous hero (पुरुषर्षभेत्यनेन दक्षिणस्य शठत्वमनुचितमिति सूच्यते).

6.32.17–18 (The Worshiped Bow and Reunion with Daśaratha)

Gazing at the counterfeit bow, Sītā recalls how she and Rāma used to honor it with sandal-paste and garlands (मया सह तव त्वयार्चितं मत्प्रियं ते धनुरिदमेतादृशं जातम्), only for it to lie cast at her feet by an enemy. In 6.32.18, Rāma Varmā, Śivasahāya, and Govindarāja construe Rāma as now united in heaven with his father Daśaratha and all his royal ancestors (सर्वैः पितृभिः सार्धं स्थितेन दशरथेन श्वशुरेण स्वर्गे समागत इत्यन्वयः).

6.32.19–20 (The Star-Crowned Dynasty and the Silent Husband)

On 6.32.19, Rāma Varmā explains that the Ikṣvāku race is `constellation-become in heaven’ (दिवि नक्षत्रभूतम्) because royal ancestors like Triśaṅku and the Sun shine among the stars (त्रिशङ्कुनक्षत्रत्वाच्छतजित्सूर्यत्वेन वंशस्य तथात्वम्), and that Rāma now abandons that line after fulfilling his great duty to his father (महता पितृवचःपालनरूपेण कर्मणा कृतम्). Reading नक्षत्रभूतस्त्वं, Govindarāja takes Rāma himself as now dwelling in a celestial car and gazing down upon his lineage (दिवि नक्षत्रभूतः विमानस्थः त्वम् समुपेक्षसे पश्यसि). In 20, Govindarāja (reading बाल्येन) notes that she was wedded by him in early youth (बालां बाल्येन संप्राप्ताम् बाल्य एव त्वयोढामित्यर्थः) and begs him to speak (सहचारिणीं मां किं न प्रेक्षसे किं न प्रतिभाषसे).

6.32.21–22 (The Pāṇigrahaṇa Vow and the Departure to Yonder World)

In 6.32.21, Rāma Varmā, Śivasahāya, and Govindarāja explain the allusion to the Vedic wedding rite of clasping the bride’s hand (pāṇigrahaṇa), where the groom vows to practice dharma together with his bride (पाणिं गृह्णता त्वया त्वया सह चरिष्यामि धर्मादिकम् इति यत्संश्रुतं प्रतिज्ञातं तत्स्मर); remembering that pledge, he should take her with him in death. In 22, Śivasahāya notes the morphology of apahāya (अपहायेति अपपूर्वकगत्यर्थकहाधातुप्रकृतिकल्यबन्तम्), asking why he has departed from this world to the next (अस्माल्लोकात् अमुं लोकं परलोकम् कस्माद्धेतोः गतोऽसि) leaving her behind.

6.32.23–24 (The Desecrated Body Denied Its Agnihotra Rites)

In 6.32.23, Sītā shudders to think that Rāma’s body, once adorned with auspicious unguents and embraced by her alone (कल्याणैरनेकमाङ्गलिकोपचारैः रुचिरं यत्तव गात्रं मया परिष्वक्तं तत्ते शरीरं क्रव्यादैर्विपरिकृष्यते), is now being torn by carrion-beasts; Govindarāja remarks that `embraced by me alone’ (मयेव परिष्वक्तं) proves Rāma’s strict monogamy (रामस्य दारान्तरं नास्तीति गम्यते). Worse still, though he performed great soma-sacrifices headed by the Agniṣṭoma with generous priestly fees, his headless trunk on a foreign battlefield is denied cremation with his own sacred Agnihotra fire.

6.32.25–27 (Lakṣmaṇa’s Solitary Return and Kausalyā’s Broken Heart)

In 6.32.25, Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya read परिप्रेक्ष्यति (will see', द्रक्ष्यतीत्यर्थः), whereas **Govindarāja** reads परिप्रक्ष्यति (will question’) and cites Pāṇini’s व्रजयजोर्भावे क्यप् for the suffix in pravrajyām: of the three who went into exile (प्रव्रज्यां प्रवासमुपपन्नानां त्रयाणामस्माकं मध्ये एकं लक्ष्मणं), Kausalyā, drowned in grief (शोकलालसा शोकमग्ना), will confront Lakṣmaṇa returning alone. Hearing from him of the monkey army’s rout (मित्रबलस्य वानरबलस्य) and Rāma’s death in sleep (सुप्तं हतं त्वद्दशायां हतमित्यर्थः), Kausalyā will die of a cleft heart (अवदीर्णेन विदीर्णेनोपलक्षिता सती नूनं न भविष्यति म्रियेतेत्यर्थः).

6.32.28–29 (Drowning in a Hoofprint after Crossing the Sea)

On 6.32.28, all three commentators highlight the proverbial irony (laukika-nyāya) of `crossing the ocean only to perish in a puddle’ (सागरमुत्तीर्य गोष्पदे हतस्तद्वन्नाशकस्येति या लोकवार्ता तद्वदिदं जातमित्यर्थः): Govindarāja notes that after vanquishing mighty foes like Khara and bridging the sea, Rāma was allegedly slain in sleep by the petty Prahasta (खरतरखरादीन् हत्वा क्षुद्रेण प्रहस्तेन हत इत्यर्थः). Calling herself a stain upon her family (स्वकुलपांसनी स्वकुलदूषणी), Sītā laments that Rāma wedded her in ignorance (मोहाद् अज्ञानात्), or that her own infatuation with the golden deer (कपटमृगादिविषयकमोहं प्राप्य) made his bride his death.

6.32.30 (Karmic Obstruction of a Gift in a Past Life)

Searching for the karmic root of her widowhood, Sītā concludes that in a former birth she must have prevented an excellent gift—specifically, notes Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja, the gift of a bride in marriage (अन्यां जातिमन्यस्मिन्जन्मन्युत्तमं दानं कन्यादानं गोभूहिरण्यादिदानं च वारितं न कृतम्)—whereby even as the wife of Rāma, who honored every guest and sheltered all beings (सर्वे अतिथयो यस्य तस्य सर्वातिथेः सर्वरक्षितुरित्यर्थः), she is condemned to weep in the prime of life.

6.32.31–33 (Sītā’s Plea for Death Beside Rāma’s Remains)

Instead of yielding to Rāvaṇa’s demand, Sītā begs him to slay her directly over Rāma’s remains (रामस्योपरि संस्थाप्येत्यर्थः). Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja gloss samānaya as unite' (समानय योजय), explaining that for a devoted wife the highest good (_kalyāṇam uttamam_) is union with her husband in death (उत्तमं कल्याणं पतिसंयोगरूपम्). In 6.32.32, **Śivasahāya** paraphrases her plea: join my severed head with Rāma’s head and my torso with his headless trunk (मे शिरः अस्य रामस्य शिरसा कायं शेषशरीरं कायेन च योजय), so that I may follow my husband’s path (भर्तुर्गतिम् अनुगमिष्यामि)'.

6.32.34 (Why Sītā’s Life Did Not Depart)

On 6.32.34, Govindarāja and Rāma Varmā gloss lālapyamānāyām as `while she was lamenting bitterly’ (लालप्यमानायां प्रलपन्त्याम्) and identify anīkasthaḥ as a palace gate-warder (अनीकस्थो द्वाररक्षी) sent by Prahasta, while Śivasahāya takes him as an intelligence officer (अनीकस्थः कश्चन चारविशेषः). Govindarāja addresses a classic literary question: why did Sītā not die instantly of grief upon seeing Rāma’s severed head? Because for an ideal couple (divyadampatī), the actual survival of the spouse—not mere belief or delusion—is the ontological cause that sustains life (भर्तृजीवितवैकल्यभावात्सा जीवति स्म).

6.32.35 (The Guard’s Salutation and Announcement of Prahasta)

The warder bows low, propitiates Rāvaṇa for intruding into the pleasure-grove, and announces that the commander-in-chief Prahasta is waiting at the gate (अनुप्राप्तं द्वारीति शेषः). Rāma Varmā notes a point of dramatic lexicon regarding the greeting āryaputra: although in dramatic convention āryaputra is most commonly used by wives addressing their husbands, it is also properly employed by servants addressing a venerable royal master (आर्यपुत्रशब्दो यद्यपि स्त्रीभिर्भर्तरि प्रयुज्यते प्रायेण तथापि पूज्यमात्रे सामान्यतः प्रयोगोऽपि बोध्यः).

6.32.36–37 (The Urgent Summons from Prahasta)

Reporting Prahasta’s arrival with the council of ministers, the messenger excuses his intrusion at an unseasonable hour. Reading the plural वयं प्रस्थापिताः in 36b, Govindarāja remarks that the solitary guard uses the plural out of trepidation, since a crowd of messengers could not enter the inner grove (वयमिति भयेन बहुवचनं बहूनामन्तःपुरे समागमासंभवात्). On 6.32.37, Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya read the vocative क्षमान्वित (O forbearing king, pardon my intrusion because of urgent state business', किंचिदात्ययिकमावश्यकं कार्यमस्ति), whereas **Govindarāja** reads क्षमान्वितम् (an urgent matter that has nonetheless waited patiently upon your royal pleasure’, मया त्वदाज्ञाप्रतीक्षणेनान्वितम्).

6.32.38–39 (Rāvaṇa’s Departure and War-Council)

Leaving the Aśoka grove, Rāvaṇa goes to grant audience to his ministers (मन्त्रिणां दर्शनम् अवलोकनार्थं ययौ). On 6.32.39, Rāma Varmā notes that Rāvaṇa first conferred privately with his inner circle (दुष्टमन्त्रिभिरेकान्ते स इत्यं विचार्येत्यर्थः) and then entered the formal assembly hall to issue orders. Govindarāja and Śivasahāya explain that having learned from his spies the true measure of Rāma’s prowess (रामविक्रमं विदित्वा सभां प्रविश्य आत्मनः सर्वं कृत्यं मन्त्रिभिः सह समर्थ्य निश्चित्य विदधे), Rāvaṇa assigned each commander his respective duty.

6.32.40 (The Vanishing of the Phantom Head and Bow)

The very instant Rāvaṇa stepped out of the grove (निर्याणं निर्गमनम्), the severed head and bow disappeared (निर्याणसमनन्तरमेवेति योजना). Rāma Varmā points out the causal reason: because they were fabricated of rākṣasa sorcery (मायामयत्वादिति भावः), the illusion dissolved as soon as the conjurer’s patron withdrew from the scene.

6.32.41–42 (Settling the War-Plan and Summoning the Marshals)

Expanding upon the summary statement in 6.32.39, Govindarāja, Rāma Varmā, and Śivasahāya explain rāmakāryaviniścayam as the definitive resolution of Rāvaṇa’s military measures against Rāma (रामकार्यविनिश्चयं रामविषयं स्वकर्तव्यनिश्चयम्). Having settled the plan with his counselors, Rāvaṇa turns to his loyal troop-commanders (बलाध्यक्षान् रावणोऽब्रवीत्) standing nearby.

6.32.43 (The Drum-Summons and the Secrecy Order)

Rāvaṇa orders the armies mustered by the loud booming of kettle-drums beaten with heavy drumsticks (स्फुटकोणाहतेन उल्बणवादनदण्डाहतिजनितेन), adding the curious proviso: and the reason for the muster must not be announced' (कारणं न वक्तव्यमिति भावः). **Rāma Varmā** explains that heralds should simply proclaim The king summons you!’ without mentioning Rāma’s siege. Govindarāja offers a subtler psychological motive: if heralds proclaimed throughout the city that the army was mustering to fight Rāma, Sītā would overhear the proclamation and realize that Rāvaṇa’s tale of Rāma’s midnight slaughter was a lie (तदानीं देव्याः सन्निधौ कथितं स्वकीयं रामसैन्यवधवृत्तान्तवाक्यमसत्यमिति तस्याः विदितं स्यादिति भावः).

6.32.44 (Muster of the Host and Theological Note)

The officers swiftly assemble the host and report to their battle-eager sovereign (समागतं बलं भर्तरि न्यवेदयँश्च, where Govindarāja glosses the locative bhartari as भर्तरि विषये). Concluding the sarga, Rāma Varmā adds an overarching theological reflection: Sītā’s grief-stricken lamentation over the illusory head serves to manifest her assumed human nature (अत्र सीताप्रलापः स्वस्य मानुषधर्मप्रकटनायेति बोध्यम्) within the divine play.

Meanwhile, immediately upon Rāvaṇa's departure (rāvaṇasyaiva niryāṇa-samanantaram), that head and that excellent bow vanished out of sight (antardhānaṃ jagāma).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Determining his strategy against Rāma, Rāvaṇa commands his generals to muster the armies by beat of drum without disclosing the reason, and the troops swiftly assemble.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

राक्षसेन्द्रस्तु तैः सार्धं मन्त्रिभिर्भीमविक्रमैः ।
समर्थयामास तदा रामकार्यविनिश्चयम् ॥ ४१ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

राक्षसेन्द्रस्तु तैः सार्धं मन्त्रिभिर्भीमविक्रमैः ।
समर्थयामास तदा रामकार्यविनिश्चयम् ॥ ४१ ॥

मूलम् - VR

राक्षस इन्द्रस् तु तैः सार्धम् मन्त्रिभिर् भीम विक्रमैः । समर्थयाम् आस तदा राम कार्य विनिश्चयम् ॥ ६-३२-४१

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

राक्षसराज रावणने अपने उन भयानक मन्त्रियोंके साथ बैठकर रामके प्रति किये जानेवाले तत्कालोचित कर्तव्यका निश्चय किया ॥ ४१ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

राक्षसराज रावणनु तन्न आ भयानक मन्त्रिगळॊन्दिगॆ कुळितु रामन कुरितु माडबहुदाद तत्कालोचित कर्तव्यवन्नु निश्चयिसिदनु.॥41॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

41. tadaa = then; raakshhasendrastu = Ravana; the king of demons; saartham bhiima vikramaiH = along with his highly powerful;mantribhiH = ministers; samartha yaamaasa = decided; raamakaaryavinishchayam = on the measures he would adopt against Rama.

अनुवादः

Then, Ravana the king of demons in consultation with his highly powerful ministers, decided on the measures he would adopt against Rama.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then the lord of the rākṣasas, together with those ministers of terrible valor (bhīma-vikramaiḥ), determined (samarthayāmāsa) the settled course of action regarding Rāma (rāma-kārya-viniścayam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अविदूरस्थितान् सर्वान् बलाध्यक्षान् हितैषिणः ।
अब्रवीत् कालसदृशं रावणो राक्षसाधिपः ॥ ४२ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अविदूरस्थितान् सर्वान् बलाध्यक्षान् हितैषिणः ।
अब्रवीत् कालसदृशं रावणो राक्षसाधिपः ॥ ४२ ॥

मूलम् - VR

अविदूर स्थितान् सर्वान् बल अध्यक्षान् हित एषिणः । अब्रवीत् काल सद्ऱ्शो रावणो राक्षस अधिपः ॥ ६-३२-४२

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

फिर राक्षसराज रावणने पास ही खड़े हुए अपने हितैषी सेनापतियोंसे इस प्रकार समयानुकूल बात कही— ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मत्तॆ रावणनु बळियल्ले निन्तिद्द तन्न हितैषि सेनापतिगळल्लि ई प्रकार समयानुकूल मातन्नु हेळिदनु.॥42॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

42. raavaNaH = Ravana; raakshhasaadhipaH = the king of demons; kaalasadR^ishaH = resembling Yama; the god of death; abraviit = addressed; sarvaan = all; balaadhyakshhaan = the generals; hitaishhiNaH = devoted to his interests; aviduurasthitaan = standing nearby.

अनुवादः

Ravana the king of demons, resembling Yama the god of death, addressed all the generals standing nearby, devoted to his interests as follows:

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Like Time himself (kāla-sadṛśam), Rāvaṇa, sovereign of the rākṣasas, spoke to all the army commanders (balādhyakṣān) who stood not far off, wishing his welfare (hitaiṣiṇaḥ):

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

शीघ्रं भेरीनिनादेन स्फुटं कोणाहतेन मे ।
समानयध्वं सैन्यानि वक्तव्यं च न कारणम् ॥ ४३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

शीघ्रं भेरीनिनादेन स्फुटं कोणाहतेन मे ।
समानयध्वं सैन्यानि वक्तव्यं च न कारणम् ॥ ४३ ॥

मूलम् - VR

शीघ्रम् भेरी निनादेन स्फुट कोण आहतेन मे । समानयध्वम् सैन्यानि वक्तव्यम् च न कारणम् ॥ ६-३२-४३

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘तुम सब लोग शीघ्र ही डंडेसे पीट-पीटकर धौंसा बजाते हुए समस्त सैनिकोंको एकत्र करो; परंतु उन्हें इसका कारण नहीं बताना चाहिये’ ॥ ४३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

नीवॆल्लरू बेगने डङ्गुरसारि समस्त सैनिकरन्नु ऒट्टुगूडिसिरि; आदरॆ अवरिगॆ इदर कारण तिळिसबारदु.॥43॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

43. bheriininaadena = by the sound of a drum; sphuTam = audibly; koNa hatena = beaten with a drumstick; shiighram = immediately; samaanayadhvam = summon; me = my; sainyaani = forces; nachavaktavyam = without expecting; kaaraNam = a reason.

अनुवादः

“By a sound of a drum beaten audibly with a drum stick, immediately summon all my forces. Do not tell them the reason for which I am calling.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Quickly muster (samānayadhvam) my armies through the resounding roll of war-drums (bherī-ninādena) struck clearly with the drumstick (sphuṭaṃ koṇāhatena); and no reason is to be stated (vaktavyaṃ ca na kāraṇam)!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततस्तथेति प्रतिगृह्य तद्वच-
स्तदैव दूताः सहसा महद् बलम् ।
समानयंश्चैव समागतं च
न्यवेदयन् भर्तरि युद्धकाङ्क्षिणि ॥ ४४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततस्तथेति प्रतिगृह्य तद्वच-
स्तदैव दूताः सहसा महद् बलम् ।
समानयंश्चैव समागतं च
न्यवेदयन् भर्तरि युद्धकाङ्क्षिणि ॥ ४४ ॥

मूलम् - VR

ततस् तथा इति प्रतिगृह्य तद् वचो । स्तदैव दूताः सहसा महाद्बलम् । समानयंसः चैव समागतम् च ते । न्यवेदयन् भर्तरि युद्ध कान्क्षिणि ॥ ६-३२-४४

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

तब दूतोंने ‘तथास्तु’ कहकर रावणकी आज्ञा स्वीकार की और उसी समय सहसा विशाल सेनाको एकत्र कर दिया; फिर युद्धकी अभिलाषा रखनेवाले अपने स्वामीको यह सूचना दी कि ‘सारी सेना आ गयी’ ॥ ४४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग दूतरु ‘हागॆये आगली’ ऎन्दु हेळि रावणन अप्पणॆयन्नु मन्निसि, आगले विशाल सैन्यवन्नु ऒट्टुगूडिसिदरु. मत्तॆ युद्धाभिलाषिगळाद तन्न ऒडॆयनिगॆ ‘ऎल्ल सैन्य सिद्धवागिदॆ’ ऎन्दु सूचिसिदरु.॥44॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

44. tataH = then; duutaaH = the messengers; pratigR^ihya = answered; tathaa iti = “So be it” ; tadvachaH = obedient to his words; sahasaa = instantly; samaanayana = gathered together; mahat = a huge; balam = army; tadaiva = then itself; nyavedayan = and informed; bharatari = their lord; yuddhakaaN^kchhiNi = longing for fight; samaagatam = that they had assembled.

अनुवादः

Then, the messengers answered, “So be it” obedient as they were to his words and instantly gathered a huge army together and informed their lord longing for fight, that they had since assembled the army.

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये युद्धकाण्डे द्वात्रिंशः सर्गः ॥ ३२ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके युद्धकाण्डमें बत्तीसवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ ३२ ॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then, accepting that command with the words “So be it (tatheti),” the envoys at once (tadaiva) rapidly mustered a mighty army (mahad balam), and reported that it had assembled before their lord, who was eager for battle (bhartari yuddha-kāṅkṣiṇi).
