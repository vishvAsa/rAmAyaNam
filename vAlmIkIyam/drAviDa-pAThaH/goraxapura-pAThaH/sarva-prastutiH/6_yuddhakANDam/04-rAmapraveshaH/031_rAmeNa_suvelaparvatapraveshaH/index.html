सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Learning from his spies that Rāma has encamped upon Mount Suvela, an agitated Rāvaṇa convenes a brief council and summons the sorcerer Vidyujjihva to the grove where Sītā dwells.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततस्तमक्षोभ्यबलं लङ्कायां नृपतेश्चराः ।
सुवेले राघवं शैले निविष्टं प्रत्यवेदयन् ॥ १ ॥
चाराणां रावणः श्रुत्वा प्राप्तं रामं महाबलम् ।
जातोद्वेगोऽभवत् किञ्चित् सचिवानिदमब्रवीत् ॥ २ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततस्तमक्षोभ्यबलं लङ्कायां नृपतेश्चराः ।
सुवेले राघवं शैले निविष्टं प्रत्यवेदयन् ॥ १ ॥
चाराणां रावणः श्रुत्वा प्राप्तं रामं महाबलम् ।
जातोद्वेगोऽभवत् किञ्चित् सचिवानिदमब्रवीत् ॥ २ ॥

ततस्तमक्षोभ्यबलं लङ्कायां नृपतेश्वराः ।
सुवेले राघवं शैले निविष्टं प्रत्यवेदयन् ॥ चाराणां रावणः श्रुत्वा प्राप्तं रामं महाबलम् ।
जातोद्वेगोऽभवत् किञ्चित् सचिवानिदमब्रवीत् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

राक्षसराज रावणके गुप्तचरोंने जब लङ्कामें लौटकर यह बताया कि श्रीरामचन्द्रजीकी सेना सुवेल पर्वतपर आकर ठहरी है और उसपर विजय पाना असम्भव है, तब उन गुप्तचरोंकी बात सुनकर और महाबली श्रीराम आ गये, यह जानकर रावणको कुछ उद्वेग हुआ । उसने अपने मन्त्रियोंसे इस प्रकार कहा— ॥ १-२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रावणन गुप्तचररु लङ्कॆगॆ बन्दु - श्रीरामचन्द्रन सैन्यवु सुवेलपर्वतदल्लि बीडु बिट्टिद्दु, अदन्नु जयिसुवुदु असम्भववॆन्दु तिळिसिदाग, अवर मातन्नु केळि, श्रीरामनु बन्दिरुवनॆन्दु तिळिदु रावणनु उद्विग्ननागि मन्त्रिगळल्लि हीगॆ हेळिदनु.॥1-2॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

1. tataH = then; nR^ipateH = the king’s; charaaH = spies; pratyavedan = informed (Ravana); tam raaghavam = that Rama; akSobhya balam = with his imperturbable army; niviSTam = was stationed; suvele shaile = at Mount Suvela; laN^kaayaam = in Lanka.

अनुवादः

The spies of king Ravana informed him that Rama with his imperturbable army was encamped at Mount Suvela in Lanka.

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

2. shrutvaa = Hearing; chaaraaNaam = throught; spies; raamam = that Rama; mahaabalam = with his mighty army; praaptam = had arrived; raavaNaH = Ravana; jaatodvegaH = was agitated; kimchit = a little; abraviit = and spoke; idam = thse words; sachivaan = to his counsellors (as follows):

अनुवादः

Hearing through his spies that Rama with his mighty army had arrived to Lanka, Ravana was a little agitated and spoke to his counsellors as follows:

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1: Then the spies (carāḥ) of the lord of Laṅkā reported (pratyavedayan) to him that Rāghava, whose army is unshakable (akṣobhya-balam), had encamped (niviṣṭam) upon Mount Suvela.

2: Hearing from his spies that Rāma of mighty strength had arrived, Rāvaṇa became somewhat agitated (jātodvegaḥ) and spoke these words to his ministers (sacivān):

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

मन्त्रिणः शीघ्रमायान्तु सर्वे वै सुसमाहिताः ।
अयं नो मन्त्रकालो हि सम्प्राप्त इति राक्षसाः ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

मन्त्रिणः शीघ्रमायान्तु सर्वे वै सुसमाहिताः ।
अयं नो मन्त्रकालो हि सम्प्राप्त इति राक्षसाः ॥ ३ ॥

मूलम् - VR

मन्त्रिणः शीघ्रमायान्तु सर्वे वै सु समाहिताः । अयम् नो मन्त्रकालो हि सम्प्रास्त इति राक्षसाः ॥ ६-३१-३

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘मेरे सभी मन्त्री एकाग्रचित्त होकर शीघ्र यहाँ आ जायँ । राक्षसो! यह हमारे लिये गुप्त मन्त्रणा करनेका अवसर आ गया है’ ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

सचिवरे! नन्न मन्त्रिगळॆल्लरू आदष्टु बेग इल्लिगॆ बरलि. नावॆल्लरू सेरि गुप्तसमालोचनॆ माडुव काल ईग सन्निहितवागिदॆ.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Let all the counselors (mantriṇaḥ), well-composed (su-samāhitāḥ), come quickly! Truly, O rākṣasas, the hour for our deliberation (mantra-kālaḥ) has now arrived.”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तस्य तच्छासनं श्रुत्वा मन्त्रिणोऽभ्यागमन् द्रुतम् ।
ततः स मन्त्रयामास राक्षसैः सचिवैः सह ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तस्य तच्छासनं श्रुत्वा मन्त्रिणोऽभ्यागमन् द्रुतम् ।
ततः स मन्त्रयामास राक्षसैः सचिवैः सह ॥ ४ ॥

मूलम् - VR

तस्य तच्चासनम् श्रुत्वा मन्त्रिणोऽभ्यागमन् द्रुतम् । ततः स मन्त्रयामास राक्षसैः सचिवैः सह ॥ ६-३१-४

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

रावणका आदेश सुनकर समस्त मन्त्री शीघ्रतापूर्वक वहाँ आ गये । तब रावणने उन राक्षसजातीय सचिवोंके साथ बैठकर आवश्यक कर्तव्यपर विचार किया ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रावणन आदेशवन्नु केळि समस्त मन्त्रिगळु शीघ्रवागि अल्लिगॆ बन्दरु. आग रावणनु आ राक्षस मन्त्रिगळॊन्दगॆ कुळितु आवश्यक कर्तव्यद कुरितु विचार माडिदनु.॥4॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

4. shrutvaa = hearing; tat = that; shaasanam = command; tasya = of his; mantriNaH = the ministers; abhyaagamam = came; drutam = soon; tataH = thereafter; saH = he; mantrayaamaasa = deliberated; sachivaiH saha = with his ministers; raakshhasaiH = the ogres.

अनुवादः

As per his command, the ministers came instantly. Ravana conferred with the ogres, his ministers.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Hearing that command (śāsanam) of his, the counselors came swiftly; thereupon he held counsel (mantrayāmāsa) together with his rākṣasa ministers.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

मन्त्रयित्वा तु दुर्धर्षः क्षमं यत् तदनन्तरम् ।
विसर्जयित्वा सचिवान् प्रविवेश स्वमालयम् ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

मन्त्रयित्वा तु दुर्धर्षः क्षमं यत् तदनन्तरम् ।
विसर्जयित्वा सचिवान् प्रविवेश स्वमालयम् ॥ ५ ॥

मूलम् - VR

मन्त्रयित्वा तु दुर्धर्षः क्षमं यत्तदन्न्तरम् । विसर्जयित्वा सचिवान् प्रविवेश स्वमालयम् ॥ ६-३१-५

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

दुर्धर्ष वीर रावणने जो उचित कर्तव्य था, उसके विषयमें शीघ्र ही विचार-विमर्श करके उन सचिवोंको विदा कर दिया और अपने भवनमें प्रवेश किया ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

दुर्धर्ष वीर रावणनु उचितवाद कर्तव्यद कुरितु बेगनॆ विचार-विमर्शॆ माडि आ सचिवरन्नु बीळ्कॊट्टु तन्न अन्तःपुरवन्नु प्रवेशिसिदनु.॥5॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

5. durdharshhaH = Ravana; the dreadful one; mantrayitvaa = deliberated; tat = that; yat = which; kshhamam = was befitting to be done; anantaram = immediately after; visarjayitvaa = sent away sachivaan = the ministers; pravivesha = and entered; svam his; aalayam = palace.

अनुवादः

The dreadful Ravana discussed about the ensuing things to be done immediately. He sent away the ministers and entered his palace.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having deliberated (mantrayitvā) immediately thereafter upon what was expedient (kṣamam), that unassailable (durdharṣaḥ) monarch dismissed his ministers and entered his own palace (svam ālayam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततो राक्षसमादाय विद्युज्जिह्वं महाबलम् ।
मायाविनं महामायं प्राविशद् यत्र मैथिली ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततो राक्षसमादाय विद्युज्जिह्वं महाबलम् ।
मायाविनं महामायं प्राविशद् यत्र मैथिली ॥ ६ ॥

मूलम् - VR

ततो राक्षसमादाय विद्युज्जिह्वम् महाबलम् । मायाविदम् महामायः प्रविशद्यत्र मैथिली ॥ ६-३१-६

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

फिर उसने महाबली, महामायावी, मायाविशारद राक्षस विद्युज्जिह्वको साथ लेकर उस प्रमदावनमें प्रवेश किया, जहाँ मिथिलेशकुमारी सीता विद्यमान थीं ॥ ६ ॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

6. tataH = thereafter; mahaamayaH = Ravana; practising conjuring tricks; aadaaya = took; vidyujjihvam = Vidyujjihva; mahaabalam = exceedingly strong; maayaavidam = who knew magic spells (along with him); * praavishat* = entered; yatra = the place where; maithilii = Seetha ( was there).

अनुवादः

Thereafter, Ravana the practiser of conjuring tricks, took the exceedingly strong Vidyujjihva who was a great sourcerer along with him and went to Seetha’s place.

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मत्तॆ अवनु महाबली, महामायावी, मायाविशारद राक्षस विद्युज्जिह्वनन्नु जॊतयल्लि करॆदु कॊण्डु सीतॆयु इरुव प्रमदावनवन्नु प्रवेशिसिदनु.॥.॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then, taking with him (ādāya) the rākṣasa Vidyujjihva of mighty strength—a conjurer (māyāvinam) and master of great illusions (mahā-māyam)—he entered the place where Maithilī was held.

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Rāvaṇa instructs Vidyujjihva to fashion a phantom head and bow of Rāma, rewards his successful conjuration with an ornament, and enters the Aśoka grove where Sītā sits grieving.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

विद्युज्जिह्वं च मायाज्ञमब्रवीद् राक्षसाधिपः ।
मोहयिष्यावहे सीतां मायया जनकात्मजाम् ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

विद्युज्जिह्वं च मायाज्ञमब्रवीद् राक्षसाधिपः ।
मोहयिष्यावहे सीतां मायया जनकात्मजाम् ॥ ७ ॥

मूलम् - VR

विद्युज्जिह्वम् च मायाज्ञ्मब्रवीद्राक्षसाधिपः । मोहयिष्यावहे सीताम् मायया जनकात्मजाम् ॥ ६-३१-७

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस समय राक्षसराज रावणने माया जाननेवाले विद्युज्जिह्वसे कहा—‘हम दोनों मायाद्वारा जनकनन्दिनी सीताको मोहित करेंगे ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग राक्षसराज रावणनु मायॆयन्नु बल्ल विद्युज्जीहनल्लि हेळिदनु-नाविब्बरू मायॆय मूलक जनकनन्दिनी सीतॆयन्नु मोहित गॊळिसुवा.॥7॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

7. raakshaasaadhipaH = Ravana the lord of demons; abraviit cha = spoke; vidyujjihvam = to Vidyujjihva; maagaajN^gam = who knew deceptions (as follows): mohayishhvaavahe maayayaa = we shall create an illusion to deceive; siitaam = Seetha; janakaatmajaam = the daughter of Janaka.

अनुवादः

Ravana the lord of demons spoke to Vidyujjihva, who was a knower of conjuring tricks as follows: “With your spells, create an illusion in order to deceive Seetha, daughter of Janaka.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

The lord of the rākṣasas spoke to Vidyujjihva, knower of illusions (māyā-jñam): “Let us two delude (mohayiṣyāvahe) Sītā, the daughter of Janaka, through sorcery (māyayā).

मां त्वं समुपतिष्ठस्व महच्च सशरं धनुः ॥ ८ ॥

मूलम्

मूलम् - VR

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘निशाचर! तुम श्रीरामचन्द्रजीका मायानिर्मित मस्तक लेकर एक महान् धनुष-बाणके साथ मेरे पास आओ’ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

निशाचरने! नीनु श्रीरामन मायानिर्मित शिरस्सन्नु मत्तु महाधनुष्य बाणगळन्नु ऎत्तिकॊण्डु नन्न बळिगॆ बा.॥8॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

8. nishaachara = O; demon!; tvam = you; samupatisthasva = approached; maam = me; gR^ihya = holding; mayaamayam = a deceptive; raaghavasya = Rama’s; shiraa = head sa sharam = along with his arrows; mahat = and the big; dhanuH cha = bow.

अनुवादः

“O, demon! Produce a head resembling Rama’s along with a great bow and arrows created deceptively and present yourself before me.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Taking an illusory (māyā-mayam) head of Rāghava, O night-ranger, and his great bow together with his arrow (sa-śaraṃ dhanuḥ), attend (samupatiṣṭhasva) closely upon me.”

दर्शयामास तां मायां सुप्रयुक्तां स रावणे ॥ ९ ॥

रावणकी यह आज्ञा पाकर निशाचर विद्युज्जिह्वने कहा—‘बहुत अच्छा’ । फिर उसने रावणको बड़ी कुशलतासे प्रकट की हुई अपनी माया दिखायी ॥ ९ ॥

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

6.31.1–2 (Spies Report the Suvela Encampment)

Opening the episode of Sītā’s delusion, Govindarāja notes that the narrative transition serves structural continuity (कथासंघटनाय). Śivasahāya explains that the spies report directly to Rāvaṇa (रावणं प्रति) that Rāghava is encamped on Suvela (निविष्टं प्रत्यवेदयन्). Both Govindarāja and Śivasahāya gloss the genitive cārāṇām in an ablative sense (चाराणां चारेभ्यः, `from the spies’). Rāma Varmā defines sacivān as deputy counselors (सचिवानुपमन्त्रिणः), while Govindarāja takes them as attendants standing nearby (मन्त्रिणः समीपस्थान्).

6.31.3–4 (The Urgent Summons to Council)

Analyzing Rāvaṇa’s summons, Govindarāja offers two syntactic construals: either the vocative O rākṣasas!' governs the statement that the hour of counsel has arrived (मन्त्रकालः प्राप्तः इति हेतोः), or _rākṣasāḥ_ qualifies the ministers themselves, while _susamāhitāḥ_ means skilled in statecraft’ (नीतिकुशला इत्यर्थः). Śivasahāya simply paraphrases no as `for us’ (अयं नोऽस्माकं) and notes that upon hearing Rāvaṇa’s decree (तस्य रावणस्य तच्छासनं श्रुत्वा), the ministers hastened to deliberate.

6.31.5–6 (Enlisting Vidyujjihva’s Sorcery)

On 6.31.5, Govindarāja, reading samanantaram, explains it as immediately after Rāma's approach' (समनन्तरं रामस्य समीपागमनानन्तरम्), whereas **Śivasahāya** connects _kṣamam_ with what is feasible regarding Sītā or the war. After entering his inner apartments (वेश्मप्रवेशानन्तरं), Rāvaṇa recruits Vidyujjihva: **Rāma Varmā** distinguishes _māyāvinam_ as possessing celebrated magical powers’ (प्रशस्तमायावन्तम्) and mahāmāyam as `an active practitioner of potent illusions’ (सारमायाप्रयोगकर्तारम्).

6.31.7–8 (The Command to Forge Rāma’s Severed Head)

Commenting on Rāvaṇa’s pact with his conjurer, Śivasahāya notes the dual verb मायया सीतां मोहयिष्यावहे (you and I shall delude Sītā by magic'), where Rāvaṇa provides the verbal fabrication and Vidyujjihva supplies the visual prop. In 6.31.8, **Śivasahāya** explains the command: bringing an illusory head of Rāghava (मायामयं राघवस्य शिरः) and his great bow with its arrow, wait closely upon me (मत्समीपे समुपतिष्ठस्व)’ inside the grove.

6.31.9–10 (Testing the Illusion and Rewarding the Sorcerer)

Before entering the grove, Vidyujjihva demonstrates his craft in Rāvaṇa’s presence (रावणसन्निधौ). On 6.31.10a, Govindarāja explains the genitive tasya as locative in sense (तस्य तस्मिन्), while Śivasahāya expands it as pleased with Vidyujjihva' (तस्य विद्युज्जिह्वस्योपरि तुष्टोऽभवत्). **Rāma Varmā** remarks that Rāvaṇa bestows an ornament upon him just as Kaikeyī once rewarded the hunchback Mantharā (कुब्जायै कैकेयीदास्यै) for her welcome malice. **Govindarāja** glosses _lālasaḥ_ as filled with desire’ (लालसः साभिलाषः).

6.31.11–12 (Sītā’s Vigil in the Aśoka Grove)

Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya observe that the four half-verses from 6.31.10b to 6.31.12b form a single syntactic unit (अर्धचतुष्टयमेकान्वयि): eager to see Sītā, Rāvaṇa entered the Aśoka grove (अशोकवनिकायां संविवेश, glossed संविवेश प्रविवेश) and beheld her guarded nearby by fierce rākṣasīs (समीपे घोराभी राक्षसीभिरुपास्यमानां सीतां धनदानुजो ददर्श). Reading अदीनार्हां, Śivasahāya explains that she who never deserved misery now sat downcast (अधोमुखीं), absorbed solely in contemplating her lord.

6.31.13–15 (Feigned Exultation and the Claim of Rāma’s Death)

On प्रहर्षं नाम कीर्तयन् (13b), Govindarāja and Rāma Varmā take the particle nāma in the sense of unreality (नामेत्यपरमार्थे), meaning Rāvaṇa proclaimed a counterfeit joy over Rāma’s supposed defeat (अपरमार्थभूतं रामजयजं प्रहर्षं कीर्तयन्), whereas Śivasahāya takes nāma as Rāvaṇa’s own royal title announced joyously as he drew near (सीतामुपसृत्य सीतासमीपं प्राप्य). Reading वल्गसे, Govindarāja glosses it as you speak harshly to me' (वल्गसे जल्पसि); **Śivasahāya** reads _vimanyase_ (you spurn my suit’). Your root—your sole support (आधारभूतम्) and cause (मूलं सर्वकारणं सर्वथा छिन्नम्)—is now severed.

6.31.16 (Coercion Through Bereavement)

Explaining vyasanenātmanaḥ, Rāma Varmā notes that by this calamity of widowhood Sītā will become Rāvaṇa’s wife of her own accord, having no other refuge (आत्मनस्तव व्यसनेन मम भार्या भविष्यसि), while Govindarāja takes vyasanena as the instrumental cause requiring no further entreaty from him. Rāma Varmā takes etāṃ matim as her devotion to Rāma (एतां मतिं रामविषयां मतिं), whereas Śivasahāya anticipates her thought of following Rāma in death: afflicted by separation (पतिवियोगदुःखेनोपलक्षिता), she must abandon the resolve to die, for a departed husband cannot be regained.

6.31.19–20 (The Fabricated Encampment and Midnight Reconnaissance)

Continuing Rāvaṇa’s mendacious narrative, Govindarāja, Rāma Varmā, and Śivasahāya construe the locative absolute दिवाकरे अस्तं व्रजति सति (`as the sun was setting’): Rāma, accompanied by a vast host, pressed hard upon the northern shore of the ocean (समुद्रस्योत्तरं तीरं महता बलेन पीड्य पीडयित्वा)—that is, the shore north of Laṅkā’s citadel—and pitched camp. In 6.31.20, Rāma Varmā identifies balam as Rāma’s army (बलं रामसेना) and explains caritam in a causative passive sense (चरितं चारितम्): while sleeping soundly at midnight after their weary march, the camp was first scouted by Rāvaṇa’s spies.

6.31.21–23 (Prahasta’s Imaginary Night-Assault)

Rāvaṇa claims that once his scouts reported the monkeys’ stupor, a massive rākṣasa force commanded by his chief general Prahasta fell upon the camp at night where Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa lay. Verses 22–23 catalogue the barrage of heavy rākṣasa weaponry—spears, iron clubs, discs, swords, pikes, and spiked maces (kūṭamudgarān)—lifted again and again (udyamyodyamya) and rained down upon the helpless monkeys. The shift from accusative weapon-nouns in verse 22 to the nominative plural passive participle nipātitāḥ in 23b reflects epic syntactic attraction across the two-verse list.

6.31.24 (The Lie of Rāma’s Decapitation)

Climactically, Rāvaṇa tells Sītā that while Rāma was still fast asleep, the ruthless and dexterous (kṛtahastena) Prahasta severed his head with a great broadsword. Commenting on the adverb asaktam (`unimpededly, in a single clean stroke’), the commentators explain that the blow fell without meeting any obstruction in bone or sinew (यथा भवति तथा छिन्नम्). By attributing the deed to Prahasta during a midnight rout rather than to a duel in open daylight, Rāvaṇa attempts to make the sudden death of the invincible Rāma seem plausible to Sītā.

6.31.25–26 (Fates Invented for Vibhīṣaṇa, Lakṣmaṇa, Sugrīva, and Hanūmān)

Extending his fabrication, Rāvaṇa claims that even as Vibhīṣaṇa leaped into the sky to escape (समुत्पत्य उड्डीय विभीषणो गृहीतः प्रहस्तेनेति शेषः), he was captured, while Lakṣmaṇa fled in all directions (प्रव्राजितः पलायितः). In 6.31.26, Rāvaṇa matches each chief’s wound to his name through etymological wordplay: Śivasahāya, reading शेते in both halves, notes that Sugrīva (Handsome-Necked') lies with a broken neck (भग्नया ग्रीवया सुग्रीवः शेते), while Hanūmān (Prominent-Jawed’) lies slain with his jaw smashed (निरस्ता हनुर्यस्य अत एव हतो हनूमान् शेते).

6.31.27–29 (The Fall of Jāmbavān, Mainda, Dvivida, and Panasa)

As Jāmbavān struggled to rise from his knees (जानुभ्यामुत्पतन् जाम्बवान् बहुभिः पट्टिशैर्निहतः, or struck on both knees, जानुभ्यां जानुनोः निहतः), he was felled by spears like a chopped tree (निकृत्तः छिन्नः पादप इव छिन्नः). Śivasahāya construes 28–29a as a one-and-a-half-verse unit (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी): the tall-bodied (व्यायतौ दीर्घशरीरौ) brothers Mainda and Dvivida were sliced across the waist (मध्ये कटिस्थाने). In 29b, Panasa groans like a burst ripe jackfruit (विदीर्णपक्वपनसफलमिव पनसोऽनुश्वसिति); Govindarāja, reading अनुतिष्ठति, glosses it as `lies hacked like a jackfruit’ (अनुतिष्ठति शेते).

6.31.30 (Darīmukha and Kumuda)

The paronomasia continues in 6.31.30a, where Darīmukha (Cave-Faced') lies riddled with iron arrows inside a mountain cave (_daryām_). In 30b, **Rāma Varmā** confirms that the reading कुमुदस्तु महातेजा निष्कूजन्सायकैर्हतः represents the ancient recension (इति प्राञ्चः पाठः). **Govindarāja** glosses the present participle _niṣkūjan_ as voiceless, uttering no sound’ (निष्कूजन् निःशब्दः)—an ironic contrast to the joyful opening of the night-lotus (kumuda)—while Śivasahāya simply glosses sāyakaiḥ as `by arrows’ (सायकैः शरैर्हतः).

6.31.31–32 (Aṅgada’s Fall and the Crushing of the Host)

On 6.31.31, Rāma Varmā, Śivasahāya, and Govindarāja explain the gerund āsādya (having closed in upon', राक्षसैरासाद्य प्राप्य छिन्नः) with the instrumental _rākṣasaiḥ_ as logical agent: overtaken by rākṣasas and riddled with shafts, Aṅgada fell disgorging blood (शरैः छिन्नः अत एव रुधिरोद्गारी सन् क्षितौ निपतितः). The repetition of _aṅgadaḥ_ frames the verse with mock pathos. In 32, reading शायिताः (felled, laid prostrate’, शायिताः शयानाः), Govindarāja and Śivasahāya note that the sleeping monkeys were trampled by war-elephants and chariot-squadrons (नागैर्गजैः रथजालैश्च मथिताः) like clouds scattered by gales.

6.31.33–34 (Panic, Flight, and Bears Turning into Tree-Climbers)

In 6.31.33, Rāma Varmā links jaghanyataḥ with the pursuit (जघन्यतोऽनुद्रुता इत्यन्वयः), while Govindarāja and Śivasahāya gloss it as from behind' (जघन्यतः पृष्ठतः): struck in the rear as they fled in terror (त्रस्ताः अत एवाभिद्रुताः), the monkeys were hunted down like elephants by lions (सिंहैः प्रहृता महाद्विपा इव अनुद्रुताः). In 34b, where **Govindarāja** reads वानरीं वृत्तिमाश्रिताः (the ground-dwelling bears adopted the tree-climbing habit of monkeys’), Śivasahāya reads वानरैर्व्यतिमिश्रिताः, explaining that the bears mingled indiscriminately with monkeys up in the branches (वानरैर्व्यतिमिश्राः विभक्ताः ऋक्षाः वृक्षानुपारूढाः).

6.31.35 (Summary of the Slaughter)

Śivasahāya notes that Rāvaṇa sums up his battle narrative across verses 35–36 (उपसंहरन्नाह सागरस्येति द्वाभ्याम्). Across shore, hill, and woodland, multitudes of tawny monkeys (पिङ्गलाः वानराः) have been slaughtered by the monstrous-eyed night-rangers (विरूपाक्षैः राक्षसैः बहवः पिङ्गलाः वानरा हताः). By contrasting the tawny forest-dwellers (piṅgalāḥ) with the fierce-eyed rākṣasas (virūpākṣaiḥ), Rāvaṇa seeks to impress upon Sītā the utter annihilation of every potential rescuer.

6.31.36–37 (Stage-Managing the Visual Proof)

Having completed his verbal fabrication, Rāvaṇa announces the physical token meant to break Sītā’s spirit: Śivasahāya paraphrases 6.31.36, noting that her husband has been slain and his head, dripping with gore (क्षतजार्द्रं रुधिरार्द्रम् अस्य रामस्य शिरस्तु आहृतम्) and begrimed with battlefield dust, has been carried back. To make the arrival of the head appear uncontrived, Rāvaṇa addresses a female guard standing near Sītā (राक्षसीं सीतासमीपवर्तिनीम्, glossed by Govindarāja as राक्षसीं समीपवर्तिनीं कांचित्) within Sītā’s direct hearing (sītāyām upaśṛṇvantyām).

6.31.38–39 (Vidyujjihva’s Entrance with the Phantom Head and Bow)

Rāvaṇa commands the rākṣasī to fetch the ruthless Vidyujjihva, claiming that he personally retrieved Rāghava’s severed head from the fray (येन राघवशिरः स्वयमाहृतं तं विद्युज्जिह्वं समानय). Śivasahāya explains that Vidyujjihva, who had been waiting on standby just outside, immediately stepped forward carrying the illusory head and bow (मायाकल्पितं सशरासनं शिरः गृह्य गृहीत्वा रावणस्याग्रतः स्थितः) and bowed low before the king.

6.31.40 (Addressing the Waiting Conjurer)

On 6.31.39–40, Govindarāja (reading ततो गृह्य) emphasizes the immediate sequence: right upon hearing the order dispatching the rākṣasī (राक्षसीप्रस्थापनवचनश्रवणानन्तरमेव), Vidyujjihva appeared and stood close beside Rāvaṇa (samīpaparivartinam). Calling him mahājihvam (great-tongued', matching his name Lightning-Tongued’), Rāvaṇa now addresses the conjurer (विद्युज्जिह्वं रावणोऽब्रवीत्) to command the final unveiling of the phantom head before Sītā’s eyes.

6.31.41–42 (Displaying the Head and the Sorcerer’s Disappearance)

Glossing 6.31.41, Govindarāja and Śivasahāya explain the final state of her husband' (भर्तुः पश्चिमाम् अन्तिमामवस्थां कृपणा सीता पश्यतु) as his death (पश्चिमामवस्थाम् मरणमित्यर्थः). In 42, **Rāma Varmā** notes that the phantom head remained lovely to behold’ just like Rāma’s true face (रामशिरोवत्प्रियदर्शनमित्यर्थः). Setting it near her (अस्याः समीपे), Vidyujjihva vanished (अन्तरधीयतापागच्छत्); citing Kataka (Śrīgopyāḥ), Rāma Varmā suggests he withdrew out of secret sympathy for Sītā (अनुकूलतयेवेति भावः), or lest closer inspection betray the illusion.

6.31.43–44 (Casting Down the Counterfeit Bow)

On cikṣepa in 6.31.43a, Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja offer the gloss drew forth' from Vidyujjihva's hand (चिक्षेप आचकर्ष, विद्युज्जिह्वहस्तादित्यर्थः), while **Śivasahāya** takes it in its primary sense (hurled down’ before Sītā). Govindarāja explains why the bow is famed throughout the three worlds: because it is the divine bow of Viṣṇu (त्रिषु लोकेषु विख्यातम् वैष्णवत्वादिति भावः). In 44, Śivasahāya harmonizes the two claims by noting that Prahasta took the bow after slaying the mortal Rāma and Vidyujjihva carried it hither (मानुषं रामं हत्वा प्रहस्तेन विद्युज्जिह्वेनानीतम्).

6.31.45 (The Final Demand for Submission)

In the concluding Vaṃśastha verse, Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja note that in some manuscripts vidyujjihvena drops a syllable metrically (विद्युज्जिह्वेनेति छन्दोवशाल्लकारलोपः). Where our text reads vinikīryamāṇaḥ (casting down', विनिकीर्यमाणः विकिरन्नित्यर्थः), **Govindarāja** reads विनिकीर्य रावणः and explains _sahaiva_ as simultaneously’ (सहस्रं युगपदेव): Vidyujjihva cast down the head while Rāvaṇa flung down the bow before Sītā (विद्युज्जिह्वः शिरः प्रक्षिप्तवान् स्वयं धनुरिति भेदः), culminating in Rāvaṇa’s blunt command: `Submit to my will!’ (bhava me vaśānugā).

हीगॆ हेळि दुर्जय राक्षस राजनाद रावणनु सीतॆयु केळुवन्ते ओर्व राक्षसिय बळि हेळिदनु.॥37॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

37. evam = thus; tava = your; bharataa = husband; sa sainyaH = together with his army; hataH = was killed; * mama senagaaH* = by my army; idam = this; shiraH = head; asya kshhata jaardram = dvenced in his blood; rajodhvastam = and covered with dust; aahR^itam = is procured.

अनुवादः

“Thus, your husband together with his army was killed by my troops. This head, drenched in his blood and besmeared with dust has been gathered up.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then Rāvaṇa, the lord of the rākṣasas, supremely unassailable (parama-durdharṣaḥ), spoke this word to a rākṣasī while Sītā was listening closely (sītāyām upaśṛṇvantyām):

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

राक्षसं क्रूरकर्माणं विद्युज्जिह्वं समानय ।
येन तद्राघवशिरः सङ्ग्रामात् स्वयमाहृतम् ॥ ३८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

राक्षसं क्रूरकर्माणं विद्युज्जिह्वं समानय ।
येन तद्राघवशिरः सङ्ग्रामात् स्वयमाहृतम् ॥ ३८ ॥

मूलम् - VR

ततः परमदुर्धर्षो रावणो राक्षसेश्वरः । सीतायामुपशृण्वत्यां राक्षसीमिद मब्रवीत् ॥ ६-३१-३८

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘तुम क्रूरकर्मा राक्षस विद्युज्जिह्वको बुला ले आओ, जो स्वयं संग्रामभूमिसे रामका सिर यहाँ ले आया है’ ॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

38. tataH = thereafter; raavaNaH = Ravana; paramadurdharshhaH = very much difficult to be attacked; raakshhashvaraH = and the lord of demns; siitaayaam upashR^iNya = while Seetha was overhearing; abraviit = spoke; idam = these words; raakshhasiim = to an ogress.

अनुवादः

Thereafter, Ravana the Lord of demons who was very much difficult to be attacked, spoke the following words to an ogress, while Seetha was overhearing them.

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

नीनु क्रूरकर्मि राक्षस विद्युज्जिह्वनन्नु करॆदु ता. अवनु स्वतः रणभूमियिन्द रामन शिरस्सन्नु इल्लिगॆ तन्दिरुवनु.॥3.॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

3. ayam = this; mantrakaalaH = time for delibration; sampraaptaH iti = has come; naH = to us; (Let); sarve = all; mantriNaH = the ministerial; raakshhasaaH = demons; shiighram = quickly; aagaantu = come; susamaahitaaH = with keenly attentive minds.

अनुवादः

“The time for deliberation has come to us. Let our ministerial colleagues come quickly with their keenly attentive minds.”

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Summon (samānaya) the rākṣasa Vidyujjihva of cruel deeds (krūra-karmāṇam), by whom that head of Rāghava was personally brought back (svayam āhṛtam) from the battle.”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

विद्युज्जिह्वस्तदा गृह्य शिरस्तत्सशरासनम् ।
प्रणामं शिरसा कृत्वा रावणस्याग्रतः स्थितः ॥ ३९ ॥
तमब्रवीत् ततो राजा रावणो राक्षसं स्थितम् ।
विद्युज्जिह्वं महाजिह्वं समीपपरिवर्तिनम् ॥ ४० ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

विद्युज्जिह्वस्तदा गृह्य शिरस्तत्सशरासनम् ।
प्रणामं शिरसा कृत्वा रावणस्याग्रतः स्थितः ॥ ३९ ॥
तमब्रवीत् ततो राजा रावणो राक्षसं स्थितम् ।
विद्युज्जिह्वं महाजिह्वं समीपपरिवर्तिनम् ॥ ४० ॥

विद्युज्जिह्वस्तदा गृह्य शिरस्तत्स शरासनम् ।
प्रमाणं शिरसा कृत्वा रावणस्याग्रतः स्थितः ॥ तमब्रवीत्ततो राजा रावणो राक्षसं स्थितम् ।
विद्युज्जिह्वं महाजिह्वं समीप परिवर्तिनम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

तब विद्युज्जिह्व धनुषसहित उस मस्तकको लेकर आया और सिर झुका रावणको प्रणाम करके उसके सामने खड़ा हो गया । उस समय अपने पास खड़े हुए विशाल जिह्वावाले राक्षस विद्युज्जिह्वसे राजा रावण यों बोला—

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आ विद्युज्जिह्वनु धनुस्सु सहित आ मस्तकवन्नु तॆगॆदुकॊण्डु बन्दु रावणनिगॆ शिरबागि वन्दिसि अवन ऎदुरिगॆ निन्तनु. आग तन्न बळि निन्तिद्द विशालजिह्वॆयुळ्ळ राक्षस विद्युजिह्वनल्लि रावणनु हीगॆ हेळिदनु.॥39-40॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

39. samaanaya = bring; vidyujjihvam = Vidyujjihva; raakshhasam = the demons; kruurakarmaaNam = of the cruel deed; yena = by whom; tat = that; raaghava shiraH = Raghava’s head; aahR^itam = was brought; svayam = by himself; saN^graamaat = from the battle-field.

अनुवादः

“Bring Vidyujjihva, the demon of the cruel deed, by whom himself Raghava’s head was brought back from the battle-field.”

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

40. tadaa = then; gR^iya; holding; tat shiraH = that head; sa sharaasanam = along with a bow; vidyujjihvaH = Vidyujjihva; kR^itva = making a respectful salute; shirasaa = with his head bent low; sthitaH = stood; agrataH = in front; raavaNasya = of Ravana.

अनुवादः

Holding that head along with a bow, Vidyujjihva making a respectful salute with his head bent low, stood in front of Ravana.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

39: Thereupon Vidyujjihva, taking that head together with the bow (sa-śarāsanam), made obeisance (praṇāmam) with his head and stood before Rāvaṇa (rāvaṇasyāgrataḥ sthitaḥ).

40: Then King Rāvaṇa spoke to that rākṣasa Vidyujjihva of huge tongue (mahā-jihvam), who stood waiting at hand (samīpa-parivartinam):

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

At Rāvaṇa’s command, Vidyujjihva sets the phantom head before Sītā and vanishes, while Rāvaṇa flings down the counterfeit bow and demands that she yield to his will.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अग्रतः कुरु सीतायाः शीघ्रं दाशरथेः शिरः ।
अवस्थां पश्चिमां भर्तुः कृपणा साधु पश्यतु ॥ ४१ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अग्रतः कुरु सीतायाः शीघ्रं दाशरथेः शिरः ।
अवस्थां पश्चिमां भर्तुः कृपणा साधु पश्यतु ॥ ४१ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘तुम दशरथकुमार रामका मस्तक शीघ्र ही सीताके आगे रख दो, जिससे यह बेचारी अपने पतिकी अन्तिम अवस्थाका अच्छी तरह दर्शन कर ले’ ॥ ४१ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

नीनु दशरथनन्दन रामन शिरस्सन्नु बेगने सीतॆय मुन्दॆ इरिसु. अदरिन्द ई बडपायि तन्न पतिय अन्तिम स्थितियन्नु चॆन्नागि नोडलि.॥41॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

41. tataH = thereafter; raajaa = the king; raavaNaH = Ravana; abraviit = spoke; tam vidyujjihvam = to that Vidyujjihva; raakshhasam = the demon; mahaajihvam = who had a huge tongue; sthitam = and who was standing; samiipa parivartinam = and moving closely.

अनुवादः

Thereafter, the king Ravana spoke the following words to Vidyujjihva, the demon who had a large tongue and who was standing and moving closely.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Quickly place the head of Daśaratha's son (dāśaratheḥ śiraḥ) before Sītā (agrataḥ sītāyāḥ); let the wretched woman (kṛpaṇā) behold well (sādhu paśyatu) the final state (avasthāṃ paścimām) of her husband!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

एवमुक्तं तु तद् रक्षः शिरस्तत् प्रियदर्शनम् ।
उपनिक्षिप्य सीतायाः क्षिप्रमन्तरधीयत ॥ ४२ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

एवमुक्तं तु तद् रक्षः शिरस्तत् प्रियदर्शनम् ।
उपनिक्षिप्य सीतायाः क्षिप्रमन्तरधीयत ॥ ४२ ॥

मूलम् - VR

अग्रतः कुरु सीतायाः श्रीघ्रं दाशरथेः शिरः । अवस्थां पशिचमां भर्तुः कृपणा साधु पश्यतु ॥ ६-३१-४२

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

रावणके ऐसा कहनेपर वह राक्षस उस सुन्दर मस्तकको सीताके निकट रखकर तत्काल अदृश्य हो गया ॥ ४२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रावणनु हीगॆ हेळिदाग आ राक्षसनु आ सुन्दर मस्तकवन्नु सीतॆय मुन्दॆ इरिसि तत्क्षण अदृश्य नादनु.॥42॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

42. kuru = keep; daasharatheH = Rama’s; shiraH = head; shiighram = immediately; agrataH = in front; siitaayaaH = of Seetha; kR^ipaNaa = (let) the miserable Seetha; pashyatu = see; saadhu = well; pashchimaam = the find; avasthaam = condition; bhartaH = of her husband.

अनुवादः

“Keep Rama’s head immediately in front of Seetha. Let the miserable Seetha see well the final condition of her husband.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thus addressed, that rākṣasa set down (upanikṣipya) that head of lovely aspect (priya-darśanam) before Sītā and swiftly vanished (kṣipram antaradhīyata).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

रावणश्चापि चिक्षेप भास्वरं कार्मुकं महत् ।
त्रिषु लोकेषु विख्यातं रामस्यैतदिति ब्रुवन् ॥ ४३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

रावणश्चापि चिक्षेप भास्वरं कार्मुकं महत् ।
त्रिषु लोकेषु विख्यातं रामस्यैतदिति ब्रुवन् ॥ ४३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

रावणने भी उस विशाल चमकीले धनुषको यह कहकर सीताके सामने डाल दिया कि यही रामका त्रिभुवनविख्यात धनुष है ॥ ४३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रावणनू कूड आ विशाल हॊळॆयुत्तिरुव धनुस्सन्नु ‘इदे रामन त्रिभुवन विख्यात धनुष्य’ ऎन्दु हेळि सीतॆय मुन्दॆ इट्टुबिट्टनु.॥43॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

43. tat rakshhaH = that demon; evam = thus; uktam = spoken; upamikshhipya = desposited in the vicinity; siitaayaaH = of Seetha; tat = that; shiraH = head; priyadarshanam = whih was pleasant to look at; kshhipram = and soon; antaradhiiyata = disappeared from the site.

अनुवादः

Hearing Ravana’s words, that demon deposited the cherished head in the vicinity of Seetha and soon disappeared from the site.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

And Rāvaṇa too cast down (cikṣepa) the great, gleaming bow (bhāsvaraṃ kārmukaṃ mahat), saying, “This is Rāma's, famed across the three worlds (triṣu lokeṣu vikhyātam)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

इदं तत् तव रामस्य कार्मुकं ज्यासमावृतम् ।
इह प्रहस्तेनानीतं तं हत्वा निशि मानुषम् ॥ ४४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

इदं तत् तव रामस्य कार्मुकं ज्यासमावृतम् ।
इह प्रहस्तेनानीतं तं हत्वा निशि मानुषम् ॥ ४४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

फिर बोला—‘सीते! यही तुम्हारे रामका प्रत्यञ्चासहित धनुष है । रातके समय उस मनुष्यको मारकर प्रहस्त इस धनुषको यहाँ ले आया है’ ॥ ४४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मत्तॆ सीते! इदे निन्न रामन हॆदॆ एरिसिद धनुस्सु आगिदॆ. रात्रियल्लि अवनन्नु कॊन्दु प्रहस्तनु ई धनुस्सन्नु इल्लिगॆ तन्दिरुवनु ऎन्दु हेळिदनु.॥44॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

44. etat = this is; raamasya = Rama’s; vikhyaatam = (bow) famous; tishhu lokeshhu = in the three worlds; raavaNashhaai = Ravana; bruvan = speaking; iti = thus; hikshhepa = threw ( in front of Seetha); bhaasvaram = the shining; mahat = great; kaarmukam = bow.

अनुवादः

“Here is Rama’s bow famous in the three worlds!” - saying thus, Ravana threw a great shining bow in front of Seetha.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Here is that bow of your Rāma, fitted with its bowstring (jyā-samāvṛtam), brought here by Prahasta after slaying that mortal (mānuṣam) at night.”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स विद्युज्जिह्वेन सहैव तच्छिरो
धनुश्च भूमौ विनिकीर्यमाणः ।
विदेहराजस्य सुतां यशस्विनीं
ततोऽब्रवीत् तां भव मे वशानुगा ॥ ४५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स विद्युज्जिह्वेन सहैव तच्छिरो
धनुश्च भूमौ विनिकीर्यमाणः ।
विदेहराजस्य सुतां यशस्विनीं
ततोऽब्रवीत् तां भव मे वशानुगा ॥ ४५ ॥

मूलम् - VR

इदम् तत्तव रामस्य कार्मुकं ज्यासमाव्ऱ्६इतम् । इह प्रहस्तेवानीतम् तम् हत्वा निशि मानुषम् ॥ ६-३१-४५

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

जब विद्युज्जिह्वने मस्तक वहाँ रखा, उसके साथ ही रावणने वह धनुष पृथ्वीपर डाल दिया । तत्पश्चात् वह विदेहराजकुमारी यशस्विनी सीतासे बोला—‘अब तुम मेरे वशमें हो जाओ’ ॥ ४५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

विद्युज्जिह्वनु मस्तकवन्नु अल्लि इरिसिदाग, रावणनू धनुस्सन्नु अल्लि नॆलद मेलॆ इट्टु विदेहकुमारि यशस्विनी सीतॆयल्लि ‘इन्नु नीनु नन्न वशळागु’ ऎन्दु हेळिदनु.॥45॥

Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy

पदच्छेदः

45. idam = this is; tat = that; kaarmukam = bow; jyaa samaavR^itam = with its cord stretched; tava raamasya = belonging to Rama; aaniitam = that was brought back; prahastena = by Prahasta; iha = here; hatvaa = after killing; tam = that; maanushham = man; nishi = in the night.

अनुवादः

“This is the bow with its cord stretched, belonging to Rama, that was brought back by Prahasta here, after killing that hero in the night.”

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये युद्धकाण्डे एकत्रिंशः सर्गः ॥ ३१ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके युद्धकाण्डमें इकतीसवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ ३१ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद युद्धकाण्डदल्लि मूवत्तॊन्दनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु.॥31॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Casting down (vinikīryamāṇaḥ) that head and bow upon the ground together with Vidyujjihva, he then spoke to that illustrious daughter of the king of Videha: “Become compliant to my will (bhava me vaśānugā)!”
