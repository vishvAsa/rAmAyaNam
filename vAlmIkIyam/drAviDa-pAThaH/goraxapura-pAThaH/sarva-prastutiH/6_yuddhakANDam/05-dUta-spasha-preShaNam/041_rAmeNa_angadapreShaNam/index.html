Murali Krishnamurthy Kopalle Murthy - Intro

Book VI : Yuddha Kanda - Book Of War

Rama advises Sugreeva not to repeat such reckless acts in future on the ground of he being a king. Then Rama tells Lakshmana about the various evil portents he had seen. Rama commands the monkey-generals to besiege all the four gates of Lanka. He himself along with Lakshmana protects the army, besieging the northern gate being guarded by Ravana. Then, Rama sends Angada to Ravana, to expostulate him and to bring him back to reason. Angada tries his best to convince Ravana, but in vain. Ravana commands his attendants to seize Angada. Angada shakes out those attendants who tried to seize him ascends the roof of Ravana’s palace, tramples ad crushes it down by his strength. Angada finally returns to Rama, who was stationed in the midst of the monkey-forces.

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

41.1–3 (Sugrīva’s Battle Scars and Rashness)

Introducing the forty-first sarga on the investment of Laṅkā (अथ लङ्कावरोधः एकचत्वारिंशे), Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa take तस्मिन् निमित्तानि as the bloody wound-marks on Sugrīva’s body (क्षतजादिरूपाणि युद्धचिह्नानि), whereas Śiromaṇi also notes the sense of portents at that moment (तस्मिन्समये). Govindarāja observes that Rāma’s rebuke `kings do not act thus’ (न कुर्वन्ति जनेश्वराः) is spoken out of respectful solicitude: when servants like us are at hand, a monarch should not hazard his own person (अस्मादृशपरिजने विद्यमाने न कुर्वन्तीत्यादरोक्तिः).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

41.4–5 (Euphemism and Overflowing Affection)

On the euphemistic clause त्वयि किंचित्समापन्ने (`if anything should happen to you’), Tilaka cites the maxim that one must never utter an inauspicious word (नाश्लीलं कीर्तयेत्) for a loved one. Govindarāja compares Rāma’s overflowing tenderness (वात्सल्यातिशयः) to a mother cow forgetting her older calf in her fondness for a newborn (अभिनववत्सवात्सल्येन पूर्ववत्समनभिलषन्ती वत्सका गौः), declaring that without Sugrīva neither Sītā—half his own self—nor Bharata, Lakṣmaṇa, Śatrughna, nor his own life would hold any value.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Declaring that he would have abandoned his life after fulfilling his vows had Sugrīva fallen, Rāma commends the monkey king’s devotion and orders Lakṣmaṇa to array the army.

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

41.6 (Love That Fears the Worst)

Anticipating Sugrīva’s protest that he was never in danger of defeat, Rāma adds जानतश्चापि ते वीर्यम् (`even though I know your valor’); Rāma Varmā quotes the proverb that deep affection always fears misfortune (अतिस्नेहः पापशङ्की), while Śivasahāya remarks that the qualifier reveals Rāma’s anxious love.

41.7–8 (Fulfilling Every Pledge Before Death)

Śivasahāya and Rāma Varmā explain why Rāma says he would first slay Rāvaṇa, crown Vibhīṣaṇa, and install Bharata before abandoning his body (त्यक्ष्ये देहम्): even in grief for Sugrīva, Rāma could not leave his martial vow unfulfilled (प्रतिज्ञानिर्वाहार्थम्), let Vibhīṣaṇa’s refuge fail (शरणागतिवैकल्यात्), or allow the ancestral Ikṣvāku realm to perish (कुलधर्मोच्छेदात्).
