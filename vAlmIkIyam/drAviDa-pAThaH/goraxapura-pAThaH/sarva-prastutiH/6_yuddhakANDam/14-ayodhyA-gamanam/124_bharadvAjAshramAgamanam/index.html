Book VI : Yuddha Kanda - Book Of War

Rama, travelling in the aerial car towards Ayodhya, lands at the hermitage of Bharadwaja the sage. When Rama enquires of Bhardwaja about the welfare of Ayodhya-City, Bharata and his mothers. Bharadwaja informs Rama about their welfare. Further, the sage informs that by virtue of his asceticism, he could know everything about the exploits of Rama from the day he left Ayodhya, till he destroyed Ravana. Then, Rama seeks a boon from Bharadwaja, asking him to make the trees on the route to Ayodhya, to bear fruit in the off-season for which the sage grants the boon. Thereupon, the troops of monkeys feasted on those fruits at will.

6.

On v. 6, Bhūṣaṇa analyses Rāma’s fourfold designation of his brother: without him (तं विना), how can Rāma bathe; as कैकयीपुत्रम्, Rāma owes him the same ungrudging love that Bharata showed despite his mother’s plot; as भरतम्, he has borne the burden of the realm free from Rākṣasa peril; and as धर्मचारिणम्, while Bharata wears bark and matted locks in grief, Rāma cannot possibly abandon his own ascetic vow to enjoy royal baths and robes (प्रथमं मे स्नानमेव नेष्टं कथं तदनन्तरभावीनि वस्त्रादीनि).

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद युद्धकाण्डदल्लि नूर इप्पत्तनाल्कनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु.॥124॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Addressed thus by Rāma, Vibhīṣaṇa, the lord of the Rākṣasas, filled with haste (tvarānvitaḥ), summoned that celestial chariot bright as the sun (vimānaṃ sūryasaṃkāśam).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

23–24.

In v. 23, Vibhīṣaṇa immediately summons the sun-bright Puṣpaka (विमानं सूर्यसंकाशमाजुहाव). Both Śiromaṇi and Bhūṣaṇa construe verses 24–29 as a single ornate syntactic period (श्लोकषट्कमेकान्वयि) describing the chariot: its frame is wrought of wondrous gold (काञ्चनमयविचित्रावयवम्), its platforms set with cat’s-eye gems (वैदूर्यमणीनां वेदिका यस्मिन्), and its decks ringed with upper chambers or pavilions (कूटागारैः शालागृहैः, Tilaka; मण्डपैः, Bhūṣaṇa).
