सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

After vanquishing Marutta, the battle-hungry Rāvaṇa demands combat or submission from mighty earthly monarchs—including Duṣyanta, Suratha, Gādhi, Gaya, and Purūravas—before arriving at Ayodhyā, well guarded by King Anaraṇya.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अथ जित्वा मरुत्तं स प्रययौ राक्षसाधिपः ।
नगराणि नरेन्द्राणां युद्धकाङ्क्षी दशाननः ॥ १ ॥

मूलम्

अथ जित्वा मरुत्तं स प्रययौ राक्षसाधिपः ।
नगराणि नरेन्द्राणां युद्धकाङ्क्षी दशाननः ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

(अगस्त्यजी कहते हैं—रघुनन्दन!) पूर्वोक्त रूपसे राजा मरुत्तको जीतनेके पश्चात् राक्षसराज दशग्रीव क्रमशः अन्य नरेशोंके नगरोंमें भी युद्धकी इच्छासे गया ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

(अगस्त्यरु हेळुत्तारॆ - रघुनन्दन !) हिन्दिनन्तॆ मरुत्तनन्नु जयिसिद बळिक राक्षसराज दशग्रीवनु क्रमवागि इतर राजरल्लियू युद्धद इच्छॆयिन्द होदनु.॥1॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then, having vanquished Marutta (jitvā maruttam), that sovereign of the rākṣasas (rākṣasādhipaḥ), Ten-Headed Rāvaṇa, eager for combat (yuddhakāṅkṣī daśānanaḥ), proceeded to the cities of the kings of men (nagarāṇi narendrāṇām).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

समासाद्य तु राजेन्द्रान् महेन्द्रवरुणोपमान् ।
अब्रवीद् राक्षसेन्द्रस्तु युद्धं मे दीयतामिति ॥ २ ॥
निर्जिताः स्मेति वा ब्रूत एष मे हि सुनिश्चयः ।
अन्यथा कुर्वतामेवं मोक्षो नैवोपपद्यते ॥ ३ ॥

अब्रवीद्राक्षसेन्द्रस्तु युद्धं मे दीयतामिति ॥ निर्जिताः स्मेति वा ब्रूत एष मे हि सुनिश्चयः ।
अव्यथा कुर्वतामेवं मोक्षो नैवोपपद्यते ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

महेन्द्र और वरुणके समान पराक्रमी उन महाराजोंके पास जाकर वह राक्षसराज उनसे कहता—‘राजाओ! तुम मेरे साथ युद्ध करो अथवा यह कह दो कि ‘हम हार गये ।’ यही मेरा अच्छी तरह किया हुआ निश्चय है । इसके विपरीत करनेसे तुम्हें छुटकारा नहीं मिलेगा’ ॥ २-३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

महेन्द्र, वरुणरन्तॆ पराक्रमि गळाद आ महाराजर बळिगॆ होगि राक्षसेन्द्रनु हेळुत्तिद्दनु - राजरे ! नीवु नन्नॊडनॆ युद्धमाडि इल्लवॆ ‘नावु सोतिद्देवॆ’ ऎन्दु हेळि. इदन्नॆ नानु माडिद निश्चयवागिदॆ. इदक्कॆ विरुद्धवागि नडॆदरॆ निमगॆ बिडुगडॆ इल्ल.॥2-3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

2: Approaching great kings who rivaled Mahendra and Varuṇa (mahendra-varuṇopamān), that lord of rākṣasas spoke these words: “Let battle be granted to me (yuddhaṃ me dīyatām),

3: “Or else declare, ‘We are vanquished’ (nirjitāḥ smeti vā brūta)—for this is my firm resolve (eṣa me hi suniścayaḥ)! For those who act otherwise (anyathā kurvatām), no release whatsoever is possible (mokṣo naivopapadyate).”

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

तब निर्भय, बुद्धिमान् तथा धर्मपूर्ण विचार रखनेवाले बहुत-से महाबली राजा परस्पर सलाह करके शत्रुकी प्रबलताको समझकर बोले—‘राक्षसराज! हम तुमसे हार मान लेते हैं’ ॥ ४ १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग निर्भय, बुद्धिवन्त, धर्मपूर्णविचार माडुव अनेक महाबलि राजरु परस्पर सलहॆ पडॆदु, शत्रुविन प्रबलतॆ अरितु राक्षसराजा! नावु निन्निन्द सोलन्नॊप्पि कॊळ्ळुत्तेवॆ ऎन्दु हेळुत्तिद्दरु.॥4½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thereupon those fearless, wise monarchs (abhīravaḥ prājñāḥ pārthivāḥ), steadfast in dharma (dharmaniścayāḥ) and endowed with exceeding might, having taken counsel with one another (mantrayitvā tato ’nyonyam), declared, “We are vanquished,” knowing the power of their adversary’s boon (jñātvā varabalaṃ ripoḥ).

एते सर्वेऽब्रुवंस्तात निर्जिताः स्मेति पार्थिवाः ।

दुष्यन्त, सुरथ, गाधि, गय, राजा पुरूरवा—इन सभी भूपालोंने अपने-अपने राजत्वकालमें रावणके सामने अपनी पराजय स्वीकार कर ली ॥ ५ १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

दुष्यन्त, सुरथ, गाधि, गय, राजा पुरूरव इवरॆल्ल राजरु तम्म राज्यभार कालदल्लि रावणन ऎदुरिगॆ तम्म पराजयवन्नु स्वीकरिसिद्दरु.॥5½॥

Duṣyanta, Suratha, Gādhi, Gaya, and King Purūravas—all these kings declared, O dear one (tāta), “We are vanquished” (nirjitāḥ smeti). Then Rāvaṇa, lord of the rākṣasas, reached Ayodhyā (ayodhyāṃ samāsādya),

Indignantly rejecting Rāvaṇa’s demand for surrender, Ayodhyā’s sovereign Anaraṇya accepts single combat and sends forth his pre-assembled host of elephants, steeds, chariots, and infantry to launch a tremendous battle.

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

इसके बाद राक्षसोंका राजा रावण इन्द्रद्वारा सुरक्षित अमरावतीकी भाँति महाराज अनरण्यद्वारा पालित अयोध्यापुरीमें आया । वहाँ पुरन्दर (इन्द्र)-के समान पराक्रमी पुरुषसिंह राजा अनरण्यसे मिलकर बोला—‘राजन्! तुम मुझसे युद्ध करनेका वचन दो अथवा कह दो कि ‘मैं हार गया ।’ यही मेरा आदेश है’ ॥ ६—८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

बळिक राक्षसाधिप रावणनु इन्द्रनिन्द सुरक्षितवाद अमरावतियन्तिरुव, महाराजा अनरण्यनिन्द पालित अयोध्यॆगॆ बन्दनु. अल्लि इन्द्रनन्तॆ पराक्रमि पुरुषसिंह अनरण्यनल्लिगॆ बन्दु हेळिदनु-राजने! नीनु नन्नॊडनॆ युद्धद मातु कॊडु इल्लवे ‘नानु सोतॆ’ ऎन्दु हेळु. इदे नन्न आदेशवागिदॆ.॥6-8॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

6: —which was well guarded by Anaraṇya (suguptām anaraṇyena) as Amarāvatī is by Śakra (śakreṇevāmarāvatīm); and approaching that tiger among men (puruṣaśārdūlam), equal to Purandara in strength (purandara-samaṃ bale)—

7: —Rāvaṇa approached the king and spoke (prāha rājānam āsādya): “Grant me battle (yuddhaṃ dehi), or else say, ‘I am vanquished’ (nirjito ’smīti vā brūhi)—such is my decree to you (mama śāsanam)!”

8: Hearing those words of that wicked-souled one (pāpātmano vacaḥ), Ayodhyā’s sovereign Anaraṇya (ayodhyādhipatiḥ … anaraṇyaḥ), filled with fierce wrath (saṅkruddhaḥ), thereupon addressed the lord of the rākṣasas:

उस पापात्माकी वह बात सुनकर अयोध्यानरेश अनरण्यको बड़ा क्रोध हुआ और वे उस राक्षसराजसे बोले— ॥ ९ ॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Single combat is granted to you by me (dīyate dvandva-yuddhaṃ te … mayā), O sovereign of the rākṣasas! Stand fast at once and make yourself ready (santiṣṭha kṣipram āyatto bhava), and so too shall I be (caivaṃ bhavāmy aham)!”

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

7.19.1–3 (Rāvaṇa’s Ultimatum to Earthly Sovereigns)

Introducing the episode after Rāvaṇa’s triumph over King Marutta (रावणकर्तृकमरुत्तविजयोत्तरकालिकं वृत्तम्), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construes verses 2–3 as a single syntactic sentence (श्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयि) conveying Rāvaṇa’s arrogant choice: give battle or verbally concede defeat. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) clarifies अन्यथा कुर्वताम् in 7.19.3: for kings who refuse either to fight or to utter निर्जिताः स्मः, there is no escape from his siege (मदुपरोधात् मोक्षो नास्तीत्यर्थः). Śivasahāya likewise glosses मोक्षः as ‘being spared or released by me’ (मत्कर्तृकस्त्यागः).

7.19.4–5 (Wise Surrender and the Chronology of Ancient Kings)

On 7.19.4, both Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya stress that these kings were not cowards (अभीरवः सन्तोऽपि): taking counsel together as a single six-quarter period (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी), they pragmatically acknowledged defeat because they understood the power of Rāvaṇa’s boon (वरबलं ज्ञात्वा). Addressing a seeming chronological paradox in 7.19.5—where kings of different epochs appear together—Rāma Varmā explains that Duṣyanta, Suratha, Gādhi, Gaya, and Purūravas belonged to distinct ages (भिन्नकालवर्तिनः), each acknowledging defeat to the long-lived Rāvaṇa during his own era on account of Brahmā’s boon.

7.19.6 (Arrival Before Anaraṇya’s Ayodhyā)

Shifting the narrative to Rāma’s own ancestral capital, Agastya compares Ayodhyā under Anaraṇya to Indra’s celestial stronghold Amarāvatī. Śivasahāya supplies the verb अतिष्ठत् (‘stood/halted’) after अयोध्यां समासाद्य in 7.19.5ef and links the Southern hemistich 7.19.6cd directly into 7.19.7 as one six-quarter sentence. Unlike the prudent monarchs who bowed to Rāvaṇa’s boon, the Ikṣvāku sovereign Anaraṇya—explicitly matched with Purandara in martial prowess—is introduced as a king who will uphold kṣatriya honor to the death.

7.19.7–9 (Anaraṇya’s Defiant Acceptance of Combat)

Completing the sentence begun in 7.19.6cd, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) supplies आज्ञा अस्ति with एवं मम शासनम् (‘such is my command’) in 7.19.7. Anaraṇya’s reply in 7.19.9 embodies the unyielding code of the Solar Dynasty: he neither parleys nor delays, offering immediate duel (dvandva-yuddha). All three commentators—Rāma Varmā (Tilaka), Śivasahāya, and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa)—resolve the concise closing phrase एवं भवाम्यहम् identically: ‘just as I bid you be ready (आयत्तो भव), so too I make myself ready (आयत्तो भवामीत्यर्थः).’

7.19.10 (Foresight of a Vigilant Monarch)

Verse 7.19.10 reveals that Anaraṇya was not caught off guard. Rāma Varmā, Śivasahāya, and Govindarāja all gloss पूर्वं श्रुतार्थेन as ‘by Anaraṇya, who had already heard news of Rāvaṇa’s advance before his arrival’ (श्रुतरावणवृत्तान्तेन), and normalize the epic verb निष्क्रामत् as निरक्रामत्. To explain the Southern reading निर्जितम् (where Baroda CE reads the transparent सज्जितम्, ‘readied’), Rāma Varmā analyzes it as an abstract past participle (भावे क्तः): ‘that great host appointed for victory’ (निर्जयं प्रति यत्सुमहद्बलं नियुक्तम्).

7.19.11–12 (The Fourfold Army of Ayodhyā Marches Forth)

Śivasahāya notes that verses 7.19.11–12ab detail the numerical strength of Ayodhyā’s fourfold host (caturaṅga-bala)—elephants, cavalry, chariots, and infantry—as a single six-quarter sentence (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी). The repeated vocatives addressed by Agastya to Rāma—नरोत्तम (‘foremost of men’) in 7.19.11d and युद्धविशारद (‘skilled in warfare’) in 7.19.12d—vividly connect this ancient defense of Ayodhyā to Rāma’s own recent war against Rāvaṇa, heightening the narrative anticipation as the clash commences.

7.19.13–15 (Oblations in Fire and Moths in the Flame)

Constructing a somber triad of fire-similes, Vālmīki depicts the annihilation of Ayodhyā’s army. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) note that despite fighting long with supreme valor, the royal host vanished into Rāvaṇa’s ranks like sacrificial ghee in fire. On 7.19.15, Śivasahāya construes 14cd–15ab as one six-quarter sentence where the surviving remnant (अवशेषितं बलम्) plunges into the enemy throng like moths into a blazing fire, whereas Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) take प्रज्वलन्तं तम् as the fiery Rāvaṇa himself, before whom the host swiftly dwindled (अल्पावशिष्टमभवत्).

7.19.16 (A Hundred Rivers Swallowed by the Ocean)

Changing the elemental imagery from fire to water, verse 7.19.16 likens the vanishing host to rivers disappearing into the sea. Both Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya gloss वनम् in वनापगशतम् in its Vedic lexical sense of ‘water’ (वनं जलं तत्पूर्णं नदीशतम्), while Rāma Varmā explicitly notes the metrical shortening of आ in आपगा (आपा ह्रस्वः). Śivasahāya also allows the secondary sense of forest-born streams losing their identity upon entering the boundless ocean—a striking contrast with the Baroda CE reading पञ्चापगा जलम्.

7.19.17–18 (Anaraṇya’s Countercharge and the Rout of Rāvaṇa’s Ministers)

Seeing his army annihilated, Anaraṇya steps forward personally (स्वयम्, emphasized by Śivasahāya), stringing a bow radiant as Indra’s rainbow (śakra-dhanuḥ-prakhyam). The Southern Recension’s verse 7.19.18 underscores the Ikṣvāku monarch’s extraordinary personal prowess: single-handedly he shatters Rāvaṇa’s chief counselors—Mārīca, Śuka, Sāraṇa, and Prahasta—sending them fleeing like startled deer (मृगा इव व्यद्रवन्त), thereby proving that Anaraṇya’s imminent fall stems from destiny rather than any lack of martial supremacy.

7.19.19–20 (Eight Hundred Arrows Like Rain on a Mountain)

Closing directly with Rāvaṇa, Anaraṇya looses eight hundred arrows squarely upon his adversary’s head. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) follows the vivid natural simile (upamā) in 7.19.20: just as torrents of rain cascading from clouds cannot dent a granite summit (गिरिमूर्धनि), so Anaraṇya’s volley inflicts not the slightest wound (क्वचित् क्षतं न चक्रिरे). The image highlights Rāvaṇa’s terrifying physical imperviousness at the zenith of his world-conquest, while simultaneously setting up the tragic dignity of a mortal king who refuses to yield.

7.19.21–22 (Struck Down Like a Lightning-Blasted Sāla Tree)

Disdaining even to draw a weapon, the enraged Rāvaṇa fells Anaraṇya from his chariot with a single open-handed blow (tala-prahāra) to the head. On the poignant simile in 7.19.22, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) glosses वज्रदग्धः as ‘struck by a thunderbolt’ (अशनिहतः), while Śivasahāya supplies the verb अदृश्यत (‘was seen lying upon the earth, trembling and shattered’). Likening a noble fallen warrior to a towering forest sāla felled by lightning is a signature Vālmīkian motif, honoring Anaraṇya’s majesty even in mortal defeat.

7.19.23 (Rāvaṇa’s Derisive Taunt to the Ikṣvāku Sovereign)

Standing over the prostrate monarch, Rāvaṇa bursts into scornful laughter (prahasya). Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explain the metonymic accusative इक्ष्वाकुम् as ‘the descendant of Ikṣvāku, Anaraṇya’ (तद्वंश्यमनरण्यम्), and both supply the biting implication left unspoken in Rāvaṇa’s question किं फलं प्राप्तम्: ‘What have you gained—by refusing to utter the simple words “I am vanquished”?’ (निर्जितोऽस्मीति वचनमनुक्त्वेति शेषः). That very insult to the Ikṣvāku house will provoke Anaraṇya’s fateful curse.

7.19.24–25 (Rāvaṇa’s Boast and Anaraṇya’s Composed Reply)

Continuing his mockery, Rāvaṇa claims no warrior in the three worlds dares face him, sneering that luxury blinded Anaraṇya to his fame. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) glosses शङ्के as मन्ये (‘I suppose’) and notes that the present न शृणोषि carries past reference (अशृणोः). Unruffled even as his life ebbs (मन्दासुः), Anaraṇya answers not with lamentation but with philosophical clarity: since Time (kāla) cannot be transgressed by any mortal (दुरतिक्रमः), resistance against one’s appointed hour is beyond human power.

7.19.26 (Time as the True Agent, Rāvaṇa Mere Instrument)

Verse 7.19.26 delivers one of the epic’s finest expressions of kṣatriya detachment. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) underscore Anaraṇya’s refusal to concede moral victory to his boastful foe (ātma-praśaṃsin): ‘I have not been conquered by you; rather, I have fallen solely through Time whose hour had arrived’ (प्राप्तेनैव कालेन विपन्न इत्यर्थः). Śivasahāya glosses विपन्नः as ‘attained dissolution’ (पञ्चत्वं प्राप्तः) and draws a striking inference: Anaraṇya implies that he marched to war fully aware that his appointed hour of death had come (स्वमृत्युकालं ज्ञात्वैवाहं युद्धे प्रवृत्तोऽस्मीति सूचितम्).

7.19.27 (Dying Without Turning One’s Back in Battle)

In the Southern Recension’s 7.19.27, Anaraṇya affirms the supreme warrior’s creed: at the moment when life departs (प्राणापगमसमये, Śivasahāya), nothing further remains to be done in combat—yet his honor stands unblemished. Śivasahāya glosses विमुखी as ‘turning away from battle’ (युद्धात्पराङ्मुखः): unlike monarchs who fled or capitulated, Anaraṇya fell facing the enemy (yudhyamāna), thereby securing heaven and earning the moral authority to pronounce the curse that follows.

7.19.28 (The Truth-Act Provoked by Dynastic Insult)

Anaraṇya utters his curse not out of personal rancor, but to avenge the slight against his sacred dynasty. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) explains इक्ष्वाकुपरिभावित्वात् as ‘because your mocking words constitute an insult to the Ikṣvāku house’ (त्वदुक्तावमाननवाक्यस्येक्ष्वाकुकुलपरिभवरूपत्वात्), glossing वचः as a curse (शापरूपम्). Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) agrees (तत्कुलपरिभावित्वात्), while Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) analyzes it agentively (‘because you are disposed to humiliate the Ikṣvākus’). Anaraṇya seals his oath by a fourfold act of truth (satyakriyā) invoking charity, sacrifice, austerity, and royal protection.

7.19.29 (The Prophecy of Rāma Dāśarathi)

The curse culminates in a luminous prophecy directly linking Anaraṇya’s martyrdom to Agastya’s royal listener in Ayodhyā: रामो दाशरथिर्नाम shall arise in this very Ikṣvāku lineage to claim Rāvaṇa’s life. Śivasahāya emphasizes the emphatic placement of इक्ष्वाकूणां कुले एव (‘in the very race of the Ikṣvākus’), rendering poetic justice upon the boastful demon. The Baroda CE variant (राजा परमतेजस्वी) keeps the prophecy unnamed, whereas the Southern Recension explicitly names Rāma as the destined fulfiller of his ancestor’s vow.

7.19.30–31 (Heavenly Ratification and Anaraṇya’s Ascension)

Nature and the gods instantly ratify the dying king’s curse. Śivasahāya glosses जलधरोदग्रः as ‘resounding mightily like a thundercloud’ (अधिकशब्दवान्) and शापे as ‘the utterance portending Rāvaṇa’s destruction’, while Rāma Varmā notes that the shower of celestial blossoms occurs here just as in earlier fateful moments (पुष्पवृष्टिरत्रापि प्राग्वत्). On 7.19.31, Śivasahāya identifies त्रिविष्टपम् as heaven, and all three commentators normalize the epic verb अपसर्पत as अपासर्पत् (‘withdrew’), leaving Rāvaṇa outwardly victorious yet silently marked for doom.

नन्न प्राणगळु होगुत्तिवॆ, आद्दरिन्द नानु एनु माडबल्लॆ? निशाचरने! नानु युद्धदल्लि बॆन्नु तोरलिल्ल इदु ननगॆ सन्तोषविदॆ. युद्ध माडुत्ता निन्न कैयिन्द सायुत्तानॆ.॥28॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Because you have insulted the line of Ikṣvāku (ikṣvāku-paribhāvitvāt), I shall utter a word, O rākṣasa (vaco vakṣyāmi rākṣasa): if I have given charity (yadi dattam), if I have offered oblations (yadi hutam), if my austerity has been well performed (yadi me sukṛtaṃ tapaḥ), and if my subjects have been rightly protected (yadi guptāḥ prajāḥ samyak), then let my word come true (tadā satyaṃ vaco ’stu me)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

इक्ष्वाकुपरिभावित्वाद् वचो वक्ष्यामि राक्षस ।
यदि दत्तं यदि हुतं यदि मे सुकृतं तपः ।
यदि गुप्ताः प्रजाः सम्यक् तदा सत्यं वचोऽस्तु मे ॥ २९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

इक्ष्वाकुपरिभावित्वाद् वचो वक्ष्यामि राक्षस ।
यदि दत्तं यदि हुतं यदि मे सुकृतं तपः ।
यदि गुप्ताः प्रजाः सम्यक् तदा सत्यं वचोऽस्तु मे ॥ २९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘परंतु राक्षस! तूने अपने व्यङ्‍ग्यपूर्ण वचनसे इक्ष्वाकुकुलका अपमान किया है, इसलिये मैं तुझे शाप दूँगा—तेरे लिये अमङ्गलजनक बात कहूँगा । यदि मैंने दान, पुण्य, होम और तप किये हों, यदि मेरे द्वारा धर्मके अनुसार प्रजाजनोंका ठीक-ठीक पालन हुआ हो तो मेरी बात सत्य होकर रहे ॥ २९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आदरॆ राक्षसने! नीनु व्यङ्ग्यपूर्ण मातुगळिन्द इक्ष्वाकुकुलद अपमान माडिदॆ. इदरिन्द निनगॆ शाप कॊडुत्तिद्देनॆ. निनगॆ अमङ्गल मातन्नु हेळुत्तेनॆ. नानु दान, पुण्य, होम, तपस्सु माडिद्दरॆ, नानु धर्मक्कनुसार प्रजॆगळन्नु चॆन्नागि पालिसिदिद्दरॆ, नन्न मातु निजवागलि.॥29॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“In this very lineage of the high-souled Ikṣvākus (kule hy asminn ikṣvākūṇāṃ mahātmanām) there shall be born (utpatsyate) one named Rāma, son of Daśaratha (rāmo dāśarathir nāma), who will take away your life-breaths (yas te prāṇān hariṣyati)!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

उत्पत्स्यते कुले ह्यस्मिन्निक्ष्वाकूणां महात्मनाम् ।
रामो दाशरथिर्नाम स ते प्राणान् हरिष्यति ॥ ३० ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

उत्पत्स्यते कुले ह्यस्मिन्निक्ष्वाकूणां महात्मनाम् ।
रामो दाशरथिर्नाम स ते प्राणान् हरिष्यति ॥ ३० ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘महात्मा इक्ष्वाकुवंशी नरेशोंके इस वंशमें ही दशरथनन्दन श्रीराम प्रकट होंगे, जो तेरे प्राणोंका अपहरण करेंगे’ ॥ ३० ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

महात्मा इक्ष्वाकुवंशी राजर वंशदल्ले दशरथनन्दन श्रीरामनु प्रकटनागि निन्न प्राणगळन्नु अपहरिसुवनु.॥30॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thereupon, as that curse was pronounced (tasminn udāhṛte śāpe), the celestial kettledrum was beaten, booming loud as a raincloud (jaladharodagras tāḍito deva-dundubhiḥ), and a shower of blossoms fell from the sky (puṣpa-vṛṣṭiś ca khāc cyutā).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततो जलधरोदग्रस्ताडितो देवदुन्दुभिः ।
तस्मिन्नुदाहृते शापे पुष्पवृष्टिश्च खाच्च्युता ॥ ३१ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततो जलधरोदग्रस्ताडितो देवदुन्दुभिः ।
तस्मिन्नुदाहृते शापे पुष्पवृष्टिश्च खाच्च्युता ॥ ३१ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

राजाके इस प्रकार शाप देते ही मेघके समान गम्भीर स्वरमें देवताओंकी दुन्दुभि बज उठी और आकाशसे फूलोंकी वर्षा होने लगी ॥ ३१ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

राजनु हीगॆ शपिसिदाग मेघगळु गम्भीर ध्वनियल्लि देवतॆगळु दुन्दुभिगळन्नु नुडिसिदरु, आकाशदिन्द पुष्पवृष्टियायितु.॥31॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then that king, O lord of kings (rājendra), departed to the abode of heaven (gataḥ sthānaṃ triviṣṭapam); and when that monarch had ascended to heaven (svargate ca nṛpe tasmin), that rākṣasa withdrew (rākṣasaḥ so ’pasarpata).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततः स राजा राजेन्द्र गतः स्थानं त्रिविष्टपम् ।
स्वर्गते च नृपे तस्मिन् राक्षसः सोऽपसर्पत ॥ ३२ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततः स राजा राजेन्द्र गतः स्थानं त्रिविष्टपम् ।
स्वर्गते च नृपे तस्मिन् राक्षसः सोऽपसर्पत ॥ ३२ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

राजाधिराज श्रीराम! तदनन्तर राजा अनरण्य स्वर्गलोकको सिधारे । उनके स्वर्गगामी हो जानेपर राक्षस रावण वहाँसे अन्यत्र चला गया ॥ ३२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

राजाधिराज श्रीरामा! बळिक अनरण्यनु स्वर्गस्थनादनु. अवनु स्वर्गगामियाद मेलॆ राक्षस रावणनु अल्लिन्द बेरॆडॆगॆ तॆरळिदनु.॥32॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये उत्तरकाण्डे एकोनविंशः सर्गः ॥ १९ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके उत्तरकाण्डमें उन्नीसवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ १९ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद उत्तर काण्डदल्लि हत्तॊम्भत्तनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु. ॥19॥
