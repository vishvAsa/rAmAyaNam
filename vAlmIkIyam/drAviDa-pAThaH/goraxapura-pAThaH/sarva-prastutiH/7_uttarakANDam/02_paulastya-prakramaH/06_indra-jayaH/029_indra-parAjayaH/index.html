सर्वाष् टीकाः
भागसूचना
  1. रावणका देवसेनाके बीचसे होकर निकलना, देवताओंका उसे कैद करनेके लिये प्रयत्न, मेघनादका मायाद्वारा इन्द्रको बन्दी बनाना तथा विजयी होकर सेनासहित लङ्काको लौटना
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

As battle-born darkness swallows the sky, gods and rākṣasas slaughter one another blindly until nine-tenths of Rāvaṇa’s host lies slain; undazed amid the gloom, an enraged Rāvaṇa roars and commands his charioteer to drive straight through the divine ranks.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततस्तमसि सञ्जाते सर्वे ते देवराक्षसाः ।
अयुद्ध्यन्त बलोन्मत्ताः सूदयन्तः परस्परम् ॥ १ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततस्तमसि सञ्जाते सर्वे ते देवराक्षसाः ।
अयुद्ध्यन्त बलोन्मत्ताः सूदयन्तः परस्परम् ॥ १ ॥

ततस्तमसि सञ्जाते सर्वे ते देवराक्षसाः ।
आयुध्यन्त बलोन्मत्ताः सूदयन्तः परस्परम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

जब सब ओर अन्धकार छा गया, तब बलसे उन्मत्त हुए वे समस्त देवता और राक्षस एक-दूसरेको मारते हुए परस्पर युद्ध करने लगे ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

ऎल्लॆडॆ कत्तलु कविदाग बलोन्मत्तराद समस्त देवतॆगळु मत्तु राक्षघिसरु परस्पर हॊडॆदाडुत्ता युद्ध माडतॊडगिदरु.॥1॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then, when darkness had arisen (tatas tamasi sañjāte), all those gods and rākṣasas (sarve te deva-rākṣasāḥ), intoxicated with might (balonmattāḥ), fought on, slaughtering one another (sūdayantaḥ parasparam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततस्तु देवसैन्येन राक्षसानां बृहद् बलम् ।
दशांशं स्थापितं युद्धे शेषं नीतं यमक्षयम् ॥ २ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततस्तु देवसैन्येन राक्षसानां बृहद् बलम् ।
दशांशं स्थापितं युद्धे शेषं नीतं यमक्षयम् ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस समय देवताओंकी सेनाने राक्षसोंके विशाल सैन्य-समूहका केवल दसवाँ हिस्सा युद्धभूमिमें खड़ा रहने दिया । शेष सब राक्षसोंको यमलोक पहुँचा दिया ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग देवतॆगळ सैन्यवु राक्षसर विशाल सैन्यवन्नु केवल हत्तनॆ ऒन्दु भाग मात्र युद्धरङ्गदल्लि इरुवन्तॆ माडि, उळिद राक्षस सैन्यवन्नु यमसदनक्कॆ अट्टिदरु.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thereupon the vast army of the rākṣasas (rākṣasānāṃ bṛhad balam) was reduced by the divine host (devasainyena) to a single tenth left standing in battle (daśāṃśaṃ sthāpitaṃ yuddhe), while the rest were dispatched to Yama’s abode (śeṣaṃ nītaṃ yamakṣayam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तस्मिंस्तु तामसे युद्धे सर्वे ते देवराक्षसाः ।
अन्योन्यं नाभ्यजानन्त युद्ध्यमानाः परस्परम् ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तस्मिंस्तु तामसे युद्धे सर्वे ते देवराक्षसाः ।
अन्योन्यं नाभ्यजानन्त युद्ध्यमानाः परस्परम् ॥ ३ ॥

तस्मिंस्तु तामसे युद्धे सर्वे ते देवराक्षसाः ।
अन्योन्यं नाभ्यजानन्त युद्धमानाः परस्परम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस तामस युद्धमें समस्त देवता और राक्षस परस्पर जूझते हुए एक-दूसरेको पहचान नहीं पाते थे ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आ तामस युद्धदल्लि समस्त देवतॆगळु मत्तु राक्षसरु परस्पर कादाडुत्ता ऒब्बरु मत्तॊब्बरन्नु गुरुतिसदे होदरु.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

In that darkened battle (tasmiṃs tu tāmase yuddhe), all those gods and rākṣasas, fighting one another (yudhyamānāḥ parasparam), could no longer recognize each other (anyonyaṃ nābhyajānanta).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

इन्द्रश्च रावणश्चैव रावणिश्च महाबलः ।
तस्मिंस्तमोजालवृते मोहमीयुर्न ते त्रयः ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

इन्द्रश्च रावणश्चैव रावणिश्च महाबलः ।
तस्मिंस्तमोजालवृते मोहमीयुर्न ते त्रयः ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

इन्द्र, रावण और रावणपुत्र महाबली मेघनाद—ये तीन ही उस अन्धकाराच्छन्न समराङ्गणमें मोहित नहीं हुए थे ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

इन्द्र, रावण मत्तु महाबली मेघनाद ई मूवरू मात्र अन्धकार तुम्बिद समराङ्गणदल्लि मोहितरागिरलिल्ल.॥4॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Indra, Rāvaṇa, and the mighty Rāvaṇi (indraś ca rāvaṇaś caiva rāvaṇiś ca mahābalaḥ)—those three alone did not succumb to bewilderment (moham īyur na te trayaḥ) amid that enveloping net of darkness (tasmiṃs tamo-jāla-vṛte).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स तु दृष्ट्वा बलं सर्वं रावणो निहतं क्षणात् ।
क्रोधमभ्यगमत् तीव्रं महानादं च मुक्तवान् ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स तु दृष्ट्वा बलं सर्वं रावणो निहतं क्षणात् ।
क्रोधमभ्यगमत् तीव्रं महानादं च मुक्तवान् ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

रावणने देखा, मेरी सारी सेना क्षणभरमें मारी गयी, तब उसके मनमें बड़ा क्रोध हुआ और उसने बड़ी भारी गर्जना की ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

तन्न सैन्यवु क्षणदल्लि हतरागिरुवुदन्नु रावणनु नोडिदाग मनस्सिनल्लि भारी क्रोध उण्टागि जोरागि गर्जिसिदनु.॥5॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Beholding almost his entire army cut down in an instant (dṛṣṭvā balaṃ sarvaṃ … nihataṃ kṣaṇāt), Rāvaṇa fell into fierce wrath (krodham abhyagamat tīvram) and let loose a thunderous roar (mahānādaṃ ca muktavān).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

क्रोधात् सूतं च दुर्धर्षः स्यन्दनस्थमुवाच ह ।
परसैन्यस्य मध्येन यावदन्तो नयस्व माम् ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

क्रोधात् सूतं च दुर्धर्षः स्यन्दनस्थमुवाच ह ।
परसैन्यस्य मध्येन यावदन्तो नयस्व माम् ॥ ६ ॥

क्रोधात्सूतं च दुर्धषः स्यन्दनस्थमुवाच ह ।
परसैन्यस्य मध्येन यावदन्तो नयस्व माम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस दुर्जय निशाचरने रथपर बैठे हुए अपने सारथिसे क्रोधपूर्वक कहा—‘सूत! शत्रुओंकी इस सेनाका जहाँतक अन्त है, वहाँतक तुम इस सेनाके मध्यभागसे होकर मुझे ले चलो ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आ दुर्जय निशाचरनु रथदल्लि कुळित्तिद्द सारथियल्लि सिट्टिनिन्द हेळिद - सारथि ! शत्रुगळ ई सैन्यद मध्यभागक्कॆ नन्नन्नु करॆदुकॊण्डु होगु.॥6॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

In fury, the unassailable king addressed his charioteer standing upon the car (sūtaṃ … syandanastham uvāca ha): “Drive me right through the midst of the enemy host all the way to its end (parasainyasya madhyena yāvad anto nayasva mām)!

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Boasting that he will slay the thirty gods and their four world-guardians to reign supreme, Rāvaṇa rebukes his hesitant charioteer and bids him drive from the Nandana grove to Mount Udaya; seeing his resolve, Indra addresses the assembled gods.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अद्यैतान् त्रिदशान् सर्वान् विक्रमैः समरे स्वयम् ।
नानाशस्त्रमहासारैर्नयामि यमसादनम् ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अद्यैतान् त्रिदशान् सर्वान् विक्रमैः समरे स्वयम् ।
नानाशस्त्रमहासारैर्नयामि यमसादनम् ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘आज मैं स्वयं अपने पराक्रमद्वारा नाना प्रकारके शस्त्रोंकी मूसलाधार वृष्टि करके इन सब देवताओंको यमलोक पहुँचा दूँगा ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

इन्दु नानु स्वतः नन्न पराक्रमदिन्द नाना रीतिय अस्त्रगळ मळॆ सुरिसि ई ऎल्ल देवतॆगळन्नु यमलोकक्कॆ अट्टिबिडुवॆनु.॥7॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Today, by my own prowess in battle (vikramaiḥ samare svayam), I shall dispatch all these thirty gods (tridaśān sarvān) to Yama’s mansion (nayāmi yamasādanam) with manifold dreadful weapons (nānā-śastrair mahāghoraiḥ)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अहमिन्द्रं वधिष्यामि धनदं वरुणं यमम् ।
त्रिदशान् विनिहत्याशु स्वयं स्थास्याम्यथोपरि ॥ ८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अहमिन्द्रं वधिष्यामि धनदं वरुणं यमम् ।
त्रिदशान् विनिहत्याशु स्वयं स्थास्याम्यथोपरि ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘मैं इन्द्र, कुबेर, वरुण और यमका भी वध करूँगा । सब देवताओंका शीघ्र ही संहार करके स्वयं सबके ऊपर स्थित होऊँगा ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

नानु इन्द्र, वरुण, यमनन्नु वधिसिबिडुवॆ. देवतॆगळॆल्लरन्नु बेगने वधिसि स्वतः ऎल्लरिगिन्त मेलॆ स्थितनागुवॆनु.॥8॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“I shall slay Indra, Dhanada, Varuṇa, and Yama (aham indraṃ vadhiṣyāmi dhanadaṃ varuṇaṃ yamam); having swiftly destroyed the thirty gods, I myself shall stand supreme above all (svayaṃ sthāsyāmy athopari)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

विषादो नैव कर्तव्यः शीघ्रं वाहय मे रथम् ।
द्विः खलु त्वां ब्रवीम्यद्य यावदन्तं नयस्व माम् ॥ ९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

विषादो नैव कर्तव्यः शीघ्रं वाहय मे रथम् ।
द्विः खलु त्वां ब्रवीम्यद्य यावदन्तं नयस्व माम् ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘तुम्हें विषाद नहीं करना चाहिये । शीघ्र मेरे रथको ले चलो । मैं तुमसे दो बार कहता हूँ, देवताओंकी सेनाका जहाँतक अन्त है, वहाँतक मुझे अभी ले चलो ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

विषादपडबेड, बेगने रथवन्नु नडॆसु. निनगॆ पुनः हेळुत्तिद्देनॆ - देवतॆगळ सैन्यद कॊनॆ इरुववरॆगॆ नन्नन्नु ईगले कॊण्डुहोगु.॥9॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Give way to no dejection—drive my chariot swiftly (viṣādo na ca kartavyaḥ śīghraṃ vāhaya me ratham)! Twice now indeed I tell you today: drive me all the way to the army’s end (dviḥ khalu tvāṃ bravīmy adya yāvad anto nayasva mām)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अयं स नन्दनोद्देशो यत्र वर्तावहे वयम् ।
नय मामद्य तत्र त्वमुदयो यत्र पर्वतः ॥ १० ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अयं स नन्दनोद्देशो यत्र वर्तावहे वयम् ।
नय मामद्य तत्र त्वमुदयो यत्र पर्वतः ॥ १० ॥

अयं स नन्दनोद्देशो यत्र वर्तामहे वयम् ।
नयमामद्य तत्र त्वमुदयो यत्र पर्वतः ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘यह नन्दनवनका प्रदेश है, जहाँ इस समय हम दोनों मौजूद हैं । यहींसे देवताओंकी सेनाका आरम्भ होता है । अब तुम मुझे उस स्थानतक ले चलो, जहाँ उदयाचल है (नन्दनवनसे उदयाचलतक देवताओंकी सेना फैली हुई है)’ ॥ १० ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

ईग नावु नन्दनवन प्रदेशदल्लि इद्देवॆ. इल्लिन्दले देवतॆगळ सैन्य प्रारम्भवागुत्तदॆ. ईग नीनु उदयाचलक्कॆ कॊण्डुहोगु. (नन्दनवनदिन्द उदयाचलदवरॆगॆ देवतॆगळ सैन्य हरडित्तु.॥10॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“This here is the region of Nandana where we now stand (ayaṃ sa nandanoddeśo yatra vartāmahe vayam); drive me today yonder to where Mount Udaya rises (naya mām adya tatra tvam udayo yatra parvataḥ)!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तस्य तद् वचनं श्रुत्वा तुरगान् स मनोजवान् ।
आदिदेशाथ शत्रूणां मध्येनैव च सारथिः ॥ ११ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तस्य तद् वचनं श्रुत्वा तुरगान् स मनोजवान् ।
आदिदेशाथ शत्रूणां मध्येनैव च सारथिः ॥ ११ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

रावणकी यह बात सुनकर सारथिने मनके समान वेगशाली घोड़ोंको शत्रुसेनाके बीचसे हाँक दिया ॥ ११ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रावणन मातन्नु केळि सारथियु मनोवेगदन्तिरुव कुदुरॆगळन्नु शत्रुसैन्यद नडुविनिन्द ओडिसिदनु.॥11॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Hearing those words of his, the charioteer urged his mind-swift steeds (turagān sa manojavān ādideśa) straight through the very midst of the foes (śatrūṇāṃ madhyenaiva ca sārathiḥ).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

7.29.1–3 (Slaughter in the Arrow-Born Darkness)

Resuming directly from the end of Sarga 28—where Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explained that the dense arrow-showers of Indra and Rāvaṇa shrouded all directions in darkness—Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) opens this canto as the sequel to that blackening of the sky (तमआवरणानन्तरकालिकं वृत्तान्तमाह). Rāma Varmā analyzes देवराक्षसाः as a straightforward dvandva (देवाश्च राक्षसाश्च). Even though Parimal brackets 7.29.2–3 as Govindarāja’s expansion, their dramatic weight is decisive: the gods annihilate nine-tenths of Laṅkā’s host before blindness engulfs both armies.

7.29.4 (Three Unshaken Titans Amid the Gloom)

Against the chaos of mutually blinding warriors, Vālmīki isolates the three supreme protagonists of the canto—Indra, Rāvaṇa, and Rāvaṇi—as the sole fighters immune to confusion (इन्द्रादयस्त्रय एव मोहं नेयुः, Śivasahāya). This deliberate triad foreshadows the canto’s structural arc: while Rāvaṇa and Indra are locked in an indecisive clash of titanic pride, the quiet inclusion of Rāvaṇi (mahābalaḥ) among the undazed trio anticipates his decisive intervention once his father is cornered.

7.29.5–6 (Rāvaṇa’s Roar and Reckless Command)

Seeing nearly his whole host obliterated in a flash, Rāvaṇa unleashes a terrible roar (Śivasahāya) and orders his charioteer to plunge straight through the center of the divine host to its farthest limit. All three commentators resolve the elliptical Southern reading यावदन्तः identically as a correlative clause: ‘as far as the end of the enemy army extends, drive me through its midst’ (परसैन्यस्यान्तो यावत्तावन्मां परसेनामध्यमार्गेण नयस्व, Rāma Varmā; echoed by Govindarāja and Śivasahāya).

7.29.7–8 (The Brāhmaṇa-Vasiṣṭha Maxim in Rāvaṇa’s Vaunt)

Having vowed to send ‘all thirty gods’ to Yama’s hall in 7.29.7 (where Śivasahāya reads महासाराणि for the weapons), why does Rāvaṇa immediately repeat that he will slay Indra, Kubera, Varuṇa, and Yama? Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) resolves the apparent redundancy through a celebrated scholastic rule—the brāhmaṇa-vasiṣṭha-nyāya (ब्राह्मणवसिष्ठन्यायेन न पूर्वेण पौनरुक्त्यम्), whereby naming the foremost members after the whole group highlights their eminence. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss अथोपरि as Rāvaṇa’s boast to reign supreme ‘above all beings’ (सर्वेषामुपरिष्टादित्यर्थः).

7.29.9 (Rebuking the Reluctant Charioteer)

Why does Rāvaṇa suddenly tell his driver not to despair and remind him ‘Twice now I tell you’? Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja reconstruct the unspoken dramatic beat: seeing his charioteer falter in dread at the prospect of plunging into the heart of the divine host (देवसेनामध्ये प्रवेशे किं भविष्यतीति विषण्णं सारथिम्), Rāvaṇa repeats his order sharply, implying ‘why then do you not drive?’ (तत्कथं न नयसीति शेषः). Śivasahāya concurs that यावदन्तम् means all the way to the enemy host’s farthest edge (रिपुसेनान्तपर्यन्तम्).

7.29.10–12 (A Battlefield Stretching from Nandana to Mount Udaya)

Because the bounds of the measureless celestial army are impossible to discern (अपरिमितदेवसेनाया अन्तस्य दुर्ज्ञेयत्वात्, Rāma Varmā), Rāvaṇa defines its cosmic scale in 7.29.10. All three commentators unpack his geography identically: the heavenly Nandana grove where they stand is the army’s vanguard (सेनाया आदिः), while the eastern Sunrise Mountain (Udaya) marks its rear limit (उदयपर्वतो यत्र … सोऽन्तो देवसेनायाः, Govindarāja and Śivasahāya). When the charioteer obeys and urges the horses straight into the enemy ranks (चालयामास, Śivasahāya), Indra discerns Rāvaṇa’s suicidal charge and rallies the gods.

7.29.13–15 (The Strategy of Capture Over Slaughter)

Faced with Rāvaṇa’s tidal onslaught—vividly compared in 7.29.14 to an ocean swelling at full or new moon (parvaṇi) as he charges on his wind-powered chariot—Indra proposes seizing him alive. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) frames 7.29.15 as answering the gods’ unspoken question, ‘Why not simply kill him?’ (ननु वध्यतामेव किमर्थं निगृह्यते): because Brahmā’s boons render him invulnerable to deities (varadānāt sunirbhayaḥ), lethal force is futile, so the gods must stay alert (यत्ता उद्यताः) to capture him.

7.29.16 (The Precedent of Bali’s Confinement)

Indra justifies non-lethal containment by citing the mythic precedent of the asura sovereign Bali. All three commentators identify बलौ as the great Daitya Bali (बलिदैत्ये, Rāma Varmā; महाबलौ, Govindarāja). Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) pinpoints the exact parallel: just as Indra enjoys cosmic sovereignty because Bali was confined without being slain (अहत्वैव कृतनिरोधे सति), so confining Rāvaṇa will neutralize his threat. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) underscores Indra’s kingly motive: such restraint appeals to him as his duty for protecting the three worlds (त्रैलोक्यरक्षार्थं मम कर्तव्यत्वेन रोचते).

7.29.17–18 (Tactical Disengagement and the Dual Flanking Charge)

Why does Indra leave Rāvaṇa’s direct front (रावणं सन्त्यज्य) in 7.29.17? Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja explain the battlefield logic identically: since Rāvaṇa cannot be struck down even by the thunderbolt (vajra), standing head-to-head before him serves no purpose (वज्रपातादिना प्रहर्तुमशक्यत्वात्पुरःस्थितिर्निष्प्रयोजनेत्याशयेन). Moving to another sector (रावणावस्थितदेशादन्यं देशम्), Indra ravages the rākṣasa ranks from the south while the relentless Rāvaṇa (अनिवर्तकः, Tilaka) bores into the divine host (देवसेनाम्) from the north.

7.29.19–21 (Indra’s Counter-Encirclement and the Rākṣasa Cry)

Rāvaṇa carves a hundred yojanas into the celestial lines, yet Indra refuses to panic (asambhrāntaḥ). Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explains 7.29.20 as Indra surrounding Daśānana with his troops and forcing him back (स्वसेनया दशाननं समावृत्य न्यवर्तयत्), while Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) notes that his calm encirclement explains how he repelled Rāvaṇa; Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) adds that Indra simultaneously rallied his scattered host (दशाननं समावृत्य बलं न्यवर्तयत्). Seeing Rāvaṇa engulfed (grastam) by Śakra, the demons raise the despairing wail हा हताः स्म (‘Alas, we are ruined!’).

7.29.22–23 (Rāvaṇi’s Entry and Deployment of Śiva’s Tāmasī Illusion)

Stung by that cry (नादश्रवणानन्तरम्, Śivasahāya), Meghanāda surges into the divine army (देवसैन्यम्, Govindarāja). Why does he immediately invoke illusion? Rāma Varmā pinpoints his sober battlefield calculus: realizing the divine host cannot be overcome by open, face-to-face fighting (पुरःस्थित्या देवसेनाजयस्याशक्यत्वान्मायां प्रयुङ्क्ते), he deploys the Tāmasī illusion won earlier from Śiva (गोपतिना शिवेन, Govindarāja; recalling Sarga 25.9–11). Both Tilaka and Śiromaṇi gloss समभिद्रवत् causatively as समभ्यद्रावयत् (‘put the host to flight’).

7.29.24 (The Invisible Predator Stalks the King of Heaven)

Disdaining lesser targets, Rāvaṇi bypasses the entire pantheon to hunt Śakra alone (शक्रमेवाभ्यधावत, Śivasahāya). Vālmīki’s abrupt closing half-line—‘and glorious Mahendra could not see his enemy’s son’—signals the decisive shift in the war: the boon granted by Paśupati in 7.25.11 (‘neither gods nor asuras can discern his movements’) is now fulfilled before the audience’s eyes, stripping Indra of his supreme visual and martial advantage.

7.29.25–27 (Fearless Endurance and Indra’s Turn to Airāvata)

Verse 7.29.25 presents an idiomatic ellipsis—‘Rāvaṇi did not make anything’ (न चकार च किञ्चन). Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) supply the understood noun भयम् (‘fear’): even battered by the gods and stripped of his mail, he felt no terror whatsoever (किमपि भयमित्यर्थः). Disabling Indra’s charioteer Mātali (इन्द्रस्य सारथिम्, Śivasahāya) and smothering Mahendra in arrows, Rāvaṇi forces Indra to abandon his car, dismiss Mātali, and mount his celestial elephant Airāvata to hunt his unseen assailant.

7.29.28–29 (Binding the King of Heaven in Illusion)

From the sky, the invisible prince wraps Indra in a web of illusion (मायापरिक्षिप्तं कृत्वा) and pelts him with arrows (Śivasahāya) until the divine sovereign wears down. Watching for the exact moment of exhaustion (pariśrāntam), Rāvaṇi binds Indra with māyā and drags him away—with both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) glossing स्वसैन्यमभितः specifically as ‘to the side/flank of his own army’ (स्वसैन्यपार्श्वम् / स्वसेनायाः पार्श्वे), sealing the feat that will earn him the title Indrajit.

7.29.30 (The Immortals’ Consternation)

Beholding their king led away by force from the battlefield, the gods are struck with stunned helplessness. Śivasahāya unpacks their anxious question किं नु स्यात् as practical bewilderment: ‘What must we do now?’ (अस्माभिः कर्तव्यमित्यर्थः). Notably, Rāma Varmā’s commentary lemma reflects the variant reading एतत्किं न्विति चुक्रुशुः (‘they cried out, “What is this?”’, matching Baroda CE 7.29.29d), capturing the shocked outcry rippling through the celestial ranks at the unthinkable abduction of Indra.

7.29.31–33 (Indra’s Counter-Lore Defeated and Rāvaṇa Overwhelmed)

Explaining why Indra’s capture astounds the gods in 7.29.31, all three commentators gloss विद्यावानपि specifically as ‘possessed of the sacred lore that shatters asura illusions’ (असुरमायासंहारविद्यावानपि, Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja; आसुरमायाविघातकविद्यायुक्तोऽपि, Śivasahāya)—proving that Paśupati’s Tāmasī transcends ordinary demonic sorcery. Meanwhile, verses 32–33 set up a stark ironic reversal: even as his son captures heaven’s king, Rāvaṇa himself is battered by the Ādityas and Vasus until he can fight no more—answering Rāma’s opening question in Sarga 1 about why the son surpassed the father.

7.29.34 (Why Rāvaṇi Speaks While Remaining Invisible)

Though the printed text shows युद्धे दर्शनस्थः without an avagraha, all three commentators read युद्धेऽदर्शनस्थः (‘standing concealed from sight’, अन्तर्हित एवाब्रवीत्, Rāma Varmā) and uncover a striking tactical subtlety: why did Rāvaṇi stay invisible even while addressing his own father? Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja explain: ‘for fear that if he appeared in view, Mahendra himself would destroy him’ (दर्शने सति महेन्द्र एव नाशयेदिति भावः), proving Indra’s terrifying latent power even in captivity.

7.29.35–36 (Declaring Victory Won and the Gods’ Pride Broken)

Addressing his bruised father with tender filial reassurance, Rāvaṇi calls off the fight (रणकर्म निवर्तताम्, ‘let battle end’, Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja). Both Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa point out the grammatical precision of जितं नः as an impersonal neuter participle (भावे निष्ठा, ‘victory is achieved by us’). All three commentators read देवबलात् in 7.29.36 as ‘seized right out of the divine army’ (देवसेनायाः), proving that with their king captive, the gods’ pride is broken.

7.29.37–38 (Cessation of Battle and the Gods’ Retreat)

Rāvaṇi’s closing logic is crisp and pragmatic: with the enemy captured, further exertion is wasted (युद्धं निष्फलमतो वृथा श्रमेण किं निवर्ततामित्यर्थः, Śivasahāya). Instantly the divine hosts break off combat and depart (स्वं स्वं स्थानम्, Śivasahāya), while Daśānana—hearing his son’s announcement—regains complete composure (svasthacetāḥ). Notably, the Southern reading preserved by Govindarāja shifts smoothly from the retreating gods in 38ab to Rāvaṇa’s quiet relief in 38cd.

7.29.39–40 (Lyric Meter and a Father’s Exultant Praise)

Switching from Anuṣṭubh to resonant lyric cadences to celebrate heaven’s downfall, Vālmīki depicts Rāvaṇa freed from the anguish of battle (विगतरणज्वरः, Govindarāja). Overflowing with pride, Rāvaṇa hails his son’s triumph as worthy of their race’s supreme valor (सदृशैः अस्मत्कुलोचितैः, Śivasahāya), acknowledging openly that it was his son—not himself—who vanquished peerless Indra and elevated the Paulastya line’s prestige.

7.29.41–42 (The Triumphal Return to Laṅkā)

Rāvaṇa gives his son the honor of leading the triumphal procession, bidding him place Vāsava atop his chariot and march to Laṅkā (नगरं लङ्कां नय, Śivasahāya) while Rāvaṇa himself follows behind with his counselors—an extraordinary public deference from the ten-headed monarch. Returning in splendor with his captive king, Rāvaṇi dismisses his battle-worn rākṣasas to their respective homes (स्वस्वभवनं प्रति, Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja), setting the stage for Brahmā’s embassy in Sarga 30.

स्वसुतवचनमादृतः प्रियं तत्
समनुनिशम्य जगाद चैव सूनुम् ॥ ३९ ॥

अथ रणविगतः स उत्तवौजा-
स्त्रिदशरिपुः प्रथितो निशाचरेन्द्रः ।
स्वसुतवचनमादृतः प्रियं तत्
समनुनिशम्य जगादचैव सूनुम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

अपने पुत्रके उस प्रिय वचनको आदरपूर्वक सुनकर महान् बलशाली देवद्रोही तथा सुविख्यात राक्षसराज रावण युद्धसे निवृत्त हो गया और अपने बेटेसे बोला— ॥ ३९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

तन्न मगन प्रिय मातन्नु केळिद महा बलशाली देवद्रोही, सुविख्यात राक्षसराज रावणनु युद्धदिन्द निवृत्तनागि मेघनादनल्लि हेळिदनु.॥39॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then that lord, sovereign of night-roamers, his fever of battle dispelled (raṇa-vigata-jvaraḥ), having attained victory (vijayam avāpya), set out rejoicing toward his own city (svabhavanam abhito jagāma hṛṣṭaḥ), and approaching his son spoke these words:

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अतिबलसदृशैः पराक्रमैस्त्वं
मम कुलवंशविवर्धनः प्रभो ।
यदयमतुल्यबलस्त्वयाद्य वै
त्रिदशपतिस्त्रिदशाश्च निर्जिताः ॥ ४० ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अतिबलसदृशैः पराक्रमैस्त्वं
मम कुलवंशविवर्धनः प्रभो ।
यदयमतुल्यबलस्त्वयाद्य वै
त्रिदशपतिस्त्रिदशाश्च निर्जिताः ॥ ४० ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘सामर्थ्यशाली पुत्र! अपने अत्यन्त बलके अनुरूप पराक्रम प्रकट करके आज तुमने जो इन अनुपम बलशाली देवराज इन्द्रको जीता और देवताओंको भी परास्त किया है, इससे यह निश्चय हो गया कि तुम मेरे कुल और वंशके यश और सम्मानकी वृद्धि करनेवाले हो ॥ ४० ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

सामर्थ्यशालि पुत्रने! तन्न बलक्कनुरूप अत्यन्त पराक्रम प्रकटिसि इन्दु नीनु ई अनुपम बलशाली देवेन्द्रनन्नु गॆद्दु, देवतॆगळन्नु सोलिसिबिट्टिरुवॆ. नीनु नन्न कुल मत्तु वंशद कीर्तियन्नु वृद्धिपडिसुववनु ऎम्बुदु निश्चयवायितु.॥40॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“By those deeds of prowess worthy of your exceeding might (atibala-sadṛśaiḥ parākramais taiḥ), you have exalted the honor of my lineage (mama kula-māna-vivardhanaṃ kṛtam), inasmuch as this peerless lord of the thirty gods and the gods besides have today been vanquished by you (tridaśapatis tridaśāś ca nirjitāḥ)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

नय रथमधिरोप्य वासवं
नगरमितो व्रज सेनया वृतस्त्वम् ।
अहमपि तव पृष्ठतो द्रुतं
सह सचिवैरनुयामि हृष्टवत् ॥ ४१ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

नय रथमधिरोप्य वासवं
नगरमितो व्रज सेनया वृतस्त्वम् ।
अहमपि तव पृष्ठतो द्रुतं
सह सचिवैरनुयामि हृष्टवत् ॥ ४१ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘बेटा! इन्द्रको रथपर बैठाकर तुम सेनाके साथ यहाँसे लङ्कापुरीको चलो! मैं भी अपने मन्त्रियोंके साथ शीघ्र ही प्रसन्नतापूर्वक तुम्हारे पीछे-पीछे आ रहा हूँ’ ॥ ४१ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मगु! इन्द्रनन्नु रथदल्लि कुळ्ळिरिसिकॊण्डु नीनु सैन्यदॊन्दिगॆ लङ्कॆगॆ तॆरळु. नानू कूड मन्त्रिगळॊन्दिगॆ सन्तोषवागि बेगने निन्न हिन्दॆ-हिन्दॆ बरुवॆनु.॥41॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Mounting Vāsava upon your chariot, lead him away and march hence to the city surrounded by the army (naya ratham adhiropya vāsavaṃ nagaram ito vraja senayā vṛtas tvam)! I too shall swiftly follow behind you as you go, together with my ministers (saha sacivair anuyāmi pṛṣṭhataḥ)!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अथ स बलवृतः सवाहन-
स्त्रिदशपतिं परिगृह्य रावणिः ।
स्वभवनमधिगम्य वीर्यवान्
कृतसमरान् विससर्ज राक्षसान् ॥ ४२ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अथ स बलवृतः सवाहन-
स्त्रिदशपतिं परिगृह्य रावणिः ।
स्वभवनमधिगम्य वीर्यवान्
कृतसमरान् विससर्ज राक्षसान् ॥ ४२ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

पिताकी यह आज्ञा पाकर पराक्रमी रावणकुमार मेघनाद देवराजको साथ ले सेना और सवारियोंसहित अपने निवासस्थानको लौटा । वहाँ पहुँचकर उसने युद्धमें भाग लेनेवाले निशाचरोंको विदा कर दिया ॥ ४२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

तन्दॆय मातन्नु केळि रावणकुमार मेघनादनु देव राजनन्नु जॊतॆयल्लि करॆदुकॊण्डु सैन्य, वाहनगळॊन्दिगॆ लङ्कॆगॆ तॆरळिदनु. अल्लिगॆ होगि युद्धदल्लि भागिगळाद निशाचररन्नु बीळ्कॊट्टनु.॥42॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये उत्तरकाण्डे एकोनत्रिंशः सर्गः ॥ २९ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके उत्तरकाण्डमें उन्तीसवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ २९ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद उत्तर काण्डदल्लि इप्पत्तॊम्भत्तनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु.॥29॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then that valiant Rāvaṇi, surrounded by his host and vehicles (balavṛtaḥ savāhanaḥ), taking the lord of the thirty gods captive (tridaśapatiṃ parigṛhya), returned to his own abode and dismissed the rākṣasas who had fought the battle (kṛtasamarān visasarja rākṣasān).
