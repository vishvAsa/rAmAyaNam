सर्वाष् टीकाः
भागसूचना
  1. सेनासहित रावणका इन्द्रलोकपर आक्रमण, इन्द्रकी भगवान् विष्णुसे सहायताके लिये प्रार्थना, भविष्यमें रावण-वधकी प्रतिज्ञा करके विष्णुका इन्द्रको लौटाना, देवताओं और राक्षसोंका युद्ध तथा वसुके द्वारा सुमालीका वध
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Crossing over Kailāsa to heaven’s realm, Rāvaṇa sends an ocean-like roar ahead of his army; Indra summons the celestial hosts to arm, yet himself approaches Viṣṇu in dejection and dread.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

कैलासं लङ्घयित्वा तु ससैन्यबलवाहनः ।
आससाद महातेजा इन्द्रलोकं दशाननः ॥ १ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

कैलासं लङ्घयित्वा तु ससैन्यबलवाहनः ।
आससाद महातेजा इन्द्रलोकं दशाननः ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

कैलास-पर्वतको पार करके महातेजस्वी दशमुख रावण सेना और सवारियोंके साथ इन्द्रलोकमें जा पहुँचा ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

महातेजस्वी दशानननु सैन्य-बलवाहगळॊडनॆ कैलास पर्वतवन्नु दाटि इन्द्रलोकक्कॆ होदनु.॥1॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then, having crossed over Kailāsa (kailāsaṃ laṅghayitvātha), that ten-necked night-roamer Rāvaṇa (daśagrīvaḥ sa rāvaṇaḥ … niśācaraḥ), of mighty splendor (mahātejāḥ), reached the world of Indra (āsasāda … indralokam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तस्य राक्षससैन्यस्य समन्तादुपयास्यतः ।
देवलोके बभौ शब्दो भिद्यमानार्णवोपमः ॥ २ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तस्य राक्षससैन्यस्य समन्तादुपयास्यतः ।
देवलोके बभौ शब्दो भिद्यमानार्णवोपमः ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

सब ओरसे आती हुई राक्षस-सेनाका कोलाहल देवलोकमें ऐसा जान पड़ता था, मानो महासागरके मथे जानेका शब्द प्रकट हो रहा हो ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

देवलोकदल्लि ऎल्लॆडॆ केळि बरुत्तिद्द रावणन सैन्यद कोलाहलवु समुद्रवन्नु कडॆयुव शब्ददन्तॆ केळिबरुत्तित्तु.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

The uproar of that rākṣasa army pressing onward from every side (samantād upayāsyataḥ) reached the world of the gods (devalokaṃ yayau śabdaḥ), resembling an ocean being churned (mathyamānārṇavopamaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

श्रुत्वा तु रावणं प्राप्तमिन्द्रश्चलित आसनात् ।
देवानथाब्रवीत् तत्र सर्वानेव समागतान् ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

श्रुत्वा तु रावणं प्राप्तमिन्द्रश्चलित आसनात् ।
देवानथाब्रवीत् तत्र सर्वानेव समागतान् ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

रावणका आगमन सुनकर इन्द्र अपने आसनसे उठ गये और अपने पास आये हुए समस्त देवताओंसे बोले— ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रावणन आगमनवन्नु केळि देवेन्द्रनु आसनदिन्द ऎद्दु, तन्न बळिगॆ बन्द समस्त देवतॆगळल्लि हेळिदनु.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Hearing that Rāvaṇa had arrived (śrutvā tu rāvaṇaṃ prāptam), Indra rose startled from his throne (indraś calita āsanāt) and spoke there to all those gods who had assembled (tān devān sarvān eva samāgatān)—

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

आदित्यांश्च वसून् रुद्रान् साध्यांश्च समरुद‍्गणान् ।
सज्जा भवत युद्धार्थं रावणस्य दुरात्मनः ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

आदित्यांश्च वसून् रुद्रान् साध्यांश्च समरुद‍्गणान् ।
सज्जा भवत युद्धार्थं रावणस्य दुरात्मनः ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उन्होंने आदित्यों, वसुओं, रुद्रों, साध्यों तथा मरुद‍्गणोंसे भी कहा—‘तुम सब लोग दुरात्मा रावणके साथ युद्ध करनेके लिये तैयार हो जाओ’ ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अवनु आदित्यरिगॆ, वसुगळिगॆ, रुद्ररिगॆ, साध्य मरुद्गणरिगू - नीवॆल्लरू दुरात्मा रावणनॊन्दिगॆ युद्धमाडलु सिद्धरागिरि ऎन्दु तिळिसिदनु.॥4॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

unto the Ādityas together with the Vasus (ādityān sa-vasūn), the Rudras, the Viśvedevas (viśvān), the Sādhyas, and the hosts of Maruts (marudgaṇān)—saying: “Make ready for battle (sajjībhavata yuddhārtham) against the wicked-souled Rāvaṇa (rāvaṇasya durātmanaḥ)!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

एवमुक्तास्तु शक्रेण देवाः शक्रसमा युधि ।
सन्नह्य सुमहासत्त्वा युद्धश्रद्धासमन्विताः ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

एवमुक्तास्तु शक्रेण देवाः शक्रसमा युधि ।
सन्नह्य सुमहासत्त्वा युद्धश्रद्धासमन्विताः ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

इन्द्रके ऐसा कहनेपर युद्धमें उन्हींके समान पराक्रम प्रकट करनेवाले महाबली देवता कवच आदि धारण करके युद्धके लिये उत्सुक हो गये ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

इन्द्रनु हीगॆ हेळिदाग युद्धदल्लि पराक्रमवन्नु तोरुव महाबलि देवतॆगळु कवचादि धरिसि युद्धक्कागि उत्सुकरादरु.॥5॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thus addressed by Śakra (evam uktās tu śakreṇa), those gods of immense mettle (sumahāsattvāḥ), equals of Śakra in battle (śakra-samā yudhi), buckled on their armor (sannahya) and stood filled with ardent eagerness for combat (yuddha-śraddhā-samanvitāḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स तु दीनः परित्रस्तो महेन्द्रो रावणं प्रति ।
विष्णोः समीपमागत्य वाक्यमेतदुवाच ह ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स तु दीनः परित्रस्तो महेन्द्रो रावणं प्रति ।
विष्णोः समीपमागत्य वाक्यमेतदुवाच ह ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

देवराज इन्द्रको रावणसे भय हो गया था । अतः वे दुःखी हो भगवान् विष्णुके पास आये और इस प्रकार बोले— ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

देवेन्द्रनिगॆ रावणनिन्द भयवित्तु, आद्दरिन्द अवनु दुःखितनागि भगवान् विष्णुविन बळिगॆ होगि इन्तॆन्दनु.॥6॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

But Mahendra himself, dejected and sorely terrified on account of Rāvaṇa (dīnaḥ paritrasto mahendro rāvaṇaṃ prati), approached the presence of Viṣṇu (viṣṇoḥ samīpam āgatya) and spoke these words:

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Confessing that Rāvaṇa’s might rests solely on Brahmā’s inviolable boon, Indra recalls past victories won through Viṣṇu’s strength and extols Nārāyaṇa as sole creator, protector, and ultimate refuge of the three worlds.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

विष्णो कथं करिष्यामि रावणं राक्षसं प्रति ।
अहोऽतिबलवद् रक्षो युद्धार्थमभिवर्तते ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

विष्णो कथं करिष्यामि रावणं राक्षसं प्रति ।
अहोऽतिबलवद् रक्षो युद्धार्थमभिवर्तते ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘विष्णुदेव! मैं राक्षस रावणके लिये क्या करूँ? अहो! वह अत्यन्त बलशाली निशाचर मेरे साथ युद्ध करनेके लिये आ रहा है ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

महाविष्णुवे! अत्यन्त बलशालि निशाचर राक्षस रावणनु नन्नॊडनॆ युद्ध माडलु बन्दिरुवनु, ईग नानेनु माडलि.॥7॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“O Viṣṇu, what shall I do (viṣṇo kathaṃ kariṣyāmi)? For yonder mighty rākṣasa (asau hi balavad rakṣaḥ), of immense valor and prowess (mahāvīrya-parākramaḥ), advances toward me for battle (yuddhārtham abhivartate)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

वरप्रदानाद् बलवान् न खल्वन्येन हेतुना ।
तत् तु सत्यं वचः कार्यं यदुक्तं पद्मयोनिना ॥ ८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

वरप्रदानाद् बलवान् न खल्वन्येन हेतुना ।
तत् तु सत्यं वचः कार्यं यदुक्तं पद्मयोनिना ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘वह केवल ब्रह्माजीके वरदानके कारण प्रबल हो गया है; दूसरे किसी हेतुसे नहीं । कमलयोनि ब्रह्माजीने जो वर दे दिया है, उसे सत्य करना हम सब लोगोंका काम है ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अवन ई उद्धटतनक्कॆ ब्रह्मदेवरिन्द पडॆद वरबलवे कारणवागिदॆ. ब्रह्मदेवरु कॊट्टिरुव वरवन्नु सत्यवागिसुवुदु नम्मॆल्लर कर्तव्यवागिदॆ.॥8॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“He is strong solely through the granting of a boon (varapradānād balavān), and by no other cause indeed (na khalv anyena hetunā); yet that word spoken by the Lotus-born [Brahmā] (yad uktaṃ padmayoninā) must be made true (satyavacaḥ kāryam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तद् यथा नमुचिर्वृत्रो बलिर्नरकशम्बरौ ।
त्वद‍्बलं समवष्टभ्य मया दग्धास्तथा कुरु ॥ ९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तद् यथा नमुचिर्वृत्रो बलिर्नरकशम्बरौ ।
त्वद‍्बलं समवष्टभ्य मया दग्धास्तथा कुरु ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘अतः जैसे पहले आपके बलका आश्रय लेकर मैंने नमुचि, वृत्रासुर, बलि, नरक और शम्बर आदि असुरोंको दग्ध कर डाला है, उसी प्रकार इस समय भी इस असुरका अन्त हो जाय, ऐसा कोई उपाय आप ही कीजिये ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

ई हिन्दॆ निन्न बलवन्नु आश्रयिसि नानु नमूचि, वृत्रासुर, बलि, नरक, शम्बर आदि असुररन्नु अन्त्यगॊळिसिदन्तॆ ईगलू रावणन अन्त्यवागुवन्तह उपायवन्नु योचिसु.॥9॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Therefore, just as Namuci, Vṛtra, Bali, Naraka, and Śambara (namucir vṛtro balir naraka-śambarau) were consumed by me (mayā dagdhāḥ) when I relied upon your might (tvad-balaṃ samavaṣṭabhya), act in like manner now (tathā kuru)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

नह्यन्यो देवदेवेश त्वदृते मधुसूदन ।
गतिः परायणं चापि त्रैलोक्ये सचराचरे ॥ १० ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

नह्यन्यो देवदेवेश त्वदृते मधुसूदन ।
गतिः परायणं चापि त्रैलोक्ये सचराचरे ॥ १० ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘मधुसूदन! आप देवताओंके भी देवता एवं ईश्वर हैं । इस चराचर त्रिभुवनमें आपके सिवा दूसरा कोई ऐसा नहीं है, जो हम देवताओंको सहारा दे सके । आप ही हमारे परम आश्रय हैं ॥ १० ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मधसूदनने! नीने देवदेवनागिरुवॆ, ई चराचर त्रिभुवनगळल्लि देवतॆगळाद नमगॆ परमाश्रयनु नीनल्लदॆ बेरॆ यारू इल्ल.॥10॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“For apart from you (tvām ṛte), O Lord of the lords of gods (devadeveśa), O Slayer of Madhu (madhusūdana), there is no other protector or supreme refuge (gatiḥ parāyaṇaṃ nāsti) in all the three worlds of moving and unmoving beings (trailokye sacarācare).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

त्वं हि नारायणः श्रीमान् पद्मनाभः सनातनः ।
त्वयेमे स्थापिता लोकाः शक्रश्चाहं सुरेश्वरः ॥ ११ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

त्वं हि नारायणः श्रीमान् पद्मनाभः सनातनः ।
1 त्वयेमे स्थापिता लोकाः शक्रश्चाहं सुरेश्वरः ॥ ११ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘आप पद्मनाभ हैं—आपहीके नाभिकमलसे जगत् की उत्पत्ति हुई है । आप ही सनातनदेव श्रीमान् नारायण हैं । आपने ही इन तीनों लोकोंको स्थापित किया है और आपने ही मुझे देवराज इन्द्र बनाया है ॥ ११ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

पद्मनाभा! निन्न नाभिकमलदिन्दले जगत्तिन उत्पत्तियागिदॆ. नीने सनातन नारायणनागिरुवॆ. ई मूरु लोकगळन्नु नीने स्थापिसि, देवराज इन्द्रनाद नन्नन्नु नीने नियोजिसिरुवॆ.॥11॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“For you are the glorious Nārāyaṇa (nārāyaṇaḥ śrīmān), the Lotus-naveled Eternal One (padmanābhaḥ sanātanaḥ); by you were these worlds established (tvayeme sthāpitā lokāḥ), and by you was I set as Śakra, lord of the gods (śakraś cāhaṃ sureśvaraḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

त्वया सृष्टमिदं सर्वं त्रैलोक्यं सचराचरम् ।
त्वामेव भगवन् सर्वे प्रविशन्तिं युगक्षये ॥ १२ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 त्वया सृष्टमिदं सर्वं त्रैलोक्यं सचराचरम् ।
त्वामेव भगवन् सर्वे प्रविशन्तिं युगक्षये ॥ १२ ॥

1 त्वया सृष्टमिदं सर्वं त्रैलोक्यं सचराचरम् ।
1 त्वामेव भगवन्सर्वे प्रविशन्ति युगक्षये ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘भगवन्! आपने ही स्थावर-जङ्गम प्राणियोंसहित इस समस्त त्रिलोकीकी सृष्टि की है और प्रलयकालमें सम्पूर्ण भूत आपमें ही प्रवेश करते हैं ॥ १२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

भगवन्त! नीने स्थावर - जङ्गम प्राणिगळ सहित ई त्रिभुवनगळन्नु सृष्टिसिरुवॆ. प्रळयकालदल्लि समस्त प्राणिगळु निन्नॊळगे प्रवेशिसुत्तवॆ.॥12॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“By you was all this threefold universe of moving and unmoving things created (tvayā sṛṣṭam idaṃ sarvaṃ trailokyaṃ sacarācaram); and into you alone, O Blessed Lord (tvām eva bhagavan), do all beings enter at the dissolution of the age (praviśanti yugakṣaye).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Asked directly whether he will wield his discus against Rāvaṇa, Nārāyaṇa calms Indra’s fear yet declines to fight now, foreseeing a tremendous rākṣasa feat and declaring that Viṣṇu never withdraws without slaying his foe.

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘इसलिये देवदेव! आप ही मुझे कोई ऐसा अमोघ उपाय बताइये, जिससे मेरी विजय हो । क्या आप स्वयं चक्र और तलवार लेकर रावणसे युद्ध करेंगे?’ ॥ १३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अदक्कागि देवदेवने! नन्न विजयवागुवन्तह यावुदादरू अमोघ उपायवन्नु तिळिसु. नीनु स्वतः चक्र मत्तु खड्ग धरिसि रावणनॊडनॆ युद्ध माडुवॆया.॥13॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Therefore tell me yourself according to the truth (tad ācakṣva yathā tattvam … mama svayam), O God of gods (devadeva): will you, aided by your discus (api cakra-sahāyas tvam), do battle against Rāvaṇa, O Lord (yotsyase rāvaṇaṃ prabho)?”

अब्रवीन्न परित्रासः कर्तव्यः श्रूयतां च मे ॥ १४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

इन्द्रके ऐसा कहनेपर भगवान् नारायणदेव बोले—‘देवराज! तुम्हें भय नहीं करना चाहिये । मेरी बात सुनो— ॥ १४ ॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thus addressed by Śakra (evam uktaḥ sa śakreṇa), the Lord God Nārāyaṇa (devo nārāyaṇaḥ prabhuḥ) spoke: “Fear must not be entertained (na paritrāsaḥ kartavyaḥ), and hearken unto my words (śrūyatāṃ ca me).

न तावदेष दुष्टात्मा शक्यो जेतुं सुरासुरैः ।
हन्तुं चापि समासाद्य वरदानेन दुर्जयः ॥ १५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘पहली बात तो यह है इस दुष्टात्मा रावणको सम्पूर्ण देवता और असुर मिलकर भी न तो मार सकते हैं और न परास्त ही कर सकते हैं; क्योंकि वरदान पानेके कारण यह इस समय दुर्जय हो गया है ॥ १५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

ई दुष्टात्मा रावणनन्नु समस्त देवतॆगळु, असुररु सेरियू कॊल्ललाररु, सोलिसलाररु; एकॆन्दरॆ इवनु वर पडॆदु ईग दुर्जयनागिद्दानॆ.॥15॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Not yet can this wicked-souled one (na tāvad eṣa duṣṭātmā) be conquered by gods or asuras (śakyo jetuṃ surāsuraiḥ), nor indeed slain upon confronting him (hantuṃ cāpi samāsādya), hard to vanquish as he is through the granting of his boon (varadānena durjayaḥ).

राक्षसः पुत्रसहितो दृष्टमेतन्निसर्गतः ॥ १६ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘अपने पुत्रके साथ आया हुआ यह उत्कट बलशाली राक्षस सब प्रकारसे महान् पराक्रम प्रकट करेगा । यह बात मुझे अपनी स्वाभाविक ज्ञानदृष्टिसे दिखायी दे रही है ॥ १६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

तन्न पुत्रनॊन्दिगॆ बन्दिरुव ई उत्कट बलशालि राक्षसनु महा पराक्रमवन्नु प्रकटगॊळिसुवनु. इदु ननगॆ नन्न स्वाभाविक ज्ञानदृष्टियिन्द काणुत्तदॆ.॥16॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“By all means, however, this rākṣasa puffed up with strength (balotkaṭaḥ), together with his son (putra-sahitaḥ), will perform a mighty deed (mahat karma kariṣyati); this has been foreseen by me through innate perception (dṛṣṭam etan nisargataḥ).

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘सुरेश्वर! दूसरी बात जो मुझे कहनी है, इस प्रकार है—तुम जो मुझसे कह रहे थे कि ‘आप ही उसके साथ युद्ध कीजिये’ उसके उत्तरमें निवेदन है कि मैं इस समय युद्धस्थलमें राक्षस रावणका सामना करनेके लिये नहीं जाऊँगा ॥ १७ ॥

सुरेश्वर! इन्नॊन्दु मातु हेळुत्तेनॆ - ‘नीने अवनॊडनॆ युद्ध माडु’ ऎन्दु नीनु हेळुत्तिरुवॆ. आदरॆ ईग नानु युद्धदल्लि रावणनन्नु इदिरिसलु होगुवुदिल्ल.॥17॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“As for what you said to me, ‘Do battle!’ (yat tu māṃ tvam abhāṣiṣṭha yudhyasveti), O lord of the gods (sureśvara), I shall not fight against that rākṣasa Rāvaṇa in battle (nāhaṃ taṃ pratiyotsyāmi rāvaṇaṃ rākṣasaṃ yudhi).

दुर्लभश्चैव कामोऽद्य वरगुप्ताद्धि रावणात् ॥ १८ ॥

‘मुझ विष्णुका यह स्वभाव है कि मैं संग्राममें शत्रुका वध किये बिना पीछे नहीं लौटता; परंतु इस समय रावण वरदानसे सुरक्षित है, इसलिये उसकी ओरसे मेरी इस विजय-सम्बन्धिनी इच्छाकी पूर्ति होनी कठिन है ॥ १८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

नानु सङ्ग्रामदल्लि शत्रुवन्नु वधिसदॆ हिन्दिरुगुवुदिल्ल इदु विष्णुवाद नन्न स्वभाववागिदॆ. आदरॆ ईग रावणनु वरदानदिन्द सुरक्षितनागिद्दानॆ. अदक्कागि नन्न विजय इच्छॆ अवनिन्द पूर्णवागुवुदु कठिणवागिदॆ.॥18॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“For Viṣṇu never turns back from battle without having slain his foe (nāhatvā samare śatruṃ viṣṇuḥ pratinivartate); and today that desire is impossible to attain (durlabhaś caiva kāmo ’dya) from Rāvaṇa, guarded as he is by his boon (varaguptād dhi rāvaṇāt).

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Vowing solemnly that he himself will become the cause of Rāvaṇa’s death when the destined hour arrives, Viṣṇu bids Indra fight fearlessly; the Vedic deities sally from heaven’s city and meet the roaring rākṣasa host at night’s end.

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

7.27.1–2 (Ascending Beyond Kailāsa to Heaven’s Realm)

Introducing Sarga 27 as the narrative of Sumāli’s slaying in battle (अथ युद्धे सुमालिवधः), Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) explains why Rāvaṇa crosses over Kailāsa to reach Indraloka: heaven is established directly above Mount Meru (तस्य मेरूपरि प्रतिष्ठितत्वात्). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) likewise links the canto directly to Rāvaṇa’s stay at Kailāsa in Sarga 26 (कैलासनिवासानन्तरकालिकं वृत्तान्तमाह). As the rākṣasa army surges forward, its roar reaches the celestial realm like a churning—or in Śivasahāya’s reading (भिद्यमानार्णवोपमः), a bursting—ocean, announcing that Rāvaṇa’s campaign now strikes the very seat of cosmic rule.

7.27.3–5 (Indra’s Mobilization of the Celestial Hosts)

Hearing of Rāvaṇa’s arrival, Indra starts from his throne and issues an urgent war-summons to heaven’s principal divisions—Ādityas, Vasus, Rudras, Viśvedevas, Sādhyas, and Maruts. Śivasahāya construes verses 7.27.3–4 as a single syntactic sentence (श्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयि), wherein the accusatives of 4ab enumerate the assembled deities addressed by अब्रवीत् in 3c. In 7.27.5, where the Southern text pairs the absolutive सन्नह्य with युद्धश्रद्धासमन्विताः without an explicit finite verb, Śivasahāya supplies बभूवुः (‘became filled with ardent zeal for battle’), highlighting the gods’ spirited readiness to fight.

7.27.6 (Mahendra’s Dread and Resort to Viṣṇu)

Verse 7.27.6 draws a striking psychological contrast: while the celestial ranks eagerly strap on armor, Indra himself grows dejected (dīna) and deeply terrified (paritrasta) before Rāvaṇa, hastening immediately to Viṣṇu. Rāma Varmā uncovers the theological cause of the king of heaven’s dread—not mere faintheartedness, but keen awareness that Brahmā’s boon must be honored and upheld (भगवद्वचनस्य परिपालनीयत्वादिति भावः). Śivasahāya traces the causal sequence directly: because Mahendra is terrified of Rāvaṇa, he is cast into dejection (रावणं प्रति परित्रस्तः अत एव दीनः) and therefore turns to Viṣṇu for counsel.

7.27.7–8 (The Paradox of Brahmā’s Boon)

Indra opens his appeal by identifying the exact dilemma confronting heaven. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) notes that 7.27.8 states the sole cause of Rāvaṇa’s terrifying strength: his invincibility flows strictly from Brahmā’s boon, not from intrinsic supremacy over the gods. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) spells out the binding obligation: the word spoken by the Lotus-born guaranteeing Rāvaṇa’s invulnerability to gods and others must be honored as true by all (पद्मयोनिना यद्देवाद्यवध्यत्वे वच उक्तं तत्सत्यं कार्यं सर्वैरिति शेषः). Cosmic order itself thus forbids Indra from overriding Brahmā’s decree by ordinary divine force.

7.27.9 (Precedents of Divine Deliverance)

Invoking five celebrated demonic foes—Namuci, Vṛtra, Bali, Naraka, and Śambara—Indra reminds Viṣṇu that heaven’s past triumphs always depended upon Viṣṇu’s strength (tvad-balam) and stratagems. All three commentators unpack the elliptical request तथा कुरु (‘do likewise’): Rāma Varmā glosses it as ‘devise and declare to me an expedient for slaying Rāvaṇa too, relying on your boundless might’ (रावणस्यापि वधोपाययत्नं कुरु मे आख्याहि); Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) similarly reads ‘contrive a means of slaying him’ (अस्यापि हननोपायं कुर्वित्यर्थः); and Śivasahāya adds that through some device Rāvaṇa too should be consumed (केनचिदुपायेन अयमपि विदग्धो भवेदित्यर्थः).

7.27.10–12 (The Vaiṣṇava Stuti of Nārāyaṇa)

Verses 7.27.10–12—largely unique to the Southern Recension—elevate Indra’s plea into a full devotional hymn to Viṣṇu as cosmic creator, sustainer, and dissolver. Rāma Varmā notes देवदेवेश as a vocative to Viṣṇu and glosses गतिः as ‘protector’ (रक्षकः) and परायणम् as ‘refuge’ (आश्रयः), a distinction echoed by Śivasahāya (परायणं परमाश्रयश्च), while Govindarāja likewise marks the vocative in 10a. On 7.27.11–12, Śivasahāya underscores the causal logic: because Nārāyaṇa is eternal (nitya), he established both the worlds and Indra’s kingship, and into him all beings (भूतानीति शेषः) dissolve at age’s end.

7.27.13–15 (Indra’s Direct Question and Viṣṇu’s Assessment)

Concluding his appeal, Indra asks Viṣṇu plainly for either a winning expedient or direct intervention. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) construe तत्त्वम् as ‘the true expedient for victory’ (तत्त्वमुपायतत्त्वम्), adding that Indra asks alternatively whether Viṣṇu himself will fight (स्वयमेव वा योत्स्यसे तद्ब्रूहीति शेषः). Reading असिचक्रसहायस्त्वं, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) paraphrases: ‘tell me how, aided by sword and discus, you will fight Rāvaṇa.’ On 7.27.14–15, Tilaka and Śiromaṇi supply वचनम् with मे: because of Brahmā’s boon, neither gods nor asuras can yet conquer or slay Rāvaṇa.

7.27.16 (Two Interpretations of Nisargataḥ)

Viṣṇu’s prophecy—that Rāvaṇa with his son Meghanāda will accomplish a momentous deed (mahat karma), foreshadowing Indra’s capture in Sarga 29—yields two rich readings of निसर्गतः (‘by nature’). Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja gloss महत्कर्म as ‘the great deed of battle’ (युद्धकर्म) and link निसर्गतः with दृष्टम्: ‘perceived and known through my innate faculty of omniscience’ (सहजज्ञानशक्त्या दृष्टमवगतम्). Śivasahāya, by contrast, construes निसर्गतः with Rāvaṇa’s conduct: ‘out of his inherent rākṣasa nature (राक्षसस्वभावात्), he will commit a fierce deed—this I have foreseen.’

7.27.17–18 (Why Viṣṇu Declines the Present Combat)

Viṣṇu’s refusal to fight Rāvaṇa here immediately invites reconciliation with his eventual triumph as Rāma. Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja supply the temporal qualifier इदानीम् (‘not now’, नाहं प्रतियोत्स्यामि इदानीमिति शेषः), confining the refusal strictly to this celestial battle. Śivasahāya unfolds the rationale of 7.27.18: Viṣṇu’s unbreakable warrior code forbids withdrawing without slaying his enemy, yet against a boon-shielded Rāvaṇa, the fulfillment of that desire—the attainment of victory (कामो विजयप्राप्तीच्छा)—remains unattainable today without violating Brahmā’s truth.

7.27.19–20 (Viṣṇu’s Solemn Pledge and the Indirect Avatāra)

Anticipating Indra’s dismay at his refusal to fight now, Viṣṇu delivers a binding pledge pointing toward his future incarnation as Rāma. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) tightens the correlative syntax (यथा भवितास्मि तथा प्रतिजाने). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) discerns a subtle theological point in 7.27.19cd: by vowing ‘I shall become the cause of his death’ (मृत्युकारणम्), Viṣṇu intimates that he will not slay Rāvaṇa directly in his divine form (स्वस्य साक्षाद्धननकर्तृत्वाभावः सूचितः), before restating the promise in another turn of phrase in 7.27.20 (तदेव भङ्ग्यन्तरेणाह)—when the appointed hour arrives, he alone will destroy Rāvaṇa and delight the gods.

7.27.21–22 (The March Out of Heaven’s City)

Reassured by Viṣṇu’s vow, Indra receives the charge to fight fearlessly with all the celestial legions (Śivasahāya supplies राक्षसैः, ‘fight against the rākṣasas’). Verse 7.27.22—preserved in the Southern Recension—mobilizes the classic Vedic pantheon: Rudras, Ādityas, Vasus, Maruts, and twin Aśvins. Rāma Varmā glosses अभितः as ‘directly facing’ (संमुखे), while Śivasahāya identifies पुरात् specifically as heaven itself (पुरात्स्वर्गात्), picturing the armed deities marching boldly beyond the ramparts of Amarāvatī to confront the invaders in the open firmament.

7.27.23–24 (The Clash at Dawn’s Edge)

The temporal detail रजनीक्षये (‘at the waning of the night’) in 7.27.23 sharpens the atmosphere, as the night-roaming demons’ battle-roar crescendos just before dawn. Both Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya analyze प्रयुद्धस्य as an intensive compound denoting fierce, preeminent combat (प्रकृष्टं युद्धं यस्य / प्रकृष्टयुद्धं कुर्वतः). On 7.27.24, Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explains the adverbial form हृष्टवत् simply as ‘rejoicing’ (हृष्टाः सन्तः), while Śivasahāya highlights the warriors’ mutual defiance as they lock eyes (अन्योन्यमभिवीक्ष्य) and surge eagerly face to face into the fray.

7.27.25–26 (Commotion in the Ranks and Outbreak of War)

The first sight of Rāvaṇa’s seemingly inexhaustible host sends a tremor (saṅkṣobha) through the celestial armies at the battle’s vanguard. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) clarifies तत् in 7.27.25 as specifically ‘that vast rākṣasa army’ (तत् राक्षसीयं महासैन्यम्) and traces a crisp causal progression in 7.27.26: because diverse weapons were brandished aloft (नानाप्रहरणानि उद्यतानि यस्मिन्), the clash erupted into a tumultuous roar (तुमुलशब्दवत्), and thereby grew dreadful (ghoram). Including dānavas alongside devas and rakṣasas enlarges the engagement into a war spanning cosmic orders.

7.27.27–29 (Rāvaṇa’s Ministers and the Epic Roster)

As combat erupts, Rāvaṇa’s fierce counselors (sacivāḥ) advance in force. Śivasahāya identifies an extended syntactic unit spanning verses 7.27.28–32ab (सार्धश्लोकचतुष्टयमेकान्वयि): though listed in the nominative here, all these warriors are gathered up by एतैः सर्वैः परिवृतः in 7.27.31 as the escort surrounding Rāvaṇa’s maternal grandfather Sumāli. Familiar names—Mārīca, Prahasta, Mahāpārśva, Mahodara, Akampana, Nikumbha, Śuka, Sāraṇa, Jambumālī, and Virūpākṣa—deliberately recall the celebrated antagonists of the Āraṇya, Sundara, and Yuddha kāṇḍas, rooting Laṅkā’s later war-council in this earlier assault on heaven.

7.27.30 (Southern Expansion and the Oleander-Eyed Champion)

Verse 7.27.30—absent from the Baroda Critical Edition—enlarges the roll with champions familiar from earlier books, including Khara, Dūṣaṇa, Triśiras (slain by Rāma at Janasthāna), Durmukha, and Yajñakopa. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) pause expressly to explain the vivid name करवीराक्षः: ‘he whose eyes resemble oleander blossoms’ (करवीरपुष्पसदृशनेत्रः / करवीरपुष्पवदक्षिणी यस्य), evoking the crimson hue of red oleander (karavīra) as a fierce emblem of bloodshot demonic rage. Together, these rosters build relentless momentum toward Sumāli’s charge.

7.27.31–33 (Sumāli’s Tempestuous Charge)

Closing the multi-verse sentence begun in 7.27.28, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) glosses आर्यकः as ‘maternal grandfather’ (मातामहः) and clarifies सैन्यम् as the divine army (देवसेनाम्) into which Sumāli plunges, ringed by Rāvaṇa’s sons and ministers. Two vivid similes capture the rout: Sumāli scatters the gods like a gale dispersing rain-clouds, and the gods flee on every side (प्रणुन्नं पलायितम्, Śivasahāya) like deer stampeded by a lion. Having risen from Pātāla earlier in Book VII, the aged rākṣasa patriarch proves himself a devastating frontline warlord.

7.27.34–35 (Counterattack of Sāvitra, Tvaṣṭṛ, and Pūṣan)

Just as heaven’s lines give way, three Vedic deities enter the arena to rally the defense: the eighth Vasu, Sāvitra, followed by the armored Ādityas Tvaṣṭṛ and Pūṣan with their host. Śivasahāya notes their fearless advance into the battlefield (रणाजिरे प्रविवेश), while the Parimal apparatus records that an extra verse amplifying Sāvitra’s charge after 7.27.34 is absent from Śivasahāya’s text (इदं पद्यं रा. पा. न दृश्यते). Calling upon these ancient solar and elemental deities underscores that the battle now engages heaven’s foundational guardians.

7.27.36 (Enraged at the Demons’ Renown)

Verse 7.27.36 contains a highly compressed clause (क्रुद्धानां रक्षसां कीर्तिम्), which all three commentators—Rāma Varmā (Tilaka), Śivasahāya, and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa)—construe identically by supplying प्रति: ‘battle arose between the gods, who were enraged at the renown of the rākṣasas who never turn back in battle, and the rākṣasas’ (समरेष्वनिवर्तिनां रक्षसां कीर्तिं प्रति क्रुद्धानां सुराणां राक्षसैः सह युद्धं समभवदिति योजना). Rather than fighting in panic, the gods now clash out of wounded martial honor, stung by the demons’ rising fame.

7.27.37–38 (Mutual Carnage Across the Celestial Field)

Verses 7.27.37–38 establish a grim symmetry of slaughter before Sumāli’s decisive onslaught. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) expands शतसहस्रशः as ‘by hundreds and by thousands’ (शतशः सहस्रशः) as the rākṣasas cut down the gods (vibudhān) standing firm in battle. In equal measure, the gods counterattack with gleaming weapons (vimalaiḥ śastraiḥ), dispatching mighty demons to the dwelling of Death (यमक्षयमुपनिन्युः). This heroic euphemism—‘conducting foes to Yama’s house’—is a signature Vālmīki battle idiom underscoring the valor and lethal parity of the opening exchange.

7.27.39–40 (Sumāli’s Renewed Assault and Oral Recapitulation)

Reintroducing Sumāli with the frame vocative राम (Agastya addressing Rāma), verses 7.27.39–40 echo 7.27.32 closely, again likening the furious demon scattering the celestial host (तत् देवसम्बन्धि सैन्यम्, Śivasahāya) to wind dispersing a rain-cloud (vāyur jaladharaṃ yathā). Rather than mere repetition, this epic recapitulation pivots cleanly from the general mêlée (7.27.36–38) into the sarga’s decisive duel: having introduced Sāvitra in 7.27.34, the poet re-centers attention on Sumāli’s unchecked rampage to motivate the eighth Vasu’s fatal counterstroke.

7.27.41 (Collapse of the Divine Formation)

Under a relentless storm of giant shafts, pikes (śūla), and barbed lances (prāsa), the celestial divisions finally lose cohesion and can no longer hold their line (न व्यतिष्ठन्त). While संहताः ordinarily means ‘compactly massed together’, Śivasahāya offers a striking etymological gloss from √han—‘even those gods whose own striking had been well performed’ (सम्यक्कृतं हननं येषां तेऽपि सुराः)—stressing that even heaven’s most effective combatants could not withstand Sumāli’s missile deluge, setting the stage for Sāvitra’s solitary stand.

7.27.42–43 (Sāvitra’s Resolute Stand)

At the crisis of the celestial rout, Sāvitra—the eighth Vasu—plants himself squarely across Sumāli’s path. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) traces the abrupt shift in momentum: while the deities are being driven back by Sumāli, Sāvitra stands unyielding (विद्राव्यमाणेषु सत्सु सावित्रोऽष्टमो वसुर्व्यवस्थितः), and ringed by his own battalions checks the raging night-roamer by sheer valor (vikrama). Breaking free of the general fray, the narrative narrows into a classic epic dvandva-yuddha—a hair-raising single combat between two warriors who never yield ground.

7.27.44 (Destruction of the Serpent-Chariot)

Verse 7.27.44a–b completes the sentence begun in 43ef by naming the two unretreating duelists, Sumāli and the Vasu. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) clarify तस्य in 44c as referring specifically to Sumāli (तस्य सुमालिनः), while Śivasahāya highlights the causal swiftness of the stroke: shattered by the Vasu’s great arrows, Sumāli’s serpent-drawn chariot (pannaga-ratha) was struck down and hence toppled in an instant (निहतः अत एव क्षणेन विनिपातितः). Unhorsing a rākṣasa mahāratha signals the decisive turn of the duel.

7.27.45 (Taking Up the Mace)

Having demolished Sumāli’s chariot—which Śivasahāya describes as thickly studded with hundreds of arrows (बाणशतैः चितं तस्य सुमालिनो रथं हत्वा)—Sāvitra abandons archery for close-quarters mace combat (gadā-yuddha) to slay him outright (तस्य सुमालिनो वधाय). In Sanskrit epic convention, seizing the mace after shattering an adversary’s chariot proclaims absolute resolve and imminent annihilation: concentrating raw elemental force into one crushing weapon, Sāvitra prepares the thunderbolt-like climax that closes the canto.

7.27.46–47 (The Meteoric Blow of Sāvitra’s Mace)

Gripping his blazing-tipped mace compared to Death’s staff (kāladaṇḍa), Sāvitra brings it crashing squarely upon Sumāli’s skull. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) clarifies the double simile of 7.27.47: glowing like a plunging meteor (ulkābhā), the mace blazed above Sumāli just like a roaring thunderbolt hurled by Indra upon a mountain (गिरौ इन्द्रप्रमुक्ता महाशनिरिव तस्य सुमालिन एव उपरि पतन्ती गदा बभौ). The imagery evokes the ancient Vedic motif of Indra shattering mountain peaks, restoring heaven’s primordial majesty through the eighth Vasu’s arm.

7.27.48 (Instantaneous Annihilation into Ash)

The impact of the solar Vasu’s mace proves not merely fatal but total: bone, skull, and flesh vanish without a trace. Śivasahāya links the two halves causally—because the slain Sumāli’s bones and body were reduced to ashes by the mace, nothing whatsoever remained visible upon the field (निहतस्य तस्य सुमालिनोऽस्थिप्रभृति गदया भस्मतां नीतमत एव न ददृशे). Fittingly, the aged patriarch who had earlier engineered Kaikasī’s union with Viśravas to revive rākṣasa supremacy meets complete incineration in heaven’s very first clash.

7.27.49 (The Rākṣasa Rout and Transition to Meghanāda)

Seeing their venerated grandfather pulverized in an instant, the demon ranks dissolve in terror. Śivasahāya notes both their panicked flight—crying out to one another as they scatter on every side (परस्परं क्रोशमानाः सन्तः व्यद्रवन्)—and the emphatic closing half-verse (अर्धमेकम्, 49ef): driven relentlessly by Sāvitra, the rākṣasas cannot rally or hold their ground. Yet heaven’s victory proves fleeting: witnessing Sumāli reduced to ashes and his own forces routed immediately provokes Rāvaṇa’s son Meghanāda to enter the battle at the opening of Sarga 28.

उसके ऊपर गिरती हुई वह गदा उल्काके समान चमक उठी, मानो इन्द्रके द्वारा छोड़ी गयी विशाल अशनि भारी गड़गड़ाहटके साथ किसी पर्वतके शिखरपर गिर रही हो ॥ ४९ १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

उल्कॆयन्तॆ काणुत्तिद्द आ गदॆयु सुमालिय तलॆय मेलॆ बीळुत्तिद्दाग इन्द्रनु प्रयोगिसिद वज्रायुधवु गर्जिसुत्ता पर्वतक्कॆ अप्पळिसुवन्तॆ काणुत्तित्तु.॥49½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Beholding him slain in the encounter (taṃ dṛṣṭvā nihataṃ saṅkhye), all those rākṣasas fled together on every side (vyadravan sahitāḥ sarve … samantataḥ), crying out to one another (krośamānāḥ parasparam); and put to flight by the Vasu, the rākṣasas could not stand their ground (vidrāvyamāṇā vasunā rākṣasā nāvatasthire).

गदया भस्मतां नीतं निहतस्य रणाजिरे ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उसकी चोट लगते ही समराङ्गणमें सुमालीका काम तमाम हो गया । न उसकी हड्डीका पता लगा, न मस्तकका और न कहीं उसका मांस ही दिखायी दिया । वह सब कुछ उस गदाकी आगसे भस्म हो गया ॥ ५० १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

गदाघातदिन्द सुमालियु मडिदु अवन ऎलुबागली, मांसवागली, तलॆयागली यावुदू काणलिल्ल. अदॆल्लवू आ गदॆय बॆङ्कियिन्द सुट्टु बूदियायितु.॥50½॥

तं दृष्ट्वा निहतं सङ्ख्ये राक्षसास्ते समन्ततः ॥ ५१ ॥
व्यद्रवन् सहिताः सर्वे क्रोशमानाः परस्परम् ।
विद्राव्यमाणा वसुना राक्षसा नावतस्थिरे ॥ ५२ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

युद्धमें सुमालीको मारा गया देख वे सब राक्षस एक-दूसरेको पुकारते हुए एक साथ चारों ओर भाग खड़े हुए । वसुके द्वारा खदेड़े जानेवाले वे राक्षस समरभूमिमें खड़े न रह सके ॥ ५१-५२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

युद्धदल्लि सुमालियु हतनादुदन्नु नोडि ऎल्ल राक्षसरु परस्पर कूगुत्ता दिक्कापालागि ओडिहोदरु. वसुवु अट्टिसिदाग आ राक्षसरु समर भूमियल्लि निल्लदादरु.॥51-52॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये उत्तरकाण्डे सप्तविंशः सर्गः ॥ २७ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके उत्तरकाण्डमें सत्ताईसवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ २७ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद उत्तर काण्डदल्लि इप्पत्तेळनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु. ॥27॥
