सर्वाष् टीकाः
भागसूचना
  1. माणिभद्र तथा कुबेरकी पराजय और रावणद्वारा पुष्पकविमानका अपहरण
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Seeing thousands of yakṣa lords put to flight, Kubera dispatches his formidable general Maṇibhadra; charging with four thousand warriors like swooping hawks, the yakṣas wage a tumultuous battle that fills heaven’s watchers with wonder.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततस्ताल्ँ लक्ष्य वित्रस्तान् यक्षेन्द्रांश्च सहस्रशः ।
धनाध्यक्षो महायक्षं माणिभद्रमथाब्रवीत् ॥ १ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततस्ताल्ँ लक्ष्य वित्रस्तान् यक्षेन्द्रांश्च सहस्रशः ।
धनाध्यक्षो महायक्षं माणिभद्रमथाब्रवीत् ॥ १ ॥

ततस्तान्ल्लक्ष्य वित्रस्थान् यक्षेन्द्रांश्च सहस्रशः ।
धनाध्यक्षो महायक्षं मणिभद्रमथाब्रवीत् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘(अगस्त्यजी कहते हैं—रघुनन्दन!) धनाध्यक्षोंने देखा, हजारों यक्षप्रवर भयभीत होकर भाग रहे हैं; तब उन्होंने माणिभद्र नामक एक महायक्षसे कहा— ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

(अगस्त्यरु हेळुत्तारॆ - रघुनन्दन !) धनाध्यक्षनाद कुबेरनु तन्न कडॆय साविरारु यक्षरु भयगॊण्डु पलायन माडुत्तिरुवुदन्नु नोडि, मणिभद्रनॆम्ब महायक्षनल्लि हेळिदनु.॥1॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then, beholding those yakṣa lords terrified in their thousands (tāṃl lakṣya vitrastān yakṣendrāṃś ca sahasraśaḥ), the Lord of Wealth [Kubera] (dhanādhyakṣaḥ) addressed the mighty yakṣa Māṇicara [Maṇibhadra] (mahāyakṣaṃ māṇicāram):

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

रावणं जहि यक्षेन्द्र दुर्वृत्तं पापचेतसम् ।
शरणं भव वीराणां यक्षाणां युद्धशालिनाम् ॥ २ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 रावणं जहि यक्षेन्द्र दुर्वृत्तं पापचेतसम् ।
1 शरणं भव वीराणां यक्षाणां युद्धशालिनाम् ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘यक्षप्रवर! रावण पापात्मा एवं दुराचारी है, तुम उसे मार डालो और युद्धमें शोभा पानेवाले वीर यक्षोंको शरण दो—उनकी रक्षा करो’ ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

यक्षश्रेष्ठने! पापात्मनाद दुराचारी रावणनन्नु नीनु कॊन्दु, युद्धदल्लि शोभितराद यक्षरिगॆ नीनु शरण्यनागि अवरन्नु रक्षिसु.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Slay the wicked, evil-minded Rāvaṇa (rāvaṇaṃ jahi … durvṛttaṃ pāpa-cetasam), O lord of yakṣas! Become the refuge of the heroic, battle-skilled yakṣas (śaraṇaṃ bhava vīrāṇāṃ yakṣāṇāṃ yuddha-śālinām)!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

एवमुक्तो महाबाहुर्माणिभद्रः सुदुर्जयः ।
वृतो यक्षसहस्रैस्तु चतुर्भिः समयोधयत् ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 एवमुक्तो महाबाहुर्माणिभद्रः सुदुर्जयः ।
वृतो यक्षसहस्रैस्तु चतुर्भिः समयोधयत् ॥ ३ ॥

एवमुक्तो महाबाहुर्मणिभद्रः सुदुर्जयः ।
वृतो यक्षसहस्रैस्तु चतुर्भिः समयोधयत् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

महाबाहु माणिभद्र अत्यन्त दुर्जय वीर थे । कुबेरकी उक्त आज्ञा पाकर वे चार हजार यक्षोंकी सेना साथ ले फाटकपर गये और राक्षसोंके साथ युद्ध करने लगे ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अत्यन्त दुर्जय वीरनाद महाबाहु मणिभद्रनु कुबेरन अप्पणॆ पडॆदु नाल्कुसाविर यक्ष सैन्यवन्नु जॊतॆगूडि महाद्वारक्कॆ बन्दु राक्षसरॊडनॆ युद्ध माडतॊडगिदनु.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Addressed thus (evam uktaḥ), the mighty-armed, most invincible Maṇibhadra (mahābāhur māṇibhadraḥ sudurjayaḥ), surrounded by four thousand yakṣas (vṛto yakṣa-sahasrais tu caturbhiḥ), joined battle (samayodhayat).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ते गदामुसलप्रासैः शक्तितोमरमुद‍्गरैः ।
अभिघ्नन्तस्तदा यक्षा राक्षसान् समुपाद्रवन् ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ते गदामुसलप्रासैः शक्तितोमरमुद‍्गरैः ।
अभिघ्नन्तस्तदा यक्षा राक्षसान् समुपाद्रवन् ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस समय यक्षयोद्धा गदा, मूसल, प्रास, शक्ति, तोमर तथा मुद‍्गरोंका प्रहार करते हुए राक्षसोंपर टूट पड़े ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग यक्षरु गदॆ, ऒनकॆ, प्रास, शक्ति, तोमर, मुद्गरगळिन्द प्रहार माडुत्ता राक्षसर मेलॆ मुगिबिद्दरु.॥4॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Striking with maces, iron pestles, and darts (gadā-musala-prāsaiḥ), and with spears, javelins, and hammers (śakti-tomara-mudgaraiḥ), those yakṣas then rushed upon the rākṣasas (rākṣasān samupādravan).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

कुर्वन्तस्तुमुलं युद्धं चरन्तः श्येनवल्लघु ।
बाढं प्रयच्छ नेच्छामि दीयतामिति भाषिणः ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 कुर्वन्तस्तुमुलं युद्धं चरन्तः श्येनवल्लघु ।
बाढं प्रयच्छ नेच्छामि दीयतामिति भाषिणः ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

वे घोर युद्ध करते हुए बाज पक्षीकी तरह तीव्रगतिसे सब ओर विचरने लगे । कोई कहता ‘मुझे युद्धका अवसर दो ।’ दूसरा बोलता—‘मैं यहाँसे पीछे हटना नहीं चाहता ।’ फिर तीसरा बोल उठता—‘मुझे अपना हथियार दो’ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

महाघोर युद्धमाडुत्तिद्द अवरु गिडुग पक्षियन्तॆ तीव्रगतियिन्द सञ्चरिसुत्ता, ‘ननगॆ युद्धद अवकाश कॊडु, नानु इल्लिन्द हिन्दॆगॆयलारॆ, ननगॆ नन्न आयुधवन्नु कॊडु’ ऎन्दु हेळुत्तिद्दरु.॥5॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Waging a tumultuous battle (kurvantas tumulaṃ yuddham) and swooping as swiftly as hawks (carantaḥ śyenaval laghu), they shouted battle-cries: “Well struck! Give me combat! I will not yield! Let it be dealt!” (bāḍhaṃ prayaccha necchāmi dīyatām iti bhāṣiṇaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततो देवाः सगन्धर्वा ऋषयो ब्रह्मवादिनः ।
दृष्ट्वा तत् तुमुलं युद्धं परं विस्मयमागमन् ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 ततो देवाः सगन्धर्वा ऋषयो ब्रह्मवादिनः ।
1 दृष्ट्वा तत् तुमुलं युद्धं परं विस्मयमागमन् ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस तुमुल युद्धको देखकर देवता, गन्धर्व तथा ब्रह्मवादी ऋषि भी बड़े आश्चर्यमें पड़ गये थे ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आ तुमुलयुद्धवन्नु नोडि, देवतॆगळु, गन्धर्वरु, ब्रह्मवादि ऋषिगळु भारी आश्चर्यचकितरादरु.॥6॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thereupon the gods together with the gandharvas (devāḥ sa-gandharvāḥ) and the Veda-chanting sages (ṛṣayo brahmavādinaḥ), beholding that tumultuous battle (dṛṣṭvā tat tumulaṃ yuddham), were filled with supreme astonishment (paraṃ vismayam āgaman).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Prahasta, Mahodara, and Mārīca cut down all four thousand yakṣas through rākṣasa illusion; undaunted, Maṇibhadra absorbs Dhūmrākṣa’s pestle-blow and fells him with his mace, drawing Rāvaṇa himself into the duel.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

यक्षाणां तु प्रहस्तेन सहस्रं निहतं रणे ।
महोदरेण चानिन्द्यं सहस्रमपरं हतम् ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

यक्षाणां तु प्रहस्तेन सहस्रं निहतं रणे ।
महोदरेण चानिन्द्यं सहस्रमपरं हतम् ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस रणभूमिमें प्रहस्तने एक हजार यक्षोंका संहार कर डाला । फिर महोदरने दूसरे एक सहस्र प्रशंसनीय यक्षोंका विनाश किया ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आ रणरङ्गदल्लि प्रहस्तनु ऒन्दु साविर यक्षरन्नु संहरिसिदनु. मत्तॆ महोदरनु बेरॆ ऒन्दु साविर प्रशंसनीयराद यक्षरन्नु विनाशगॊळिसिदनु.॥7॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Yet a thousand of the yakṣas (yakṣāṇāṃ … sahasram) were slain in battle by Prahasta (prahastena nihataṃ raṇe), and another blameless thousand (anindyaṃ sahasram aparam) were cut down by Mahodara (mahodareṇa … hatam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

क्रुद्धेन च तदा राजन् मारीचेन युयुत्सुना ।
निमेषान्तरमात्रेण द्वे सहस्रे निपातिते ॥ ८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

क्रुद्धेन च तदा राजन् मारीचेन युयुत्सुना ।
निमेषान्तरमात्रेण द्वे सहस्रे निपातिते ॥ ८ ॥

क्रुद्धेन च तदा राजन् मारिचेन युयुत्सुना ।
निमेषान्तरमात्रेण द्वे सहस्रे निपातिते ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

राजन्! उस समय कुपित हुए रणोत्सुक मारीचने पलक मारते-मारते शेष दो हजार यक्षोंको धराशायी कर दिया ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

राजने! आग कुपितनाद रणोत्सुक मारीचनु कण्णुरॆप्पॆ हॊडॆयुवष्टरल्लि उळिद ऎरडु साविर यक्षरन्नु धराशायियागिसिदनु.॥8॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

And by the enraged Mārīca, eager for battle (kruddhena … mārīcena yuyutsunā), O King [Rāma], two thousand were felled (dve sahasre nipātite) in the mere space of the twinkling of an eye (nimeṣāntara-mātreṇa).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

क्व च यक्षार्जवं युद्धं क्व च मायाबलाश्रयम् ।
रक्षसां पुरुषव्याघ्र तेन तेऽभ्यधिका युधि ॥ ९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 क्व च यक्षार्जवं युद्धं क्व च मायाबलाश्रयम् ।
1 रक्षसां पुरुषव्याघ्र तेन तेऽभ्यधिका युधि ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

पुरुषसिंह! कहाँ यक्षोंका सरलतापूर्वक युद्ध?और कहाँ राक्षसोंका मायामय संग्राम? वे अपने मायाबलके भरोसे ही यक्षोंकी अपेक्षा अधिक शक्तिशाली सिद्ध हुए ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

पुरुषसिंहने! यक्षर ऋजुमार्गद युद्धवॆल्लि? राक्षसर मायामय सङ्ग्रामवॆल्लि? राक्षसरु मायाबलदिन्द यक्षरिगिन्त हॆच्चु शक्तिशालिगळागिद्दरु.॥9॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

What comparison is there, O tiger among men (puruṣavyāghra), between the straightforward warfare of the yakṣas (yakṣārjavaṃ yuddham) and the combat of the rākṣasas grounded in the power of illusion (māyā-balāśrayam rakṣasām)? For that reason they prevailed in battle (tena te ’bhyadhikā yudhi)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

धूम्राक्षेण समागम्य माणिभद्रो महारणे ।
मुसलेनोरसि क्रोधात् ताडितो न च कम्पितः ॥ १० ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

धूम्राक्षेण समागम्य माणिभद्रो महारणे ।
मुसलेनोरसि क्रोधात् ताडितो न च कम्पितः ॥ १० ॥

धूम्राक्षेण समागम्य मणभद्रो महारणे ।
मुसलेनोरसि क्रोधात्ताडितो न च कम्पितः ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस महासमरमें धूम्राक्षने आकर क्रोधपूर्वक माणिभद्रकी छातीमें मूसलका प्रहार किया; किंतु इससे वे विचलित नहीं हुए ॥ १० ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आ महासङ्ग्रामदल्लि धूम्राक्षनु बन्दु क्रोधदिन्द मणिभद्रन ऎदॆगॆ मुसलदिन्द प्रहरिसिदनु. आदरॆ इदरिन्द अवनु विचलितनागलिल्ल.॥10॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Encountering Dhūmrākṣa in that great battle (dhūmrākṣeṇa samāgamya … mahāraṇe), Maṇibhadra was struck fiercely upon the chest with an iron pestle (musalenorasi krodhāt tāḍitaḥ), yet did not even waver (na ca kampitaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततो गदां समाविध्य माणिभद्रेण राक्षसः ।
धूम्राक्षस्ताडितो मूर्ध्नि विह्वलः स पपात ह ॥ ११ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततो गदां समाविध्य माणिभद्रेण राक्षसः ।
धूम्राक्षस्ताडितो मूर्ध्नि विह्वलः स पपात ह ॥ ११ ॥

ततो गदां समाविध्य मणिभद्रेण राक्षसः ।

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

7.15.1–3 (Kubera Summons General Maṇibhadra)

Introducing Sarga 15 as ‘the seizure of Puṣpaka’ (अथ पुष्पकग्रहः, Rāma Varmā), the narrative resumes directly after Rāvaṇa’s slaughter of Sūryabhānu and the yakṣas’ rout in Sarga 14. All three commentators—Rāma Varmā (Tilaka), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi), and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa)—identify माणिचारम् in 7.15.1d as Kubera’s chief commander Maṇibhadra (माणिचारो माणिभद्रः), while Tilaka and Śiromaṇi normalize the epic absolutive लक्ष्य as लक्षयित्वा (‘having perceived’). Charged to become the shelter (शरणमाश्रयः, Śivasahāya) of his beleaguered warriors, Maṇibhadra leads four thousand elite yakṣas back into the fray.

7.15.4–5 (Hawk-Like Fury and Elliptical Battle-Cries)

Wielding six heavy shock weapons—maces, pestles, darts, spears, javelins, and hammers—the yakṣas counterattack with hawk-like speed (श्येनवल्लघु क्षिप्रम्). Śivasahāya supplies the implied martial objects behind each abrupt, clipped cry in 7.15.5cd: ‘Give me combat!’ (प्रयच्छ युद्धमिति शेषः), ‘I have no wish to turn back!’ (नेच्छामि निवर्तितुमिति शेषः), and ‘Yield your weapon—or let this foe’s weapon be hewn down!’ (दीयतां स्वशस्त्रम् … छिद्यतामस्य शस्त्रमिति शेषः). Such staccato imperatives vividly convey the breathless din of hand-to-hand melee.

7.15.6 (Celestial Spectators of the Tumult)

Hovering in the sky above Kailāsa, the gods, gandharvas, and Vedic seers (brahmavādinaḥ) watch the clash in supreme amazement (परं विस्मयमागमन्). Although omitted in the Baroda CE, verses 7.15.5–6 heighten the heroic mood (vīra-rasa) in the Southern Recension: by building up the ferocity and discipline of Maṇibhadra’s counteroffensive before heaven’s eyes, the poet sharpens the dramatic shock of the very next verses, where Rāvaṇa’s ministers annihilate all four thousand yakṣas in an instant.

7.15.7–8 (Exact Annihilation of Four Thousand Yakṣas)

The poet tallies the rout with stark precision: Prahasta slays one thousand, Mahodara another thousand, and the battle-hungry Mārīca fells the remaining two thousand in a single blink (निमेषान्तरमात्रेण)—erasing Maṇibhadra’s entire division. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) summarizes each commander’s toll verbatim, underscoring the lethal stature of Rāvaṇa’s senior generals (all of whom Rāma’s host would later confront in Book VI). Agastya’s framing vocatives राजन् (7.15.8) and पुरुषव्याघ्र (7.15.9) gently remind the listener that this ancient campaign is being narrated in Rāma’s court.

7.15.9 (Straightforward Valor Versus Demonic Māyā)

Pausing mid-battle, Agastya explains why righteous bravery succumbed so abruptly. Śivasahāya unfolds the rhetorical idiom क्व च … क्व च: an unbridgeable gulf (अनयोर्महदन्तरम्) divides the candid, straightforward fighting of the yakṣas (यक्षाणामार्जवं सरलता यस्मिंस्तद्युद्धम्) from the rākṣasas’ reliance on deceptive sorcery (मायाबलाश्रयम्). Precisely for that reason, ते—explicitly glossed as the rākṣasas (ते राक्षसाः) by Rāma Varmā (Tilaka)—proved superior in combat, foreshadowing Rāvaṇa’s own resort to māyā against Kubera later in this sarga.

7.15.10–12 (Maṇibhadra Fells Dhūmrākṣa, Provoking Rāvaṇa)

Undismayed though his troops lie slain, Maṇibhadra proves his individual mettle against Dhūmrākṣa. Absorbing a furious pestle-blow on the chest without flinching (उरसि मुसलेन ताडितोऽपि न कम्पितः, Śivasahāya), he grips and swings his mace firmly (गदां समाविध्य दृढं गृहीत्वा, Śivasahāya) to crack Dhūmrākṣa’s skull. Seeing his commander drop blood-drenched to the earth (पतितं शोणितोक्षितम्), Rāvaṇa personally storms into the duel, escalating the battle toward its climactic encounter between brothers.

7.15.13–15 (The Etiology of Pārśvamauli)

Pierced by Maṇibhadra’s three spears, Rāvaṇa retaliates with his mace—explicitly supplied as गदया by both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa)—smashing Maṇibhadra’s crown (मुकुटस्थले) askew to one side (एकपार्श्वमागतम्). On line 15ab, Govindarāja clarifies that though hard-pressed by the battling Maṇibhadra, Rāvaṇa stood unshaken (युध्यमानेन माणिभद्रेण विष्टब्धो निरुद्धो न व्यकम्पत), leaving the yakṣa forever named Pārśvamauli. Rāma Varmā identifies the ensuing roar (संनादः) as the rākṣasas’ shout (रक्षसाम्), while Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) links it to Maṇibhadra’s retreat from battle.

7.15.16 (Kubera’s Retinue of Treasures and Ministers)

Drawn by the tumult on Kailāsa, Kubera himself enters the battlefield mace in hand. All three commentators carefully distinguish the two pairs flanking him: Rāma Varmā, Śivasahāya, and Govindarāja concur that पद्मशङ्खसमावृतः denotes the presiding deities of the divine nidhis Padma and Śaṅkha (शङ्खपद्मनिध्यभिमानिदेवैर्वृतः), while Tilaka and Śiromaṇi identify Śukra and Prauṣṭhapada as treasure-guarding ministers (निधिरक्षकमन्त्रिभ्याम्) and Bhūṣaṇa glosses them simply as counsellors (मन्त्रिणौ). Advancing with these sacred guardians rather than sorcery, Kubera embodies legitimate royal sovereignty confronting usurping aggression.

7.15.17–18 (Viśravas’s Curse and the Warning of Hell)

All three commentators explain शापाद्विभ्रष्टगौरवम् in 7.15.17 by recalling Viśravas’s prenatal pronouncement to Kaikasī (Sarga 9) that her sons would be fierce of nature (पितृकृतदारुणस्वभावो भविष्यतीति शापात्), leaving Rāvaṇa devoid of the reverence due an elder (वन्दनादिप्रयोजकज्येष्ठगौरवरहितम्). Speaking words befitting Brahmā’s line (पैतामहे कुले उत्पत्तेः), Kubera warns in 7.15.18 that spurning restraint from evil (असद्वृत्तात्, Tilaka) will bring bitter remorse once Rāvaṇa’s transgressions ripen in hell (परिपाकं प्राप्य … परितप्स्यसे).

7.15.19 (The Maxim of Swallowed Poison)

Answering the implicit objection—how can deeds bring hell if the doer perceives no sin in them?—Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explains 7.15.19 as a proof of objective karma: just as a fool who swallows poison out of ignorance (अविवेकात्) detects no harm at the moment, yet discovers its lethality once digestion completes (परिणामान्ते रूपान्तरप्राप्तौ), so too even unrecognized wrongdoing inevitably yields its fruit (अज्ञातस्यापि कर्मणः फलं भवतीति सिद्धम्). Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) likewise calls this a dṛṣṭānta (illustrative analogy) of harm perceived only upon karmic maturation.

7.15.20 (Divine Aversion and Moral Blindness)

The commentators offer two illuminating constructions of 7.15.20. Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) take धर्मयुक्तेन with Rāvaṇa’s absent virtue: because dharma has vanished from him, the gods—Brahmā, Rudra, and the rest—turn away from him (धर्माभावात्त्वयि विमुखा भवन्ति), and through that divine estrangement (देवतावैमुख्येन) he has been reduced to such cruelty (क्रूरं भावम्) that he cannot see his doom. Śivasahāya instead links धर्मयुक्तेन to the dharma-abiding gods who take no delight in Rāvaṇa’s violence against sages, signaling that his hour of ruin has arrived (तव विनाशकालः संप्राप्त इति सूचितम्).

7.15.21–24 (Reverence for Elders and the Impermanent Body)

On 7.15.21, Rāma Varmā notes that ‘mother, father, and teacher’ stand as synecdoche for all venerable elders (मात्रादिपदं पूज्योपलक्षणम्)—pointedly including Kubera himself—while Śivasahāya observes that scorning them delivers the offender to Yama. On 7.15.22, all three gloss तपोर्जनम् as earning austerity (तपःसंपादनम्, specified by Śivasahāya as penance pleasing to the Supreme Self, परमात्मतोषकतपसः संग्रहम्) before this fleeting body perishes. Verses 7.15.23–24, preserved in Govindarāja’s recension, crystallize Kubera’s discourse into two crisp subhāṣita maxims on dharma and self-inflicted suffering.

7.15.25–26 (Right Mind Born of Service and Merit)

Explaining why Rāvaṇa’s intellect remains blinded, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi), and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) all converge on छन्दतः in 7.15.25: wholesome wisdom (शुभमतिः) never arises by mere self-will (स्वेच्छामात्रेण) without humble service to parents and teachers (पितृमात्रादिसेवां विना / गुर्वादिसेवामन्तरा). Deprived of such discipline, the fool reaps the exact fruit of forbidden acts—underscored by the proverbial line 25cd on fate’s compulsion—whereas intelligence, beauty, sons, and valor (7.15.26) are won solely through meritorious deeds (पुण्यकर्मभिः).

7.15.27–28 (Severing Speech and Routing the Ministers)

Concluding his moral rebuke, Kubera declares that silence is the sole settled verdict toward the incorrigibly wicked—glossed uniformly by all three commentators as असद्वृत्तेषु सतां भाषणाभाव एव निर्णयः. Remarkably, reading passive plural एवमुक्ताः in 7.15.28, Rāma Varmā, Śivasahāya, and Govindarāja all construe it to mean that Rāvaṇa’s ministers—Mārīca and the rest—were told they too were unworthy of discourse like their master (रावणवदेव यूयमप्यसंभाष्या इत्युक्ताः) before Kubera’s fierce blows drove them turning their backs in rout across the mountainside.

7.15.29–30 (The Brothers Locked in Stalemate)

Immediately after scattering the ministers (ततो विद्रावणानन्तरम्, Śivasahāya), Kubera brings his heavy mace crashing down upon Rāvaṇa’s head, yet the boon-fortified king refuses to budge even a single step (स्थानान्न प्रकम्पितः). Verse 7.15.30 portrays the fratricidal duel at peak physical equilibrium: trading thunderous blows in close quarters, neither wrathful half-brother yields to dizziness or exhaustion (न विह्वलौ न च श्रान्तौ), proving Rāvaṇa’s immense physical resilience and compelling Kubera to escalate from maces to celestial missiles.

7.15.31–32 (Elemental Missiles and Descent into Illusion)

Shifting from hand-to-hand combat to divine astras, Kubera launches the fire-missile (आग्नेयमस्त्रम्), which Rāvaṇa neutralizes with Varuṇa’s water-missile (वारुणेन). Unable to break his brother cleanly, Rāvaṇa resorts to demonic sorcery (राक्षसीं मायाम्). Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) glosses मायां प्रविष्टः as becoming invisible (अदृश्यत्वं प्राप्तः), while Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) specifies विनाशाय as ‘for Dhanada’s destruction’ (धनदनाशाय) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) as ‘for the ruin of his foes’ (अरीणां विध्वंसाय), unleashing a hundred thousand phantoms.

7.15.33–34 (The Protean Phantasmagoria)

Verses 7.15.33–34 enumerate eight shifting guises—tiger, boar, storm-cloud, mountain, ocean, tree, yakṣa, and daitya—spanning beast, inanimate nature, and superhuman spirit. Both Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya untangle the compressed clause बहूनि च करोति स्म दृश्यन्ते न त्वसौ in 7.15.34ab: Rāvaṇa kept fabricating manifold illusory forms (बहूनि मायारूपाणि), and only those conjured tigers and phantoms remained visible to the eye, never Rāvaṇa’s own true body (मायाप्रयुक्तव्याघ्रादय एव दृश्यन्ते न त्वसौ), screening his ambush from Kubera’s sight.

7.15.35 (Kubera Felled Like an Aśoka Tree)

Connecting 7.15.34cd directly to 35ab, Śivasahāya identifies the महदस्त्रम् seized by Rāvaṇa as the colossal mace itself (महदस्त्रं गदां प्रतिगृह्य व्याविध्य प्रक्षिप्य धनदं मूर्ध्नि जघान). Blind-sided from within māyā, Kubera collapses blood-soaked to the earth—confirming Agastya’s earlier judgment in 7.15.9 that rākṣasa deceit alone overcame yakṣa valor. Vālmīki’s poignant upamā—कृत्तमूल इवाशोकः (‘like an aśoka tree cut at its root’, glossed तदभिधवृक्ष इव by Śivasahāya)—pairs Kubera’s scarlet blood with crimson blossoms while echoing the sorrow (śoka) now befalling Alakā.

7.15.36 (Revival by the Divine Nidhis and Ārṣa Sandhi)

As Kubera falls unconscious, his guardian Treasures immediately intervene. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi), and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) all gloss निधिभिः as the presiding deities of the divine nidhis (निधिदेवताभिः), who convey Kubera to the celestial Nandana grove and restore his breath (नन्दनमानीय उच्छ्वासितः). Remarkably, all three commentators pause on the striking coalescence धनदोच्छ्वासितः (for धनद उच्छ्वासितः) to register it explicitly as an archaic epic sandhi (सन्धिरार्षः).

7.15.37 (Puṣpaka Seized as a Trophy of Conquest)

Exulting over his humbled elder brother, Rāvaṇa appropriates Puṣpaka. Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja gloss जयलक्षणम् as ‘a visible token proclaiming his victory’ (जयचिह्नम्, supplying ख्यापयन्तीति शेषः), underscoring that Rāvaṇa claims the celestial car not merely as spoil, but as a monument to his triumph over a World-Guardian. Śivasahāya construes the entire five-verse expanse from 7.15.37 through 7.15.41 as one grand periodic sentence (श्लोकपञ्चकमेकान्वयि) governed by तस्य विमानं जग्राह.

7.15.38–39 (Jeweled Splendor of the Flying Palace)

Beginning a lavish five-verse ekphrasis of the chariot that Rāma himself will one day ride back to Ayodhyā (and return to Kubera in Sarga 41), Vālmīki fuses celestial architecture with living magic. Śivasahāya unpacks each compound systematically: वैडूर्यमणितोरणम् denotes outer portals of cat’s-eye gem (बहिर्द्वारम्), सर्वकामफलद्रुमम् wish-yielding trees growing aboard it, and तप्तकाञ्चनवेदिकम् platforms of refined gold; interestingly, he reads कामरूपं विहङ्गमम् as a bahuvrīhi (‘housing shape-shifting birds’), alongside the simpler sense of a shape-shifting sky-chariot.

7.15.40–41 (Brahmā’s Masterpiece and All-Season Comfort)

Completing the five-verse portrait of Puṣpaka, both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) clarify ब्रह्मणा परिनिर्मितम् in 7.15.40d: Brahmā caused the vehicle to be fashioned through the divine artisan Viśvakarman (ब्रह्मणा विश्वकर्मणा परिनिर्मापितम्). Śivasahāya glosses भक्तिचित्रम् as adorned with well-proportioned architectural chambers (यथोचितगृहविभागेन चित्रम्), and explains न शीतं न चोष्णम् in 7.15.41cd as free from extremes of biting cold or scorching heat (अतिशीतभिन्नम् … अत्युष्णभिन्नम्), keeping it comfortable across every season.

7.15.42 (The Intoxication of Hubris and Kataka’s Count)

Boarding the self-moving car won by force (वीर्यनिर्जितम्), Rāvaṇa yields completely to swollen pride (दर्पोत्सेकात्सुदुर्मतिः): having felled his elder brother, he imagines all three worlds already subdued (त्रिभुवनं जितं मेने, Śivasahāya), and descends from Kailāsa straight toward his humiliating encounter with Śiva in Sarga 16. At the sarga’s end, Rāma Varmā records that the ancient exegete Kataka counted only thirty-three verses in Sarga 15 (त्रयस्त्रिंशच्छ्लोकसंख्यामत्र सर्गे लिलेख), closely echoing the shorter text preserved in Baroda CE.

7.15.43 (The Puṣpitāgrā Finale—Fire in the Hall)

Shifting from Anuṣṭubh into the stately lyric rhythm of Puṣpitāgrā, Vālmīki seals the sarga with a luminous closing tableau. Śivasahāya paraphrases the stanza concisely: gleaming with spotless diadem and garlands (विमलकिरीटहारवान्) after winning his vast triumph, Rāvaṇa shines atop the supreme aerial car like a bright fire set within an assembly hall (सदसि गतः सन् अनल इव रराज). The image captures Rāvaṇa’s perilous grandeur—dazzling in sovereign splendor, yet a consuming flame poised to scorch the cosmos.

निर्मल किरीट हारगळिन्द विभूषितनाद आ प्रतापि निशाचरनु तन्न तेजदिन्द आ महाविजयवन्नु पडॆदु, आ उत्तम विमानदल्लि आरूढनागि यज्ञमण्टपदल्लि प्रज्वलित यज्ञनारायणनन्तॆ शोभिसतॊडगिदनु.॥44॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये उत्तरकाण्डे पञ्चदशः सर्गः ॥ १५ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके उत्तरकाण्डमें पंद्रहवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ १५ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद उत्तर काण्डदल्लि हदिनैदनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु. ॥15॥
