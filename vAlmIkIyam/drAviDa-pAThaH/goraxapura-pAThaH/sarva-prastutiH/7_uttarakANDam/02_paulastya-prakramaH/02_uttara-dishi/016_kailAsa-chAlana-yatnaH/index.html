सर्वाष् टीकाः
भागसूचना
  1. नन्दीश्वरका रावणको शाप, भगवान् शङ्करद्वारा रावणका मान-भङ्ग तथा उनसे चन्द्रहास नामक खड्गकी प्राप्ति
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having vanquished Kubera and seized Puṣpaka, Rāvaṇa flies to the radiant golden reed-forest of Skanda’s birth; halted suddenly near Mount Kailāsa, he and his minister Mārīca ponder the unseen power arresting the chariot.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स जित्वा धनदं राम भ्रातरं राक्षसाधिपः ।
महासेनप्रसूतिं तद् ययौ शरवणं महत् ॥ १ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स जित्वा धनदं राम भ्रातरं राक्षसाधिपः ।
महासेनप्रसूतिं तद् ययौ शरवणं महत् ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

(अगस्त्यजी कहते हैं—) रघुकुलनन्दन राम! अपने भाई कुबेरको जीतकर राक्षसराज दशग्रीव ‘शरवण’ नामसे प्रसिद्ध सरकंडोंके विशाल वनमें गया, जहाँ महासेन कार्तिकेयजीकी उत्पत्ति हुई थी ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

(अगस्त्यरु हेळुत्तारॆ - ) राघव! रावणनु कुबेरनन्नु गॆद्दु, महासेननु हुट्टिद शरवण वॆम्ब विशालवाद नूजॆहुल्लिन वनक्कॆ होदनु.॥1॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having vanquished his brother Dhanada [Kubera], O Rāma, that sovereign of the Rākṣasas (rākṣasādhipaḥ) journeyed unto that great reed-forest (śaravaṇaṃ mahat), the birthplace of Mahāsena [Skanda] (mahāsena-prasūtim).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अथापश्यद् दशग्रीवो रौक्मं शरवणं महत् ।
गभस्तिजालसंवीतं द्वितीयमिव भास्करम् ॥ २ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अथापश्यद् दशग्रीवो रौक्मं शरवणं महत् ।
गभस्तिजालसंवीतं द्वितीयमिव भास्करम् ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

वहाँ पहुँचकर दशग्रीवने सुवर्णमयी कान्तिसे युक्त उस विशाल शरवण (सरकंडोंके जंगल)-को देखा, जो किरण-समूहोंसे व्याप्त होनेके कारण दूसरे सूर्यदेवके समान प्रकाशित हो रहा था ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अल्लिगॆ होगि दशग्रीवनु सुवर्णमय कान्तियिन्द युक्तवाद, किरणसमूहगळिन्द व्याप्तवागि इन्नॊब्ब सूर्यनन्तॆ कङ्गॊळिसुव आ शरवण वनवन्नु नोडिदनु.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then Daśagrīva beheld (athāpaśyad) that vast golden reed-forest (raukmaṃ śaravaṇaṃ mahat), wreathed in a network of radiant beams (gabhasti-jāla-saṃvītam) like a second sun (dvitīyam iva bhāskaram).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स पर्वतं समारुह्य कञ्चिद् रम्यवनान्तरम् ।
प्रेक्षते पुष्पकं तत्र राम विष्टम्भितं तदा ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स पर्वतं समारुह्य कञ्चिद् रम्यवनान्तरम् ।
प्रेक्षते पुष्पकं तत्र राम विष्टम्भितं तदा ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उसके पास ही कोई पर्वत था, जहाँकी वनस्थली बड़ी रमणीय थी । श्रीराम! जब वह उसपर चढ़ने लगा, तब देखता है कि पुष्पकविमानकी गति रुक गयी ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अदर बळियल्ले इद्द पर्वत, वनस्थळ तुम्बा रमणीयवागित्तु. श्रीरामा! अदन्नु हत्तलु तॊडगिदाग पुष्पक विमानद गति निन्तुहोदन्तॆ कण्डितु.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having ascended near a certain mountain graced with lovely woodland glades (ramya-vanāntaram), he beheld the Puṣpaka aerial car there, O Rāma, suddenly brought to a standstill (viṣṭambhitam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

विष्टब्धं किमिदं कस्मान्नागमत् कामगं कृतम् ।
अचिन्तयद् राक्षसेन्द्रः सचिवैस्तैः समावृतः ॥ ४ ॥
किन्निमित्तमिच्छया मे नेदं गच्छति पुष्पकम् ।
पर्वतस्योपरिष्ठस्य कर्मेदं कस्यचिद् भवेत् ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

विष्टब्धं किमिदं कस्मान्नागमत् कामगं कृतम् ।
अचिन्तयद् राक्षसेन्द्रः सचिवैस्तैः समावृतः ॥ ४ ॥
किन्निमित्तमिच्छया मे नेदं गच्छति पुष्पकम् ।
पर्वतस्योपरिष्ठस्य कर्मेदं कस्यचिद् भवेत् ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

तब वह राक्षसराज अपने उन मन्त्रियोंके साथ मिलकर विचार करने लगा—‘क्या कारण है कि यह पुष्पकविमान रुक गया? यह तो स्वामीकी इच्छाके अनुसार चलनेवाला बनाया गया है । फिर आगे क्यों नहीं बढ़ता? कौन-सा ऐसा कारण बन गया, जिससे यह पुष्पकविमान मेरी इच्छाके अनुसार नहीं चल रहा है? सम्भव है, इस पर्वतके ऊपर कोई रहता हो, उसीका यह कर्म हो सकता है?’ ॥ ४-५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग आ राक्षसराजनु तन्न मन्त्रिगळॊन्दिगॆ ई पुष्पक विमान एकॆ निन्तिदॆ ऎन्दु विचार माडतॊडगिदनु. इदादरो ऒडॆयन इच्छॆगनुसार नडॆयुवुदु. हीगिद्दरू मुन्दक्कॆ एकॆ होगुवुदिल्ल? नन्न ई पुष्पकविमान नन्न इच्छॆगनुसार नडॆयदिरलु कारणवेनु? ई पर्वतद मेलॆ यारो इद्दु, अवनदे ई कर्मविरबहुदु.॥4-5॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

4: Seeing Puṣpaka halted (viṣṭabdham)—though fashioned to move at will (kāmagaṃ kṛtam), now rendered immobile (agamam)—the lord of the Rākṣasas, surrounded by those ministers of his (sacivais taiḥ samāvṛtaḥ), reflected deeply:

5: “For what reason (kin-nimittam) does this Puṣpaka not move at my will (icchayā me)? This must surely be the doing (karmedaṃ … bhavet) of someone dwelling atop this mountain (parvatasyopariṣṭhasya)!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततोऽब्रवीत् तदा राम मारीचो बुद्धिकोविदः ।
नेदं निष्कारणं राजन् पुष्पकं यन्न गच्छति ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततोऽब्रवीत् तदा राम मारीचो बुद्धिकोविदः ।
नेदं निष्कारणं राजन् पुष्पकं यन्न गच्छति ॥ ६ ॥

ततोऽब्रवीत्तदा राम मारीचो युद्धकोविदः ।
नेदं निष्कारणं राजन् पुष्पकं यन्न गच्छति ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

श्रीराम! तब बुद्धिकुशल मारीचने कहा—‘राजन्! यह पुष्पकविमान जो आगे नहीं बढ़ रहा है, इसमें कुछ-न-कुछ कारण अवश्य है । अकारण ही ऐसी घटना घटित हो गयी हो, यह बात नहीं है ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

श्रीरामा! आग बुद्धिकुशल मारीचनु हेळिदनु- राजा! ई पुष्पकविमान मुन्दॆ होगदिरुवु दरल्लि यावुदे कारण अवश्यवागिदॆ. कारणविल्लदॆ हीगॆ ऎन्दिगू आगलारदु.॥6॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thereupon, O Rāma, the shrewd-minded Mārīca (mārīco buddhi-kovidaḥ) spoke to him: “It is not without cause (nedaṃ niṣkāraṇam), O King, that this Puṣpaka refuses to go forward (puṣpakaṃ yan na gacchati).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

As Mārīca wonders whether Puṣpaka refuses to serve anyone save Kubera, Śiva’s formidable attendant Nandīśvara abruptly appears to forbid entry onto Kailāsa, provoking a furious Rāvaṇa to descend and challenge Śaṅkara.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अथवा पुष्पकमिदं धनदान्नान्यवाहनम् ।
अतो निस्पन्दमभवद् धनाध्यक्षविनाकृतम् ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 अथवा पुष्पकमिदं धनदान्नान्यवाहनम् ।
अतो निस्पन्दमभवद् धनाध्यक्षविनाकृतम् ॥ ७ ॥

1 अथवा पुष्पकमिदं धनदान्नान्यवाहनम् ।
1 अतो निष्पन्दमभवद् धनाध्यक्ष विनाकृतम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘अथवा यह पुष्पकविमान कुबेरके सिवा दूसरेका वाहन नहीं हो सकता, इसीलिये उनके बिना यह निश्चेष्ट हो गया है’ ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अथवा ई पुष्पकविमान कुबेरनल्लदॆ बेरॆयवर वाहन आगलारदु; अदक्कागि अवनिल्लदॆ इदु निश्चेष्टितवागिदॆ.॥7॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Or else (athavā), this Puṣpaka will serve as the vehicle of none save Dhanada (dhanadān nānya-vāhanam); therefore, deprived of the Lord of Wealth (dhanādhyakṣa-vinākṛtam), it has become motionless (niṣpandam abhavat).”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

इति वाक्यान्तरे तस्य करालः कृष्णपिङ्गलः ।
वामनो विकटो मुण्डी नन्दी ह्रस्वभुजो बली ॥ ८ ॥
ततः पार्श्वमुपागम्य भवस्यानुचरोऽब्रवीत् ।
नन्दीश्वरो वचश्चेदं राक्षसेन्द्रमशङ्कितः ॥ ९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 इति वाक्यान्तरे तस्य करालः कृष्णपिङ्गलः ।
वामनो विकटो मुण्डी नन्दी ह्रस्वभुजो बली ॥ ८ ॥
ततः पार्श्वमुपागम्य भवस्यानुचरोऽब्रवीत् ।
नन्दीश्वरो वचश्चेदं राक्षसेन्द्रमशङ्कितः ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उसकी इस बातके बीचमें ही भगवान् शङ्करके पार्षद नन्दीश्वर रावणके पास आ पहुँचे, जो देखनेमें बड़े विकराल थे । उनकी अङ्गकान्ति काले एवं पिङ्गल वर्णकी थी । वे नाटे कदके विकट रूपवाले थे । उनका मस्तक मुण्डित और भुजाएँ छोटी-छोटी थीं । वे बड़े बलवान् थे । नन्दीने निःशङ्क होकर राक्षसराज दशग्रीवसे इस प्रकार कहा— ॥ ८-९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अष्टरल्ले भगवान् शङ्करन पार्षद नन्दीश्वरनु रावणन बळिगॆ बन्दनु. अवनु नोडलु विकारनागिद्दनु. अङ्गकान्तियु कप्पु हागू पिङ्गलवागित्तु. कुळ्ळगागिद्दु, विकटरूपदिन्द इद्दनु. बोळुतलॆयिद्दु भुजगळु चिक्कदागिद्दवु. बहळ बलिष्ठनाद आ नन्दीश्वरनु निःशङ्कनागि राक्षसेश्वर रावणनल्लि हेळिदनु.॥8-9॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

8: Even in the midst of those words of his (iti vākyāntare tasya), fierce-visaged, dark-tawny (kṛṣṇa-piṅgalaḥ), dwarfish, deformed, shaven-headed (muṇḍī), stoop-armed (prahva-bhujaḥ), and mighty—Nandī,

9: —that attendant of Bhava [Śiva] (bhavasyānucaraḥ), Nandīśvara, approached his side (pārśvam upāgamya) and fearlessly (aśaṅkitaḥ) spoke these words unto the lord of the Rākṣasas:

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

निवर्तस्व दशग्रीव शैले क्रीडति शङ्करः ।
सुपर्णनागयक्षाणां देवगन्धर्वरक्षसाम् ॥ १० ॥
सर्वेषामेव भूतानामगम्यः पर्वतः कृतः ।

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

निवर्तस्व दशग्रीव शैले क्रीडति शङ्करः ।
सुपर्णनागयक्षाणां देवगन्धर्वरक्षसाम् ॥ १० ॥
सर्वेषामेव भूतानामगम्यः पर्वतः कृतः ।

निवर्तस्व दशग्रीव शैले क्रीडती शङ्करः ।
सुपर्णनाग यक्षाणां देवगन्धर्व रक्षसाम् ॥
सर्वेषामेव भूतानामगम्यः पर्वतः कृतः ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘दशग्रीव! लौट जाओ । इस पर्वतपर भगवान् शङ्कर क्रीडा करते हैं । यहाँ सुपर्ण, नाग, यक्ष, देवता, गन्धर्व और राक्षस सभी प्राणियोंका आना-जाना बंद कर दिया गया है’ ॥ १० १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

दशग्रीवने! मरळिहोगु. ई पर्वतदल्लि भगवान् शङ्करनु क्रीडिसुत्तिरुवनु. इल्लिगॆ सुपर्ण, नाग, यक्ष, देवता, गन्धर्व, राक्षस हीगॆ याव प्राणियु बन्दु होगुवुदु निषेधिसलागिदॆ.॥10½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Turn back, O Daśagrīva (nivartasva daśagrīva)! Upon this mountain Śaṅkara is sporting (śaile krīḍati śaṅkaraḥ). To Suparṇas, Nāgas, and Yakṣas, to gods, Gandharvas, and Rākṣasas—

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

इति नन्दिवचः श्रुत्वा क्रोधात् कम्पितकुण्डलः ॥ ११ ॥
रोषात् तु ताम्रनयनः पुष्पकादवरुह्य सः ।
कोऽयं शङ्कर इत्युक्त्वा शैलमूलमुपागतः ॥ १२ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 इति नन्दिवचः श्रुत्वा क्रोधात् कम्पितकुण्डलः ॥ ११ ॥
रोषात् तु ताम्रनयनः पुष्पकादवरुह्य सः ।

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

7.16.1–2 (The Golden Śaravaṇa of Skanda)

Opening the famed Kailāsa episode immediately after Rāvaṇa’s seizure of Puṣpaka in Sarga 15, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) introduces this canto as the sequel to the chariot’s capture (पुष्पकविमानानयनानन्तरकालिकं वृत्तमाह), while Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) observes that अपश्यत् and the following verses expand the brief summary given at the close of Sarga 15 (पूर्वसर्गान्तोक्तसंक्षेपप्रपञ्चनम्). All three commentators identify महासेनप्रसूतिम् as the sacred reed-thicket where Skanda was born (महासेनस्य स्कन्दस्य प्रसूतिर्जन्म यस्मात्तच्छरवणम्, Rāma Varmā; स्कन्दप्रसूतिस्थानम्, Govindarāja). Its solar simile (upamā)—wreathed in rays like a second sun—foreshadows the incandescent sanctity of Śiva’s mountain precinct.

7.16.3–4 (Puṣpaka’s Sudden Arrest)

Reaching the slopes of Kailāsa, Rāvaṇa encounters his first inexplicable check. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja gloss विष्टम्भितम् identically as ‘having its motion arrested’ (निरुद्धसञ्चारम्), while Śivasahāya construes verses 2–3 together as a single syntactic unit (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी) and notably interprets वन in रम्यवनान्तरम् in its lexical sense of ‘waters’ (वनानि जलान्यन्तरे यस्य). On 7.16.4, Rāma Varmā unpacks the paradox of कामगं कृतम् (‘fashioned to move at will’): constructed to obey its master’s every thought (स्वामिचित्तानुकूलसञ्चारवत्कृतम्), the chariot now stands frozen in defiance of its conqueror.

7.16.5–6 (Rāvaṇa’s Conjecture and Mārīca’s Confirmation)

On 7.16.5, Rāma Varmā observes that after wondering why Puṣpaka disobeys his wish, Rāvaṇa immediately infers the presence of a superior power (स्वयमेव तर्कयति)—an arresting act (स्तम्भनरूपं कर्म) worked by some personage dwelling atop the mountain (पर्वतोपरिभागे स्थितस्य, Śivasahāya; कस्य पुरुषस्य, Govindarāja). In 7.16.6, Rāma Varmā notes that the astute counselor Mārīca echoes and reinforces his king’s deduction (मारीचोऽपि तदुक्तमेवानुवदति), while Śivasahāya paraphrases his double negative नेदं निष्कारणम् affirmatively: there is assuredly a definite cause (किञ्चित्कारणमस्त्येवेत्यर्थः) behind the aerial car’s stoppage.

7.16.7 (Mārīca’s Second Hypothesis)

Continuing his counsel from 7.16.6, Mārīca ventures a plausible alternative (कारणमूहयित्वाऽऽह, Rāma Varmā): perhaps Puṣpaka is loyal solely to its rightful owner Kubera (धनदादन्यस्य वाहनं नेत्यर्थः) and therefore refuses to move when separated from the Lord of Wealth. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) similarly glosses निष्पन्दम् as ‘bereft of motion’ (गमनरहितमभवत्). Dramatically, Mārīca’s suggestion subtly highlights the illegitimacy of Rāvaṇa’s plunder even before the true divine impediment manifests—showing that stolen celestial splendor does not automatically bend to brute force.

7.16.8–9 (The Sudden Apparition of Nandīśvara)

Before Mārīca can even conclude, Śiva’s dread chamberlain intervenes. Śivasahāya construes verses 8–9 as a single four-quarter period (अर्धचतुष्टयमेकान्वयि), glossing वाक्यान्तरे as ‘right in the midst of Mārīca’s speech’ (कथनमध्यसमये), करालः as ‘terrifying’ (भयंकरः), and वामनः as ‘hunchbacked/dwarfish’ (कुब्जः). Rāma Varmā clarifies the elliptical syntax of 7.16.9 by supplying ‘Rāvaṇa’s side’ (पार्श्वे रावणस्येति शेषः) and linking अशङ्कितः directly with Nandī’s approach: entirely undaunted by the conqueror of Kubera, the humble-looking attendant confronts the demon-king as an equal.

7.16.10–12 (The Interdict on Kailāsa and Rāvaṇa’s Fury)

Construing 7.16.10–11ab together (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी), Śivasahāya sets forth Nandī’s firm proclamation that Kailāsa is closed to all celestials and demons while Śiva sports there, with Rāma Varmā supplying ‘by the Lord’ (ईश्वरेणेति शेषः) as the agent of कृतः in 7.16.11. Yet Rāvaṇa’s intoxicated arrogance cannot brook any boundary. Both Śivasahāya (construing 11cd–12 as one period) and Rāma Varmā (glossing शैलमूलमुपागतः as descending from the sky to the mountain’s base, अन्तरिक्षादवतीर्य प्राप्तः) capture his reckless hubris in the contemptuous sneer कोऽयं शङ्करः—‘Who is this Śaṅkara?’

7.16.13–14 (The Second Śaṅkara and Rāvaṇa’s Fatal Mockery)

Reaching the mountain’s base, Rāvaṇa beholds Nandī stationed close to Śiva (देवस्य शिवस्यादूरतः स्थितम्, Śivasahāya) and leaning on a gleaming trident like a second Śaṅkara. Yet blinded by pride of form, Rāvaṇa sees only a monkey’s face (vānara-mukha), treats him with scorn (अवज्ञाय अनादृत्य, Śivasahāya), and erupts into booming laughter (प्रहासं मुमुचे कृतवानित्यर्थः, Rāma Varmā) likened to a heavy thundercloud (satoya iva toyadaḥ). This single act of derision against a seemingly lowly outward appearance becomes a cardinal karmic turning point in the entire Rāmāyaṇa.

7.16.15–17 (Nandī’s Curse—The Origin of the Vānara Nemesis)

Verse 15 explicitly affirms Nandī’s divine potency as ‘a second body of Śaṅkara’ (शङ्करस्यापरा तनुः, noted by Śivasahāya). Construing 7.16.16–17 as a unified four-quarter sentence (अर्धचतुष्टयमेकान्वयि), Śivasahāya links the exact offense (yasmāt) to its poetic retribution (tasmāt): because Rāvaṇa mocked Nandī’s simian visage with a thunderbolt-like jeer, monkeys possessing Nandī’s very form, valor, and luster will arise to annihilate his lineage. Agastya thus reveals to Rāma the deeper cosmic root of the Yuddhakāṇḍa: the vānara army that overwhelmed Laṅkā was decreed here at Kailāsa.

7.16.18 (The Terrifying Martial Profile of the Vānaras)

Painting a prophetic portrait of the future monkey warriors—Hanūmān, Sugrīva, Nīla, and their hosts—Vālmīki packs six vivid epithets into a single Anuṣṭubh verse. Śivasahāya construes 7.16.18–19 together (अर्धचतुष्टयमेकान्वयि), analyzing मनःसम्पातरंहसः as ‘those whose speed equals the very flight of thought’ (मनःसम्पात इव रंहो वेगो येषाम्) and highlighting the striking simile शैला इव विसर्पिणः (‘striding forth like roaming mountains’). Needing neither forged steel nor sorcery, these claw-and-fang warriors will overturn the heavily fortified citadel of Rāvaṇa.

7.16.19–20 (Inward Pride, Outward Bulk, and Self-Inflicted Doom)

Concluding his prophecy, Nandī promises that the vānaras will dismantle both Rāvaṇa’s दर्प and उत्सेध. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) draws a crisp distinction: दर्पः is inward arrogance (आन्तरः), while उत्सेधः is towering bodily height and physical might (शारीरः; शरीरोन्नतत्वम्, Śivasahāya). Why does Nandī not strike him dead on the spot? Both commentators resolve 7.16.20 as a concessive statement: although Nandī could slay him instantly, Rāvaṇa is already morally dead through his own evil deeds (स्वदुष्कृतैर्हत एवासि, Rāma Varmā), reserved to be slain in future time by beings bearing Nandī’s very form (कालान्तरे मद्रूपैरेव हन्तव्यो भविष्यसीति भावः).

7.16.21–22 (Heaven’s Applause and Rāvaṇa’s Blind Defiance)

Immediately upon Nandī’s pronouncement (देवे नन्दीशे उदीरितवाक्ये सति, Śivasahāya), celestial drums thunder and flowers rain from the sky. Rāma Varmā explains the gods’ sudden jubilation: they rejoice at the certainty of Rāvaṇa’s ultimate downfall now sealed by Nandī’s curse (तच्छापेन रावणवधनिश्चयजसंतोषात्). Yet even this unmistakable cosmic omen leaves the demon-king wholly unchastened (नन्दिवाक्यमचिन्तयित्वा, Śivasahāya). Instead of retreating in awe, Rāvaṇa marches straight up to Kailāsa’s base, escalating verbal insolence into physical sacrilege.

7.16.23–24 (The Vow to Uproot Kailāsa)

Turning his wrath upon the mountain itself, Rāvaṇa addresses Nandī with the vocative गोपते, which Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) glosses as ‘O lord of bulls’ (हे वृषभपते). On यत्कृते in 7.16.23, Rāma Varmā explains that Rāvaṇa blames the mountain precisely as the pleasure-ground whose hosting of Śiva’s sport caused Puṣpaka’s arrest (यदाश्रयक्रीडानिमित्तं गच्छतो मम पुष्पकस्य गतिश्छिन्ना). In 7.16.24, his boast reaches peak dramatic irony: mocking Bhava for supposedly failing to recognize an approaching ‘source of peril’ (उपस्थितभयस्थानम्, Śivasahāya), Rāvaṇa himself stands wholly blind to the supreme majesty awaiting him above.

7.16.25–26 (Kailāsa Shaken and Pārvatī’s Embrace)

Executing his boast, Rāvaṇa slips his arms underneath the mountain—Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) explicitly glosses the locative पर्वते as ‘beneath the mountain’ (पर्वताधस्तात्)—and lifts the vast peak until it quakes (अत एव शैलः समकम्पत, Śivasahāya). As Śiva’s attendants reel, Pārvatī too feels the tremor and throws her arms around Maheśvara. Rāma Varmā notes the exact grammatical force of आश्लिष्टा in 7.16.26 as an active past participle (आश्लिष्टवती । कर्तरि क्तः), capturing the iconic sculptural motif (Rāvaṇānugrahamūrti) celebrated across Indian temple art.

7.16.27–28 (The Effortless Great Toe of Mahādeva)

Vālmīki juxtaposes Rāvaṇa’s utmost physical exertion against Śiva’s serene omnipotence: without even rising or shifting posture, Hara presses Kailāsa back down with a single great toe in sheer playfulness (पादाङ्गुष्ठेन लीलयेव पीडयामास, Śivasahāya). Immediately Rāvaṇa’s arms beneath the mountain are pinned and crushed (पर्वतस्याधोभागगता भुजाः पीडिता आसन्, Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja; Śivasahāya reads शैलस्तम्भोपमाः, ‘like mountain-pillars’). Witnessing their seemingly invincible monarch immobilized by a casual flick of a toe, his counselors stand transfixed in shock.

7.16.29–30 (The Roar Like Doomsday’s Thunderbolt)

Pain and wounded rage fuse into a stupendous cry (विरावो रोदनशब्दः, Śivasahāya, who construes 29–30ab as a unified period). Rāma Varmā explains that Rāvaṇa’s own ministers mistook his shriek for the shattering clash of thunderbolts at the dissolution of a cosmic age (युगक्षये जायमानं वज्रनिष्पेषं मेनिरे). Interpreting the compressed compound वर्त्मस्थचलिताः in 7.16.30cd, Rāma Varmā clarifies that at the moment of hearing that sound, the gods stationed along their celestial paths were dislodged from their tracks (वर्त्मस्थिता देवा वर्त्मभ्यः प्रचलिताः), while Śivasahāya identifies those paths specifically as the starry firmament (तारापथेषु स्थिताः).

7.16.31–33 (Cosmic Upheaval and the Ministers’ Counsel of Surrender)

Extending the cosmic shockwave of 7.16.30, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construes 30cd–31 as a single period (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी): seeing the oceans churned and peaks shaken, Yakṣas, Vidyādharas, and Siddhas ask in alarm what cataclysm has struck. Faced with their master’s helpless agony, Rāvaṇa’s counselors (अथ तन्मन्त्रिण आहुः, Rāma Varmā) deliver stark realism: because no refuge exists apart from Mahādeva (महादेवं विना अन्यं शरणं न पश्यामः, Śivasahāya), Rāvaṇa must abandon defiance, bow humbly with hymns, and cast himself upon Umā’s Blue-throated Lord.

7.16.34–35 (A Millennium of Sāman Chants and Tears)

Appealing to Śiva’s hallmark attribute of swift compassion (kṛpāluḥ), the ministers assure Daśānana that a pleased Śaṅkara will grant grace (तुष्टः शङ्करस्ते प्रसादं विधास्यति, Śivasahāya). Heeding them, the proud titan submits and chants diverse hymns for a full thousand years while weeping beneath Kailāsa’s weight. Notably, Śivasahāya glosses सामभिः in 7.16.35 as ‘soothing, conciliatory hymns’ (सान्त्वनकारकैः विविधैः स्तोत्रैः), capturing both the liturgical resonance of Vedic Sāman melodies—later celebrated in tradition as Rāvaṇa’s Śivatāṇḍavastotra—and the rhetorical art of appeasement (sāman).

7.16.36 (The Grace of the Summit-Dwelling Lord)

After a millennium of unbroken penance and praise, Mahādeva—known across Purāṇic theology as Āśutoṣa (‘easily pleased’)—relents from his station atop Kailāsa (शैलाग्रे विष्ठितः प्रभुः) and lifts the crushing weight from Rāvaṇa’s arms (अस्य रावणस्य भुजान्मुक्त्वा दशाननं प्राह, Śivasahāya). The dramatic reversal underscores a core dharmic principle of the Uttarakāṇḍa: while brute pride meets immediate humiliation before the divine, even a violent demon who turns genuinely to humility, endurance, and devotional praise receives gracious absolution from Śiva.

7.16.37–39 (The Etymology of the Name ‘Rāvaṇa’)

Having heretofore called the demon Daśagrīva or Daśānana, the epic now unveils the origin of his most famous appellation. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) glosses शौण्डीर्यात् in 7.16.37 as ‘from surpassing might’ (अतिबलात्), explaining that Śiva is delighted by the tremendous roar emitted even while crushed beneath the peak (शैलाक्रान्तेन … यो रावस्त्वया मुक्तस्तेन तव रवेण प्रीतोऽस्मि). In 7.16.38, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) glosses the causative participle रावितम् as ‘made to resound/cry out’ (शब्दितम्): because he made the three worlds cry aloud in dread, gods, mortals, and Yakṣas alike shall celebrate him as रावणो लोकरावणः (Śivasahāya).

7.16.40 (Lifting the Ban on Puṣpaka)

Dismissing Paulastya graciously, Mahādeva assures him safe passage along any course he chooses. Śivasahāya unpacks विस्रब्धम् as ‘with full confidence and trust toward me’ (मद्विषये सविश्वासं यथा भवति तथा), reassuring the shaken demon that no lingering wrath remains. Crucially, both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) connect मयाभ्यनुज्ञातः directly back to the sarga’s opening crisis by supplying ‘permitted to travel aboard Puṣpaka’ (पुष्पकेण गमनायेति शेषः)—signaling that the divine freeze laid upon the aerial car in 7.16.3 is now formally lifted.

7.16.41 (Daśānana’s Opportunistic Petition)

Rather than departing content with his life and a celebrated title, the lord of Laṅkā immediately seizes the moment of divine favor. Both Rāma Varmā (यदि प्रीतो यदि च वरं ददासि तदा याचतो मे देहीत्यर्थः) and Śivasahāya (यदि त्वं प्रीतस्तदा याचतो मे वरं देहि) note the conditional deference with which Rāvaṇa presses his advantage. Characteristically, even humiliation beneath Kailāsa cannot curb his ambition: having survived Śiva’s toe, he now seeks to convert mercy into fresh martial supremacy.

7.16.42–43 (Fatal Contempt for Mortals)

Specifying the boon he seeks (वराकारं बोधयितुमाह, Śivasahāya), Rāvaṇa reviews the shield of invulnerability already won from Brahmā against every celestial and subterranean class, yet fatally repeats his old blind spot: ‘I do not reckon mortals’ (न गणे न गणयामि, Rāma Varmā, Govindarāja, and Śivasahāya). Rāma Varmā glosses स्वल्पाः as ‘insignificant in strength and valor’ (स्वल्पबलवीर्याः). Spoken directly within Agastya’s narration to Rāma—the mortal prince who has just slain him—this boast rings with exquisite dramatic irony, reaffirming the loophole through which Viṣṇu incarnated as man.

7.16.44 (Preserving the Unspent Lifespan)

Why does Rāvaṇa ask Śiva for the ‘remainder of his lifespan’ (वाञ्छितं चायुषः शेषम्) when Brahmā already gave him long life? Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja resolve this subtle question identically: the opening portion of Brahmā’s granted longevity has already elapsed, so Rāvaṇa begs that the remaining balance not be prematurely cut short by his present offenses (ब्रह्मदत्तदीर्घायुष्याद्यो भागो गतस्तस्माच्छेषमवशिष्टमायुर्वाञ्छितं … दुष्कर्मभिर्यथा विनष्टं न भवति तथा प्रयच्छ). Rāma Varmā adds that he seeks a divine weapon (शस्त्रं दिव्यम्) to secure universal triumph throughout that remaining span.

7.16.45–46 (The Gift of Candrahāsa and Its Solemn Condition)

Construing 7.16.44cd–45 together (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी), Śivasahāya records Śiva’s dual gift: the blazing sword renowned as Candrahāsa (‘Moon-laughter’) and the intact remainder of Rāvaṇa’s years. Yet Śambhu attaches a strict liturgical condition in 7.16.46: the weapon must never be slighted (नावज्ञेयम्). Both Rāma Varmā (शस्त्रस्यास्य पूजौदासीन्यं न कार्यमित्यर्थः) and Govindarāja (इदं खड्गरूपास्त्रं नावज्ञेयम्) explain that Rāvaṇa must never neglect daily reverence toward the blade, lest it depart and return forthwith to Śiva—a solemn reminder that divine armaments endure only with ongoing devotion.

7.16.47–48 (Departure from Kailāsa and World-Conquest)

Having endured a thousand years of penance, received his enduring name directly from Maheśvara (महेश्वरेणैव कृतनामा), and acquired the Candrahāsa sword, Rāvaṇa offers parting obeisance to Mahādeva and reascends Puṣpaka (रावणः पुष्पकमारुरोह, Śivasahāya). Yet his humility proves strictly situational. No sooner is the aerial car aloft again than he resumes his campaign of aggression across the earthly realm (महीतलं पर्यक्रामत्, Śivasahāya), sweeping hither and thither to provoke and torment the valiant Kṣatriya kings—setting the immediate stage for his fateful encounter with Vedavatī in Sarga 17.

7.16.49 (The Fate of Defiant Warriors)

Explaining the exact import of बाधमानः (‘harassing’) in 7.16.48, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) introduces 7.16.49 as an explicit elaboration of that oppression (बाधमेव विशदयन्नाह). High-spirited Kṣatriyas who were fiercely proud of combat (yuddha-durmadāḥ) refused to bow to Rāvaṇa’s diktat (tac-chāsanam akurvantaḥ), choosing martial honor over vassalage; as a result, they were utterly annihilated by Rāvaṇa together with their armies and royal retinues (क्षत्रिया विनेशुः रावणेन विनाशिताः, Śivasahāya). Their gallant but futile resistance dramatizes the sheer terror of Rāvaṇa’s boon-fortified supremacy.

7.16.50 (The Prudence of Wise Kings)

Contrasting with those fallen heroes, verse 50 depicts sovereigns respected by the wise (प्राज्ञैः सम्मताः, Śivasahāya) who apply classical statecraft (nīti): assessing accurately that the boon-guarded Rākṣasa was unconquerable by ordinary human might (दुर्जयं रक्षो जानन्तः), they spared their subjects pointless slaughter by formally pronouncing submission—‘We are conquered!’ (जिताः स्म इति राक्षसं रावणमभाषन्त). Sarga 16 thus closes on a masterful double note: Rāvaṇa stands at the zenith of earthly terror, even as Nandī’s unalterable curse already ticks beneath his triumph.

Newly named by Maheśvara himself, Rāvaṇa bows to Śiva, reascends Puṣpaka, and sweeps across the earth harassing mighty Kṣatriya kings—destroying the defiant in battle while wiser monarchs prudently acknowledge defeat.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततो महीतलं राम पर्यक्रामत रावणः ।
क्षत्रियान् सुमहावीर्यान् बाधमानस्ततस्ततः ॥ ४७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततो महीतलं राम पर्यक्रामत रावणः ।
क्षत्रियान् सुमहावीर्यान् बाधमानस्ततस्ततः ॥ ४७ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

श्रीराम! इसके बाद रावण समूची पृथ्वीपर दिग्विजयके लिये भ्रमण करने लगा । उसने इधर-उधर जाकर बहुत-से महापराक्रमी क्षत्रियोंको पीड़ा पहुँचायी ॥ ४७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

श्रीराम! अनन्तर रावणनु इडी पृथिवियल्लि दिग्विजयक्कागि सञ्चरिसतॊडगिदनु. अवनु ऎल्लॆडॆगळिगॆ होगि अनेक महापराक्रमिगळन्नु पीडिसिदनु.॥47॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having thus received his very name from Maheśvara himself (maheśvareṇaiva kṛta-nāmā), that Rāvaṇa bowed in reverence to Mahādeva (abhivādya mahādevam) and then ascended Puṣpaka (ārurohātha puṣpakam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

केचित् तेजस्विनः शूराः क्षत्रिया युद्धदुर्मदाः ।
तच्छासनमकुर्वन्तो विनेशुः सपरिच्छदाः ॥ ४८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 केचित् तेजस्विनः शूराः क्षत्रिया युद्धदुर्मदाः ।
1 तच्छासनमकुर्वन्तो विनेशुः सपरिच्छदाः ॥ ४८ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

कितने ही तेजस्वी क्षत्रिय जो बड़े ही शूरवीर और रणोन्मत्त थे, रावणकी आज्ञा न माननेके कारण सेना और परिवारसहित नष्ट हो गये ॥ ४८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अनेक तेजस्वी क्षत्रियरु, शूरवीर रणोन्मत्तरु रावणन आज्ञॆयन्नु पालिसदिरलु अवरॆल्ल सैन्य परिवार सहित नाशवागि होदरु.॥48॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thereafter, O Rāma, Rāvaṇa ranged across the face of the earth (mahī-tale … paryakrāmata), harassing mighty-valored Kṣatriyas (kṣatriyān sumahāvīryān bādhamānaḥ) on every side (itas tataḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अपरे दुर्जयं रक्षो जानन्तः प्राज्ञसम्मताः ।
जिताः स्म इत्यभाषन्त राक्षसं बलदर्पितम् ॥ ४९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 अपरे दुर्जयं रक्षो जानन्तः प्राज्ञसम्मताः ।
1 जिताः स्म इत्यभाषन्त राक्षसं बलदर्पितम् ॥ ४९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

दूसरे क्षत्रियोंने, जो बुद्धिमान् माने जाते थे और उस राक्षसको अजेय समझते थे, उस बलाभिमानी निशाचरके सामने अपनी पराजय स्वीकार कर ली ॥ ४९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

बुद्धिवन्तराद इतर क्षत्रियरु राक्षसनन्नु अजेयनॆन्दु तिळिदु, आ बलाभिमानि निशाचरन ऎदुरिगॆ तम्म पराजयवन्नु स्वीकरिसिकॊण्डरु.॥49॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये उत्तरकाण्डे षोडशः सर्गः ॥ १६ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके उत्तरकाण्डमें सोलहवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ १६ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद उत्तर काण्डदल्लि हदिनारनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु. ॥16॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Certain spirited and heroic Kṣatriyas (tejasvinaḥ śūrāḥ kṣatriyāḥ), intoxicated with battle-ardor (yuddha-durmadāḥ), refusing to submit to his command (tac-chāsanam akurvantaḥ), perished together with all their retinues (vineśuḥ sa-paricchadāḥ).
