सर्वाष् टीकाः
भागसूचना
  1. वालीके द्वारा रावणका पराभव तथा रावणका उन्हें अपना मित्र बनाना
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Released by Kārtavīrya Arjuna yet undaunted in pride, Rāvaṇa roams the earth challenging every mighty warrior; arriving at Kiṣkindhā to summon Vālī, he is told by the monkey ministers that Vālī is away performing twilight rites across the four oceans.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अर्जुनेन विमुक्तस्तु रावणो राक्षसाधिपः ।
चचार पृथिवीं सर्वामनिर्विण्णस्तथा कृतः ॥ १ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अर्जुनेन विमुक्तस्तु रावणो राक्षसाधिपः ।
चचार पृथिवीं सर्वामनिर्विण्णस्तथा कृतः ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

अर्जुनसे छुटकारा पाकर राक्षसराज रावण निर्वेदरहित हो पुनः सारी पृथ्वीपर विचरण करने लगा ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

राघव! अर्जुननिन्द बिडुगडॆ हॊन्दि रावणनु खेदविल्लदवनागि पुनः पृथिवियल्लि सञ्चरिस तॊडगिदनु.॥1॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Released by Arjuna (arjunena vimuktas tu), Rāvaṇa, sovereign of the rākṣasas (rāvaṇo rākṣasādhipaḥ), roamed across the entire earth (cacāra pṛthivīṃ sarvām), rendered wholly free from dejection (anirviṇṇas tathā kṛtaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

राक्षसं वा मनुष्यं वा शृणुते यं बलाधिकम् ।
रावणस्तं समासाद्य युद्धे ह्वयति दर्पितः ॥ २ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

राक्षसं वा मनुष्यं वा शृणुते यं बलाधिकम् ।
रावणस्तं समासाद्य युद्धे ह्वयति दर्पितः ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

राक्षस हो या मनुष्य, जिसको भी वह बलमें बढ़ा-चढ़ा सुनता था, उसीके पास पहुँचकर अभिमानी रावण उसे युद्धके लिये ललकारता था ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

भूमण्डलदल्लि अधिक बलविरुव राक्षसनागली, मनुष्यनागली, अवन बळिगॆ होगि युद्धक्कागि आह्वानिसुत्तिद्दनु.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Whenever he hears of any rākṣasa or mortal superior in strength (rākṣasaṃ vā manuṣyaṃ vā śṛṇute ’yaṃ balādhikam), Rāvaṇa approaches him (taṃ samāsādya) and arrogantly challenges him to battle (yuddhe hvayati darpitaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततः कदाचित् किष्किन्धां नगरीं वालिपालिताम् ।
गत्वाऽऽह्वयति युद्धाय वालिनं हेममालिनम् ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततः कदाचित् किष्किन्धां नगरीं वालिपालिताम् ।
गत्वाऽऽह्वयति युद्धाय वालिनं हेममालिनम् ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

तदनन्तर एक दिन वह वालीद्वारा पालित किष्किन्धापुरीमें जाकर सुवर्णमालाधारी वालीको युद्धके लिये ललकारने लगा ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अनन्तर ऒन्दु दिन अवनु वालियिन्द पालितवाद किष्किन्धॆगॆ होगि सुवर्णमालाधारी वालियन्नु युद्धक्कागि आह्वानिसिदनु.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then on a certain occasion (tataḥ kadācit), having gone to the city of Kiṣkindhā, protected by Vālī (kiṣkindhāṃ nagarīṃ vālipālitām), he challenges to combat Vālī, wearer of the golden garland (hvayati yuddhāya vālinaṃ hemamālinam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततस्तु वानरामात्यास्तारस्तारापिता प्रभुः ।
उवाच वानरो वाक्यं युद्धप्रेप्सुमुपागतम् ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततस्तु वानरामात्यास्तारस्तारापिता प्रभुः ।
उवाच वानरो वाक्यं युद्धप्रेप्सुमुपागतम् ॥ ४ ॥

ततस्तु वानरामात्यस्तारस्तारापिता प्रभुः ।
उवाच वानरो वाक्यं युद्धप्रेप्सुमुपागतम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस समय युद्धकी इच्छासे आये हुए रावणसे वालीके मन्त्री तार, ताराके पिता सुषेण तथा युवराज अङ्गद एवं सुग्रीवने कहा— ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग युद्धद इच्छॆयिन्द बन्दिरुव रावणनल्लि वालिय मन्त्रि तार, तारापित सुषेण, युवराज अङ्गद हागू सुग्रीवनु हेळिदरु.॥4॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thereupon the monkey minister Tāra (vānarāmātyas tāraḥ), along with Tārā’s father [Suṣeṇa], the lord [Sugrīva], and the vānara chieftains (tārāpitā prabhuḥ … vānaraḥ), spoke these words to him who had come eager for battle (uvāca … vākyaṃ yuddhaprepsum upāgatam):

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

राक्षसेन्द्र गतो वाली यस्ते प्रतिबलो भवेत् ।
कोऽन्यः प्रमुखतः स्थातुं तव शक्तः प्लवङ्गमः ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

राक्षसेन्द्र गतो वाली यस्ते प्रतिबलो भवेत् ।
कोऽन्यः प्रमुखतः स्थातुं तव शक्तः प्लवङ्गमः ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘राक्षसराज! इस समय वाली तो बाहर गये हुए हैं । वे ही आपकी जोड़के हो सकते हैं । दूसरा कौन वानर आपके सामने ठहर सकता है ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

राक्षसेन्द्रने! ईग वालियु हॊरगॆ होगिरुवनु. अवनन्नु बिट्टु बेरॆ याव वानरनु निन्न ऎदुरिगॆ निल्लबल्लनु? अवने निनगॆ सरिसमाननागबल्लनु.॥5॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“O lord of the rākṣasas (rākṣasendra), Vālī—the one who could be an equal match for you (yas te pratibalo bhavet)—has gone away (gato vālī); what other monkey is able to stand before you in combat (ko ’nyaḥ pramukhataḥ sthātuṃ tava śaktaḥ plavaṅgamaḥ)?

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

चतुर्भ्योऽपि समुद्रेभ्यः सन्ध्यामन्वास्य रावण ।
इदं मुहूर्तमायाति वाली तिष्ठ मुहूर्तकम् ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

चतुर्भ्योऽपि समुद्रेभ्यः सन्ध्यामन्वास्य रावण ।
इदं मुहूर्तमायाति वाली तिष्ठ मुहूर्तकम् ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘रावण! चारों समुद्रोंसे सन्ध्योपासन करके वाली अब आते ही होंगे । आप दो घड़ी ठहर जाइये ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रावण! नाल्कु समुद्रगळल्लियू सन्ध्योपासनॆ माडुव वालियु ईगले बरुवनु. नीनु स्वल्प हॊत्तु इल्लिये इरु.॥6॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Having duly worshipped the twilight across all four oceans (caturbhyo ’pi samudrebhyaḥ sandhyām anvāsya), O Rāvaṇa, Vālī returns at this very hour (imaṃ muhūrtam āyāti vālī); therefore wait but a brief moment (tiṣṭha muhūrtakam).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Pointing to conch-white heaps of bones left by past challengers, the ministers warn that even ambrosia could not save Rāvaṇa from Vālī; spurning Tāra, Rāvaṇa flies in Puṣpaka to the southern sea and beholds Vālī at twilight prayer.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

एतानस्थिचयान् पश्य य एते शङ्खपाण्डुराः ।
युद्धार्थिनामिमे राजन् वानराधिपतेजसा ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

एतानस्थिचयान् पश्य य एते शङ्खपाण्डुराः ।
युद्धार्थिनामिमे राजन् वानराधिपतेजसा ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘राजन्! देखिये, ये जो शङ्खके समान उज्ज्वल हड्डियोंके ढेर लग रहे हैं, ये वालीके साथ युद्धकी इच्छासे आये हुए आप-जैसे वीरोंके ही हैं । वानरराज वालीके तेजसे ही इन सबका अन्त हुआ है ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

राजने! नोडु, ई शङ्खदन्तॆ बॆळ्ळगॆ इरुव ऎलुबिन राशियु वालियॊडनॆ युद्धद इच्छॆयिन्द बन्दिरुव निन्नन्तह वीररद्दागिदॆ. वानरराज वालिय तेजदिन्द इवरॆल्लर अन्त्यवागिदॆ.॥7॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Behold these heaps of bones, gleaming pale as conch-shells (etān asthicayān paśya ya ete śaṅkhapāṇḍurāḥ)—these belong to warriors eager for battle (yuddhārthinām ime), O King, felled by the fiery might of the monkey sovereign (vānarādhipa-tejasā).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

यद्वामृतरसः पीतस्त्वया रावण राक्षस ।
तदा वालिनमासाद्य तदन्तं तव जीवितम् ॥ ८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

यद्वामृतरसः पीतस्त्वया रावण राक्षस ।
तदा वालिनमासाद्य तदन्तं तव जीवितम् ॥ ८ ॥

युद्वामृतरसः पीतस्त्वया रावण राक्षस ।
तदा वालिनमासाद्य तदन्तं तव जीवितम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘राक्षस रावण! यदि आपने अमृतका रस पी लिया हो तो भी जब आप वालीसे टक्कर लेंगे, तब वही आपके जीवनका अन्तिम क्षण होगा ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

राक्षस रावणने! नीनु अमृतपान माडिद्दरू वालियॊडनॆ युद्ध माडिदाग अदे निन्न जीवनद कॊनॆय गळिगॆयागुवुदु.॥8॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Even if the elixir of immortality has been drunk by you (yadvā ’mṛtarasaḥ pītas tvayā), O Rāvaṇa, O rākṣasa, even then, upon encountering Vālī (tadā vālinam āsādya), your life will find its end right there (tadantaṃ tava jīvitam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

पश्येदानीं जगच्चित्रमिमं विश्रवसः सुत ।
इदं मुहूर्तं तिष्ठस्व दुर्लभं ते भविष्यति ॥ ९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 पश्येदानीं जगच्चित्रमिमं विश्रवसः सुत ।
1 इदं मुहूर्तं तिष्ठस्व दुर्लभं ते भविष्यति ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘विश्रवाकुमार! वाली सम्पूर्ण आश्चर्यके भण्डार हैं । आप इस समय इनका दर्शन करेंगे । केवल इसी मुहूर्ततक उनकी प्रतीक्षाके लिये ठहरिये; फिर तो आपके लिये जीवन दुर्लभ हो जायगा ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

विश्रवस्सुकुमार! वालियु समस्त आश्चर्यद भण्डारवागिद्दानॆ. ईग नीनु अवन दर्शन माडुवॆ. केवल इदे मुहूर्तदवरॆगॆ प्रतीक्षॆ माडु, मत्तॆ निन्न जीवन दुर्लभवादीतु.॥9॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Behold now this marvel of the world (paśyedānīṃ jagaccitram imam), O son of Viśravas (viśravasaḥ suta); wait for this single moment (idaṃ muhūrtaṃ tiṣṭhasva), for soon life itself will become hard for you to keep (durlabhaṃ te bhaviṣyati).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अथवा त्वरसे मर्तुं गच्छ दक्षिणसागरम् ।
वालिनं द्रक्ष्यसे तत्र भूमिष्ठमिव पावकम् ॥ १० ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अथवा त्वरसे मर्तुं गच्छ दक्षिणसागरम् ।
वालिनं द्रक्ष्यसे तत्र भूमिष्ठमिव पावकम् ॥ १० ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘अथवा यदि आपको मरनेके लिये बहुत जल्दी लगी हो तो दक्षिण समुद्रके तटपर चले जाइये । वहाँ आपको पृथ्वीपर स्थित हुए अग्निदेवके समान वालीका दर्शन होगा’ ॥ १० ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

इल्लवे निनगॆ सायलु बहळ अवसरविद्दरॆ दक्षिण समुद्रद तीरक्कॆ होगु. अल्लि निनगॆ पृथिवियल्लि स्थितनाद यज्ञेश्वरनन्तह वालिय दर्शनवागबहुदु.॥10॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Or else, if you are in haste to die (athavā tvarase martum), go to the southern ocean (gaccha dakṣiṇasāgaram)—there you shall behold Vālī (vālinaṃ drakṣyase tatra), like a blazing fire stationed upon the earth (bhūmistham iva pāvakam)!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स तु तारं विनिर्भर्त्स्य रावणो लोकरावणः ।
पुष्पकं तत् समारुह्य प्रययौ दक्षिणार्णवम् ॥ ११ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स तु तारं विनिर्भर्त्स्य रावणो लोकरावणः ।
पुष्पकं तत् समारुह्य प्रययौ दक्षिणार्णवम् ॥ ११ ॥

स तु तारं विनिर्भर्स्य रावणो लोकरावणः ।
पुष्पकं तत्समारुह्य प्रययौ दक्षिणार्णवम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

तब लोकोंको रुलानेवाले रावणने तारको भला-बुरा कहकर पुष्पकविमानपर आरूढ़ हो दक्षिण समुद्रकी ओर प्रस्थान किया ॥ ११ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग जगत्तन्नु अळुवन्तॆ माडुव रावणनु तारनन्नु निन्दिसि पुष्पक विमानवन्नेरि दक्षिण समुद्रद कडॆगॆ तॆरळिदनु.॥11॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Spurning Tāra with contempt (sa tu tāraṃ vinirbhartsya), Rāvaṇa, who makes the worlds weep (rāvaṇo lokarāvaṇaḥ), ascended that aerial car Puṣpaka (puṣpakaṃ tat samāruhya) and sped forth to the southern ocean (prayayau dakṣiṇārṇavam).

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then that foremost of monkeys released Rāvaṇa from his armpit (rāvaṇaṃ tu mumocātha svakakṣāt kapisattamaḥ), and laughing heartily again and again (prahasan … muhuḥ), asked Rāvaṇa: “Whence have you come (kutas tvam iti covāca)?”

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

7.34.1–3 (Undaunted Pride and the Challenge at Kiṣkindhā)

Launching the episode of Vālī’s humbling of Rāvaṇa (अथ रावणस्य वालिधर्षणम्, Rāma Varmā), the poet explains why defeat by Kārtavīrya Arjuna failed to curb Rāvaṇa’s arrogance. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) glosses अनिर्विण्णः कृतः as ‘freed from the dejection born of defeat’ (पराभवजनितनिर्वेदरहितः कृतः), since Pulastya had secured his release, forged friendship between them, and Arjuna dismissed him with honors. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) concurs (युद्धवैराग्यरहितः), while all three commentators gloss युद्धे ह्वयति as issuing challenges for battle (युद्धनिमित्तमाह्वयति स्म, Govindarāja) until Rāvaṇa arrives before Kiṣkindhā.

7.34.4 (Who Addressed Rāvaṇa at Kiṣkindhā?)

The string of singular nouns in 7.34.4—वानरामात्यः, तारः, तारापिता, प्रभुः, and वानरः—prompts lively exegetical distinction among the commentators. Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) identify three distinct spokesmen: the minister Tāra, Tārā’s father Suṣeṇa (तारापिता सुषेणः), and the crown prince Sugrīva (प्रभुर्युवराजः सुग्रीवश्चोवाच). Śivasahāya goes further, distinguishing four figures—Tāra, Suṣeṇa, lord Aṅgada (प्रभुरङ्गदश्च), and the monkey Sugrīva—while also offering an alternative construal taking all these epithets strictly as qualifiers of Tāra and Tārā’s father (तारतारापित्रोरेव सर्वविशेषणं वा).

7.34.5–6 (Vālī’s Daily Twilight Circuit Across Four Seas)

Tactfully deferring combat, the ministers declare Vālī absent—gone to the ocean shores (समुद्रसमीप इति शेषः, Śivasahāya)—and uniquely able to match Rāvaṇa in battle (ते तुभ्यं प्रतिबलः युद्धं दातुमिति शेषः, Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja). On 7.34.6, both Tilaka and Śiromaṇi normalize the ablative चतुर्भ्यः समुद्रेभ्यः as locative ‘in the four oceans’ (चतुर्षु समुद्रेषु), with Rāma Varmā defining सन्ध्याम् philosophically as meditating upon Brahman, the deity duly contemplated at twilight (सम्यग्ध्येयदेवतां ब्रह्मरूपामन्वास्य ध्यात्वा), before Vālī’s swift return.

7.34.7–8 (The Conch-White Bones and the Limits of Ambrosia)

Turning from polite notice to stern deterrent, the ministers warn Rāvaṇa that fighting Vālī is beyond his measure (तेन ते युद्धमयोग्यमित्याह, Rāma Varmā; वालिना युद्धयोग्यता तव नास्ति, Śivasahāya). Pointing to the bleached bone-heaps of earlier challengers slain by Vālī’s prowess, Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) draws their pointed implication: ‘so too shall you perish’ (एवं त्वमपि मरिष्यसीति भावः). All three commentators take यद्वा … तदा in 7.34.8 as concessive (यद्यपि … तथापि): even had Rāvaṇa drunk nectar, his life lasts only until confronting Vālī (तत्समीपगमनपर्यन्तम्, Tilaka; तद्वालियुद्धमेवान्तोऽवसानं यस्य, Śiromaṇi).

7.34.9–10 (A Marvel of the World and Fire Upon the Earth)

Commenting on the Southern Recension verse 7.34.9, the exegetes offer two vivid readings of जगच्चित्रम्: Rāma Varmā takes masculine इमम् of Vālī himself (‘behold this Vālī, marvel of the world’, सर्वाश्चर्यभूतम् इमं वालिनं पश्य) and supplies जीवितम् (‘life will be hard to keep’); Govindarāja glosses जगच्चित्रम् as ‘the wondrous spectacle of the entire world’ (आश्चर्यभूतं व्यापारम्), while Śivasahāya reads ‘behold this wondrous world now, for soon it will be hard for you to see since you are about to die’ (मरिष्यमाणत्वादित्यर्थः). In 7.34.10 they point Rāvaṇa southward to Vālī, blazing like earthly fire.

7.34.11–12 (Rāvaṇa’s Contempt and the Vision on the Southern Shore)

Stung by the warning, Rāvaṇa brusquely reviles Tāra—glossed by both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja as ‘Tāra and the other ministers’ (तारादीन्), though Govindarāja also notes a construal in which Tāra alone had spoken earlier (पूर्वं तार एवोक्तवान्). Ascending Puṣpaka to the southern sea, Rāvaṇa beholds a majestic contrast to his own arrogance: Vālī, golden-mountain-like and dawn-faced, completely immersed in meditating upon Brahman at twilight (सम्यग्ध्येयब्रह्मध्यानतत्परम्, Rāma Varmā). Śivasahāya supplies अतिष्ठत् (‘paused’) after दृष्ट्वा, preparing Rāvaṇa’s stealthy descent.

7.34.13–15 (Stealthy Approach and Lion-Like Unconcern)

Visual contrast sharpens the comedy of the encounter: golden, sun-faced Vālī sits motionless while collyrium-black Rāvaṇa tiptoes forward with hushed footfalls (निःशब्दचरणविन्यासम्, Govindarāja; शब्दरहितचरणप्रक्षेपविशिष्टम्, Śivasahāya). Glancing up by chance, Vālī spots him bent on capturing him (बन्धनाभिप्रायवान्, Govindarāja) yet feels zero alarm. In 7.34.15, both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja gloss the particle वा in सिंहो वा as comparative इव (‘like a lion spotting a hare’), while Śivasahāya notes historic present न चिन्तयति as past ‘reckoned as nothing’ (अचिन्तयत् अगणयत्).

7.34.16–17 (Vālī’s Inner Resolve and the Garuḍa Simile)

All three commentators explicitly delimit verses 16–17 (through इत्येवम् in 7.34.18) as Vālī’s unspoken mental plan (वालिनो विचारः). Resolving to complete his remaining three-ocean circuit with Rāvaṇa clamped beneath his arm, Vālī pictures the public humiliation awaiting his foe: Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja supply देवादयः (‘the gods and celestials will behold’), while Śivasahāya supplies लोकाः (‘the worlds’). Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa gloss अङ्कस्थम् as ‘tucked under my armpit’ (कक्षस्थम्) and carry it into the simile of a limp serpent dangling from Garuḍa’s flank.

7.34.18 (Attentive Ear and Vedic Mantras on a Vānara’s Lips)

Two illuminating exegetical points anchor 7.34.18. Explaining Govindarāja’s reading कर्णमुपाश्रितः (whereas Śivasahāya follows मौनमुपास्थितः, ‘abiding in silence’), Govindarāja glosses it as ‘keeping his mind intently focused upon his ear to detect footsteps’ (पादशब्दश्रवणार्थं कर्णमुपाश्रितमनाः). Both Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa gloss नैगमान् as ‘Vedic’ (वैदिकान्) and address why a vānara recites Vedic hymns: Rāma Varmā affirms that all Vedas shine spontaneously within Vālī and his peers (स्वयंप्रतिभातसकलवेदाः), while Govindarāja grounds his Vedic eligibility in his divine birth as Indra’s son (देवकुमारत्वान्मन्त्रवत्वम्).

7.34.19–20 (The Blind Backhand Capture)

As both proud monarchs strain toward the same act of capture (तत्कर्म ग्रहणकर्म, Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya), Vālī’s composure triumphs over Rāvaṇa’s stealth. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss हस्तग्राहम् as ‘intent on seizing with his hands’ (हस्तग्रहणाभिप्रायवन्तम्) and पादशब्देन as ‘by the faint rustle of footsteps’ (सूक्ष्मपादशब्देन). Tilaka also notes the variant हस्तग्राह्यम् (‘within hand’s reach’), glosses पराङ्मुखोऽपि as ‘without even turning to look back’ (प्रतिवलोकनरहितोऽपि), and identifies अण्डजः as Garuḍa (अण्डजो गरुडः, echoed by Śivasahāya).

7.34.21–22 (Helpless Struggles in Mid-Air)

Inverting Rāvaṇa’s ambition with crisp verbal irony (ग्रहीतुकामं तं गृह्य), Vālī tucks the conqueror of the three worlds under his arm and vaults skyward. In 7.34.22, the Southern text keeps Rāvaṇa as the object of both participles: Śivasahāya explains that Rāvaṇa repeatedly pierced Vālī with his nails and thereby tried to torment him (नखैर्वितुदन्तमत् एव पीडयमानं रावणम्), yet Vālī carried him off as effortlessly as a gale sweeps a dark cloud. Govindarāja invokes Pāṇini’s rule णिचश्च (1.3.74) to justify the middle participle आपीडयमानम्.

7.34.23–24 (The Screaming Pursuit and the Solar Simile)

Seeing their king whisked aloft, Rāvaṇa’s ministers charge through the sky clamoring to free him. Rāma Varmā analyzes the rare desiderative derivative मुमोक्षयिषवः as formed from the denominative-causative stem of मोक्ष (मोक्षशब्दात्तत्करोतीति ण्यन्तात्सन्) and glosses रवमाणाः as ‘shouting aloud’ (क्रोशन्तः); Śivasahāya paraphrases ‘desiring to liberate Rāvaṇa’ (रावणं मोचयितुमिच्छवः). Verse 7.34.24 closes the tableau with a striking upamā: golden Vālī pursued by dark rākṣasas mirrors the blazing sun trailed by black storm-clouds across the zenith.

7.34.25–26 (Exhausted Pursuers and Recoiling Mountains)

Resolving the sandhi तेऽशक्नुवन्तः (अशक्नुवन्त इति पदम्, Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja), the poet shows Rāvaṇa’s ministers halting breathless in Vālī’s slipstream (व्यवस्थिता बभूवुः, Śivasahāya). Verse 7.34.26 then deploys the classical kaimutika-nyāya (argument a fortiori): as all three commentators explain, if even immovable mountain lords drew back from Vālī’s path, what need to speak of flesh-and-blood beings clinging to life? Śivasahāya additionally remarks on Parimal’s reading पर्वतेन्द्रापीत्यत्र सन्धिरार्षः, while Govindarāja records the plural variant पर्वतेन्द्रा हि.

7.34.27–28 (Oceans Beyond Birds’ Flight and Sky-Dweller Acclaim)

Defining अपक्षिगणसम्पातान्, Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja explain that no flock of birds could traverse those four oceans within the brief span Vālī took (यावता कालेन वाली गच्छति तावता कालेन पक्षिगणैरप्यप्राप्यान्), while Śivasahāya takes it simply as seas uncrossed by birds. Both Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa gloss सन्ध्याकालम् as worshiping Brahman embodied in the morning twilight deity (प्रातःसन्ध्याकालध्येयदेवतारूपं ब्रह्म). Honored by celestials (सभाज्यमानः पूज्यमानः, Govindarāja), Vālī reaches the western sea with Rāvaṇa still pinned beneath his arm (कक्षस्थरावणसहितः, Tilaka).

7.34.29–30 (A Celebrated Debate on Performing Sandhyā at Four Seas)

How could Vālī perform morning sandhyā sequentially at four oceans? Govindarāja adopts the older view of Kataka and Tīrtha, distributing the rite’s limbs across the seas—purification (शौचम्) in the south, bathing in the west, sprinkling (प्रोक्षणादिकम्) in the north, and अर्घ्य in the east—each limb metonymically called sandhyā. Rāma Varmā sharply refutes Kataka and Tīrtha, citing 7.34.18 (जपन्वै नैगमान्मन्त्रान्) to prove Vālī was already chanting Vedic mantras at the southern sea, and concluding that meditating upon Brahman as the essence of Gāyatrī is the primary meaning of sandhyā across śruti, smṛti, and purāṇa.

7.34.31–33 (Completing the Four-Ocean Circuit and Alighting at Kiṣkindhā)

Concluding his astonishing aerial pilgrimage at the northern and eastern oceans, Vālī—whom all three commentators gloss as Indra’s son (वासवस्यापत्यं वासविः)—sweeps back to Kiṣkindhā’s pleasure-grove. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) carefully glosses अपतत् in 7.34.33 not as falling in weakness but simply as descending to earth (किष्किन्धोपवने अपतत् प्राप्तोऽभूत्), acknowledging that bearing the mountainous rākṣasa across four seas had finally produced a touch of fatigue (रावणोद्वहनः अत एव श्रान्तः) even in Indra’s peerless son.

7.34.34–35 (Vālī’s Mirth and Rāvaṇa’s Exhausted Wonder)

Setting Rāvaṇa down in the garden, Vālī laughs repeatedly and asks “Whence are you?” Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) capture the comic irony behind Vālī’s laughter (प्रहसन्): ‘Seeking to seize me, this wicked fool has been seized himself!’ (मां निगृह्णन् स्वयमेव दुरात्मा निगृहीत इति प्रहसन्). For once stripped of bluster, Rāvaṇa is overcome by genuine awe (vismaya), his eyes unsteady from the ordeal (श्रमेण लोलानि चञ्चलानि निरीक्षणानि नयनानि यस्य, Śivasahāya).

7.34.36 (Candid Confession Before Indra’s Likeness)

Addressing Vālī with unstinted respect as ‘equal to Mahendra’ (महेन्द्राभ)—aptly recognizing Indra’s own radiance in his son—Rāvaṇa introduces himself plainly and admits his humiliation. The commentators highlight two complementary nuances in the past participle आसादितः: both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja read it with wry literalism as ‘consigned to a lodging inside your armpit’ (कक्षनिवेशं प्रापितः / कक्षनिक्षेपं प्रापितः), while Śivasahāya renders it more broadly as ‘overcome and afflicted’ (पीडितः).

7.34.37–38 (Rāvaṇa’s Threefold Praise and Mountainous Weight)

With triple exclamations of awe (अहो आश्चर्यम्, Śivasahāya), Rāvaṇa salutes Vālī’s physical power (bala), heroic energy (vīrya), and serene inner depth (gāmbhīrya)—evinced by carrying him off like a trussed beast (पशुवद्गृहीत्वा) without interrupting his prayers. On 7.34.38, both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss अश्रान्तवत् as ‘wholly unwearied’ (अश्रान्तः सन्) and unpack the emphatic मां चैव as ‘carrying even me, whose bulk equals a great mountain’ (पर्वतसमानभारम्, Tilaka; महाद्रिसमानभारम्, Govindarāja), while Śivasahāya underscores the rhetorical negative न कोऽपीत्यर्थः.

7.34.39 (Velocity Equal to Mind, Wind, and Garuḍa)

Elevating Vālī’s feat to cosmic proportions, Rāvaṇa ranks his aerial swiftness beside the three proverbial paragons of speed in Indian lore: thought (manas), the wind (anila), and Garuḍa (suparṇa). Śivasahāya paraphrases cleanly that such velocity belongs solely to those three and to Vālī as well (एषा एतादृशी गतिः मनआदीनां त्रयाणां तव च). The comparison subtly foreshadows Book V and Book VII’s praise of Hanūmān, son of the Wind, whom Rāma will immediately inquire about in Sarga 35.

7.34.40–41 (The Fire-Witnessed Pact of Undivided Brotherhood)

Recognizing superior prowess with pragmatic shrewdness, Rāvaṇa proposes an enduring covenant before Agni (pāvakāgrataḥ). All three commentators flag the idiomatic dative तुभ्यम् in 7.34.40 as equivalent to genitive तव (‘having witnessed your strength’, दृष्टबलस्तवेत्यर्थः). In 7.34.41, Śivasahāya observes that Rāvaṇa spells out the exact terms of their fellowship (सख्याकारमाह): everything from wives and sons to kingdom and fare shall remain undivided between them (नौ आवयोः, Rāma Varmā and Śiromaṇi)—a sweeping formula whose irony will later echo when Vālī’s own brother Sugrīva allies with Rāma before fire.

7.34.42–43 (Sworn Brotherhood Before Agni)

Sealing their sudden reconciliation with Vedic solemnity, Vālī and Rāvaṇa kindle fire, embrace, and formally adopt brotherhood (भ्रातृत्वमुपसम्पन्नौ). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) links verses 42 and 43 smoothly into a continuous narrative sequence, supplying बभूवतुः with उपसम्पन्नौ and normalizing the archaic dual विशतुः as classical विविशतुः. Linking hands (अन्योन्यं लम्बितकरौ), the two titans stride into Kiṣkindhā like a pair of lions entering a mountain cave—a memorable image of warrior chivalry transcending animosity once supremacy has been decisively proven.

7.34.44 (A Month in Kiṣkindhā Like Sugrīva)

Rāvaṇa remains Vālī’s honored guest for a full month before his ministers come to retrieve him for renewed campaigns of conquest (त्रैलोक्योत्सादनार्थिभिः). Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) supply सह with आगतैः (‘accompanied by his arriving ministers’), and Govindarāja unpacks the telling comparison सुग्रीव इव as ‘dwelling with the intimate affection of a brother’ (भ्रातृभावेनेत्यर्थः)—an ironic portrait of harmony before the later fraternal rift between Vālī and Sugrīva.

7.34.45–46 (Agastya’s Finale: The Moth in Rāma’s Fire)

Drawing his tale of Rāvaṇa’s earthly humiliations to its close (उपसंहारमाह, Śivasahāya), Agastya summarizes how Vālī first subdued Rāvaṇa and then befriended him before Agni with uterine-brother affection (सोदरभावतया स्नेहः कृतः, Rāma Varmā; सोदरभावतया स्नेहीकृतः, Govindarāja). Verse 7.34.46 pivots the entire sarga back to Rāma’s own transcendent might: even Vālī, whose peerless prowess toyed with Rāvaṇa across four seas, was burned to ash by Rāma like a moth in flame (वह्निना शलभ इव त्वया निर्दग्धः, Śiromaṇi), prompting Rāma’s next question on Hanūmān in Sarga 35.

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद उत्तरकाण्डदल्लि मूवत्तनाल्कनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु. ॥34॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Peerless and supreme was the strength of Vālī (balam apratimaṃ … vālino ’bhavad uttamam), O Rāma—yet even he was utterly consumed by you (so ’pi tvayā vinirdagdhaḥ), like a moth by blazing fire (śalabho vahninā yathā)!
