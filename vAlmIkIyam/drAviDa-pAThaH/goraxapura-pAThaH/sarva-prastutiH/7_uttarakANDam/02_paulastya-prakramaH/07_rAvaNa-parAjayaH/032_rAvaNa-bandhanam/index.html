सर्वाष् टीकाः
भागसूचना
  1. अर्जुनकी भुजाओंसे नर्मदाके प्रवाहका अवरुद्ध होना, रावणके पुष्पोपहारका बह जाना, फिर रावण आदि निशाचरोंका अर्जुनके साथ युद्ध तथा अर्जुनका रावणको कैद करके अपने नगरमें ले जाना
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Not far from where Rāvaṇa worships Śiva upon the Narmadā sandbank, the thousand-armed King Arjuna Kārtavīrya sports in the river with his women and dams its stream with his arms, sending a monsoon-like surge backward against the current.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

नर्मदापुलिने यत्र राक्षसेन्द्रः स दारुणः ।
पुष्पोपहारं कुरुते तस्माद् देशाददूरतः ॥ १ ॥
अर्जुनो जयतां श्रेष्ठो माहिष्मत्याः पतिः प्रभुः ।
क्रीडते सह नारीभिर्नर्मदातोयमाश्रितः ॥ २ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

नर्मदापुलिने यत्र राक्षसेन्द्रः स दारुणः ।
पुष्पोपहारं कुरुते तस्माद् देशाददूरतः ॥ १ ॥
अर्जुनो जयतां श्रेष्ठो माहिष्मत्याः पतिः प्रभुः ।
क्रीडते सह नारीभिर्नर्मदातोयमाश्रितः ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘नर्मदाजीके तटपर जहाँ क्रूर राक्षसराज रावण महादेवजीको फूलोंका उपहार अर्पित कर रहा था, उस स्थानसे थोड़ी दूरपर विजयी वीरोंमें श्रेष्ठ माहिष्मतीपुरीका शक्तिशाली राजा अर्जुन अपनी स्त्रियोंके साथ नर्मदाके जलमें उतरकर क्रीडा कर रहा था ॥ १-२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

क्रूरियाद राक्षसेन्द्र रावणनु नर्मदॆय तीरदल्लि ईश्वरनिगॆ पुष्पोहार अर्पिसिद स्वल्प दूरदल्ले विजयिगळल्लि श्रेष्ठ माहिष्मती पुरिय ऒडॆय शक्तिशाली राजा अर्जुननु तन्न पत्नियरॊन्दिगॆ नर्मदॆयल्लि जलक्रीडॆयाडुत्तिद्दनु.॥1-2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1: Not far from that spot (tasmād deśād adūrataḥ) upon the sandbank of the Narmadā (narmadā-puline) where the dread lord of rākṣasas (rākṣasendraḥ sudāruṇaḥ) was performing his flower-offering (puṣpopahāraṃ kurute)—

2: Arjuna, foremost of conquerors (jayatāṃ śreṣṭhaḥ), the mighty lord and ruler of Māhiṣmatī (māhiṣmatyāḥ patiḥ prabhuḥ), was sporting with his women (krīḍate saha nārībhiḥ), immersed in the waters of the Narmadā (narmadā-toyam āśritaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तासां मध्यगतो राजा रराज च तदार्जुनः ।
करेणूनां सहस्रस्य मध्यस्थ इव कुञ्जरः ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तासां मध्यगतो राजा रराज च तदार्जुनः ।
करेणूनां सहस्रस्य मध्यस्थ इव कुञ्जरः ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘उन सुन्दरियोंके बीचमें विराजमान राजा अर्जुन सहस्रों हथिनियोंके मध्यभागमें स्थित हुए गजराजके समान शोभा पाता था ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आ सुरसुन्दरियर नडुवॆ विराजमान राजा अर्जुननु साविरारु हॆण्णानॆगळ मध्यदल्लि स्थितनाद गजराजनन्तॆ शोभिसुत्तिद्दनु.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Standing in the midst of those women (tāsāṃ madhyagato rājā), King Arjuna shone resplendent (rarāja ca tadārjunaḥ), like a lordly bull elephant standing in the midst of a thousand cow-elephants (kareṇūnāṃ sahasrasya madhyastha iva kuñjaraḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

जिज्ञासुः स तु बाहूनां सहस्रस्योत्तमं बलम् ।
रुरोध नर्मदावेगं बाहुभिर्बहुभिर्वृतः ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

जिज्ञासुः स तु बाहूनां सहस्रस्योत्तमं बलम् ।
रुरोध नर्मदावेगं बाहुभिर्बहुभिर्वृतः ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘अर्जुनके हजार भुजाएँ थीं । उनके उत्तम बलको जाँचनेके लिये उसने उन बहुसंख्यक भुजाओंद्वारा नर्मदाके वेगको रोक दिया ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

तनगिरुव साविर तोळुगळ बलवॆष्टॆम्बुदन्नु तिळियलु तन्न बहुसङ्ख्यक भुजगळिन्द नर्मदॆय प्रवाहवन्नु अड्डगट्टिदनु.॥4॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Eager to test the supreme might (jijñāsuḥ … uttamaṃ balam) of his thousand arms (bāhūnāṃ sahasrasya), ringed about by his multitude of arms (bāhubhir bahubhir vṛtaḥ), he checked the rushing current of the Narmadā (rurodha narmadā-vegam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

कार्तवीर्यभुजासक्तं तज्जलं प्राप्य निर्मलम् ।
कूलोपहारं कुर्वाणं प्रतिस्रोतः प्रधावति ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

कार्तवीर्यभुजासक्तं तज्जलं प्राप्य निर्मलम् ।
कूलोपहारं कुर्वाणं प्रतिस्रोतः प्रधावति ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘कृतवीर्य-पुत्र अर्जुनकी भुजाओंद्वारा रोका हुआ नर्मदाका वह निर्मल जल तटपर पूजा करते हुए रावणके पासतक पहुँच गया और उसी ओर उलटी गतिसे बहने लगा ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

कार्तवीर्यार्जुनन भुजगळिन्द तडॆद नर्मदॆय निर्मल जलवु तीरदल्लि पूजिसुत्तिद्द रावणन बळिगॆ होगि हिन्दॆ-मुन्दॆ हरियतॊडगितु.॥5॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Colliding with Kārtavīrya’s arms (kārtavīrya-bhujāsaktam), that limpid water (taj jalaṃ … nirmalam), sweeping up the riverbank offerings in its wake (kūlopahāraṃ kurvāṇam), rushed swiftly upstream against the current (pratisrotaḥ pradhāvati).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

समीननक्रमकरः सपुष्पकुशसंस्तरः ।
स नर्मदाम्भसो वेगः प्रावृट्काल इवाबभौ ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

समीननक्रमकरः सपुष्पकुशसंस्तरः ।
स नर्मदाम्भसो वेगः प्रावृट्काल इवाबभौ ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘नर्मदाके जलका वह वेग मत्स्य, नक्र, मगर, फूल और कुशास्तरणके साथ बढ़ने लगा । उसमें वर्षाकालके समान बाढ़ आ गयी ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

नर्मदॆय जलद आ वेगवु मत्स्य, मॊसळॆ, हूवु, कुशास्तरणगळॊन्दिगॆ, मळॆगालद महापूरदन्तॆ हॆच्चतॊडगितु.॥6॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Filled with fish, crocodiles, and makaras (sa-mīna-nakra-makaraḥ), and bearing flowers and mats of kuśa grass (sa-puṣpa-kuśa-saṃstaraḥ), that surging rush of Narmadā’s waters (sa narmadāmbhaso vegaḥ) shone like the river in the monsoon season (prāvṛṭ-kāla ivābabhau).

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

When the back-rushing flood sweeps away his floral offerings, Rāvaṇa halts his unfinished rite, observes the river surging from the west while remaining serene to the east, and dispatches Śuka and Sāraṇa westward to discover the cause.

स वेगः कार्तवीर्येण सम्प्रेषित इवाम्भसः ।
पुष्पोपहारं सकलं रावणस्य जहार ह ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स वेगः कार्तवीर्येण सम्प्रेषित इवाम्भसः ।
पुष्पोपहारं सकलं रावणस्य जहार ह ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘जलका वह वेग, जिसे मानो कार्तवीर्य अर्जुनने ही भेजा हो, रावणके समस्त पुष्पोपहारको बहा ले गया ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

कार्तवीर्यानने कळिसिदनो ऎम्बन्तॆ आ जल प्रवाहवु रावणन समस्त पुष्पोहार कॊच्चिकॊण्डु होयितु.॥7॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

That rushing surge of water (sa vegaḥ … ambhasaḥ), as though dispatched by Kārtavīrya himself (kārtavīryeṇa sampreṣita iva), swept away the entire floral offering of Rāvaṇa (puṣpopahāraṃ sakalaṃ rāvaṇasya jahāra ha).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

रावणोऽर्धसमाप्तं तमुत्सृज्य नियमं तदा ।
नर्मदां पश्यते कान्तां प्रतिकूलां यथा प्रियाम् ॥ ८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

रावणोऽर्धसमाप्तं तमुत्सृज्य नियमं तदा ।
नर्मदां पश्यते कान्तां प्रतिकूलां यथा प्रियाम् ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘रावणका वह पूजन-सम्बन्धी नियम अभी आधा ही समाप्त हुआ था, उसी दशामें उसे छोड़कर वह प्रतिकूल हुई कमनीय कान्तिवाली प्रेयसीकी भाँति नर्मदाकी ओर देखने लगा ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रावणन आ पूजॆ अर्ध मुगिदित्तु अष्टे. आ स्थितियल्लि अदन्नु बिट्टु अवनु कोपिसिकॊण्ड कमनीय कान्तियुळ्ळ प्रेयसियन्नु नोडिदन्तॆ नर्मदॆयन्नु नोडतॊडगिदनु.॥8॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Abandoning that half-completed sacred rite (ardha-samāptaṃ tam utsṛjya niyamam), Rāvaṇa then gazed upon the lovely Narmadā (narmadāṃ paśyate kāntām), turned contrary like a perverse beloved (pratikūlāṃ yathā priyām).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

पश्चिमेन तु तं दृष्ट्वा सागरोद‍्गारसन्निभम् ।
वर्धन्तमम्भसो वेगं पूर्वामाशां प्रविश्य तु ॥ ९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

पश्चिमेन तु तं दृष्ट्वा सागरोद‍्गारसन्निभम् ।
वर्धन्तमम्भसो वेगं पूर्वामाशां प्रविश्य तु ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘पश्चिमसे आते और पूर्व दिशामें प्रवेश करके बढ़ते हुए जलके उस वेगको उसने देखा । वह ऐसा जान पड़ता था, मानो समुद्रमें ज्वार आ गया हो ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

पश्चिमदिन्द बन्दु पूर्वक्कॆ हरियुव नीरिन प्रवाहवन्नु नोडिदनु. अदु समुद्रक्कॆ भरत बन्दन्तॆ अनिसुत्तित्तु.॥9॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Seeing from the west (paścimena tu taṃ dṛṣṭvā) that swelling torrent of water (vardhantam ambhaso vegam), resembling the tidal upwelling of the ocean (sāgarodgāra-sannibham), pressing into the eastern quarter (pūrvām āśāṃ praviśya tu),

ततोऽनुद्‍भ्रान्तशकुनां स्वभावे परमे स्थिताम् ।
निर्विकाराङ्गनाभासामपश्यद् रावणो नदीम् ॥ १० ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततोऽनुद्‍भ्रान्तशकुनां स्वभावे परमे स्थिताम् ।
निर्विकाराङ्गनाभासामपश्यद् रावणो नदीम् ॥ १० ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘उसके तटवर्ती वृक्षोंपर रहनेवाले पक्षियोंमें कोई घबराहट नहीं थी । वह नदी अपनी परम उत्तम स्वाभाविक स्थितिमें स्थित थी—उसका जल पहले ही जैसा स्वच्छ एवं निर्मल दिखायी देता था । उसमें वर्षाकालिक बाढ़के समय जो मलिनता आदि विकार होते थे, उनका उस समय सर्वथा अभाव था । रावणने उस नदीको विकारशून्य हृदयवाली नारीके समान देखा ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आदरॆ नदिय तीरदल्लिद्द वृक्षगळ मेलॆ इद्द पक्षिगळु कॊञ्चवू गाबरिगॊळ्ळदॆ शान्तवागिद्दवु. नदिय स्वभावदल्लियू याव व्यत्यास काणुत्तिरलिल्ल. मळॆगालदल्लि उण्टागुव कॆसर नीरिनन्तॆ इरदॆ स्वच्छ निर्मलवागि काणुत्तित्तु. विकारशून्यळाद स्त्रीयन्तॆ इद्द आ महानदियन्नु रावणनु नोडिदनु.॥10॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Rāvaṇa then looked eastward and beheld the river (apaśyad rāvaṇo nadīm) with its waterbirds undisturbed (anubhrānta-śakunām), halted in the suspension of its natural course (svabhāvoparame sthitām), looking like an unperturbed woman (nirvikārāṅganābhāsām).

सव्येतरकराङ्गुल्या ह्यशब्दास्यो दशाननः ।
वेगप्रभवमन्वेष्टुं सोऽदिशच्छुकसारणौ ॥ ११ ॥

सव्यतेरकराङ्गुल्या ह्यशब्दास्यो दशाननः ।
वेगप्रभवमन्वेष्टुं सोऽदिशच्छुकसारणौ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘उसके मुखसे एक शब्द भी नहीं निकला । उसने मौनव्रतकी रक्षाके लिये बिना बोले ही दाहिने हाथकी अङ्गुलीसे संकेतमात्र करके बाढ़के कारणका पता लगानेके निमित्त शुक और सारणको आदेश दिया ॥ ११ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

7.32.1–4 (Kārtavīrya’s Aquatic Sport and Trial of Strength)

Introducing Sarga 32 as the incident coinciding with Rāvaṇa’s Śiva-worship (रावणकर्तृकशिवार्चनकालिकं वृत्तान्तमाह), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) links 7.32.1–2 into a single syntactic period (श्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयि), glossing कुरुते in a past sense (अकरोत्) and अदूरतः as ‘nearby’ (निकटे). Just as Rāvaṇa worships solemnly upon the sandbank, King Arjuna Kārtavīrya of Māhiṣmatī sports playfully downstream amid his queens like a tusker amid a thousand cow-elephants. On 7.32.4, Śivasahāya observes that बाहुभिः construes doubly (उभयान्वयि) with both वृतः and रुरोध: encircled by his own thousand arms, Arjuna impulsively tests their supreme strength by damming the Narmadā.

7.32.5 (The Backflow and the Two Senses of Kūlopahāra)

Struck against Kārtavīrya’s living weir, the Narmadā reverses course. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss प्रतिस्रोतः as ‘against the flow’ (प्रतिप्रवाहम्), Rāma Varmā adding प्रतिकूलमिति यावत्. On कूलोपहारं कुर्वाणम्, Śivasahāya offers an ingenious syntactic reading: rather than describing the flood eroding the banks (as in Baroda CE’s कूलापहारम्), he supplies रावणम् as object of प्राप्य—the reversed water surged upstream ‘reaching Rāvaṇa, who was performing his worship-offering upon the riverbank’ (कूले पूजां कुर्वाणं रावणं प्राप्य), sharpening the narrative collision between ascetic devotion and royal sport.

7.32.6 (Monsoon Fury in Clear Weather)

Turning a serene river into an unseasonal torrent, the backwash sweeps up fish, crocodiles, makaras, blossoms, and mats of sacred kuśa grass. Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja gloss कुशसंस्तरः as ‘strewn layers of kuśa grass’ (कुशास्तरणम्), with Rāma Varmā adding a realistic riverside detail: those grass mats had been spread along the sandbank by companies of sages for their daily Vedic rites (स तु तत्पुलिने कर्मनिमित्तं ऋषिगणकृतः). Śivasahāya notes that laden with aquatic creatures and floral debris, the Narmadā’s sudden spate resembled the full fury of the monsoon (प्रावृट्काले इव).

7.32.7–9 (The Disrupted Worship and Ocean-Tide Comparison)

Supplying रावणं प्रति (‘dispatched against Rāvaṇa’) in 7.32.7, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) frames the flood as Kārtavīrya’s unwitting gauntlet. Forced to break off his Śiva-worship midway, Rāvaṇa looks upon the lovely Narmadā flowing backward like a cross mistress (pratikūlā, punning on ‘against the banks’ and ‘contrary’). On 7.32.9, Rāma Varmā explains सागरोद्गारः as the moonrise tidal bore (चन्द्रोदयकालिकसमुद्रपरिवृद्धिः), noting that ocean-bound rivers are well known to reverse course near the coast; citing Kataka (कतकसम्मतश्च), he defends प्रविश्य तु of the flood advancing eastward, whereas Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) glosses समुद्रपरीवाहसन्निभम् and takes Rāvaṇa as subject of प्रविश्य.

7.32.10 (Diagnosing an Artificial Disturbance)

Comparing the westward backwash with the eastward river, Rāvaṇa deduces human interference. Reading स्वभावे परमे and joining 7.32.9–10 (श्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयि), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) observes that since the river shows no monsoon agitation (प्रावृट्कालाभावेन विकारसमयरहिताम्) and its birds remain unstartled (अनुद्भ्रान्तशकुनाम्), Rāvaṇa concludes that some outside agent caused this upheaval (अन्येन केनचिदयमुपद्रवः कृत इति रावणेन निर्धारितम्). Commenting on our text’s स्वभावोपरमे, Govindarāja glosses it as ‘standing in the cessation of its river-nature’ (नदीस्वभावस्योपरमेऽभावे), adding that Rāvaṇa looked specifically to determine whether the surge arose naturally or from an external cause.

7.32.11–12 (The Right-Hand Signal to Śuka and Sāraṇa)

The commentators offer two vivid portraits of Rāvaṇa’s command in 7.32.11. Commenting on सशब्दं च, Govindarāja explains that Rāvaṇa beckoned with his right forefinger accompanied by a sharp finger-snap and stern exclamation (हुङ्कारसहितम्), while Rāma Varmā glosses सव्येतरकराङ्गुल्या as gesturing urgently with his right hand (दक्षिणहस्ताङ्गुल्या शीघ्रान्वेषणद्योतकाभिनयं कृत्वा). Reading the Parimal variant निःशब्दास्यः, Śivasahāya explains that Rāvaṇa remained strictly silent under his ritual vow of worship (नियमेन मौनीभूतः), mutely motioning Śuka and Sāraṇa westward along the sky-path (आकाशमार्गगौ, Tilaka) to trace the current’s source (वेगोत्पत्तिकारणम्).

7.32.13–15 (The Vision of the Thousand-Armed Titan)

Flying just half a yojana upstream, Rāvaṇa’s scouts discover the living cause of the flood. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) normalize the epic optative पश्येताम् in 7.32.13 as past indicative अपश्यताम्. Construing 7.32.14–17 as a single four-verse sentence (श्लोकचतुष्टयमेकान्वयि), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) offers two nuances for तोयव्याकुलमूर्धजम्—hair dipping into the waters or thoroughly drenched and tangled—and notes मदरक्तान्तनयनम् as eyes flushed red at their corners with drink. In 7.32.15, Rāma Varmā highlights the monumental simile comparing Arjuna’s thousand arms damming the river to a mountain’s thousand foothills (pāda) spanning the earth.

7.32.16–17 (The Elephant-Lord Amid His Queens and the Scouts’ Awe)

Echoing the poet’s own opening simile in 7.32.3, Vālmīki now filters that regal tableau through the astonished eyes of the rākṣasa spies: Arjuna is ringed by a thousand young beauties like a tusker surrounded by a thousand rutting cow-elephants. The superlative अद्भुततमम् (‘most wondrous’) in 7.32.17 captures the shock of veteran demon scouts who have witnessed the conquerors of heaven yet have never beheld a mortal monarch of such stupendous scale. Wisely refraining from confrontation, Śuka and Sāraṇa wheel back immediately to report to Rāvaṇa.

7.32.18 (A Living Dam—Grammatical Notes on Rodhavat and Krīḍāpayati)

Reporting breathlessly to their king, the scouts do not yet know the stranger’s name (कोऽप्यसौ) and describe him solely through his awe-inspiring stature and feat. All three commentators pause over the pair of epic forms in 7.32.18cd. Rāma Varmā derives रोधः instrumentally as a dam or barrage (रुध्यतेऽनेनेति रोधः सेतुस्तद्वदित्यर्थः, echoed by Śivasahāya as सेतुवत्), whereas Govindarāja glosses तटवत् (‘like an embankment’) and notes the thematic stem in -a as epic (अकारान्तत्वमार्षम्). Both Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa likewise flag the causative क्रीडापयति (for क्रीडयति) as taking an archaic p-augment (पुगार्षः).

7.32.19–20 (Rāvaṇa’s Instant Recognition)

Closing their report, Śuka and Sāraṇa describe the river hurling repeated swells like moonrise tides (चन्द्रोदयकालिकसागरवृद्धिसदृशान्, Śivasahāya). Rāvaṇa needs no name to identify the stranger: both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explain that he exclaims “Arjuna!” by immediate inference that no other being possesses such thousand-armed power (अन्यस्यैवंविधसामर्थ्यासंभवयुक्त्या). While both gloss निशाम्य as ‘seeing’ (दृष्ट्वा), Śivasahāya reads निशम्य (‘hearing their report’) and notes how Rāvaṇa’s hunger for combat (yuddha-lālasa) instantly overrides his interrupted devotion.

7.32.21–22 (Dire Portents of Blood and Storm)

As Rāvaṇa marches toward Arjuna, nature itself warns of his impending humiliation. Śivasahāya analyzes the violent, roaring, dust-choked gale (सरजाः) of 7.32.21, while all three commentators stress the sinister portent (utpāta) of 7.32.22ab: a solitary thunderclap accompanied by bloody rain-drops. Rāma Varmā cites the earlier exegete Kataka (रक्तवर्षकमेघानां सकृद्रावोऽप्युत्पात इति कतकः), and Govindarāja agrees that a single isolated boom from rain-bearing clouds is a classic ill omen. Blinded by hubris, Rāvaṇa ignores every warning and advances flanked by his five foremost captains.

7.32.23–24 (Arrival at the Narmadā Pool)

Moving swiftly with Mahodara, Mahāpārśva, Dhūmrākṣa, Śuka, and Sāraṇa, the collyrium-dark titan reaches the deep Narmadā pool where Arjuna still sports carefree among his queens. On 7.32.24, Govindarāja cites the Amarakośa (वाशिता स्त्री करेण्वां च) to gloss वाशिताभिः as ‘cow-elephants’ (करेणुभिः), and both he and Rāma Varmā gloss पश्यते as पश्यति स्म. The closing hemistich (roṣād rakta-nayanaḥ … baloddhataḥ) carries directly into the next spread as Rāvaṇa thunders his challenge to Arjuna’s ministers.

7.32.25–27 (Rāvaṇa’s Challenge and the Ministers’ Irony)

Continuing the syntax of 7.32.24, Rāvaṇa demands that Arjuna’s counselors immediately summon their monarch. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss इत्येवम् as ‘in the following manner’ (वक्ष्यमाणप्रकारेण). Unintimidated, the Haihaya ministers rise armed and answer with biting sarcasm, which both Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa label व्यङ्ग्योक्तिः (‘ironic speech’). Rāma Varmā unpacks their taunt: “Bravo, Rāvaṇa! How finely you judge the proper hour for war—daring to challenge a king flushed with drink (मदपानमत्तम्) in the company of his ladies!” Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) adds that विज्ञातः ironically signifies न विज्ञातः (‘utterly uncomprehended’).

7.32.28–29 (Warrior Dharma vs. Predatory Ambush)

The ministers’ simile in 7.32.28ef exposes the breach of kṣatriya code: attacking an unarmed king enjoying water-sport before his queens is like a tiger ambushing a rutting bull elephant amid his herd. Govindarāja glosses क्षीबम् as मत्तम् and supplies इह (‘spend the night here’) with उष्यताम्. On 7.32.29, Rāma Varmā notes that they drop sarcasm to speak in earnest (स्वमतेनाहुः): endure today’s delay (युद्धविलम्बं सहस्व) and fight Arjuna at dawn, supplying the elliptical verb युध्यस्व (while Śivasahāya supplies युद्धं प्रवर्तयेः).

7.32.30 (The Ministers’ Defiant Ultimatum)

Anticipating that Rāvaṇa’s belligerent pride will reject civilized restraint, the counselors draw a firm line in 7.32.30: if his thirst for combat cannot wait until sunrise, he must first cut his way through them before ever reaching Arjuna. Both Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya paraphrase the Parimal reading (यदि वापि त्वरा तुभ्यं … निपात्यास्मान्), while our text preserves Govindarāja’s reading (यद्यद्यास्ति मतिर्योद्धुं … निहत्यास्मान्). Their loyal stand immediately triggers the skirmish on the Narmadā bank.

7.32.31–32 (Cannibal Violence and the Uproar on the Bank)

Without deigning to reply, Rāvaṇa’s followers answer the ministers’ chivalrous appeal with rākṣasa savagery—not merely slaying Arjuna’s counselors (हिंसिताः, Śivasahāya), but devouring them on the spot (bhakṣitāś ca bubhukṣitaiḥ). Instantly the quiet riverbank erupts into pandemonium. On 7.32.32, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) notes the acoustic figure (शब्दालङ्कारः) in the tumultuous cry हलहला and glosses अनुयात्राः as Arjuna’s attendant officers and counselors (अनुचराः मन्त्रिणः), while Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) likewise glosses अनुयात्राणाम् as ‘followers’ (अनुयायिनाम्), confirming the proper reading behind the corrupted Southern print अनुयातऽणाम्.

7.32.33–34 (The Haihaya Counter-Surge)

Rallied by the uproar, the Haihaya host closes in around the invaders. Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) identify कर्षणम् in 7.32.33 as a distinct weapon class (आयुधविशेषः, a hooked or tearing shaft), and both Tilaka and Śivasahāya supply अर्जुनयोधाः … अभूवुः as the understood subject harassing Rāvaṇa and his retinue (रावणसहितान् राक्षसान्). In 7.32.34, Śivasahāya matches the terrifying momentum (vega) of the Haihaya warriors directly to the roar of a monster-filled sea (नक्रादिसहितसमुद्रस्य निःस्वन इव सुदारुण आसीत्), echoing the aquatic imagery of 7.32.6.

7.32.35–36 (Rāvaṇa’s Captains Prevail and the Alarm Reaches Arjuna)

Despite the Haihaya army’s bravery, Rāvaṇa’s seasoned warlords—Prahasta, Śuka, and Sāraṇa—turn the tide, consuming Kārtavīrya’s ranks with fiery martial energy (tejas). Only now does the crisis interrupt the royal recreation: on 7.32.36, Rāma Varmā glosses समन्त्रिणः as ‘of Rāvaṇa together with his ministers’ (मन्त्रिसहितस्य रावणस्य). Whereas the Parimal text depicts the messengers as ‘terrified men’ (पुरुषैर्भयविह्वलैः), our Southern text follows Govindarāja’s reading द्वाररक्षिभिः (‘by the royal gatekeepers’ posted to guard the king’s riverside pavilion).

7.32.37–38 (From Aquatic Sport to Doomsday Fire)

Arjuna’s transformation from playful monarch to cosmic avenger occupies a single breath. On 7.32.37, both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) supply उक्त्वा to bridge the compressed syntax (‘having heard, and having told the women “Do not fear!”’—a reading Baroda CE explicitly adopts), and Rāma Varmā identifies अञ्जनः as the directional elephant (दिग्गजः) emerging from the Gaṅgā. On 7.32.38, Rāma Varmā points out the metaphor (रूपकम्) in अर्जुनपावकः (‘the fire that is Arjuna’) and glosses युगान्ते as the hour of cosmic dissolution (प्रलयकाले).

7.32.39–40 (Solar Charge and Garuḍa’s Swoop)

Gleaming in golden armlets (वरहेमाङ्गदः, Śivasahāya), Arjuna charges the night-roamers like the sun scattering darkness—a verb (अभिदुद्राव) that Rāma Varmā glosses with causative force as ‘put them to flight’ (द्रावयामास). On 7.32.40, all three commentators unpack बाहुविक्षेपकरणाम् as a mace whose whirling is wrought by the sweep of his arms (बाहुभिर्विक्षेपकरणं भ्रमसंपादनं यस्यास्ताम्, Tilaka; भ्रमणम्, Govindarāja). All three likewise highlight the emphatic एव in आपपातैव: adopting Garuḍa’s velocity, Arjuna descended upon them instantaneously without the slightest delay (न तु विलम्बितवान्, Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa; मनागपि विलम्बं न चक्रे, Śiromaṇi).

7.32.41–42 (Prahasta as Mount Vindhya)

Stepping boldly between Arjuna and Rāvaṇa, the veteran general Prahasta halts the king’s charge. On 7.32.41, Rāma Varmā recalls the Purāṇic myth behind the double Vindhya simile: jealous of Meru, Mount Vindhya once grew skyward to obstruct the sun’s daily chariot (विन्ध्यपर्वतोऽर्कमार्गं रुद्ध्वा स्थित इति कथा) until humbled by Agastya—an apt comparison for Prahasta barring the solar Arjuna. On 7.32.42, both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja clarify the genitive अस्य प्रेषयन् as ‘hurling it for Arjuna’s destruction’ (अर्जुनस्य संहाराय प्रक्षिपन्), as Prahasta lets fly his iron-bound pestle with a thunderous roar.

7.32.43–44 (The Blazing Pestle and Arjuna’s Unimpaired Valor)

All three commentators elucidate अशोकापीडः in 7.32.43 as a bright red crest or chaplet of Aśoka blossoms (अशोकपुष्पशिखा, Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya; अशोकपुष्पकृतः आपीडः, Govindarāja), capturing the crimson trail of fire streaming from Prahasta’s iron weapon. On 7.32.44, Govindarāja directly glosses our text’s playful alliteration सगदोऽगदविक्रमः: ‘holding a mace (गदासहितः) and possessing unimpaired, unobstructed valor’ (अगदः अरोगः अप्रतिबन्ध इति यावत् विक्रमो यस्य), glossing वञ्चयामास as ‘parried’ (प्रतिहतवान्). Reading the Parimal variant गदया गतविह्वलः, Tilaka glosses ‘fearless’ (गतभयः), while Śivasahāya takes निपुणम् either of Prahasta’s skilled throw or Arjuna’s deft parry.

7.32.45–46 (The Five-Hundred-Armed Blow and Prahasta’s Fall)

Counterattacking at once, Arjuna wields a weapon scaled to his mythic anatomy. All three commentators analyze पञ्चबाहुशतोच्छ्रयाम् in 7.32.45 as a massive mace whose upward heave requires five hundred arms (पञ्चबाहुशतैरुच्छ्रयः उत्क्षेपो यस्यास्ताम्). The single impact shatters Prahasta’s mountain-like stance from 7.32.41: while Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja construe स्थितः directly with the mountain (शैल इव स्थितो निपपात), Śivasahāya takes it of the stalwart demon himself (स्थिरबुद्धिः प्रहस्तः), felled like a crag split by Indra’s thunderbolt (vajri-vajra).

7.32.47–48 (Flight of the Demon Captains and Rāvaṇa’s Entry)

Prahasta’s sudden collapse breaks the nerve of Laṅkā’s veteran commanders: Mārīca, Śuka, Sāraṇa, Mahodara, and Dhūmrākṣa hastily withdraw (निवृत्ता बभूवुः, Śivasahāya) from the field. Left standing alone in 7.32.48—framed by the crisp double locative absolute of his ministers’ flight and Prahasta’s fall—Rāvaṇa wastes no words and charges directly at Arjuna, initiating the epic clash between twenty arms and a thousand.

7.32.49–52 (A Ninefold Simile Garland of Cosmic Combat)

Introducing the duel through the stark numerical contrast of a thousand arms against twenty, Vālmīki unleashes an unbroken three-verse cascade of nine dual similes—oceans, uprooted mountains, suns, fires, elephants, rival bulls, thunderclouds, lions, and finally Rudra and Time the Destroyer. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construes 7.32.50–52 as a single periodic sentence (श्लोकत्रयमेकान्वयि) culminating in their ferocious mace exchange. On 7.32.51, both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss वाशितार्थे as ‘contending for a female’ (करिणीरूपप्रयोजनाय युयुधाते), applying vāśitā to a prized cow or cow-elephant.

7.32.53 (Mountain-Like Endurance)

Moving from aggressive simile to defensive fortitude, 7.32.53 compares the combatants’ resilience to primeval winged mountains absorbing Indra’s thunderbolts (vajra-prahāra). Śivasahāya highlights the exact symmetry of the verse: just as steadfast peaks bore dread thunder-strokes without yielding, so man and rākṣasa (nara-rākṣasau) withstood each other’s crushing mace impacts. The juxtaposition of nara (‘mortal man’) and rākṣasa subtly foreshadows Book VI, where another mortal king will finally break Rāvaṇa’s boon-shielded pride.

7.32.54 (Thunderbolt Echoes Across the Quarters)

Completing the sensory crescendo with sound, 7.32.54 matches the clang of iron maces to lightning crashes reverberating through the sky. All three commentators elucidate its rare vocabulary: Rāma Varmā glosses अशनिरवेभ्यः as the booming of falling thunderbolts (अशनिपातध्वनिभ्यः), प्रतिश्रुतिः as ‘echo’ (प्रतिध्वनिः), गदापोथैः as ‘mace impacts’ (गदापातैः), and प्रतिश्रुताः as ‘resounding with echoes’ (प्रतिध्वनिमत्यः). Govindarāja likewise glosses गदापोथैः as गदापातैः and analyzes प्रतिश्रुताः as a possessive participle (प्रतिश्रुतवन्तः, मत्वर्थीयः), with Śivasahāya concurring.

7.32.55–56 (Lightning and Meteor Upon Titanic Chests)

Shifting from sound to visual brilliance, Vālmīki contrasts the two combatants’ strikes. On 7.32.55, both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) read a double illumination: just as Saudāminī lightning turns the sky bright gold, Arjuna’s mace falling upon his foe’s chest (अहितोरसि रावणोरसि) lit up Rāvaṇa’s breast with golden sparks (सौदामनी नभ इव रावणोरः काञ्चनाभं स्वर्णवर्णं चकार). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construes नभः directly as object of चक्रे (‘turned the sky golden’), and on 7.32.56 pairs this with Rāvaṇa’s mace blazing like a meteor (ulkā) against the immovable mountain of Arjuna’s torso.

7.32.57–58 (Tireless Parity and the Duel of Bali and Indra)

Neither warrior shows a trace of fatigue (खेदं नायाति), prompting Śivasahāya to connect their stamina directly to the mythological precedent: because both remain equally unwearied, their battle rivaled the ancient contest of the asura emperor Bali and Indra (बलीन्द्रयोरिव सममासीत्)—a motif that will culminate in Rāvaṇa’s capture in 7.32.63 and 7.32.72. On 7.32.58, Rāma Varmā unpacks the compressed वृषायुध्यन् as ‘like bulls’ (वृषाविव), while Śivasahāya notes the epic number-shift (vacana-vyatyaya) in the plural verb अयुध्यन् used of the two duelists.

7.32.59–60 (The Shattered Mace Against Brahmā’s Boon)

Pouring his entire vital force (सर्वप्राणेन सर्वप्रयत्नेन, Śivasahāya) into a decisive throw, Arjuna drives his mace square between Rāvaṇa’s breasts. Yet the weapon breaks in half. All three commentators pinpoint the theological cause in वरदानकृतत्राणे: shielded by Brahmā’s boon of invulnerability, Rāvaṇa’s chest remains unpierced. Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja explain दुर्बलेव यथावेगम् closely: as if stripped of strength (बलरहितेव) and unable to deliver a blow equal to its own velocity (स्ववेगोचितं प्रहर्तुमसमर्था), the mace split in two on its own and dropped to the ground.

7.32.61–63 (Stunned by Shock and Bound Like Bali)

Though Brahmā’s boon saves Rāvaṇa’s life, it cannot spare him the sheer concussive impact of Arjuna’s blow. On 7.32.61, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) notes that अपासर्पत् implies staggering backward without turning tail (अपराङ्मुख एव पृष्ठतोऽगच्छत्), while both he and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss निष्टनन् as ‘groaning in pain’ (रुदन्; Śivasahāya शब्दयन्). Spotting his foe dazed (vihvala), Arjuna turns instantly from weaponry to bare-handed grappling—pouncing like Garuḍa on a snake and binding Daśānana with his thousand arms just as Vāmana-Nārāyaṇa bound the asura sovereign Bali.

7.32.64 (Heavenly Applause and the Grammar of Sādhvīti)

The celestials’ relief bursts forth in floral rain and cries of acclaim. All three commentators address the lengthened ī in साध्वीतिवादिनः (where साधु + इति would classically yield साध्विति). Rāma Varmā treats the lengthening as epic license (दीर्घ आर्षः), whereas Govindarāja takes साध्वी as a regular feminine adjective modifying an understood ‘deed’ (इयं क्रिया साध्वीति वदन्तः). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) cites both positions, observing that while elders (वृद्धाः) call the long vowel ārṣa, construing साध्वी with an implied feminine noun such as बन्धनक्रिया (‘this act of binding is noble’) eliminates any need to posit irregularity.

7.32.65–66 (Triumphant Roar and Prahasta’s Rally)

Holding the dreaded overlord of Laṅkā as easily as a tiger carries a deer or a lion subdues an elephant, Arjuna lets out repeated roars of exhilaration (आनन्दस्वनं चकार, Śivasahāya on the variant ररास). Yet the battle has one last flare-up: recovering consciousness just in time to see his master captive, the loyal general Prahasta rallies the scattered rākṣasas and launches a desperate final charge to rescue Daśānana.

7.32.67–68 (The Monsoon Charge and Irregular Sandhi)

Advancing like dark rainclouds rising over the sea at summer’s end (आतपापाये वर्षाकाले, Rāma Varmā; ग्रीष्मापगमे, Śivasahāya), the rākṣasas shout frantically for Rāvaṇa’s release. The singular verb सोत्ससर्ज in 7.32.68 following plural भाषन्तः prompts two solutions: Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) flags the form as ārṣa with plural sense (सर्व एव त्यक्तवन्तः, matching Baroda CE’s उत्ससर्जुः), whereas Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) supplies प्रापुः for the shouting rākṣasas and takes सः of Prahasta hurling pestles and pikes, noting the ārṣa sandhi in स + उत्ससर्ज.

7.32.69–70 (Midair Interception with Spare Arms)

Even while pinning down the ten-headed emperor, Arjuna remains completely unruffled (asambhrānta). Rāma Varmā vividly clarifies the physical feat in 7.32.69: while part of his thousand arms secured Rāvaṇa, Arjuna caught the oncoming demon missiles in midair before they reached his body (स्वसमीपमनागतान्येव अन्तरिक्षस्थान्येव) with his remaining free hands (रावणग्राहि-करव्यतिरिक्त-करैः जग्राह). Turning those very weapons back upon their throwers in 7.32.70, he disperses the cloud-like rākṣasa host like a gale scattering storm clouds.

7.32.71–72 (Triumphal Entry into Māhiṣmatī in Vaṃśastha)

Routing the remnant forces, Arjuna marches captive Rāvaṇa into Māhiṣmatī flanked by jubilant friends. Shifting into the ornate twelve-syllable Vaṃśastha meter for the finale—which Śivasahāya notes restates the triumph in elevated lyrical mode (तदेव भङ्ग्यन्तरेणाह)—Vālmīki pictures Brahmins and townsfolk raining heaps of blossoms and auspicious unbroken rice (कुसुमाक्षतानां समूहैः) upon the victor. The closing simile comparing thousand-armed Arjuna to thousand-eyed Indra (sahasra-locana) leading captive Bali crowns the sarga’s sustained Indra–Bali parallel.

Having seized him like a tiger carrying off a deer (vyāghro mṛgam ivādāya) or a lion seizing a bull elephant (mṛgarāḍ iva dantinam), the Haihaya king roared again and again with joy like a thundercloud (nanāda haihayo rājā harṣād ambudavan muhuḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

व्याघ्रो मृगमिवादाय मृगराडिव कुञ्जरम् ।
ररास हैहयो राजा हर्षादम्बुदवन्मुहुः ॥ ६६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

व्याघ्रो मृगमिवादाय मृगराडिव कुञ्जरम् ।
ररास हैहयो राजा हर्षादम्बुदवन्मुहुः ॥ ६६ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘जैसे व्याघ्र किसी हिरणको दबोच लेता है अथवा सिंह हाथीको धर दबाता है, उसी प्रकार रावणको अपने वशमें करके हैहयराज अर्जुन हर्षातिरेकसे मेघके समान बारम्बार गर्जना करने लगा ॥ ६६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

हुलियु जिङ्कॆयन्नु, सिंहवु आनॆयन्नु हिडियुवन्तॆये रावणनन्नु तन्न वशपडिसिकॊडु हैहयराजनु हर्षातिरेकदिन्द मेघदन्तॆ पुनः पुनः गर्जिसतॊडगिदनु.॥66॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Meanwhile Prahasta, having regained his senses (prahastas tu samāśvastaḥ) and beholding Daśānana bound (dṛṣṭvā baddhaṃ daśānanam), charged furiously against the king together with those rākṣasas (saha tai rākṣasaiḥ kruddhaś cābhidudrāva pārthivam).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

As Prahasta and the rākṣasas charge like monsoon clouds hurling pestles and pikes, Arjuna catches their missiles in midair, scatters them with their own weapons, and enters Māhiṣmatī with captive Rāvaṇa amid floral showers.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

प्रहस्तस्तु समाश्वस्तो दृष्ट्वा बद्धं दशाननम् ।
सहसा राक्षसः क्रुद्धो ह्यभिदुद्राव हैहयम् ॥ ६७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

प्रहस्तस्तु समाश्वस्तो दृष्ट्वा बद्धं दशाननम् ।
सहसा राक्षसः क्रुद्धो ह्यभिदुद्राव हैहयम् ॥ ६७ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘इसके बाद प्रहस्तने होश सँभाला । दशमुख रावणको बँधा हुआ देख वह राक्षस सहसा कुपित हो हैहयराजकी ओर दौड़ा ॥ ६७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

बळिक प्रहस्तनु ऎच्चरगॊण्डु, दशमुख रावणनु बन्धितनादुदन्नु नोडि राक्षसनु कुपितनागि हैहयन कडॆगॆ ओडिदनु.॥67॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

The surging rush of those charging night-roamers (naktañcarāṇāṃ vegas tu teṣām āpatatām) looked like the rising host of rainclouds over the ocean at the passing of summer heat (udbhūta ātapāpāye payodānām ivāmbudhau).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

नक्तञ्चराणां वेगस्तु तेषामापततां बभौ ।
उद्भूत आतपापाये पयोदानामिवाम्बुधौ ॥ ६८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

नक्तञ्चराणां वेगस्तु तेषामापततां बभौ ।
उद्भूत आतपापाये पयोदानामिवाम्बुधौ ॥ ६८ ॥

नक्तञ्चराणां वेगस्तु शेषामापततां बभौ ।
उद्भूत आतपापाये पयोदानामिवाम्बुधौ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘जैसे वर्षाकाल आनेपर समुद्रमें बादलोंका वेग बढ़ जाता है, उसी प्रकार वहाँ आक्रमण करते हुए उन निशाचरोंका वेग बढ़ा हुआ प्रतीत होता था ॥ ६८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मळॆगाल बन्दाग समुद्रदल्लि उब्बर बरुवन्तॆ अल्लि आक्रमिसुत्तिरुव आ निशाचरर वेग हॆच्चिदन्तॆ अनिसुत्तित्तु.॥68॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Shouting again and again, “Release him! Release him!” and “Halt! Halt!” (muñca muñceti bhāṣantas tiṣṭha tiṣṭheti cāsakṛt), he [Prahasta] hurled iron pestles and pikes at Arjuna (musalāni sa-śūlāni sotsasarja tadārjune).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

मुञ्चमुञ्चेति भाषन्तस्तिष्ठतिष्ठेति चासकृत् ।
मुसलानि च शूलानि सोत्ससर्ज तदा रणे ॥ ६९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

मुञ्चमुञ्चेति भाषन्तस्तिष्ठतिष्ठेति चासकृत् ।
मुसलानि च शूलानि सोत्ससर्ज तदा रणे ॥ ६९ ॥

मुञ्च मञ्चेति भाषं तस्तिष्ठ तिष्ठेति चासकृत् ।
मुसलानि च शूलानि सोत्ससर्ज तदा रणे ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘छोड़ो, छोड़ो, ठहरो, ठहरो’ ऐसा बारम्बार कहते हुए राक्षस अर्जुनकी ओर दौड़े । उस समय प्रहस्तने रणभूमिमें अर्जुनपर मूसल और शूलके प्रहार किये ॥ ६९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

बिडु, बिडु, निल्लु, ऎन्दु पदे-पदे हेळुत्ता राक्षसरु अर्जुनन कडॆगॆ ओडिदरु. आग प्रहस्तरु रणभूमियल्लि अर्जुनन मेलॆ मुसल, शूलगळिन्द प्रहरिसिदनु.॥69॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Unperturbed, Arjuna, destroyer of foes (asambhrāntas tadārjunaḥ … ari-niṣūdanaḥ), swiftly caught in midair before they even reached him (aprāptāny eva tāny āśu … jagrāha) all those weapons of the enemies of the gods (āyudhāny amarārīṇām).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अप्राप्तान्येव तान्याशु असम्भ्रान्तस्तदार्जुनः ।
आयुधान्यमरारीणां जग्राहारिनिषूदनः ॥ ७० ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अप्राप्तान्येव तान्याशु असम्भ्रान्तस्तदार्जुनः ।
आयुधान्यमरारीणां जग्राहारिनिषूदनः ॥ ७० ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘परंतु अर्जुनको उस समय घबराहट नहीं हुई । उस शत्रुसूदन वीरने प्रहस्त आदि देवद्रोही निशाचरोंके छोड़े हुए उन अस्त्रोंको अपने शरीरतक आनेसे पहले ही पकड़ लिया ॥ ७० ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आदरॆ अर्जुननु आग गाबरिगॊळ्ळलिल्ल. आ शत्रुसूदन वीरनु प्रहस्तादि देवद्रोहि निशाचररु बिट्टिरुव अस्त्रगळु तन्न बळि बरुव मॊदले हिडिदुकॊण्डनु.॥70॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then, piercing the rākṣasas with those very formidable, choice weapons of theirs (tatas tair eva rakṣāṃsi durdharaiḥ pravarāyudhaiḥ bhittvā), he scattered them in flight as the wind scatters storm clouds (vidrāvayām āsa vāyur ambudharān iva).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततस्तैरेव रक्षांसि दुर्धरैः प्रवरायुधैः ।
भित्त्वा विद्रावयामास वायुरम्बुधरानिव ॥ ७१ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततस्तैरेव रक्षांसि दुर्धरैः प्रवरायुधैः ।
भित्त्वा विद्रावयामास वायुरम्बुधरानिव ॥ ७१ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘फिर उन्हीं दुर्धर एवं श्रेष्ठ आयुधोंसे उन सब राक्षसोंको घायल करके उसी तरह भगा दिया, जैसे हवा बादलोंको छिन्न-भिन्न करके उड़ा ले जाती है ॥ ७१ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मत्तॆ अदे दुर्धर श्रेष्ठ आयुधगळिन्द आ ऎल्ल राक्षसरन्नु गायगॊळिसि गाळियु मोडगळन्नु छिन्न-भिन्न माडि हारिसिबिडुवन्तॆ ओडिसिबिट्टनु.॥71॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having struck terror into the rākṣasas (rākṣasāṃs trāsayitvā tu), Kārtavīrya Arjuna then took Rāvaṇa captive (rāvaṇaṃ gṛhya) and entered his city surrounded by his kinsmen and friends (nagaraṃ praviveśa suhṛd-vṛtaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

राक्षसांस्त्रासयामास कार्तवीर्यार्जुनस्तदा ।
रावणं गृह्य नगरं प्रविवेश सुहृद‍्वृतः ॥ ७२ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

राक्षसांस्त्रासयामास कार्तवीर्यार्जुनस्तदा ।
रावणं गृह्य नगरं प्रविवेश सुहृद‍्वृतः ॥ ७२ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘उस समय कार्तवीर्य अर्जुनने समस्त राक्षसोंको भयभीत कर दिया और रावणको लेकर वह अपने सुहृदोंके साथ नगरमें आया ॥ ७२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग कार्तवीर्यार्जुननु समस्त राक्षसरन्नु भयगॊळिसि, रावणनन्नु करॆदुकॊण्डु अवनु तन्न सुहृदरॊन्दिगॆ नगरक्कॆ होदनु.॥72॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Showered with heaps of flowers and unbroken rice-grains (kīryamāṇaḥ kusumākṣatotkaraiḥ) by Brahmins and citizens alike (dvijaiḥ sa-pauraiḥ), Arjuna, peer of Indra (puruhūta-sannibhaḥ), entered that royal city (sampraviveśa tāṃ purīm) like the thousand-eyed Śakra entering heaven after capturing Bali (baliṃ nigṛhyeva sahasra-locanaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स कीर्यमाणः कुसुमाक्षतोत्करै-
र्द्विजैः सपौरैः पुरुहूतसन्निभः ।
ततोऽर्जुनः स्वां प्रविवेश तां पुरीं
बलिं निगृह्येव सहस्रलोचनः ॥ ७३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स कीर्यमाणः कुसुमाक्षतोत्करै-
र्द्विजैः सपौरैः पुरुहूतसन्निभः ।
ततोऽर्जुनः स्वां प्रविवेश तां पुरीं
बलिं निगृह्येव सहस्रलोचनः ॥ ७३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘नगरके निकट आनेपर ब्राह्मणों और पुरवासियोंने अपने इन्द्रतुल्य तेजस्वी नरेशपर फूलों और अक्षतोंकी वर्षा की और सहस्र नेत्रधारी इन्द्र जैसे बलिको बंदी बनाकर ले गये थे, उसी प्रकार उस राजा अर्जुनने बँधे हुए रावणको साथ लेकर अपनी पुरीमें प्रवेश किया’ ॥ ७३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

नगरक्कॆ बन्दाग ब्राह्मणरु, प्रजाजनरु तम्म इन्द्रतुल्य तेजस्वी राजन मेलॆ हूवू, अक्षतॆगळन्नु चॆल्लिदरु. सहस्राक्ष इन्द्रनु बलियन्नु बन्दियागिसिकॊण्डु होदन्तॆये राजा अर्जुननु बन्धिसिद रावणनन्नु जॊतॆगॆ तॆगॆदुकॊण्डु पुरियन्नु प्रवेशिसिदनु.॥73॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये उत्तरकाण्डे द्वात्रिंशः सर्गः ॥ ३२ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके उत्तरकाण्डमें बत्तीसवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ ३२ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद उत्तर काण्डदल्लि मूवत्तॆरडनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु. ॥32॥
