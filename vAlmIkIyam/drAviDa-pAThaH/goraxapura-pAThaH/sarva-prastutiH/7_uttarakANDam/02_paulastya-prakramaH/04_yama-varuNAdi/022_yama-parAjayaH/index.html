सर्वाष् टीकाः
भागसूचना
  1. यमराज और रावणका युद्ध, यमका रावणके वधके लिये उठाये हुए कालदण्डको ब्रह्माजीके कहनेसे लौटा लेना, विजयी रावणका यमलोकसे प्रस्थान
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Hearing Rāvaṇa’s roar, furious Yama senses his army’s rout and mounts his divine chariot flanked by Death, the incarnate Staff of Time, unbroken Time-nooses, and a blazing mace, making all three worlds tremble.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स तस्य तु महानादं श्रुत्वा वैवस्वतः प्रभुः ।
शत्रुं विजयिनं मेने स्वबलस्य च सङ्क्षयम् ॥ १ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स तस्य तु महानादं श्रुत्वा वैवस्वतः प्रभुः ।
शत्रुं विजयिनं मेने स्वबलस्य च सङ्क्षयम् ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

(अगस्त्यजी कहते हैं—रघुनन्दन!) रावणके उस महानादको सुनकर सूर्यपुत्र भगवान् यमने यह समझ लिया कि ‘शत्रु विजयी हुआ और मेरी सेना मारी गयी’ ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

(अगस्त्यरु हेळुत्तारॆ-रघुनन्दन !) रावणन आ महानादवन्नु केळि सूर्यपुत्र भगवान् यमनु शत्रु विजयियागिद्दानॆ हागू नन्न सैन्यवु नाशवायितॆन्दु तिळिदनु.॥1॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Hearing that mighty roar of his (tasya tu mahānādaṃ śrutvā), Lord Vaivasvata (vaivasvataḥ prabhuḥ) realized that the foe was victorious (śatruṃ vijayinaṃ mene) and that his own army had been destroyed (svabalasya ca saṅkṣayam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स हि योधान् हतान् मत्वा क्रोधसंरक्तलोचनः ।
अब्रवीत् त्वरितः सूतं रथो मे उपनीयताम् ॥ २ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

स हि योधान् हतान् मत्वा क्रोधसंरक्तलोचनः ।
अब्रवीत् त्वरितः सूतं रथो मे उपनीयताम् ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘मेरे योद्धा मारे गये’—यह जानकर यमराजके नेत्र क्रोधसे लाल हो गये और वे उतावले होकर सारथिसे बोले—‘मेरा रथ ले आओ’ ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

तन्न योधरु हतरादरॆन्दु तिळिदु यमन कण्णुगळु क्रोधदिन्द कॆम्पादवु. सारथिगॆ - ‘नन्न रथवन्नु तॆगॆदुकॊण्डु बा’ ऎन्दु आज्ञापिसिदनु.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Realizing that his warriors had been slain (yodhān hatān matvā), with eyes blood-red in wrath (krodha-saṃrakta-locanaḥ), he swiftly commanded his charioteer (abravīt tvaritaṃ sūtam): “Let this chariot be brought forth (ratho ’yam upanīyatām)!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तस्य सूतस्तदा दिव्यमुपस्थाप्य महारथम् ।
स्थितः स च महातेजा अध्यारोहत तं रथम् ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तस्य सूतस्तदा दिव्यमुपस्थाप्य महारथम् ।
स्थितः स च महातेजा अध्यारोहत तं रथम् ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

तब उनके सारथिने तत्काल एक दिव्य एवं विशाल रथ वहाँ उपस्थित कर दिया और वह सामने विनीतभावसे खड़ा हो गया । फिर वे महातेजस्वी यम देवता उस रथपर आरूढ़ हुए ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग सारथियु कूडले दिव्यविशालवाद रथवन्नु तन्दु निल्लिसि विनीत भावदिन्द ऎदुरिगॆ निन्तुकॊण्डनु. मत्तॆ महातेजस्वी यमदेवतॆ रथदल्लि आरूढननादनु.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then his charioteer brought up that great divine chariot (divyam upasthāpya mahāratham) and stood ready (sthitaḥ); that greatly resplendent lord mounted that chariot (sa ca mahātejā hy adhyārohata taṃ ratham), while Death, spear and mace in hand (prāśa-mudgara-hastaś ca mṛtyuḥ), stood before him (tasyāgrataḥ sthitaḥ)—

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

प्रासमुद‍्गरहस्तश्च मृत्युस्तस्याग्रतः स्थितः ।
येन सङ्क्षिप्यते सर्वं त्रैलोक्यमिदमव्ययम् ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

प्रासमुद‍्गरहस्तश्च मृत्युस्तस्याग्रतः स्थितः ।
येन सङ्क्षिप्यते सर्वं त्रैलोक्यमिदमव्ययम् ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उनके आगे प्रास और मुद‍्गर हाथमें लिये साक्षात् मृत्यु-देवता खड़े थे, जो प्रवाहरूपसे सदा बने रहनेवाले इस समस्त त्रिभुवनका संहार करते हैं ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अवन मुन्दुगडॆ प्रास, मुद्गरगळन्नु धरिसिद्द मृत्युदेवतॆ निन्तिद्दनु. अवनु प्रवाहरूपदिन्द सदा इरुव ई त्रिभुवनगळन्नु नाशमाडुववनागिद्दनु.॥4॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

he by whom this entire imperishable triple world is gathered in (yena saṅkṣipyate sarvaṃ trailokyam idam avyayam)—and at his side stood the incarnate Staff of Time (kāladaṇḍas tu pārśvastho mūrtimān asya cābhavat), Yama’s divine weapon blazing with fiery splendor (yama-praharaṇaṃ divyaṃ tejasā jvalad-agnimat).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

कालदण्डस्तु पार्श्वस्थो मूर्तिमानस्य चाभवत् ।
यमप्रहरणं दिव्यं तेजसा ज्वलदग्निवत् ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

कालदण्डस्तु पार्श्वस्थो मूर्तिमानस्य चाभवत् ।
यमप्रहरणं दिव्यं तेजसा ज्वलदग्निवत् ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उनके पार्श्वभागमें कालदण्ड मूर्तिमान् होकर खड़ा हुआ, जो उनका मुख्य एवं दिव्य आयुध है । वह अपने तेजसे अग्निके समान प्रज्वलित हो रहा था ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अवन पक्कदल्लि यमन मुख्य, दिव्य आयुधवाद कालदण्डवु मूर्तिमन्तवागि निन्तित्तु. अदु अग्नियन्तॆ प्रज्वलिसुत्तित्तु.॥5॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

At his flanks stood the unbroken Nooses of Time (tasya pārśveṣu nicchidrāḥ kālapāśāḥ pratiṣṭhitāḥ), and there stood the embodied Mace, burning like the touch of fire (pāvaka-sparśa-saṅkāśaḥ sthito mūrtaś ca mudgaraḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तस्य पार्श्वेषु निश्छिद्राः कालपाशाः प्रतिष्ठिताः ।
पावकस्पर्शसङ्काशः स्थितो मूर्तश्च मुद‍्गरः ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तस्य पार्श्वेषु निश्छिद्राः कालपाशाः प्रतिष्ठिताः ।
1 पावकस्पर्शसङ्काशः स्थितो मूर्तश्च मुद‍्गरः ॥ ६ ॥

1 तस्य पार्श्वेषु निच्छिद्राः कालपाशाः प्रतिष्ठिताः ।
1 पावकस्पर्श सङ्काशः स्थितो मूर्तश्च मुद्गरः ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उनके दोनों बगलमें छिद्ररहित कालपाश खड़े थे और जिसका स्पर्श अग्निके समान दुःसह है, वह मुद‍्गर भी मूर्तिमान् होकर उपस्थित था ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

पक्कदल्लि छिद्ररहित कालपाशवु निन्तिद्दु, अदर स्पर्श अग्नियन्तॆ दुःसहवागित्तु. अवन मुद्गरवू मूर्तिमन्तवागि उपस्थितवागित्तु.॥6॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thereupon the three worlds were thrown into turmoil (tato lokatrayaṃ kṣubdham) and the celestials trembled (akampanta divaukasaḥ), beholding Time thus enraged (kālaṃ dṛṣṭvā tathā kruddham), terrifying to all the worlds (sarva-loka-bhayāvaham).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Urging Yama’s radiant, mind-swift steeds into battle with a dreadful roar, the charioteer puts Rāvaṇa’s terrified ministers to headlong flight, yet Daśagrīva stands utterly unshaken as Yama closes in to pierce his vitals.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततो लोकत्रयं क्षुब्धमकम्पन्त दिवौकसः ।
कालं दृष्ट्वा तथा क्रुद्धं सर्वलोकभयावहम् ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततो लोकत्रयं क्षुब्धमकम्पन्त दिवौकसः ।
कालं दृष्ट्वा तथा क्रुद्धं सर्वलोकभयावहम् ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

समस्त लोकोंको भय देनेवाले साक्षात् कालको कुपित हुआ देख तीनों लोकोंमें हलचल मच गयी । समस्त देवता काँप उठे ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

समस्त लोकगळन्नु भय गॊळिसुव साक्षात् कालनु कुपितनागिरुवुदन्नु नोडि मूरु लोकगळल्लि गलिबिलियुण्टायितु. समस्त देवतॆगळु नडुगि होदरु.॥7॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Then the charioteer, urging on those steeds of radiant sheen (pracodayan sūtas tān aśvān rucira-prabhān), sped forth with a dreadful roar (prayayau bhīma-sannādaḥ) to where the lord of rākṣasas stood (yatra rakṣaḥpatiḥ sthitaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततस्त्वचोदयत् सूतस्तानश्वान् रुचिरप्रभान् ।
प्रययौ भीमसन्नादो यत्र रक्षःपतिः स्थितः ॥ ८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततस्त्वचोदयत् सूतस्तानश्वान् रुचिरप्रभान् ।
1 प्रययौ भीमसन्नादो यत्र रक्षःपतिः स्थितः ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

तदनन्तर सारथिने सुन्दर कान्तिवाले घोड़ोंको हाँका और वह रथ भयानक आवाज करता हुआ उस स्थानपर जा पहुँचा, जहाँ राक्षसराज रावण खड़ा था ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

बळिक सारथियु सुन्दर कान्तियुळ्ळ कुदुरॆगळन्नु नडॆसिदनु. आ रथवु भयानक शब्दमाडुत्ता रावणनु इद्दल्लिगॆ बन्दु निन्तितु.॥8॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

In a single moment (muhūrtena), those horses equal to Indra’s steeds (hayā hari-hayopamāḥ) and swift as thought (manasas tulyāḥ) bore Yama to where that battle had been joined (yatra tat prastutaṃ raṇam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

मुहूर्तेन यमं ते तु हया हरिहयोपमाः ।
प्रापयन् मनसस्तुल्या यत्र तत् प्रस्तुतं रणम् ॥ ९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 मुहूर्तेन यमं ते तु हया हरिहयोपमाः ।
1 प्रापयन् मनसस्तुल्या यत्र तत् प्रस्तुतं रणम् ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

इन्द्रके घोड़ोंके समान तेजस्वी और मनके समान शीघ्रगामी उन घोड़ोंने यमराजको क्षणभरमें उस स्थानपर पहुँचा दिया, जहाँ वह युद्ध चल रहा था ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

इन्द्रन कुदुरॆगळन्तिद्द, मनोवेगदन्तॆ शीघ्रगामि कुदुरॆगळु यमराजनन्नु क्षणार्धदल्लि युद्ध नडॆयुत्तिद्द रणरङ्गक्कॆ तन्दु बिट्टवु.॥9॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Beholding that monstrous chariot attended by Death (dṛṣṭvā tathaiva vikṛtaṃ rathaṃ mṛtyu-samanvitam), the ministers of the rākṣasa lord (sacivā rākṣasendrasya) suddenly fled in every direction (sahasā vipradudruvuḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

दृष्ट्वा तथैव विकृतं रथं मृत्युसमन्वितम् ।
सचिवा राक्षसेन्द्रस्य सहसा विप्रदुद्रुवुः ॥ १० ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

दृष्ट्वा तथैव विकृतं रथं मृत्युसमन्वितम् ।
सचिवा राक्षसेन्द्रस्य सहसा विप्रदुद्रुवुः ॥ १० ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

मृत्युदेवताके साथ उस विकराल रथको आया देख राक्षसराजके सचिव सहसा वहाँसे भाग खड़े हुए ॥ १० ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मृत्युदेवतॆ रथदल्लि बन्दिरुवुदन्नु नोडि राक्षसराजन सचिवरु अल्लिन्द ओडिहोदरु.॥10॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Of slight courage (laghu-sattvatayā), bereft of their wits and racked with terror (naṣṭa-sañjñā bhayārditāḥ), crying, “We are unable to fight here!” (neha yoddhuṃ samarthāḥ sma), they fled to the four quarters (prayayur diśaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

लघुसत्त्वतया ते हि नष्टसञ्ज्ञा भयार्दिताः ।
नेह योद्धुं समर्थाः स्म इत्युक्त्वा प्रययुर्दिशः ॥ ११ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

लघुसत्त्वतया ते हि नष्टसञ्ज्ञा भयार्दिताः ।
नेह योद्धुं समर्थाः स्म इत्युक्त्वा प्रययुर्दिशः ॥ ११ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उनकी शक्ति थोड़ी थी । इसलिये वे भयसे पीड़ित हो अपना होश-हवाश खो बैठे और ‘हम यहाँ युद्ध करनेमें समर्थ नहीं हैं’ ऐसा कहकर विभिन्न दिशाओंमें भाग गये ॥ ११ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अल्प शक्तियुळ्ळ अवरु भयगॊण्डु ‘नावु युद्ध माडलु समर्थरल्ल’ ऎन्दु अन्दुकॊण्डु ऎच्चर तप्पिदवरन्तॆ दिक्कापालागि ओडिहोदरु.॥11॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Yet even upon beholding such a world-terrifying chariot (taṃ tādṛśaṃ dṛṣṭvā rathaṃ loka-bhayāvaham), Daśagrīva neither trembled (nākṣubhyata daśagrīvaḥ) nor admitted any fear (na cāpi bhayam āviśat).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

7.22.1–3 (Vaivasvata Mobilizes and Death Takes His Post)

Connecting seamlessly to Sarga 21’s closing roar, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) notes that Yama instantly deduces both Rāvaṇa’s triumph and his own army’s ruin (शत्रुं विजयिनं स्वबलस्य संक्षयं च मेने). On 7.22.2, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) clarify the direct command उपनीयतामित्यब्रवीत्. As Yama ascends his celestial chariot, personified Death (Mṛtyu) takes his station at the prow—which Śivasahāya highlights as signifying Death’s subordinate status as Yama’s attendant (एतेन मृत्योर्यमानुचरत्वं सूचितम्).

7.22.4 (The Imperishable Yet Dissolving Triple World)

Describing Mṛtyu as he who dissolves the entire avyaya (‘imperishable’) triple world poses a philosophical puzzle. Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja resolve it identically: at the close of an aeon, Death gathers the entire cosmos back in (युगान्ते सर्वं संह्रियते), and the world is called अव्ययम् not as statically unchanging, but as ‘eternal in continuous cyclic stream’ (अव्ययं प्रवाहनित्यम्). Alongside Mṛtyu stands the incarnate Kāladaṇḍa, Yama’s fiery divine Staff (Śivasahāya).

7.22.5–6 (Time’s Embodied Arsenal and Cosmic Tremor)

Verse 7.22.5—bracketed in Parimal as anticipating 7.22.32—completes the terrifying iconography of Yama’s war-chariot: Govindarāja glosses निच्छिद्राः as ‘unbroken, gapless’ (निरन्तराः) of the Time-nooses arrayed at Yama’s flanks beside the incarnate fire-searing Mace (Mudgara). Śivasahāya traces the immediate cosmic reaction in 7.22.6: seeing wrathful Time (Kāla) arrayed for war, the three worlds are shaken and even the immortal gods quake in dread.

7.22.7–8 (The Twenty-Second Gāyatrī Syllable and Mind-Swift Steeds)

Marking a cherished traditional milestone in the 24,000-verse epic, Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) observes on 7.22.7 that the syllable चो in प्रचोदयन् marks the twenty-second syllable of the sacred Gāyatrī mantra (ततः प्रचोदयन्सूतस्तान् … इत्यत्र चो इति गायत्री). On 7.22.8, both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja gloss मनसस्तुल्याः as ‘equal to thought in velocity’ (मनःसमानवेगाः), and Rāma Varmā glosses प्रस्तुतम् as ‘already commenced’ (प्रारब्धम्): Yama’s Indra-like steeds bridge the distance in an instant.

7.22.9–10 (Flight of Rāvaṇa’s Ministers)

Confronted with Death’s dread chariot, Rāvaṇa’s counselors—so staunch against Yama’s foot-soldiers in Sarga 21—collapse in panic. On 7.22.9, Rāma Varmā glosses विकृतम् as ‘terrifying and hideous in its roar’ (घोरं विकृतसंनादं च), echoed by Govindarāja (घोरसंनादरूपविकारवन्तम्) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi). On 7.22.10, all three explain लघुसत्त्वतया as ‘because of their meager fortitude’ (अल्पबलतया, अल्पवीर्यतया), with Śivasahāya specifying their feebleness in comparison with Death (मृत्यूपेक्षयाल्पबलेन हेतुना).

7.22.11–12 (Daśagrīva’s Unshaken Fortitude and First Clash)

The counselors’ flight throws Rāvaṇa’s titanic courage into bold relief. Śivasahāya emphasizes that Daśagrīva neither flinched nor admitted terror (नाक्षुभ्यत भयं नाविशत्) even before the world-appalling chariot of Death. Seizing the initiative in 7.22.12, the enraged Yama closes within weapon range, unleashing a storm of javelins and darts that tear straight into Rāvaṇa’s vital organs (मर्माणि), launching the direct duel between Death and the Ten-Headed king.

7.22.13–14 (Cloud of Arrows and Yama’s Crushing Counter-Volley)

Undaunted by his wounds, Rāvaṇa regains poise (svasthaḥ) and showers Yama’s car with arrows like a monsoon cloud (Śivasahāya, Śiromaṇi). Yet Yama’s immediate counter-barrage briefly overwhelms him: reading महाशक्तिशतैः (‘hundreds of mighty spears’), Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) highlights the sheer physical toll on Rāvaṇa’s broad chest (mahorasi) as embedded shafts render him temporarily incapable of striking back (प्रतिकर्तुं नाशक्नोत्).

7.22.15–16 (Seven Nights of Unyielding Combat)

On the temporal accusative सप्तरात्रम् in 7.22.15, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) supplies युद्धं कृत्वा (‘having waged battle for seven nights’), while Śivasahāya traces how Yama’s relentless barrage left Rāvaṇa dazed (visañjñaḥ) and driven back (vimukhaḥ)—a notable divergence from Baroda CE’s ‘neither broken nor vanquished’. Linking 7.22.16 directly to the close of that week (सप्तरात्रातिकाले, Śivasahāya), the poet portrays both champions rebounding into an even fiercer struggle, neither willing to yield a step.

7.22.17–18 (Brahmā’s Assembly and the Specter of Saṃvarta)

As the clash escalates beyond mortal scale, heaven itself gathers as audience. On 7.22.17, Rāma Varmā glosses समेताः as ‘arrived’ (प्राप्ताः), and Śivasahāya supplies बभूवुः: gods, gandharvas, siddhas, and great sages descend with Prajāpati Brahmā at their head. On 7.22.18, Śivasahāya glosses संवर्तः as ‘the age of universal dissolution’ (प्रलयकालः)—dramatically foreshadowing why Brahmā himself has arrived to prevent cosmic catastrophe.

7.22.19–21 (Yama’s Delayed Wrath and the Fire from His Mouth)

Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) follows Rāvaṇa’s sudden escalation—filling the sky seamlessly with shafts and piercing Death, the charioteer, and Yama’s own vitals—until a roaring flame of fury erupts from Yama’s mouth. On 7.22.21, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) offers a penetrating theological insight: Yama’s sudden blaze of wrath proves that until this moment Lord Yama, mindful of Brahmā’s boon, had deliberately withheld lethal intent and confined himself to mere combat (एतावत्पर्यन्तं भगवान्यमो ब्रह्मवरदानं स्मृत्वा मारणबुद्धिं विहाय युद्धमात्रं कृतवानिति सूचितम्).

7.22.22–23 (Death and Time Rejoice; Death Demands Leave)

Seeing flames pour from Yama’s mouth (Śivasahāya), Death and Time surge with fierce anticipation. Rāma Varmā clarifies that they rejoiced specifically ‘for Rāvaṇa’s destruction’ (प्रहर्षितौ वधायेति शेषः) and glosses संरब्धौ as ‘exceedingly enraged’ (अतिक्रुद्धौ). On 7.22.23, Rāma Varmā glosses मुञ्च as ‘command, grant me permission’ (आज्ञापय) and unpacks मर्यादा निसर्गतः as Death’s innate, nature-born decree (स्वभावसिद्धा) that no foe he confronts shall live out the day.

7.22.24 (The Catalogue of Fallen Primordial Titans)

Śivasahāya explains the rhetorical transition into Death’s mythic roll-call: anticipating the objection of how so formidable a titan as Rāvaṇa can be felled, Death invokes the greatest asuras of ancient epochs—Hiraṇyakaśipu, Namuci, Śambara, Visandhi, Dhūmaketu, and Bali Vairocana—none of whom could evade his universal law. The catalogue steadily builds Death’s case before Yama himself asserts supreme authority.

7.22.25–27 (All Creation Perishes at Death’s Glance)

Completing Death’s sweeping catalogue—where Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) identifies Dambha/Dambhu simply as an ancient asura (कश्चिदसुरः)—Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construes 7.22.24–27a as a single sentence climaxing in the dissolution of earth, seas, mountains, and rivers at yugānta. On 7.22.27e, both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja gloss विनिपन्नाः as ‘brought to ruin’ (विनाशं प्राप्ताः) and sharpen दृष्टाः to ‘merely beheld’ (दृष्टमात्रा एव): beside cosmic dissolution, a single rākṣasa is nothing.

7.22.28–29 (Cosmic Ordinance, Not Personal Boast)

On 7.22.28b, Govindarāja cites Pāṇini’s rule (यावद्योगे भविष्यते लट्) explaining why present निहन्मि carries future force (हनिष्यामि, Rāma Varmā). On 7.22.29, both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja clarify बलं मम न खल्वेतत्: Death is not boasting of personal prowess (बलप्रकाशनमात्रपरं न खलु), but stating the beginningless cosmic boundary (अनादिसृष्टिसमयेनैषा मर्यादा) fixed by nature upon every mortal lifespan—a point Śivasahāya echoes.

7.22.30 (Yama Claims the Final Blow Himself)

Rather than unleashing his eager subordinate, Dharmarāja takes personal command of the decisive stroke. While Śivasahāya paraphrases our reading directly (त्वं तिष्ठ अहमेव निहन्मि), both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja comment on the variant अयमेनं निहन्म्यहम्, glossing the emphatic demonstrative as ‘right here, without delay, I slay him’ (अविलम्बेन हन्मीत्यर्थः)—setting up Yama’s fateful raising of the Kāladaṇḍa.

7.22.31–33 (Poising the Infallible Kāladaṇḍa)

The duel reaches its zenith as Vaivasvata raises the ultimate emblem of cosmic mortality. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construes verses 7.22.31–33 as a single grammatical unit (श्लोकत्रयमेकान्वयि), glossing तोलयामास as ‘lifted up and grasped’ (उत्थाप्य जग्राह): flanked by the Time-nooses and embodied lightning-mace, the Kāladaṇḍa strips away life by its mere glance—making the effect of its actual touch or descent unthinkable. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) likewise carries यस्य पार्श्वे smoothly into the weapon’s terrifying retinue.

7.22.34–35 (The Throbbing Weapon and Universal Panic)

On 7.22.34, Śivasahāya analyzes ज्वालापरिवारः as a bahuvrīhi (‘having flame itself as its retinue’, ज्वालैव परिवारो यस्य सः): at Yama’s mighty touch, the dread weapon pulsates (asphurat) as though already incinerating Rāvaṇa. In 7.22.35, Rāma Varmā glosses दण्डोद्यतम् as ‘with Staff raised aloft’ (उद्यतदण्डम्) and reads सर्वे universally as all living beings present on the field (सर्वे प्राणिनः), whereas Śivasahāya specifies the rākṣasa onlookers (सर्वे राक्षसाः) while even the celestials tremble.

7.22.36 (Brahmā’s Timely Epiphany)

Frozen at the exact split-second before impact (prahartu-kāme), the scene shifts from martial fury to cosmic jurisprudence. Grandfather Brahmā intervenes in person. Both Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya unpack the compressed causative यमं साक्षाद्दर्शयित्वा: Tilaka explains it as ‘becoming directly visible to Yama in his own divine form’ (स्वदिव्यमूर्त्या प्रत्यक्षो भूत्वा), while Śiromaṇi supplies आत्मानम् (‘having shown himself directly to Yama’), arresting the blow before an irreversible contradiction can occur.

7.22.37–38 (Brahmā’s Urgent Prohibition and the Boon)

Brahmā’s opening double negative न खलु … न हन्तव्यः captures the urgency of the moment: Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) explains it as emphatic repetition (‘must not be slain, must not be slain!’, न हन्तव्यो न हन्तव्य इत्यावृत्तिः), while Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) ingeniously cites the lexicon Medinī to read the first न as a vocative meaning ‘protector’. On 7.22.38, both Tilaka and Śiromaṇi identify वरः specifically as Rāvaṇa’s invulnerability to gods and divine beings (देवगणावध्यत्वरूपः), which Yama must uphold.

7.22.39–40 (Why Brahmā’s Word Upholds Cosmic Truth)

On 7.22.39, Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) glosses अनृतम् as ‘a speaker of falsehood’ (असत्यवादिनम्), and Rāma Varmā states the ontological ground of Brahmā’s warning: ‘because I am the sum and source of all three worlds’ (सर्वसमष्टित्वात् ममेति भावः), falsifying the Creator unravels truth throughout creation. On 7.22.40, both Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya anticipate a subtle objection—might the Staff spare Rāvaṇa out of deference to Brahmā’s boon? No: hurled in fury, it obliterates dear and hated alike (प्रियाप्रिये, a samāhāra-dvandva, Tilaka).

7.22.41 (The Primordial Design of the Kāladaṇḍa)

Brahmā grounds his warning in the Staff’s own origin, for he himself fashioned it (mayā sṛṣṭaḥ pūrvam). Śivasahāya glosses अमोघः as ‘never barren of fruit’ (मिथ्याफलको न), and Rāma Varmā supplies विनिपातने (‘for the felling of every creature created by Brahmā’, echoing Baroda CE). Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja observe that मृत्युपुरस्कृतः can be parsed either as a tatpuruṣa (‘honored/placed forward by Death’) or a bahuvrīhi (‘having Death marching in its vanguard’).

7.22.42–43 (The Metaphysical Dilemma—Ubhayato ’nṛtam)

Verse 7.22.43 crystallizes one of the finest logical knots in the Uttarakāṇḍa: a collision between two divine absolutes fashioned by Brahmā himself—the infallible Kāladaṇḍa and Rāvaṇa’s boon of invulnerability. All three commentators unpack उभयतोऽनृतम् (‘falsehood either way’) identically: Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) explains that if Rāvaṇa survives the Staff’s fall, the Staff’s infallibility becomes a lie; if he dies, Brahmā’s boon becomes a lie (दण्डपातेऽपि रावणजीवने दण्डस्यानृतत्वं रक्षोमरणे मदुक्तवरवचनस्यानृतत्वमित्यर्थः), echoed verbatim in sense by Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi).

7.22.44 (Appeal to Lokasaṅgraha)

Only one course preserves both cosmic institutions intact: withholding the blow. Brahmā seals his plea with an appeal to Yama’s highest vocation as guardian of universal order (लोकांस्त्वं यद्यवेक्षसे, ‘if you watch over the welfare of the worlds’): turn back the raised Staff from Laṅkā’s king and keep Brahmā’s word true. Śivasahāya highlights the clear conditional structure linking cosmic preservation directly to retracting the weapon.

7.22.45 (Dharmarāja’s Instant Obedience)

Without a trace of bruised pride or hesitation, Yama earns his epithet dharmātmā: ‘Here is the Staff withdrawn, for you are our sovereign lord!’ All three commentators gloss प्रभविष्णुः as ‘supreme master / sovereign’ (Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja: स्वामी; Śivasahāya paraphrases Yama’s humble deference). By submitting his invincible weapon to cosmic truth, Yama demonstrates that true divine power serves ṛta rather than ego.

7.22.46–47 (Yama’s Dignified Withdrawal)

Explaining why he leaves the arena altogether, Yama reasons that if this boon-protected adversary cannot be killed, lingering in battle serves no purpose (न किमपीत्यर्थः, Śivasahāya; both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja supply संचिन्त्य). On 7.22.47, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss दर्शनात् as ‘from the path of sight’ (दर्शनपथात्), with Govindarāja citing the Dhātupāṭha root √naś (णश अदर्शने) to render प्रणश्यामि as ‘I shall become invisible’ (अन्तर्हितो भविष्यामि), and both note that Yama spoke these parting words directly to Brahmā (ब्रह्माणमिति शेषः).

7.22.48 (Rāvaṇa’s Technical Victory by Grace)

Left alone on the battlefield, Daśagrīva observes the ancient kṣatriya custom of proclaiming his own name (आत्मनो नाम विश्राव्य, Śivasahāya) as conqueror before remounting Puṣpaka and exiting Yama’s domain. Lest anyone misread तं जित्वा (‘having conquered Yama’) as genuine superiority over Death, Rāma Varmā supplies a piercing theological qualification: ‘having conquered him strictly through the grace of the Lord’s boon’ (भगवत्कृपयेति शेषः). Rāvaṇa’s apparent triumph over Death itself thus ironically tightens the noose of the human-mortality loophole that awaits him in Rāma.

7.22.49 (Why Vaivasvata Ascends Joyful to Heaven)

The sarga’s final verse presents a poignant surprise: Yama departs to heaven with the Brahmā-led gods and the witnessing sage Nārada not in humiliation, but joyful (हृष्टः). Rāma Varmā resolves this paradox cleanly: ‘Yama and the gods rejoiced because Brahmā’s truthfulness had been faithfully preserved’ (ब्रह्मणः सत्यपरिपालनं संवृत्तमिति यमादीनां हर्षः). Śivasahāya and Govindarāja close the episode with Vaivasvata and Nārada ascending together to tridiva, their cosmic harmony unbroken even as Rāvaṇa marches onward toward Varuṇa.

एष तस्मात् प्रणश्यामि दर्शनादस्य रक्षसः ।
इत्युक्त्वा सरथः साश्वस्तत्रैवान्तरधीयत ॥ ४९ ॥

एष तस्मात् प्रणश्यामि दर्शनादस्य रक्षसः ।
इत्युत्त्वासरथः साश्वस्तत्रैवान्तरधीयत ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘इसलिये अब मैं इसकी दृष्टिसे ओझल होता हूँ, यों कहकर यमराज रथ और घोड़ोंसहित वहीं अन्तर्धान हो गये ॥ ४९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अदरिन्द नानु इवन कण्णिन्द मरॆयागुवॆनु. हीगॆ हेळि यमराजनु रथ - कुदुरॆगळ सहित अल्ले अन्तर्धाननादनु.॥49॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

And that Lord Vaivasvata (sa tu vaivasvataḥ), joyful (hṛṣṭaḥ), together with the gods led by Brahmā (devaiḥ saha brahma-purogamaiḥ), departed to heaven (jagāma tridivam), and with him went the great sage Nārada (nāradaś ca mahāmuniḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

दशग्रीवस्तु तं जित्वा नाम विश्राव्य चात्मनः ।
आरुह्य पुष्पकं भूयो निष्क्रान्तो यमसादनात् ॥ ५० ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

इस प्रकार यमराजको जीतकर अपने नामकी घोषणा करके दशग्रीव रावण पुष्पकविमानपर आरूढ़ हो यमलोकसे चला गया ॥ ५० ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

हीगॆ यमराजनन्नु गॆद्दु तन्न हॆसरन्नु घोषिसुत्ता रावणनु पुष्पकविमान हत्ति यमलोकदिन्द हॊरटु होदनु.॥50॥

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

स तु वैवस्वतो देवैः सह ब्रह्मपुरोगमैः ।
जगाम त्रिदिवं हृष्टो नारदश्च महामुनिः ॥ ५१ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

तदनन्तर सूर्यपुत्र यमराज तथा महामुनि नारदजी ब्रह्मा आदि देवताओंके साथ प्रसन्नतापूर्वक स्वर्गमें गये ॥ ५१ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

बळिक सूर्यपुत्र यमराज हागू महामुनि नारदरु, ब्रह्मादि देवतॆगळु सन्तोषदिन्द स्वर्गलोकक्कॆ तॆरळिदरु.॥51॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये उत्तरकाण्डे द्वाविंशः सर्गः ॥ २२ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके उत्तरकाण्डमें बाईसवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ २२ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद उत्तर काण्डदल्लि इप्पत्तॆरडनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु. ॥22॥
