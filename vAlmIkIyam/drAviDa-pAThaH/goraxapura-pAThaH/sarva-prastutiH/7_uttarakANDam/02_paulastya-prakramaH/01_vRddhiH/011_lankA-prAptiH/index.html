सर्वाष् टीकाः
भागसूचना
  1. रावणका संदेश सुनकर पिताकी आज्ञासे कुबेरका लङ्काको छोड़कर कैलासपर जाना, लङ्कामें रावणका राज्याभिषेक तथा राक्षसोंका निवास
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Learning that Rāvaṇa and his brothers have obtained boons from Brahmā, Sumālī casts off his dread of Viṣṇu, ascends from Rasātala with his ministers, embraces Daśagrīva, and rejoices that their long exile is over.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

सुमाली वरलब्धांस्तु ज्ञात्वा चैतान् निशाचरान् ।
उदतिष्ठद् भयं त्यक्त्वा सानुगः स रसातलात् ॥ १ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

सुमाली वरलब्धांस्तु ज्ञात्वा चैतान् निशाचरान् ।
उदतिष्ठद् भयं त्यक्त्वा सानुगः स रसातलात् ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

रावण आदि निशाचरोंको वर प्राप्त हुआ है, यह जानकर सुमाली नामक राक्षस अपने अनुचरोंसहित भय छोड़कर रसातलसे निकला ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रावणादि निशाचररु ब्रह्मनिन्द वर पडॆदु कॊण्डिरुवुदन्नु तिळिद सुमालि ऎम्ब राक्षसनु अनुचररॊन्दिगॆ भयतॊरॆदु रसातळदिन्द हॊरटु भूमण्डलक्कॆ बन्दनु.॥1॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Learning that those night-roamers had obtained their boons (varalabdhāṃs tu jñātvā), Sumālī cast aside all fear (bhayaṃ tyaktvā) and rose up from Rasātala together with his retinue (sānugaḥ sa rasātalāt).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

मारीचश्च प्रहस्तश्च विरूपाक्षो महोदरः ।
उदतिष्ठन् सुसंरब्धाः सचिवास्तस्य रक्षसः ॥ २ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

मारीचश्च प्रहस्तश्च विरूपाक्षो महोदरः ।
उदतिष्ठन् सुसंरब्धाः सचिवास्तस्य रक्षसः ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

साथ ही मारीच, प्रहस्त, विरूपाक्ष और महोदर—ये उस राक्षसके चार मन्त्री भी रसातलसे ऊपरको उठे । वे सब-के-सब रोषावेषसे भरे हुए थे ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अवनॊडनॆ मारीच, प्रहस्त, विरूपाक्ष, महोदर ऎम्ब नाल्वरु मन्त्रिगळू रोषावेशदिन्द तुम्बिद्दु रसातळदिन्द मेलक्कॆ बन्दरु.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Mārīca, Prahasta, Virūpākṣa, and Mahodara—the ministers of that rākṣasa (sacivās tasya rakṣasaḥ)—also rose up in eager excitement (udatiṣṭhan susaṃrabdhāḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

सुमाली सचिवैः सार्धं वृतो राक्षसपुङ्गवैः ।
अभिगम्य दशग्रीवं परिष्वज्येदमब्रवीत् ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

सुमाली सचिवैः सार्धं वृतो राक्षसपुङ्गवैः ।
अभिगम्य दशग्रीवं परिष्वज्येदमब्रवीत् ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

श्रेष्ठ राक्षसोंसे घिरा हुआ सुमाली अपने सचिवोंके साथ दशग्रीवके पास गया और उसे छातीसे लगाकर इस प्रकार बोला— ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

राक्षसश्रेष्ठरिन्द सुत्तुवरॆद सुमालियु दशग्रीवन बळिगॆ बन्दु अवनन्नु अप्पिकॊण्डु इन्तॆन्दनु.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Surrounded by his ministers and foremost rākṣasa champions (vṛto rākṣasa-puṅgavaiḥ), Sumālī approached Daśagrīva (abhigamya daśagrīvam), embraced him (pariṣvajya), and spoke these words:

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

दिष्ट्या ते वत्स सम्प्राप्तश्चिन्तितोऽयं मनोरथः ।
यस्त्वं त्रिभुवनश्रेष्ठाल्लब्धवान् वरमुत्तमम् ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

दिष्ट्या ते वत्स सम्प्राप्तश्चिन्तितोऽयं मनोरथः ।
यस्त्वं त्रिभुवनश्रेष्ठाल्लब्धवान् वरमुत्तमम् ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘वत्स! बड़े सौभाग्यकी बात है कि तुमने त्रिभुवनश्रेष्ठ ब्रह्माजीसे उत्तम वर प्राप्त किया, जिससे तुम्हें यह चिरकालसे चिन्तित मनोरथ उपलब्ध हो गया ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

वत्स! नीनु त्रिभुवनश्रेष्ठ ब्रह्मदेवरिन्द उत्तम वर पडॆदिरुवुदु सौभाग्यद मातागिदॆ. इदरिन्द चिरकालदिन्द इच्छिसिद मनोरथ प्राप्तवागिदॆ.॥4॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“By good fortune, my child (diṣṭyā te vatsa), this cherished desire has been fulfilled (samprāptaś cintito ’yaṃ manorathaḥ), since you have obtained a supreme boon (labdhavān varam uttamam) from the foremost of the three worlds (tribhuvana-śreṣṭhāt)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

यत्कृते च वयं लङ्कां त्यक्त्वा याता रसातलम् ।
तद‍्गतं नो महाबाहो महद्विष्णुकृतं भयम् ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

यत्कृते च वयं लङ्कां त्यक्त्वा याता रसातलम् ।
तद‍्गतं नो महाबाहो महद्विष्णुकृतं भयम् ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘महाबाहो! जिसके कारण हम सब राक्षस लङ्का छोड़कर रसातलमें चले गये थे, भगवान् विष्णुसे प्राप्त होनेवाला हमारा यह महान् भय दूर हो गया ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

महाबाहो! नावॆल्लरू विष्णुविन भयदिन्दागि रसातळक्कॆ होगिद्दॆवु. ईग आ नम्म भयवु दूरवायितु.॥5॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“That great terror wrought by Viṣṇu (mahad viṣṇu-kṛtaṃ bhayam), on account of which we abandoned Laṅkā and departed to Rasātala (laṅkāṃ tyaktvā yātā rasātalam), has now vanished from us, O mighty-armed one (tad gataṃ no mahābāho)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

असकृत् तद्भयाद् भग्नाः परित्यज्य स्वमालयम् ।
विद्रुताः सहिताः सर्वे प्रविष्टाः स्म रसातलम् ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

असकृत् तद्भयाद् भग्नाः परित्यज्य स्वमालयम् ।
विद्रुताः सहिताः सर्वे प्रविष्टाः स्म रसातलम् ॥ ६ ॥

असकृत्तद्भयाद् भग्नाः परित्यज्य स्वमालयम् ।
विद्रुताः सहिताः सर्वे प्रविष्ठाः स्म रसातलम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘हम सब लोग बारम्बार भगवान् विष्णुके भयसे पीड़ित होनेके कारण अपना घर छोड़ भाग निकले और सब-के-सब एक साथ ही रसातलमें प्रविष्ट हो गये ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

नावु हलवारु बारि अवनिन्द भग्नरादॆवु. इदरिन्दले अवन भयदिन्द नावु नम्म निवासस्थानवाद लङ्कॆयन्नु बिट्टु ऒट्टिगॆ रसातलवन्नु सेरिकॊण्डॆवु.॥6॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“More than once, terrified by dread of him (asakṛt tad-bhayād bhītāḥ), we abandoned our own abode (parityajya svam ālayam), fled in rout (vidrutāḥ), and all together entered Rasātala (praviṣṭāḥ sma rasātalam).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Sumālī urges Rāvaṇa to reclaim Laṅkā from Kubera by diplomacy, gifts, or force; when Rāvaṇa respectfully refuses to wrong his elder brother, Sumālī falls silent, until Prahasta later approaches him with a reasoned plea.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

अस्मदीया च लङ्केयं नगरी राक्षसोषिता ।
निवेशिता तव भ्रात्रा धनाध्यक्षेण धीमता ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

अस्मदीया च लङ्केयं नगरी राक्षसोषिता ।
निवेशिता तव भ्रात्रा धनाध्यक्षेण धीमता ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘यह लङ्कानगरी जिसमें तुम्हारे बुद्धिमान् भाई धनाध्यक्ष कुबेर निवास करते हैं, हमलोगोंकी है । पहले इसमें राक्षस ही रहा करते थे ॥ ७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

धीमन्तनाद निन्नण्ण धनाध्यक्षनु वासमाडुत्तिरुव लङ्कॆयु नम्मदे आगिदॆ. हिन्दॆ राक्षसरे अल्लि वास माडुत्तिद्दरु.॥7॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“This city of Laṅkā, which is ours and befitting the rākṣasas (asmadīyā … nagarī rākṣasocitā), has been occupied (niveśitā) by your wise brother, the Lord of Wealth (dhanādhyakṣeṇa dhīmatā).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

यदि नामात्र शक्यं स्यात् साम्ना दानेन वानघ ।
तरसा वा महाबाहो प्रत्यानेतुं कृतं भवेत् ॥ ८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

यदि नामात्र शक्यं स्यात् साम्ना दानेन वानघ ।
तरसा वा महाबाहो प्रत्यानेतुं कृतं भवेत् ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘निष्पाप महाबाहो! यदि साम, दान अथवा बलप्रयोगके द्वारा भी पुनः लङ्काको वापस लिया जा सके तो हमलोगोंका काम बन जाय ॥ ८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

महाबाहो! साम, दान अथवा बलप्रयोग दिन्दलादरू लङ्कॆयन्नु नीनु वशपडिसिकॊण्डरॆ नम्म कार्य साधिसिदन्तागुवुदु.॥8॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“O sinless, mighty-armed one (anagha … mahābāho), if indeed it were possible to bring it back (śakyaṃ syāt … pratyānetum) by conciliation, by gifts, or by sheer force (sāmnā dānena vā … tarasā vā), our purpose would be achieved (kṛtaṃ bhavet).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

त्वं च लङ्केश्वरस्तात भविष्यसि न संशयः ।
त्वया राक्षसवंशोऽयं निमग्नोऽपि समुद‍्धृतः ॥ ९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

त्वं च लङ्केश्वरस्तात भविष्यसि न संशयः ।
1 त्वया राक्षसवंशोऽयं निमग्नोऽपि समुद‍्धृतः ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘तात! तुम्हीं लङ्काके स्वामी होओगे, इसमें संशय नहीं है; क्योंकि तुमने इस राक्षसवंशका जो रसातलमें डूब गया था, उद्धार किया है ॥ ९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अय्या! नीने लङ्केश्वरनागुवुदरल्लि संशयवे इल्ल. रसातलदल्लि मुळुगिहोगिद्द राक्षस वंशवन्नु नीनीग मेलॆत्तिदन्तागुवुदु.॥9॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Without a doubt, my son (tāta … na saṃśayaḥ), you shall become the Lord of Laṅkā (laṅkeśvaraḥ); by you this rākṣasa lineage, though sunk in ruin, has been uplifted (nimagno ’pi samuddhṛtaḥ), and you shall become sovereign over us all (sarveṣāṃ naḥ prabhuś caiva), O mighty one!”

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

सर्वेषां नः प्रभुश्चैव भविष्यसि महाबल ।
अथाब्रवीद् दशग्रीवो मातामहमुपस्थितम् ॥ १० ॥
वित्तेशो गुरुरस्माकं नार्हसे वक्तुमीदृशम् ।

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

सर्वेषां नः प्रभुश्चैव भविष्यसि महाबल ।
अथाब्रवीद् दशग्रीवो मातामहमुपस्थितम् ॥ १० ॥
वित्तेशो गुरुरस्माकं नार्हसे वक्तुमीदृशम् ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘महाबली वीर! तुम्हीं हम सबके राजा होओगे ।’ यह सुनकर दशग्रीवने पास खड़े हुए अपने मातामहसे कहा—‘नानाजी! धनाध्यक्ष कुबेर हमारे बड़े भाई हैं, अतः उनके सम्बन्धमें आपको मुझसे ऐसी बात नहीं कहनी चाहिये’ ॥ १० १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

महाबलि वीरने! नीने नम्मॆल्लरिगॆ राजनागुवॆ. इदन्नु केळि दशग्रीवनु बळियल्लि निन्तिद्द मातामहनाद सुमालियल्लि हेळिदनु - अज्जा! कुबेरनु ननगॆ अण्णनागिद्दानॆ. अदरिन्द इन्तह मातन्नु नीवु हेळबारदु.॥10½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thereupon Daśagrīva spoke to his maternal grandfather standing before him (mātāmaham upasthitam): “The Lord of Wealth is our elder (vitteśo gurur asmākam); you ought not to speak in such a manner (nārhase vaktum īdṛśam).”

साम्ना हि राक्षसेन्द्रेण प्रत्याख्यातो गरीयसा ॥ ११ ॥
किञ्चिन्नाह तदा रक्षो ज्ञात्वा तस्य चिकीर्षितम् ।

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

साम्ना हि राक्षसेन्द्रेण प्रत्याख्यातो गरीयसा ॥ ११ ॥
किञ्चिन्नाह तदा रक्षो ज्ञात्वा तस्य चिकीर्षितम् ।

साम्ना हि राक्षसेन्द्रेण प्रत्याख्यातो गरीयसा ॥
किञ्चिन्नाह तदा रक्षो ज्ञात्वा तस्य चिकीर्षिताम् ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

7.11.1–2 (Ascent from Rasātala and Sumālī’s Ministers)

Introducing the eleventh sarga, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) frames its central theme as Rāvaṇa’s entry into Laṅkā after displacing Kubera (अथ धनदमपसार्य रावणस्य लङ्कापुरप्रवेशः). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) links the action directly to the close of Sarga 10: learning that Rāvaṇa and his brothers had secured Brahmā’s boons, Sumālī casts off his dread and rises from the netherworld (रावणादीन् वरलब्धान् ज्ञात्वा अत एव भयं त्यक्त्वा रसातलादुदतिष्ठत्). Both commentators specify that तस्य रक्षसः सचिवाः in 7.11.2 refers to Sumālī’s own veteran counselors—Mārīca, Prahasta, Virūpākṣa, and Mahodara—who will soon form the core of Rāvaṇa’s war council.

7.11.3–4 (The Maternal Grandfather’s Embrace and Fulfilled Design)

Approaching Daśagrīva with his retinue, Sumālī embraces him warmly before speaking. Śivasahāya glosses दिष्ट्या in 7.11.4 as ‘by good fortune’ (भाग्यवशेन), noting the causal link between the two halves of the verse: precisely because Rāvaṇa gained an unsurpassed boon from Brahmā (tribhuvana-śreṣṭha), the long-meditated design (चिन्तितो मनोरथः) has reached fruition (संप्राप्तः). The phrase recalls Sumālī’s calculated scheme in Sarga 9—sending his daughter Kaikasī to Viśravas specifically to sire a champion capable of restoring the shattered rākṣasa hegemony.

7.11.5–6 (Dispelling the Ancient Terror of Viṣṇu)

Sumālī immediately turns from personal felicitation to collective memory. On 7.11.5, Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) glosses तद्गतम् as signifying that the immense fear inspired by Viṣṇu has now been dispelled (महद्विष्णुकृतं यद्भयं तन्निरस्तमित्यर्थः). On 7.11.6, Śivasahāya—reading भग्नाः (‘shattered’) where Govindarāja reads भीताः (‘terrified’)—explains विद्रुताः as ‘fled in rout’ (पलायिताः), evoking the traumatic defeat of Mālyavān, Sumālī, and Mālin recounted in Sargas 7–8. By vividly rehearsing their humiliation and loss of home, Sumālī prepares the emotional ground for his demand to reclaim Laṅkā.

7.11.7–9 (Sumālī’s Political Counsel and Promise of Kingship)

Pressing his case, Sumālī frames Laṅkā as ancestral rākṣasa property now held (निवेशिता, glossed by Rāma Varmā as अधिष्ठिता) by Kubera. On 7.11.8, both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) supply कार्यम् with कृतं भवेत् (‘our goal would be fulfilled if Laṅkā can be recovered by conciliation and other expedients’), while Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) glosses तरसा as martial prowess (शौर्येण). Anticipating Rāvaṇa’s query regarding personal gain (न च तेन मम किं सेत्स्यतीत्यत आह), Śivasahāya notes how 7.11.9 crowns the appeal: Rāvaṇa himself will become Lord of Laṅkā and supreme ruler of the rescued rākṣasa race.

7.11.10–11 (Rāvaṇa’s Filial Scruple and Sumālī’s Strategic Silence)

Remarkably, Rāvaṇa initially recoils from fraternal aggression, invoking the dharma of seniority: Kubera is his elder (guru). Rāma Varmā clarifies नार्हसे as ‘you ought not to advise ousting an elder brother’ (ज्येष्ठनिरसनं वक्तुं नार्हसीत्यर्थः). On 7.11.11, Śivasahāya observes that once gently rebuffed by the formidable Rāvaṇa (रावणेन साम्ना प्रत्याख्यातः), the shrewd Sumālī perceived Rāvaṇa’s present resolve (चिकीर्षितं ज्ञात्वा) and prudently held his tongue rather than provoking stubborn resistance.

7.11.12 (Prahasta’s Timed Intervention)

Waiting until the immediate tension subsides (कस्यचित्कालस्यापगमे सति, Śivasahāya), Sumālī’s eloquent minister Prahasta takes up the persuasion. Where Sumālī appealed to tribal grievance and pride, Prahasta approaches Daśagrīva deferentially (praśritam) and with deliberate argumentation (sa-kāraṇam, Govindarāja’s reading noted in Parimal). This shift in spokesman reveals Vālmīki’s acute political psychology: Rāvaṇa resists naked familial incitement, yet will soon succumb to an ideological justification dressed in heroic precedent.

7.11.13 (Realpolitik Against Brotherhood)

Reversing Rāvaṇa’s own rebuke (नार्हसे वक्तुमीदृशम्, 7.11.10), Prahasta bluntly tells him that it is he who should not speak thus to Sumālī (सुमालिनं प्रतीति शेषः, Śivasahāya), advancing a stark maxim of imperial ambition: ‘among warriors there is no brotherhood’ (सौभ्रात्रं नास्ति शूराणाम्). Prahasta exploits Rāvaṇa’s warrior pride—suggesting that scruples over kinship belong to weaklings rather than conquerors—while cleverly preparing to cloak ruthless realpolitik under the prestige of cosmic precedent.

7.11.14–16 (The Precedent of Aditi and Diti’s Sons)

Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) identifies Prahasta’s rhetorical strategy as invoking the authority of ‘exemplary conduct’ (तत्र शिष्टाचारं प्रमाणयति). Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss सहिते in 7.11.14 as हिते परस्परानुरक्ते (‘mutually affectionate’), with Tilaka adding that Aditi and Diti were half-sisters (भिन्नोदरभगिन्यौ) married to the same father Kaśyapa—a pointed parallel to Kaikasī and Ilavilā (Kubera’s mother), both wives of Viśravas. On 7.11.16, Śivasahāya reads पुरेयम् (पुरा इयम्) to show that the entire earth once belonged to the Daityas, who ruled as mighty sovereigns (प्रभविष्णवः प्रभाववन्तः).

7.11.17–18 (Normalizing Fraternal Usurpation)

Concluding his syllogism, Prahasta points out that even though the Devas and Daityas were paternal half-brothers, Viṣṇu slew the Daityas in battle to transfer the three worlds to the gods (Rāma Varmā, Śivasahāya). Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja explicitly gloss विपर्ययम् in 7.11.18 as ‘treachery against a brother’ (भ्रातृद्रोहरूपम्), with Govindarāja supplying मार्गम् (‘a path trodden by gods and asuras’). Śivasahāya sharpens the point: appropriating another’s city (परस्वामिकपुरादिस्वाधीनीकरणम्) is no novel sin of Rāvaṇa’s alone, for both cosmic factions have acted thus—a sophistry that swiftly overcomes Rāvaṇa’s conscience.

7.11.21–22 (The Embassy and Why Kubera Is Called Nairṛta-Puṅgava)

Encamped menacingly on Trikūṭa itself, Rāvaṇa nevertheless opens with the first political expedient (sāman, conciliation), sending the eloquent Prahasta as ambassador (dautyena) to deliver his message (मम वचसा मत्संदेशरूपेण, Śivasahāya). Why does Rāvaṇa address Kubera—a Yakṣa and Lord of the North—as नैर्ऋतपुङ्गवम् (‘bull among the Nairṛtas/rākṣasas’)? Govindarāja explains that the epithet reflects Kubera’s lordship over the rākṣasa-associated region while residing in Laṅkā (लङ्कायां पूर्वस्थितनैर्ऋताधिपत्यात्), a diplomatic compliment subtly reinforcing Rāvaṇa’s claim to the city’s rākṣasa identity.

7.11.23–24 (Polite Ultimatum Framed as Dharma)

Rāvaṇa’s dictated message blends honeyed honorifics (rājan, saumya, anagha, atula-vikrama) with a pointed moral reproach: occupying (निवेशिता अधिष्ठिता, Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya) a city belonging to the noble rākṣasas is unbecoming of Kubera. Śivasahāya unpacks the logical connector तत् in 7.11.24 as ‘because the city belongs to the rākṣasas’ (राक्षसीयत्वात् हेतोः), so that voluntarily ceding it today will both gratify Rāvaṇa and uphold dharma. Thus Rāvaṇa masks coercion as righteous restitution, leaving the threat of Trikūṭa’s assembled host unspoken.

7.11.25–27 (Prahasta’s Diplomatic Courtesy)

Entering Laṅkā—still well-fortified by Dhanada (धनदेन सुरक्षिताम्)—Prahasta adopts a tone of extravagant deference toward the ‘supremely generous’ (paramodāra) Kubera, hailing him as steadfast in vows, foremost of warriors, and learned in all scriptures. On 7.11.26–27, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construes the verses as a single sentence (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी) and notes that the present tense ब्रवीति carries past force (‘the message Daśānana spoke’, यद्दशाननोऽब्रवीत्). Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) clarifies वचनं मम by supplying मुखात् (‘hear from my mouth the words that Daśānana utters’).

7.11.28–29 (Two Readings of Tena in the Formal Petition)

Delivering the substance of the claim, Prahasta discreetly omits Rāvaṇa’s blunt accusation (‘this is not fitting for you’, 7.11.23) and instead stresses the rākṣasas’ prior possession under Sumālī (bhukta-pūrvā). The commentators diverge interestingly on तेन in 7.11.29a: Rāma Varmā takes it causally as तेन हेतुना (‘for the reason that it was previously enjoyed by rākṣasas, let it now be given—this very petition is submitted’), whereas Śivasahāya takes तेन as the agent रावणेन (‘by Rāvaṇa this matter—namely Laṅkā—is respectfully requested’, glossing विज्ञाप्यते as याच्यते). Both construals underscore the calculated meekness of याचतः सामतः (‘begging peacefully’).

7.11.30 (Kubera’s Measured Composure)

The Southern Recension’s five-verse embassy (7.11.25–29) gives dramatic texture to Prahasta’s skill as vākya-kovida, setting up Kubera’s serene dignity as vākya-viśārada (‘expert in speech’). Unruffled by the veiled menace behind his younger half-brother’s petition, the divine Vaiśravaṇa listens attentively (अभिसंश्रुत्य, Śivasahāya) and prepares a response rooted not in territorial possessiveness, but in fraternal generosity and filial obedience.

7.11.31–32 (Kubera’s Quiet Correction and Magnanimous Offer)

Kubera gently refutes any charge of usurpation: as Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explains, Laṅkā stood utterly abandoned by the rākṣasas (निशाचरैः शून्या) when Viśravas granted it to him, and he populated it righteously through generosity and honor (दानमानादिभिर्यथायोग्यं संस्थापिता). Yet rather than clinging to legal title, Kubera rises above strife and offers full co-sovereignty (अकण्टकं यथा भवति तथा भुङ्क्ष्व), embodying the fraternal harmony that Prahasta had cynically dismissed.

7.11.33–34 (Seeking the Father’s Verdict)

Though he has already spoken generously to Prahasta, Kubera refuses to finalize so grave a transfer without his father’s sanction, since Viśravas was the original donor of Laṅkā. Śivasahāya construes the six-quarter verse 7.11.33 with 7.11.34 as a single narrative arc: bowing reverently before his father, Kubera summarizes Daśagrīva’s embassy and its blunt demand, humbly asking मयात्र यदनुष्ठेयं तन्ममाचक्ष्व—‘tell me what course of duty I ought to follow here.’

7.11.35–36 (Viśravas’s Prior Rebuke and the Sandhi Bahuśoktaḥ)

Receiving his dutiful son who stands with joined palms (prāñjali), the brahmarṣi Viśravas reveals that Rāvaṇa had already broached this covetous ambition before him and suffered repeated reprimands (nirbhartsitaḥ). All three commentators single out the irregular sandhi बहुशोक्तः (बहुशः + उक्तः) in 7.11.36d: Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) labels it epic (संधिरार्षः), Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) calls it Vedic (संधिश्छान्दसः), and Śivasahāya justifies it by citing the occasional suspension of asiddhatva in Pāṇini’s Tripādī (त्रिपाद्याः क्वचिदसिद्धत्वाभावबोधनात्).

7.11.37–38 (Rāvaṇa’s Moral Ruin and Viśravas’s Natal Curse)

All three commentators interpret ध्वंसते / ध्वंससे in 7.11.37 morally rather than merely as physical ruin: Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss it as ‘becoming one whose bounds of dharma and justice are shattered’ (ध्वस्तधर्मन्यायमर्यादो जायते), while Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) supplies मर्यादाम् (‘shattering propriety’). On मम शापात् in 7.11.38, all three concur that Viśravas refers to his fateful pronouncement when Kaikasī approached him at the dreadful twilight hour in Sarga 9 (तन्मातृदर्शनकालकृतात्); Śivasahāya adds that Rāvaṇa’s inability to distinguish the venerable from the base shows he rejected his father’s rebuke (मदुक्तं न स्वीकृतमिति सूचितम्).

7.11.39 (The Counsel to Relocate to Kailāsa)

Because Rāvaṇa’s boon-intoxicated ferocity makes peaceful coexistence impossible, Viśravas advises a strategic and dharmic withdrawal (śreyo-’bhiyuktaṃ dharmyaṃ ca): leave Laṅkā behind and establish a new seat on Mount Kailāsa. Glossing the elliptical causative imperative निवेशय, Rāma Varmā supplies पुरम् (‘found a city’), Govindarāja glosses निवेशनं पुरं कुरु, and Śivasahāya explains स्वगृहान् संस्थापय (‘establish your own abodes’).

7.11.40–41 (The Celestial Loveliness of Kailāsa and the Mandākinī)

To demonstrate that Kailāsa is a far nobler home than Laṅkā (तत्स्थानस्य वासयोग्यत्वं बोधयन्नाह, Śivasahāya), Viśravas paints a radiant Himalayan idyll. Rāma Varmā glosses सूर्यसङ्काशैः as ‘blazing bright as the sun’ (सूर्यवदुज्ज्वलैः), while Śivasahāya construes the six-quarter verse 7.11.40 as a unified description of the Mandākinī carpeted with golden lotuses, night-lilies, and fragrant blooms, and notes on 7.11.41 that gods, Gandharvas, Apsarasas, Uragas, and Kiṃnaras make Kailāsa their perpetual playground (सर्वदा आश्रिताः सन्तः सततं रमन्ते).

7.11.42 (Prudent Restraint Before Boon-Born Power)

Sealing his counsel with sober realism, Viśravas reminds Kubera of the unmatched boon Rāvaṇa has just wrung from Brahmā. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) glosses क्षमम् as योग्यम् (‘fitting / advisable’): knowing what immunity Rāvaṇa enjoys (अनेन रावणेन यथा वरो लब्धः तथा त्वं जानीषे), provoking conflict with him would be neither prudent nor fruitful. Viśravas thus harmonizes spiritual virtue with political foresight, sparing his elder son a needless fratricidal war.

7.11.43 (Filial Reverence and the Peaceful Exodus)

Without a syllable of complaint or bitterness, Kubera immediately embraces his father’s command out of filial reverence (pitṛ-gauravāt) and departs for Kailāsa (कैलासमिति शेषः, Śivasahāya) with his household, counselors, retinue, and treasury intact. His unhesitating renunciation of golden Laṅkā at a father’s word quietly foreshadows Rāma’s own iconic obedience to Daśaratha in the Ayodhyākāṇḍa, setting Kubera’s serene self-mastery in sharp contrast to Rāvaṇa’s grasping pride.

7.11.44–45 (Prahasta’s Exultant Report)

Returning to Daśagrīva, who awaits amid his brothers and ministers, Prahasta joyfully announces the bloodless triumph. Śivasahāya outlines the crisp causal structure of 7.11.45: Laṅkā is empty because Dhanada has vacated it (लङ्का शून्या तत्र हेतुः धनदः एनां लङ्कां त्यक्त्वा गतः); therefore Rāvaṇa should enter with his kinsmen and govern according to his royal duty (svadharma). Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) adds that upon hearing Prahasta’s tidings, Rāvaṇa too became elated at heart (प्रहस्तवचः श्रुत्वा रावणः प्रहृष्टात्माभवदिति शेषः), poised to step into the vacant throne.

7.11.46–47 (The Foe of the Gods Ascends Like Indra)

Entering Laṅkā with his brothers and host, Rāvaṇa claims the abandoned fortress. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) observes that 7.11.47 restates the same entry in heightened poetic imagery (तदेव भङ्ग्यन्तरेणाह): ascending Laṅkā with its wide, symmetrical avenues (सुविभक्ता महापथा यस्याम्), the ‘foe of the gods’ (devāri) mirrors the lord of the gods (devādhipa) ascending heaven. The pointed antithesis between devāri and devādhipa signals Rāvaṇa’s audacious rivalry with Indra, which will dominate the coming sargas.

7.11.48 (Vaṃśastha—Rāvaṇa’s Consecration and Cloud-Dark Host)

Shifting into stately twelve-syllable Vaṃśastha, Vālmīki marks a historical turning point: consecrated king by the rākṣasas (क्षणदाचरैरभिषिक्तः, Śivasahāya), Daśānana repeoples Laṅkā until it teems completely (निकामपूर्णामत्यन्तपूर्णाम्, Rāma Varmā). The simile comparing the swarming rākṣasas to dark-blue storm clouds (nīla-balāhakopamaiḥ) lends an ominous majesty to the restored demon capital, contrasting visually with the luminous whiteness of Kailāsa in the final stanza.

7.11.49 (The Twin Capitals—Moon-Bright Alakā and Amarāvatī)

Closing the sarga in ornate lyric cadence, the poet balances Rāvaṇa’s storm-dark Laṅkā with Kubera’s radiant new capital (Alakā) on ‘the moon-pure mountain’—glossed explicitly as Kailāsa (कैलासे) by both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa). Śivasahāya, reading पुरन्दरः स्वरिव यथामरावतीम्, construes the simile as Kubera founding his mountain city just as Indra established Amarāvatī in heaven, while Rāma Varmā specifically remarks on the metrical implications (छन्दोभङ्गाभावाय) of the variant पुरन्दरस्येव तदामरावतीम्. Thus the sarga ends in serene symmetry: ambition claims Laṅkā, while filial dharma builds a celestial city.

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

प्रहस्तके ऐसा कहनेपर महाबली दशग्रीवने अपनी सेना, अनुचर तथा भाइयोंसहित कुबेरद्वारा त्यागी हुई लङ्कापुरीमें प्रवेश किया । उस नगरीमें सुन्दर विभागपूर्वक बड़ी-बड़ी सड़कें बनी थीं । जैसे देवराज इन्द्र स्वर्गके सिंहासनपर आरूढ़ हुए थे, उसी प्रकार देवद्रोही रावणने लङ्कामें पदार्पण किया ॥ ४९-५० ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

प्रहस्तनु हीगॆ हेळिदाग महाबलि दशग्रीवनु तन्न सैन्य, अनुचर हागू सहोदररॊन्दिगॆ कुबेरनु त्यजिसिद लङ्कॆयन्नु प्रवेशिसिदनु. आ नगरदल्लि विभागपूर्वक सुन्दर दॊड्ड-दॊड्ड राजबीदिगळिद्दवु. स्वर्गद सिंहासनदल्लि देवेन्द्रनु कुळितिरुवन्तॆये देवद्रोहि रावणनु लङ्कॆयन्नु प्रवेशिसिदनु.॥49-50॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Meanwhile, out of reverence for his father’s word (pitṛ-vākya-gauravāt), the Lord of Wealth founded upon the moon-bright mountain (nyaveśayac chaśi-vimale girau) a city adorned with richly embellished palaces (svalaṅkṛtair bhavana-varair vibhūṣitām), like Amarāvatī of Purandara in that hour (purandarasyevāmarāvatīm).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

निवेशयामास पुरीं दशाननः ।
निकामपूर्णा च बभूव सा पुरी
निशाचरैर्नीलबलाहकोपमैः ॥ ५१ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस समय निशाचरोंने दशमुख रावणका राज्याभिषेक किया । फिर रावणने उस पुरीको बसाया । देखते-देखते समूची लङ्कापुरी नील मेघके समान वर्णवाले राक्षसोंसे पूर्णतः भर गयी ॥ ५१ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग निशाचररु दशमुख रावणनिगॆ राज्याभिषेक माडिदरु. नोडु, नोडुत्तिरुवन्तॆ इडी लङ्कॆय नीलमेघदन्तॆ राक्षसरिन्द तुम्बिहोयितु.॥51॥

धनेश्वरस्त्वथ पितृवाक्यगौरवा-

पुरन्दरस्येव तदाऽमरावतीम् ॥ ५२ ॥

पुरन्दरः स्वरिव यथामरावतीम् ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

धनके स्वामी कुबेरने पिताकी आज्ञाको आदर देकर चन्द्रमाके समान निर्मल कान्तिवाले कैलास पर्वतपर शोभाशाली श्रेष्ठ भवनोंसे विभूषित अलकापुरी बसायी, ठीक वैसे ही जैसे देवराज इन्द्रने स्वर्गलोकमें अमरावती पुरी बसायी थी ॥ ५२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

धनेश्वर कुबेरनु तन्दॆय आज्ञॆयन्नु मन्निसि चन्द्रदन्तॆ निर्मल कान्तियुळ्ळ कैलास पर्वतदल्लि शोभामय श्रेष्ठभवनगळिन्द विभूषित अलकापुरियन्नु नॆलॆगॊळिसि, देवराज इन्द्रन अमरावतियन्तॆये आ पुरि इत्तु.॥52॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये उत्तरकाण्डे एकादशः सर्गः ॥ ११ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके उत्तरकाण्डमें ग्यारहवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ ११ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद उत्तरकाण्डदल्लि हन्नॊन्दनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु. ॥11॥
