भागसूचना

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Consecrated in Laṅkā, Rāvaṇa marries Śūrpaṇakhā to the Kālakeya Vidyujjihva; while hunting in a lonely forest he meets the Dānava Maya with a fawn-eyed maiden and hears how the gods bestowed the apsaras Hemā upon him.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

राक्षसेन्द्रोऽभिषिक्तस्तु भ्रातृभिः सहितस्तदा ।
ततः प्रदानं राक्षस्या भगिन्याः समचिन्तयत् ॥ १ ॥

मूलम्

ततः प्रदानं राक्षस्या भगिन्याः समचिन्तयत् ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

(अगस्त्यजी कहते हैं—श्रीराम!) अपना अभिषेक हो जानेपर जब राक्षसराज रावण भाइयोंसहित लङ्कापुरीमें रहने लगा, तब उसे अपनी बहिन राक्षसी शूर्पणखाके ब्याहकी चिन्ता हुई ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

(अगस्त्यरु हेळुत्तारॆ - श्रीरामा !) राक्षसेश्वर रावणनु पट्टाभिषेकवाद बळिक लङ्कापुरियल्लि इरतॊडगिदनु. आग सहोदरि शूर्पणखिय विवाहद चिन्तॆ उण्टायितु.॥1॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Consecrated as king (abhiṣiktas tu) along with his two brothers (bhrātṛbhyāṃ sahitas tadā), the lord of rākṣasas (rākṣasendraḥ) next pondered the marriage (pradānaṃ … samacintayat) of his rākṣasa sister (rākṣasyā bhaginyāḥ).

स्वसारं कालकेयाय दानवेन्द्राय राक्षसीम् ।
ददौ शूर्पणखां नाम विद्युज्जिह्वाय राक्षसः ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

उस राक्षसने दानवराज विद्युज्जिह्वको, जो कालकाका पुत्र था, अपनी बहिन शूर्पणखा ब्याह दी ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आ रावणनु कालकॆय पुत्र दानवराज विज्युज्दिह्वनिगॆ तन्न तङ्गिय मदुवॆ माडिकॊट्टनु.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

He gave his rākṣasa sister (svasāraṃ … rākṣasīm), Śūrpaṇakhā by name (śūrpaṇakhāṃ nāma), to the Kālakeya lord of Dānavas (kālakeyāya dānavendrāya) known by the name Vidyujjihva (vidyujjihvāya nāmataḥ).

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

श्रीराम! बहिनका ब्याह करके राक्षस रावण एक दिन स्वयं शिकार खेलनेके लिये वनमें घूम रहा था । वहाँ उसने दितिके पुत्र मयको देखा । उसके साथ एक सुन्दरी कन्या भी थी । उसे देखकर निशाचर दशग्रीवने पूछा—‘आप कौन हैं, जो मनुष्यों और पशुओंसे रहित इस सूने वनमें अकेले घूम रहे हैं? इस मृगनयनी कन्याके साथ आप यहाँ किस उद्देश्यसे निवास करते हैं?’ ॥ ३-४ १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

श्रीरामा! तङ्गिय मदुवॆ माडि रावणनु ऒन्दु दिन बेटॆगागि काडिनल्लि तिरुगाडुत्तिद्दनु. अल्लि अवनु दितिपुत्र मयनन्नु नोडिदनु. अव नॊन्दिगॆ ओर्व सुन्दरि कन्यॆयू इद्दळु. अवनन्नु नोडि दशग्रीवनु केळिदनु - नीवु यारु? निर्जन वनवाद ई शून्यवनदल्लि अलॆयुत्तिरुवॆयल्ल! ई मृगनयनी कन्यॆय जॊतॆगॆ नीवु इल्लि याव उद्देशदिन्द वासिसुत्तिरुवॆ.॥3-4½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

3: Then, having himself bestowed her (dattvā svayam), that rākṣasa wandered about hunting (mṛgayām aṭate sma tat); and there, O Rāma, he beheld a son of Diti named Maya (mayaṃ nāma diteḥ sutam).

4: Seeing him accompanied by a maiden (kanyā-sahāyaṃ taṃ dṛṣṭvā), the night-roamer Daśagrīva (daśagrīvo niśācaraḥ) asked: “Who are you, standing alone (ko bhavān ekaḥ) in this forest empty of men and beasts (nirmanuṣya-mṛge vane)? And for what purpose do you abide here with this fawn-eyed girl (anayā mṛgaśāvākṣyā kimarthaṃ saha tiṣṭhasi)?”

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

श्रीराम! इस प्रकार पूछनेवाले उस निशाचरसे मय बोला—‘सुनो, मैं अपना सारा वृत्तान्त तुम्हें यथार्थरूपसे बता रहा हूँ ॥ ५ १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

श्रीरामा! हीगॆ केळिद आ निशाचरनल्लि मयनु हेळुत्तानॆ - नानु नन्न ऎल्ल वृत्तान्तवन्नु यथार्थवागि तिळिसुत्तेनॆ, केळु.॥5½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Maya thereupon spoke thus, O Rāma (mayas tathābravīd rāma), to that questioning night-roamer: “Listen (śrūyatām), I shall recount in full (sarvam ākhyāsye) all that has happened to me exactly as it befell (yathāvṛttam idaṃ mama).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Maya relates how Hemā returned to heaven after centuries together, leaving him lonely in his magic golden city, and how—fearing a daughter’s risk to two lineages—he brought her forth to seek a husband, naming also his sons Māyāvī and Dundubhi.

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘तात! तुमने पहले कभी सुना होगा, स्वर्गमें हेमा नामसे प्रसिद्ध एक अप्सरा रहती है । उसे देवताओंने उसी प्रकार मुझे अर्पित कर दिया था, जैसे पुलोम दानवकी कन्या शची देवराज इन्द्रको दी गयी थीं । मैं उसीमें आसक्त होकर एक सहस्र वर्षोंतक उसके साथ रहा हूँ । एक दिन वह देवताओंके कार्यसे स्वर्गलोकको चली गयी, तबसे चौदह वर्ष बीत गये । मैंने उस हेमाके लिये मायासे एक नगरका निर्माण किया था, जो सम्पूर्णतः सोनेका बना है । हीरे और नीलमके संयोगसे वह विचित्र शोभा धारण करता है । उसीमें मैं अबतक उसके वियोगसे अत्यन्त दुःखी एवं दीन होकर रहता था ॥ ६—९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अय्या! स्वर्गदलि हेमा ऎम्ब प्रसिद्ध अप्सरॆ इद्दळॆन्दु, नीनु हिन्दॆ ऎन्दादरू केळिरबहुदु. प्रलोम दानवन कन्यॆ शचियन्नु देवेन्द्रनिगॆ कॊट्टन्तॆ, देवतॆगळु आकॆयन्नु ननगॆ अर्पिसिद्दरु. नानु आकॆयल्लि आसक्तनागि ऒन्दु साविरवर्ष आकॆयॊन्दिगॆ इद्दॆ. ऒन्दु दिन अवळु देवतॆगळ कार्यक्कागि स्वर्गक्कॆ होदळु, आगिनिन्द हदिनाल्कु वर्ष कळॆयितु. नानु आ हेमळिगागि मायॆयिन्द ऒन्दु नगरवन्नु पूर्ण बङ्गारदिन्द निर्मिसिद्दॆ. नील, वज्रगळिन्द अदु विचित्रवागि शोभिसुत्तित्तु. अदरल्ले नानु इष्टरवरॆगॆ आकॆय वियोगदिन्द अत्यन्त दीन, दुःखियागि इरुत्तिद्दॆ.॥6-9॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

6: “There is an apsaras named Hemā, O dear one (hemā nāmāpsarās tāta), if perchance you have heard of her before (śrutapūrvā yadi tvayā); she was bestowed upon me by the gods (daivatair mama sā dattā), even as Paulomī was given to Indra of a hundred sacrifices (paulomīva śatakratoḥ).

7: “Devoted to her with all my heart, O dear one (tasyāṃ saktamanās tāta), I passed five hundred years (pañca-varṣa-śatāny aham); then she departed on an errand of the gods (sā ca daivata-kāryeṇa), and thirteen years have elapsed since (trayodaśa samā gatāḥ)—

8: “And now the fourteenth year has passed as well (varṣaṃ caturdaśaṃ caiva). Thereafter a city wrought of gold (hemamayaṃ puram), adorned with diamonds and cat’s-eye gems (vajra-vaiḍūrya-citram), was fashioned by me through my wondrous skill (māyayā nirmitaṃ mayā); there I dwelt wretched (tatrāham avasaṃ dīnaḥ), bereft of her and deeply grieved (tayā hīnaḥ suduḥkhitaḥ).

9: “Taking my daughter from that city (tasmāt purād duhitaraṃ gṛhītvā), I have come into this forest (vanam āgataḥ). This is my own daughter, O King (iyaṃ mamātmajā rājan), nurtured within her womb (tasyāḥ kukṣau vivardhitā).

‘उसी नगरसे इस कन्याको साथ लेकर मैं वनमें आया हूँ । राजन्! यह मेरी पुत्री है, जो हेमाके गर्भमें ही पली है और उससे उत्पन्न होकर मेरे द्वारा पालित हो बड़ी हुई है ॥ १० ॥

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

7.12.1–2 (Consecration in Laṅkā and Śūrpaṇakhā’s Marriage)

Following Rāvaṇa’s bloodless occupation of Laṅkā and consecration at the close of Sarga 11, Agastya turns to the domestic alliances that consolidate the rākṣasa court. Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) introduces the sarga as recounting Rāvaṇa’s duties immediately after entering Laṅkā, noting his first royal obligation as elder brother: arranging his sister Śūrpaṇakhā’s marriage (भगिन्याः शूर्पणखायाः प्रदानं समचिन्तयत्). Giving her to Vidyujjihva—glossed by Govindarāja as ‘son of Kālaka’ (कालकपुत्राय) and by Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) as the Dānava chieftain—forges a strategic tie between rākṣasas and Kālakeya asuras.

7.12.3–4 (Encountering Maya in the Solitary Forest)

Setting out on a royal hunt after his sister’s wedding (दत्त्वा शूर्पणखामिति शेषः, Śivasahāya), Rāvaṇa encounters Maya, the renowned architect of the asuras, standing alone in the wilds with a maiden. Construing 7.12.4–5ab as a single syntactic unit (श्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयि), Śivasahāya offers two distinct readings of निर्मनुष्यमृगे वने: either ‘an exceedingly terrifying wood from which both men and beasts have fled’ (निर्गता मनुष्यमृगा यस्मादतिभयङ्करमित्यर्थः), or ‘a wood devoid of men and frequented solely by wild deer’ (मनुष्यरहिता मृगा यस्मिन् मृगमात्रसेवितमित्यर्थः), glossing एकः as ‘without any other male escort’ (पुरुषान्तररहितः).

7.12.5–6 (Maya’s Candid Narrative and the Gift of Hemā)

Addressing Rāvaṇa courteously as tāta (‘dear one’), Maya promises to relate his history faithfully, without deviating from what occurred (वृत्तमनतिक्रम्य, Śivasahāya), while Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) notes राम in 7.12.5a as Agastya’s vocative to Rāma. Maya begins by invoking his celestial union with the apsaras Hemā, comparing the gods’ bestowal of her upon himself to the marriage of Paulomī (Śacī) to Indra (Śatakratu)—thus immediately establishing his own high standing and his daughter’s semi-divine maternal pedigree.

7.12.7–8 (Hemā’s Departure and the Golden City of Sorrow)

Unpacking the compressed timeline of 7.12.7–8, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) supplies ‘to the celestial world’ (देवलोकमिति शेषः) with गता: thirteen years elapsed after Hemā’s departure on divine business, and a fourteenth year too passed while Maya pined without her (चतुर्दशं च तद्विरहितस्य गतमित्यनुकर्षः). Both Tilaka and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss मायया as Maya’s ‘wondrous architectural power’ (विचित्रनिर्माणशक्त्या), by which he raised a gold-abounding, jewel-inlaid palace (सुवर्णप्रचुरम्, Tilaka), yet even amid splendor he languished in separation (Śivasahāya, construing 7.12.7cd–8 as one sentence, श्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयि).

7.12.9–11ab (A Father’s Anxious Quest Echoing Sumālī)

Presenting the maiden nurtured in Hemā’s womb (हेमाया गर्भे, Rāma Varmā; हेमायाः कुक्षौ, Śivasahāya and Govindarāja), Maya explains his forest journey as a search for a worthy bridegroom. Remarkable is the verbal echo of Sumālī’s lament in 7.9.9–10: Tilaka glosses मानकाङ्क्षिणाम् as ‘those desirous of honor’ (अभिमानेच्छूनाम्), observing that fathering a daughter brings distress because it threatens one’s pride (अभिमाननिवर्तकत्वात्). Construing 7.12.10–11ab together (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी), Śivasahāya notes that Maya speaks of ‘two families’ (कुलद्वयम्)—his own and her husband’s—held in constant suspense.

7.12.11cd–12 (Māyāvī and Dundubhi—Echoes of the Kiṣkindhākāṇḍa)

Completing his family chronicle, Maya mentions his two sons by Hemā, Māyāvī the elder and Dundubhi the younger (प्रथमो मायावी तदनन्तरो दुन्दुभिः, Śivasahāya). For Rāma listening to Agastya, these two names carry vivid resonance from the Kiṣkindhākāṇḍa, where both titanic brothers perished in legendary duels with Vālin—the very conflicts that triggered Vālin’s rift with Sugrīva and left Dundubhi’s colossal skeleton before Ṛśyamūka. Having answered Rāvaṇa’s questions with full transparency (yāthātathyena), Maya prepares to ask his stranger’s identity in turn.

7.12.13–14 (Rāvaṇa’s Courteous Self-Introduction and Descent from Brahmā)

Answering Maya’s polite inquiry (Śivasahāya) with uncharacteristic courtesy (vinītam), Rāvaṇa foregrounds not his rākṣasa conquest of Laṅkā, but his luminous paternal genealogy. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) carefully clarify तृतीयो ब्रह्मणोऽभवत् in 7.12.14: Viśravas was the third generation counting from Brahmā—Brahmā, Pulastya, and Viśravas (यो मुनिर्विश्रवा ब्रह्मणस्तृतीयापत्यं तस्य भगवतो विश्रवसोऽहं तनय इत्यर्थः, Tilaka). By invoking his brahmarṣi father, Daśagrīva presents himself as the ideal, highborn bridegroom Maya has been seeking.

7.12.15–16 (Maya’s Immediate Joy and Formal Betrothal)

Construing the three-line verse 7.12.15 as a single sentence (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी, Śivasahāya), Agastya depicts Maya’s instant delight upon recognizing a great sage’s son (महर्षेस्तनयं ज्ञात्वा). Rāma Varmā notes that Maya resolved to give his daughter right then, at the very moment of discovering Rāvaṇa’s noble pedigree (तत्र वै महाकुलप्रसूतत्वज्ञानकाले). In 7.12.16, Maya seals the gift physically: Śivasahāya glosses करेण तु करं तस्या ग्राहयित्वा as Maya causing Rāvaṇa’s hand to clasp his daughter’s hand (तस्याः कन्यायाः करं रावणहस्तेन ग्राहयित्वा) with a pleased smile.

7.12.17–18 (Mandodarī’s Bestowal and the Forest Nuptial Fire)

Presenting his slender-waisted daughter Mandodarī, Maya explicitly recalls her celestial maternity (हेमयाप्सरसा धृता), which both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gloss as ‘conceived in the womb and brought forth by Hemā’ (धृता धृतगर्भा प्रसूतेत्यर्थः). Rāvaṇa accepts instantly with the solemn assent बाढम् (‘so be it!’), and—as Śivasahāya notes—kindles a sacred fire on the spot to celebrate the Vedic hand-taking ceremony (अग्निं प्रज्वाल्य पाणिग्रहणविधिमकरोत्), establishing Mandodarī as his chief, ritually wedded queen (paṭṭamahiṣī).

7.12.19 (Why Maya Wedded Mandodarī Despite Viśravas’s Prophecy)

All three commentators probe the striking tension in 7.12.19: if Maya already knew of Viśravas’s dread forecast in Sarga 9 that Rāvaṇa would be cruel of nature (दारुणप्रकृतिर्भविष्यतीति श्रुतशापवृत्तान्तः, Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja), why did he gladly give him Mandodarī? Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construes the verse concessively: despite knowing the sage’s curse, Maya honored Rāvaṇa’s descent from Grandfather Brahmā (तथापि तस्य पैतामहं कुलं ज्ञात्वा दत्ता), and Govindarāja agrees that noble lineage outweighed all else (केवलं कुलमेव गण्यत इत्यर्थः). Rāma Varmā adds a shrewd realist motive: Maya also realized that if refused, the fierce rākṣasa would overpower him and seize her by force (मां निगृह्य बलादपि ग्रहीष्यतीति ज्ञानेन).

7.12.20 (The Dowry of the Unfailing Spear)

Alongside Mandodarī, Maya presents an extraordinary dowry: an infallible celestial spear (amoghā śakti) won through Maya’s own severe austerity (लब्धाम् । मयेनेति शेषः, Rāma Varmā). Agastya pauses to tie this ancient gift directly to Rāma’s own recent ordeal in the Yuddhakāṇḍa: it was this very weapon with which Rāvaṇa felled Lakṣmaṇa on the battlefield (यया लक्ष्मणं जघ्निवान्, Śivasahāya). Thus Vālmīki weaves the biographical backstory of Book VII tightly into the climactic memories of Book VI.

7.12.21–24 (Brides for Kumbhakarṇa and Vibhīṣaṇa—Saramā’s Birth)

Returning to Laṅkā as a wedded sovereign, Rāvaṇa fulfills his fraternal duty by procuring wives for his brothers through proper nuptial rites (विवाहविधिना प्रापयत्, Śivasahāya; Rāma Varmā notes the variant समुदावहत्). The choices mirror each brother’s temperament: colossal Kumbhakarṇa receives the fiery asura-princess Vajrajvālā, granddaughter of Bali Vairocana, whereas righteous Vibhīṣaṇa wins Saramā—daughter of the Gandharva king Śailūṣa—whose compassionate loyalty later comforts Sītā in the Yuddhakāṇḍa. On 7.12.24, Tilaka announces the charming etymology of her name (सरमाया नाम निर्वक्ति), set on the rain-swollen shore of Lake Mānasa.

7.12.25–26 (Saramā’s Folk Etymology and Wedded Bliss in Laṅkā)

Continuing 7.12.24 (श्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयि, Śivasahāya), Agastya completes the poetic nirukti of Saramā’s name. As monsoon waters swelled Lake Mānasa right up to the newborn infant (तच्छिशुपर्यन्तं ववृधे, Rāma Varmā), the frightened mother cried out tenderly (आक्रन्दितं विह्वलं वचः, Śivasahāya), ‘Lake, do not rise!’ (सरो मा वर्धयस्व / वर्धत). From the opening syllables saro mā, the child received the name Saramā—a phonetic contraction both Tilaka and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) validate as grammatically sound under Pāṇini’s pṛṣodarādi class (पृषोदरादित्वात्साधुः). Verse 7.12.26 briefly shows all three couples rejoicing in Laṅkā like Gandharvas in Nandana.

7.12.27–29 (The Birth and Naming of Meghanāda)

Returning at last to Rāma’s original question from Sarga 1, Agastya announces Mandodarī’s son Meghanāda—identifying him directly as ‘this very one whom you all call Indrajit’ (युष्माभिरिन्द्रजिन्नामाभिधीयते, Śivasahāya). Construing 7.12.28–29 as one syntactic unit (अर्धचतुष्टयमेकान्वयि, Śivasahāya), the poet explains his birth-name: the instant he was born, his weeping loosed a thunderous roar—glossed by Rāma Varmā as ‘like the booming of a rain-cloud’ (जलधरनादोपमः)—that stunned all Laṅkā into silence (jaḍīkṛtā), prompting Rāvaṇa himself to name him Meghanāda (‘Cloud-Roar’).

7.12.30 (A Fire Hidden Beneath Wood)

Construing the closing three-line stanza as a single sentence (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी, Śivasahāya), Agastya closes Sarga 12 with an unforgettable simile: tended by noble ladies in Rāvaṇa’s inner palace and delighting his parents, the young Meghanāda grew up ‘like a fire concealed beneath firewood’ (channaḥ kāṣṭhair ivānalaḥ). The image works on two levels: outwardly, the tender child is sheltered and nourished by the palace women just as kindling shields a flame; prophetically, it warns of the latent, all-consuming blaze of martial power that will soon burst forth to conquer Indra himself.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Thereafter Mandodarī brought forth a son, Meghanāda (putraṃ meghanādam ajījanat)—this very one is he who is spoken of by you all under the name Indrajit (sa eṣa indrajin-nāma yuṣmābhir abhidhīyate)!

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

ततो मन्दोदरी पुत्रं मेघनादमजीजनत् ॥ २८ ॥
स एष इन्द्रजिन्नाम युष्माभिरभिधीयते ।

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

ततो मन्दोदरी पुत्रं मेघनादमजीजनत् ॥ २८ ॥
स एष इन्द्रजिन्नाम युष्माभिरभिधीयते ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

तदनन्तर कुछ कालके बाद मन्दोदरीने अपने पुत्र मेघनादको जन्म दिया, जिसे आपलोग इन्द्रजित् के नामसे पुकारते थे ॥ २८ १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अनन्तर कॆल कालदल्लि मन्दोदरियु मेघनादनिगॆ जन्मवित्तळु, अवनन्नु जनरु इन्द्रजितु ऎन्दू करॆयुत्तिद्दरु.॥28½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

For in former days, the moment he was born (jātamātreṇa hi purā), that weeping son of Rāvaṇa (rudatā … rāvaṇa-sūnunā) let forth a mighty cry resembling a thundercloud (sumahān mukto nādo jaladharopamaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

जातमात्रेण हि पुरा तेन रावणसूनुना ॥ २९ ॥
रुदता सुमहान् मुक्तो नादो जलधरोपमः ।

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

जातमात्रेण हि पुरा तेन रावणसूनुना ॥ २९ ॥
रुदता सुमहान् मुक्तो नादो जलधरोपमः ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

पूर्वकालमें उस रावणपुत्रने पैदा होते ही रोते-रोते मेघके समान गम्भीर नाद किया था ॥ २९ १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आ रावणपुत्रनु हुट्टुत्तले अळुवाग मेघदन्तॆ गम्भीर नाद माडिद्दनु.॥29½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

And all Laṅkā was stunned into numbness by that roar of his, O Rāghava (jaḍīkṛtā ca sā laṅkā tasya nādena rāghava); thereupon his father himself gave him the name Meghanāda (pitā tasyākaron nāma meghanāda iti svayam).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

जडीकृता च सा लङ्का तस्य नादेन राघव ॥ ३० ॥
पिता तस्याकरोन्नाम मेघनाद इति स्वयम् ।

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

जडीकृता च सा लङ्का तस्य नादेन राघव ॥ ३० ॥
पिता तस्याकरोन्नाम मेघनाद इति स्वयम् ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

रघुनन्दन! उस मेघतुल्य नादसे सारी लङ्का जडवत् स्तब्ध रह गयी थी; इसलिये पिता रावणने स्वयं ही उसका नाम मेघनाद रखा ॥ ३० १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रघुनन्दन! आ मेघदन्तह नाददिन्द इडी लङ्कॆ जडदन्तॆ स्थब्दवागित्तु; अदरिन्द रावणने स्वतः अवन हॆसरु मेघनादनॆन्दु इट्टनु.॥30½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

He grew up there, O Rāma (so ’vardhata tadā rāma), within Rāvaṇa’s auspicious inner palace (rāvaṇāntaḥpure śubhe), watched over by noble women (rakṣyamāṇo varastrībhiḥ), like a fire concealed beneath firewood (channaḥ kāṣṭhair ivānalaḥ), bringing boundless joy to his mother and father (mātāpitror mahāharṣaṃ janayan rāvaṇātmajaḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

सोऽवर्धत तदा राम रावणान्तःपुरे शुभे ॥ ३१ ॥
रक्ष्यमाणो वरस्त्रीभिश्छन्नः काष्ठैरिवानलः ।
मातापित्रोर्महाहर्षं जनयन् रावणात्मजः ॥ ३२ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

सोऽवर्धत तदा राम रावणान्तःपुरे शुभे ॥ ३१ ॥
रक्ष्यमाणो वरस्त्रीभिश्छन्नः काष्ठैरिवानलः ।
1 मातापित्रोर्महाहर्षं जनयन् रावणात्मजः ॥ ३२ ॥

सोऽवर्धत तदा राम रावणान्तःपुरे शुभे ॥ रक्षमाणो वरस्त्रीभिश्फन्नः काष्ठैरिवानलः ।
1 मातापित्रोर्महाहर्षं जनयन् रावणात्मजः ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

श्रीराम! उस समय वह रावणकुमार रावणके सुन्दर अन्तःपुरमें माता-पिताको महान् हर्ष प्रदान करता हुआ श्रेष्ठ नारियोंसे सुरक्षित हो काष्ठसे आच्छादित हुई अग्निके समान बढ़ने लगा ॥ ३१-३२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

श्रीरामा! आग आ रावण कुमारनु रावणन सुन्दर अन्तःपुरदल्लि तन्दॆ-तायियरिगॆ हर्षवन्नुण्टु माडुत्ता श्रेष्ठनारियरिन्द सुरक्षितनागि कट्टिगॆयल्लि अडगिद बॆङ्कियन्तॆ बॆळॆदनु.॥31-32॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये उत्तरकाण्डे द्वादशः सर्गः ॥ १२ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके उत्तरकाण्डमें बारहवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ १२ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

श्रीवाल्मीकि विरचित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यद उत्तर काण्डदल्लि हन्नॆरडनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु. ॥12॥
