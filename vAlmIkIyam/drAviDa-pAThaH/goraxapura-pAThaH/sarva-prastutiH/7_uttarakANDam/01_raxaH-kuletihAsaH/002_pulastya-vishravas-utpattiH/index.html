सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Answering Rāma’s inquiry into Indrajit’s prowess, the pitcher-born sage Agastya begins by tracing Rāvaṇa’s lineage back to the Kṛta age and Prajāpati’s radiant son, the Brahmarṣi Pulastya.

राघवस्य महात्मनः ।
कुम्भयोनिर्महातेजा
वाक्यमेतदुवाच ह ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

महात्मा रघुनाथजीका वह प्रश्न सुनकर महातेजस्वी कुम्भयोनि अगस्त्यने उनसे इस प्रकार कहा— ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

महात्मा रघुनाथन प्रश्नॆयन्नु केळि महातेजस्वि कुम्भयोनि अगस्त्यरु हीगॆ नुडिदरु.॥1॥

Hearing those words of the high-souled Rāghava (rāghavasya mahātmanaḥ), the pitcher-born sage Agastya of mighty luster (kumbhayonir mahātejāḥ) spoke thus to Rāma (rāmam etad uvāca ha):

जघान शत्रून् येनासौ न च वध्यः स शत्रुभिः ॥ २ ॥

‘श्रीराम! इन्द्रजित् के महान् बल और तेजके उद्देश्यसे जो वृत्तान्त घटित हुआ है, उसे बताता हूँ, सुनो । जिस बलके कारण वह तो शत्रुओंको मार गिराता था, परंतु स्वयं किसी शत्रुके हाथसे मारा नहीं जाता था; उसका परिचय दे रहा हूँ ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

श्रीराम! इन्द्रजितुविन महाबल मत्तु तेजद कुरितु नडॆद वृत्तान्तवन्नु हेळुवॆनु केळु. याव बलदिन्द अवनु शत्रुगळन्नु कॊल्लुत्तिद्दनो, आदरॆ तानु यावुदे शत्रुविनिन्द सोलुत्तिरलिल्लवो अदर परिचय माडिसुत्तेनॆ.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Hear, O Rāma, the true account of his mighty fiery strength (kathā-vṛttaṃ tasya tejo-balaṃ mahat), by which he slew his foes (jaghāna śatrūn) and could not be slain by enemies (na ca vadhyaḥ sa śatrubhiḥ).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

7.2.1–2 (Agastya Launches the Paulastya Chronicle)

Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) frames Sarga 2 as a necessary genealogical prelude: to account for the origins of Rāvaṇa and Indrajit, Agastya first narrates the origin of their forefather Pulastya (अथ रावणाद्युत्पत्तिशेषतया तत्कुलस्थपौलस्त्योत्पत्तिमाह). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construes 7.2.2 smoothly as a single relative clause: ‘Hear as it truly happened that great fiery power by which Indrajit slew his foes and remained invulnerable to enemies’ (येन तेजोबलेन असौ इन्द्रजित् शत्रून् जघान स्वयं च शत्रुभिरवध्यः). Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) clarify that असौ points specifically to Rāvaṇi, whose invincibility is the cause Rāma must hear (तत्कारणं शृण्विति संबन्धः).

7.2.3–4 (From Rāvaṇa’s Lineage to Pulastya in the Kṛta Age)

Why does Agastya begin with Rāvaṇa when Rāma asked about Indrajit? All three commentators highlight the adverb तावत् (‘first’) in 7.2.3: Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja explain that Agastya first expounds Rāvaṇa’s lineage, birth, and boons, reserving Indrajit’s account for afterward (तावत्प्रथमं रावणस्य कुलादि ब्रवीमि पश्चाद्रावणेश्च वक्ष्यामीति शेषः). Opening the genealogy in the golden Kṛta age, both Tilaka and Bhūṣaṇa identify Pulastya as one of the ten Prajāpatis (दशप्रजापतिष्वेकः). While Govindarāja glosses पितामह इव simply as ‘like his father Brahmā’ (पितृसदृश इत्यर्थः), Rāma Varmā specifies the exact likeness: his ascetic power to punish and bestow grace (तपोवैभवजनितनिग्रहानुग्रहसामर्थ्यैरिति भावः).

7.2.5–6 (Ineffable Virtues and Universal Esteem)

Explaining the striking claim of 7.2.5, Rāma Varmā observes that Pulastya’s virtues of righteousness and character cannot be enumerated exhaustively (वर्णयितुमशक्यत्वात्); therefore, merely declaring his identity as Prajāpati’s son implies his possession of boundless auspicious qualities. Śivasahāya offers a complementary interpretation: Pulastya’s virtues are not merely known by external report (अनुकीर्त्या कथनेनैव न ज्ञायन्ते), for they shine forth of their own accord (स्वत एव प्रकटिताः), and the mere utterance of his name reveals him as Prajāpati’s son. On 7.2.6, Śivasahāya paraphrases Pulastya’s standing as dear to the entire world (सर्वस्य लोकस्य च वल्लभः प्रियः), bridging into his austerities upon Mount Meru.

7.2.7–8 (Pulastya’s Penance at Tṛṇabindu’s Hermitage)

Moving from Pulastya’s birth to his ascetic discipline, Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) glosses धर्मप्रसङ्गेन in 7.2.7 as ‘out of desire to accomplish austerity’ (तपःसंपादनेच्छयेत्यर्थः), noting that Tṛṇabindu is the name of a sage (तृणबिन्दुर्नाम ऋषिः). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) similarly renders धर्मप्रसङ्गेन as ‘devotion to austerity’ (तपआसक्त्या) at the hermitage standing upon Meru’s flank. On 7.2.8, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) thoughtfully exonerates the visiting maidens of malice: they caused distraction (विघ्नं कुर्वन्ति) merely by coming into that quiet precinct, not out of deliberate intent to ruin his penance (तत्रागमनमात्रेण न तु बुद्धिपूर्वम्).

7.2.9–11 (The All-Season Grove and Innocent Distraction)

Explaining why celestial, nāga, and royal-sage maidens flocked there daily, Śivasahāya notes that 7.2.10 supplies the cause of their arrival (तत्रागमने हेतुं वदन्नाह): the forest remained inviting in every season. Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja gloss तं देशम् (‘that place’) specifically as the hermitage grounds (आश्रमपदम्). On the three-line verse 7.2.11—which Śivasahāya construes as a single syntactic unit (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी)—Rāma Varmā clarifies the maidens’ pastimes: वादयन्त्यः implies playing lutes and other instruments (वीणादिकमिति शेषः), while लासयन्त्यः means dancing gracefully (नृत्यन्त्यः), innocently disrupting (तपोविघातम्) Pulastya’s focus.

7.2.12 (The Sage’s Curse of Conception)

Driven beyond endurance by the recurring interruption of his Vedic study, the fiery sage utters a solemn decree in 7.2.12. Śivasahāya marks the sequence directly: immediately following the disruption of his austerity (अथ तपोविघातानन्तरम्), the angered Pulastya proclaimed that whichever maiden came within his field of vision would instantly conceive (या मम दर्शनमागच्छेत् सा गर्भं धारयिष्यति). Remarkably, rather than cursing the innocent maidens with death or disfigurement, Pulastya invokes a strictly deterrent, generative curse—one that will unexpectedly become the providential instrument through which the Paulastya line continues through Viśravas.

7.2.13–15 (Unaware of the Curse During Vedic Recitation)

On the dramatic irony of 7.2.13–14, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) glosses तत् as the curse-pronouncement (शापवाक्यम्) and clarifies that the present tense न शृणोति bears past meaning (न शुश्राव). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) concurs (नाशृणोत्), construing 7.2.13ef–14 as a single unit (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी): precisely because she had not heard Pulastya’s warning, she strolled fearlessly (सुनिर्भया सती) into his deserted grove. On 7.2.15, all three commentators gloss स्वाध्यायम् as Vedic study or recitation (वेदाध्ययनम्; Govindarāja: वेदमपठदित्यर्थः), while Śivasahāya renders तपसा भावितः as ‘illumined by austerity’ (प्रकाशितः).

7.2.16 (Instantaneous Conception at the Sound of the Veda)

All three commentators explain why Tṛṇabindu’s daughter drew near Pulastya in 7.2.16: hearing the resonant Vedic chant (वेदध्वनिं श्रुत्वा), she approached out of innocent curiosity to see who was reciting (कोऽधीत इति गत्वा; Śivasahāya: उच्चारयितृनिश्चयार्थमवलोक्य). The moment she entered his sight, the Brahmarṣi’s curse took immediate physical effect. Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja gloss पाण्डुदेहा as turning pale from sudden pregnancy (गर्भोत्पत्त्या, गर्भिणीत्वात्), and unpack सुव्यञ्जितशरीरजा as ‘she in whom the body-born fetus became unmistakably manifest’ (शरीराज्जायत इति शरीरजो गर्भः सुप्रकाशितो यस्याः सा).

7.2.17–18 (Dismay and a Father’s Grave Question)

Bewildered by the inexplicable change in her frame, the maiden returns in distress to her father. Rāma Varmā glosses तद्दोषम् in 7.2.17 as ‘that blemish consisting in sudden pregnancy’ (गर्भसंभवरूपदोषम्), while Govindarāja interprets इदं मे किन्त्विति ज्ञात्वा as reflecting in puzzlement upon what could have caused it (गर्भकारणमिदं किन्त्विति ज्ञात्वेत्यर्थः). When Tṛṇabindu confronts her in 7.2.18, Rāma Varmā glosses असदृशम् as ‘unbecoming of maidenhood’ (कन्यात्वस्यायुक्तम्), and Śivasahāya adds ‘unworthy of your noble lineage—a pregnant form during maidenhood’ (स्वकुलायोग्यमेतत् कन्यात्वसमये गर्भयुक्तम्).

7.2.19–21 (The Maiden’s Candid Confession)

Far from concealing her movements, Tṛṇabindu’s daughter responds with filial candor and distress (dīnā). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) observes that in 7.2.20–21 she recounts her exact actions so that her father may discern the unseen cause (कारणज्ञानार्थं स्ववृत्तमाह): she had gone alone to Pulastya’s sacred hermitage solely to look for her friends. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) glosses पूर्वं गता as ‘went earlier’ (प्रथमं गता), while Śivasahāya explicitly normalizes her vivid present-tense verb न पश्यामि in 7.2.21 as past (नापश्यम्), underscoring her terrified rush home upon witnessing her body’s transformation (rūpasya viparyāsam).

7.2.22 (Yogic Insight Into the Unseen Cause)

Rather than reacting in rash anger, the royal sage Tṛṇabindu exemplifies disciplined spiritual discernment. Radiant with accumulated penance (tapasā dyotita-prabhaḥ), he enters deep yogic contemplation (ध्यानं विवेश) to inspect the truth directly. Rāma Varmā glosses the pronoun तच्चापि as ‘that too, namely the cause of her pregnancy’ (गर्भकारणमित्यर्थः). Śivasahāya expands ऋषिकर्मजम् precisely as ‘arisen from Pulastya’s curse’ (तद्गर्भम् ऋषिकर्मजं पुलस्त्यशापसमुद्भूतम् अपश्यत् अजानात्), confirming that his daughter remained completely blameless and that the child belonged strictly to Pulastya’s ascetic potency.

7.2.23 (Taking Responsibility With Dharma and Tact)

Once Tṛṇabindu perceives the truth in meditation, he acts immediately and without resentment toward the Brahmarṣi. Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) clarify the streamlined sequence of gerunds in 7.2.23cd (तनयां गृहीत्वा पुलस्त्यं गत्वेत्यन्वयः): taking his daughter by the hand, he personally escorts her straight to Pulastya’s hermitage. By refraining from counter-cursing or rebuking the elder sage and instead approaching him with dignified humility, Tṛṇabindu transforms an accidental breach into an auspicious alliance that will honor both families.

7.2.24 (The Unsolicited Alms—A Dharmaśāstra Appeal)

How can Tṛṇabindu persuade a strict celibate sage (ūrdhvaretas) to accept a bride? Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) identify Tṛṇabindu’s masterstroke in calling her ‘self-offered alms’ (भिक्षां … स्वयमुद्यताम्). Anticipating Pulastya’s objection—‘How may I, a celibate ascetic, accept a woman, and what need have I of her?’ (ननूर्ध्वरेतसा मया कथं ग्राह्या)—both commentators cite Manusmṛti 4.248: ‘An unsolicited alms brought of its own accord without prior announcement, Prajāpati deemed fit to accept even from a wrongdoer’ (उद्यतामाहृतां भिक्षां पुरस्तादप्रवेदिताम् । भोज्यां मेने प्रजानाथोऽपि दुष्कृतकर्मणः). Because she is offered unsought, dharma itself obliges Pulastya to accept her.

7.2.25–26 (Devoted Service and the Sage’s Assent)

Having invoked the scriptural rule of unsolicited alms, Tṛṇabindu supplies the practical benefit in 7.2.25—which Rāma Varmā labels the fruit of acceptance (तत्प्रतिग्रहफलमाह) and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) its purpose (तत्प्रयोजनमाह): she will attend tirelessly to the sage whose body grows weary from intense austerity (ते शुश्रूषणपरा भविष्यति). Persuaded both by dharma and by the royal sage’s rectitude, Pulastya—already inclined to accept the maiden (कन्यां जिघृक्षुः सन्, Śivasahāya)—gives his succinct, dignified Vedic assent: बाढम् (‘So be it!’).

7.2.27–28 (Lawful Marriage and Marital Harmony)

Crucially, Tṛṇabindu does not simply leave his daughter behind: both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja gloss यथान्यायम् (‘according to rule’) in 7.2.27 as signifying formal solemnization through the wedding rite of hand-taking (यथान्यायं पाणिग्रहणपूर्वकमिति शेषः), thereby legitimizing both the union and the unborn son. Returning to his own hermitage, Tṛṇabindu leaves her to win Pulastya’s heart completely. On 7.2.28, Śivasahāya notes that Pulastya grew thoroughly gratified by her inner disposition (śīla) and outward conduct (vṛtta), preparing the auspicious boon of the concluding spread.

7.2.29–30a (The Boon of a Son Uniting Both Lineages)

Rewarding his wife’s faultless service, Pulastya transforms what began as an accidental curse into an explicit paternal blessing. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construes 7.2.29–30a as a single six-quarter sentence (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी): delighted by her abundance of virtues (गुणानां संपत्त्या), Pulastya grants her a son equal to himself (आत्मसमं स्वसदृशम्), famous as Paulastya. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) clarify the dual genitive उभयोः in 7.2.30a as denoting the lineages of both mother and father (मातुः पितुश्चेत्यर्थः), uniting Tṛṇabindu’s royal-sage house with Pulastya’s Brahmarṣi line.

7.2.30b–31a (The Nirukta Etymology of ‘Viśravas’)

Giving the child his prophetic name, Pulastya offers a classic epic nirukta linking Viśravas directly to viśruta (‘distinctly heard’). All three commentators normalize the archaic participle अध्ययतः as classical अधीयानस्य (‘while I was reciting the Veda’). Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja add a subtle causal link to वेदो विश्रुतः: they supply गर्भहेतुरिति शेषः—because the Veda distinctly heard by her during Pulastya’s recitation became the very occasion and seed of her conception, the child born from that sacred encounter shall be named Viśravas (विश्रवा नाम भविष्यति).

7.2.31b–33 (Birth and Ascetic Stature of Viśravas)

Rejoicing in her inner heart (prahṛṣṭenāntarātmanā), Tṛṇabindu’s daughter soon gives birth to Viśravas, whom Śivasahāya links across 7.2.31c–32 as a single unit (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी). The closing portrait of 7.2.33 establishes Viśravas as a figure of spotless Brahminical stature—learned in Vedic lore (śrutimān), impartial toward all beings (samadarśī), steadfast in vows, and matching Pulastya himself in austerity—pointing out an ārṣa hiatus (पूर्वरूपाभाव आर्षः) in his reading of 7.2.33d. This luminous origin heightens the moral tragedy to follow, as this very ascetic lineage will produce both Kubera and Rāvaṇa in the sargas ahead.

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘रघुनन्दन! इस प्रस्तुत विषयका वर्णन करनेके लिये मैं पहले आपको रावणके कुल, जन्म तथा वरदान-प्राप्ति आदिका प्रसङ्ग सुनाता हूँ ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

रघुनन्दन! ई प्रस्तुत विषयवन्नु वर्णिसुव मॊदलु, रावणन कुल, जन्म, वरप्राप्ति इत्यादि प्रसङ्गगळन्नु निनगॆ तिळिसुत्तेनॆ.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“First (tāvat), O Rāghava, I shall relate to you this lineage and birth of Rāvaṇa (rāvaṇasyedaṃ kulaṃ janma ca), and likewise the granting of boons (vara-pradānam) that was bestowed upon him.

पुलस्त्यो नाम ब्रह्मर्षिः साक्षादिव पितामहः ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

पुरा कृतयुगे राम प्रजापतिसुतः प्रभुः ।

“To that righteous royal sage speaking those words (taṃ bruvāṇaṃ tu tad-vākyaṃ rājarṣiṃ dhārmikam), that twice-born sage, willing to accept the maiden (jighṛkṣur … kanyām), replied simply: ‘So be it!’ (bāḍham ity eva).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तं ब्रुवाणं तु तद् वाक्यं राजर्षिं धार्मिकं तदा ।
जिघृक्षुरब्रवीत् कन्यां बाढमित्येव स द्विजः ॥ २७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तं ब्रुवाणं तु तद् वाक्यं राजर्षिं धार्मिकं तदा ।
जिघृक्षुरब्रवीत् कन्यां बाढमित्येव स द्विजः ॥ २७ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

ऐसी बात कहते हुए उन धर्मात्मा राजर्षिको देखकर उनकी कन्याको ग्रहण करनेकी इच्छासे उन ब्रह्मर्षिने कहा—‘बहुत अच्छा’ ॥ २७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

हीगॆ हेळुत्तिरुव आ धर्मात्मा राजर्षियन्नु नोडि, अवर कन्यॆयन्नु ग्रहणमाडुव इच्छॆयिन्द अवरल्लि ‘सरि, हागे आगलि’ ऎन्दु हेळिदरु.॥27॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Having bestowed her in accordance with sacred ordinance (dattvā sa tu yathānyāyam), he returned to his own hermitage precinct (svam āśrama-padaṃ gataḥ); and that maiden dwelt there, gratifying her husband by her virtues (toṣayantī patiṃ guṇaiḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

दत्त्वा तु तनयां राजा स्वमाश्रमपदं गतः ।
सापि तत्रावसत् कन्या तोषयन्ती पतिं गुणैः ॥ २८ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

दत्त्वा तु तनयां राजा स्वमाश्रमपदं गतः ।
सापि तत्रावसत् कन्या तोषयन्ती पतिं गुणैः ॥ २८ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘तब उन महर्षिको अपनी कन्या देकर राजर्षि तृणबिन्दु अपने आश्रमपर लौट आये और वह कन्या अपने गुणोंसे पतिको संतुष्ट करती हुई वहीं रहने लगी ॥ २८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

आग महर्षिगॆ तन्न कन्यॆयन्नु ऒप्पिसि राजर्षि तृणबिन्दु तम्म आश्रमक्कॆ मरळिदरु. आ कन्यॆयु तन्न गुणगळिन्द पतियन्नु सन्तोषपडिसुत्ता अल्ले इरतॊडगिदळु.॥28॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“With her character and noble conduct (śīla-vṛttābhyām), that foremost of sages was deeply pleased (tutoṣa munipuṅgavaḥ); and thus gratified (prītaḥ), the sage of mighty luster spoke these words to her:

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Delighted by his bride’s wealth of virtues, Pulastya blesses her with a son equal to himself—named Viśravas because she conceived while hearing his Vedic chant—and she soon bears that illustrious, ascetic sage.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तस्यास्तु शीलवृत्ताभ्यां तुतोष मुनिपुङ्गवः ।
प्रीतः स तु महातेजा वाक्यमेतदुवाच ह ॥ २९ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 तस्यास्तु शीलवृत्ताभ्यां तुतोष मुनिपुङ्गवः ।
प्रीतः स तु महातेजा वाक्यमेतदुवाच ह ॥ २९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘उसके शील और सदाचारसे वे महातेजस्वी मुनिवर पुलस्त्य बहुत संतुष्ट हुए और प्रसन्नतापूर्वक यों बोले— ॥ २९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अकॆय शील-सदाचारदिन्द आ महातेजस्वी मुनिश्रेष्ठ पुलस्त्यरु बहळ सन्तुष्टरागि सन्तोषदिन्द हीगॆ नुडिदरु.॥29॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“‘O fair-hipped lady (suśroṇi), I am exceedingly pleased with the wealth of your virtues (parituṣṭo ’smi … guṇānāṃ sampadā bhṛśam); therefore today, O noble lady (devi), I bestow upon you a son equal to myself (putram ātma-samaṃ tava)—

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

परितुष्टोऽस्मि सुश्रोणि गुणानां सम्पदा भृशम् ।
तस्माद् देवि ददाम्यद्य पुत्रमात्मसमं तव ॥ ३० ॥
उभयोर्वंशकर्तारं पौलस्त्य इति विश्रुतम् ।

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

परितुष्टोऽस्मि सुश्रोणि गुणानां सम्पदा भृशम् ।
तस्माद् देवि ददाम्यद्य पुत्रमात्मसमं तव ॥ ३० ॥
उभयोर्वंशकर्तारं पौलस्त्य इति विश्रुतम् ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘सुन्दरि! मैं तुम्हारे गुणोंके वैभवसे अत्यन्त प्रसन्न हूँ । देवि! इसीलिये आज मैं तुम्हें अपने समान पुत्र प्रदान करता हूँ, जो माता और पिता दोनोंके कुलकी प्रतिष्ठा बढ़ायेगा और पौलस्त्य नामसे विख्यात होगा ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

सुन्दरि! नानु निन्न सद्गुणगळिन्द अत्यन्त प्रसन्ननागिद्देनॆ. देवि! अदक्कागि इन्दु नानु निनगॆ नन्नन्तह पुत्रनन्नु करुणिसुवॆनु. अवनु उभयकुलद गौरव हॆच्चिसि पौलस्त्य ऎन्दु विख्यातनागुवनु.॥30½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“‘—who shall perpetuate both our lineages (ubhayor vaṃśa-kartāram) and be celebrated as Paulastya (paulastya iti viśrutam). Moreover, because this Veda was heard by you while I was reciting it (yasmāt tu viśruto vedas tvayaiṣo ’dhyayato mama),

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

यस्मात् तु विश्रुतो वेदस्त्वयेहाध्ययतो मम ॥ ३१ ॥
तस्मात् स विश्रवा नाम भविष्यति न संशयः ।

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

यस्मात् तु विश्रुतो वेदस्त्वयेहाध्ययतो मम ॥ ३१ ॥
तस्मात् स विश्रवा नाम भविष्यति न संशयः ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘देवि! मैं यहाँ वेदका स्वाध्याय कर रहा था, उस समय तुमने आकर उसका विशेषरूपसे श्रवण किया, इसलिये तुम्हारा वह पुत्र विश्रवा या विश्रवण कहलायेगा; इसमें संशय नहीं है’ ॥ ३१ १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

देवि! नानु इल्लि वेदाध्ययन माडुवाग नीनु अदन्नु विशेषवागि केळिद्दॆ, आद्दरिन्द निन्न पुत्रनु विश्रवण ऎन्दु प्रसिद्धनागुवनु; इदरल्लि संशयविल्ल.॥31½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“‘—therefore shall he be named Viśravas (viśravā nāma bhaviṣyati), of that there is no doubt!’ Addressed thus, that noble lady, with her inner spirit brimming with joy (prahṛṣṭenāntarātmanā),

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

एवमुक्ता तु सा देवी प्रहृष्टेनान्तरात्मना ॥ ३२ ॥
अचिरेणैव कालेनासूत विश्रवसं सुतम् ।
त्रिषु लोकेषु विख्यातं यशोधर्मसमन्वितम् ॥ ३३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

एवमुक्ता तु सा देवी प्रहृष्टेनान्तरात्मना ॥ ३२ ॥
अचिरेणैव कालेनासूत विश्रवसं सुतम् ।
त्रिषु लोकेषु विख्यातं यशोधर्मसमन्वितम् ॥ ३३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘पतिके प्रसन्नचित्त होकर ऐसी बात कहनेपर उस देवीने बड़े हर्षके साथ थोड़े ही समयमें विश्रवा नामक पुत्रको जन्म दिया, जो यश और धर्मसे सम्पन्न होकर तीनों लोकोंमें विख्यात हुआ ॥ ३२-३३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

पतियु प्रसन्नचित्तदिन्द हीगॆ हेळिदाग आ देवियु कॆलवे दिनगळल्लि विश्रवा ऎम्ब पुत्रनिगॆ जन्म नीडिदळु. अवनु धर्म-यश सम्पन्ननागि मूरु लोकगळल्लि ख्यातनादनु.॥32-33॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

32: “In no long time gave birth to her son Viśravas (acireṇaiva kālenāsūta viśravasaṃ sutam), renowned throughout the three worlds (triṣu lokeṣu vikhyātam) and endowed with glory and righteousness (yaśo-dharma-samanvitam).

33: “Learned in scripture (śrutimān), equal-visioned toward all (samadarśī ca), and devoted to vows and righteous conduct (vratācāra-rataḥ), the sage Viśravas became endowed with austerity just like his father (piteva tapasā yukto hy abhavad viśravā muniḥ).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

श्रुतिमान् समदर्शी च व्रताचाररतस्तथा ।
पितेव तपसा युक्तो ह्यभवद् विश्रवा मुनिः ॥ ३४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

1 श्रुतिमान् समदर्शी च व्रताचाररतस्तथा ।
पितेव तपसा युक्तो ह्यभवद् विश्रवा मुनिः ॥ ३४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘विश्रवा मुनि वेदके विद्वान्, समदर्शी, व्रत और आचारका पालन करनेवाले तथा पिताके समान ही तपस्वी हुए’ ॥ ३४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

विश्रवा मुनियु वेदविद्वांसनू, समदर्शियू, व्रताचरणॆयन्नु पालिसुववनू, तन्दॆयन्तॆ तपस्वियू आगिद्दनु.॥34॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये उत्तरकाण्डे द्वितीयः सर्गः ॥ २ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके उत्तरकाण्डमें दूसरा सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ २ ॥
