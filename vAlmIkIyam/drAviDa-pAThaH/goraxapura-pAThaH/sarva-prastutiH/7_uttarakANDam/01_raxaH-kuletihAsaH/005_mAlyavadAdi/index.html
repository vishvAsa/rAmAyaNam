The Gandharva Grāmaṇī gives his radiant daughter Devavatī in marriage to the boon-favored Rākṣasa Sukeśa, who begets upon her three formidable sons—Mālyavān, Sumālī, and Mālin.

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

(अगस्त्यजी कहते हैं—रघुनन्दन!) तदनन्तर एक दिन विश्वावसुके समान तेजस्वी ग्रामणी नामक गन्धर्वने राक्षस सुकेशको धर्मात्मा तथा वरप्राप्त वैभवसे सम्पन्न देख अपनी देववती नामक कन्याका उसके साथ ब्याह कर दिया । वह कन्या दूसरी लक्ष्मीके समान दिव्य रूप और यौवनसे सुशोभित एवं तीनों लोकोंमें विख्यात थी । धर्मात्मा ग्रामणीने राक्षसोंकी मूर्तिमती राजलक्ष्मीके समान देववतीका हाथ सुकेशके हाथमें दे दिया ॥ १-२ १/२ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

(अगस्त्यरु हेळुत्तिद्दारॆ - रघुनन्दन!) अनन्तर ऒन्दु दिन विश्वावसुविनन्तॆ तेजस्वी ग्रामणी ऎम्ब गन्धर्वनु राक्षस सुकेशनिगॆ धर्मात्मा हागू वरप्राप्त वैभवदिन्द सम्पन्ननॆन्दु नोडि, देववती ऎम्ब तन्न कन्यॆयन्नु अवनॊन्दिगॆ विवाह माडिदनु. आ कन्यॆयु इन्नोर्व लक्ष्मियन्तॆ दिव्य रूप-यौवनदिन्द सुशोभितळागिद्दु, मूरू लोकगळल्लि विख्यातळागिद्दळु. धर्मात्मा ग्राणियु राक्षसर मूर्तिमति राजलक्ष्मियन्तॆ देववतियन्नु सुकेशनिगॆ ऒप्पिसिदनु.॥1-2½॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1: Beholding the righteous Rākṣasa Sukeśa (sukeśaṃ dhārmikam), who had obtained Śiva's boon (vara-labdham), a Gandharva named Grāmaṇī, equal in splendor to Viśvāvasu (viśvāvasu-samaprabhaḥ),

2: —whose daughter named Devavatī was like a second Śrī (dvitīyā śrīr ivātmajā), renowned throughout the three worlds and graced with beauty and youth (rūpa-yauvana-śālinī)—bestowed her righteously upon Sukeśa (dharmeṇa dadau), as though conferring the very fortune of the Rākṣasas (rakṣaḥ-śriyam).

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having obtained that beloved husband whose sovereignty was forged by a divine boon (vara-dāna-kṛtaiśvaryam), Devavatī was filled with joy (tuṣṭā), like a pauper upon gaining wealth (dhanaṃ prāpyeva nirdhanaḥ).

7.5.4–5 (The Birth of Mālyavān, Sumālī, and Mālin)

On the majestic simile in 7.5.4cd, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka), Govindarāja, and Śivasahāya all identify अञ्जनात् as the celestial quarter-elephant Añjana (अञ्जनाख्यदिग्गजादभिनिष्कान्त उत्पन्नः): united with Devavatī, Sukeśa radiated the splendor of a prime bull-elephant accompanied by his mate (करेण्वा सह). Introducing the birth of his three famous sons in 7.5.5, Rāma Varmā records the alternative reading तान्पुत्रान् (‘those celebrated sons about to be named’, तान्प्रसिद्धान्वक्ष्यमाणान्) in place of त्रीन्पुत्रान्. Śivasahāya notes that Sukeśa sired these three princes—Mālyavān, Sumālī, and Mālin—at the proper season (काले उत्पत्तिसमये), matching the three-eyed Śiva himself in vigor, splendor, and terrifying prowess.

7.5.7 (Unchecked Growth and Archaic Sandhi)

Extending the medical metaphor introduced at the close of the previous verse, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) glosses उपेक्षिताः as maladies left untreated without medical intervention (अचिकित्सिता व्याधय इवेत्यर्थः), underscoring the peril latent in the brothers’ unchecked rise. Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explicitly identify the coalesced sandhi व्याधयोपेक्षिताः as archaic epic license (सन्धिरार्षः), while Govindarāja notes that the variant त्रेताग्निसमवर्चसः emphasizes their surpassing fiery luster (तेजोऽतिशय उक्तः). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) similarly highlights how Sukeśa’s three sons rapidly expanded in strength (विवृद्धिमगमन्) just like neglected diseases spreading unrestrained through the body.

7.5.8–10 (Austerity on Mount Meru)

On 7.5.8, Śivasahāya explains that once the three brothers realized their father’s boon and sovereign splendor had been won through ascetic power, they formed a resolute determination and journeyed to Mount Meru to perform penance (तपस्तप्तुं कृतनिश्चयाः सन्तो मेरुं गताः). Narratively, Vālmīki underscores a telling moral irony in 7.5.9–10: even formidable Rākṣasas can command cosmic potency only by practicing genuine ascetic disciplines—truth, uprightness, and inner composure (सत्यादिभिरुपेतैः तपोभिः). Yet because their ultimate goal remains domination rather than self-transcendence, the ferocious heat (tapas) of their terrible vows scorches gods, Asuras, and mortals alike throughout the three worlds.

7.5.11–12 (Brahmā’s Advent and the Brothers’ Awe)

When the searing radiance of their penance reaches heaven, Śivasahāya observes that the four-faced Grandsire (चतुर्वक्त्रो ब्रह्मा), seated upon his supreme celestial car, directly summons Sukeśa’s sons (सुकेशपुत्रानामन्त्र्य सम्बोध्य) and announces himself ready to bestow a boon (वरदोऽस्मीत्यभाषत). Beholding the Creator attended by Indra and the assembled hosts of celestials, the three fearsome ascetics stand with palms reverently folded (प्राञ्जलयः) and quiver like wind-shaken trees (वेपमाना इव द्रुमाः)—a poignant simile capturing simultaneously their emaciated physical state after harsh vows and their instinctive tremor of awe before the supreme Lord of Creation.

7.5.13 (The Fourfold Boon and Archaic Morphology)

Dissecting the brothers’ carefully crafted petition, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) glosses दिशसे as ‘you bestow’ (ददासि), अजेयाः as ‘incapable of being conquered’ (जेतुमनर्हाः), प्रभविष्ण्वः as ‘sovereign lords’ (प्रभव इति यावत्), and परस्परमनुव्रताः as ‘mutually devoted to one another’ (अनुरक्ताश्च भवामेत्यन्वयः), a gloss echoed closely by Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa). Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) construes all three lines as a single grammatical unit (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी), glosses प्रभविष्ण्वः as ‘possessed of mighty sway’ (प्रभाववन्तः), and defends its syncopated form by invoking the Pāṇinian maxim that rules prefaced by a technical term (guṇa) are non-obligatory.

7.5.14–16 (Brahmā’s Departure and the Reign of Terror)

Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja resolve the dative ब्रह्मलोकाय in 7.5.14c as a goal of motion signifying ‘in order to reach Brahmā’s realm’ (तं प्राप्तुम् / ब्रह्मलोकं गन्तुम्). Rāma Varmā observes that the epithet ब्राह्मणवत्सलः (‘tender toward Brāhmaṇas’) reflects Brahmā’s paternal regard for the primeval lineage of his own creation. Immediately thereafter, the boon breeds unrestrained tyranny: Śivasahāya remarks that made utterly fearless by Brahmā’s grant (वरदानेन सुनिर्भयाः), the three night-roamers harass gods and Asuras alike, leaving celestials, sages, and Cāraṇas completely helpless, like mortals plunged into the torments of hell (निरयस्था नरा इव त्रातारं नाधिगच्छन्ति).

7.5.17–18 (Approaching Viśvakarman)

Having asserted supremacy across the worlds, the three jubilant brothers seek a royal capital worthy of their dominion. Rāma Varmā identifies ते as Mālyavān and his brothers (माल्यवदादयः) and glosses अव्ययम् as ‘immortal’ or ‘long-lived’ (चिरजीविनम्). Linking 7.5.18 syntactically with 7.5.19 as a single speech (श्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयी, Śivasahāya), Rāma Varmā explains the calculated flattery of their address: since Viśvakarman alone fashions by his own creative power (आत्मतेजसा आत्मसामर्थ्येन) the heart-desired mansions of the mighty gods endowed with vigor, radiance, and strength, he ought now to erect an equally splendid palace for them.

7.5.19–21 (A Palace Like Śiva’s and the Twin Southern Peaks)

Having demanded a residence matching Maheśvara’s abode on the sacred northern ranges, the Rākṣasas are deftly redirected southward by Viśvakarman, who describes a citadel rivaling Indra’s own Amarāvatī (शक्रस्यामरावतीमिव तेषां निवासं कथयामास, Śivasahāya). Construing 7.5.21–24a as a single extended period (दक्षिणस्येत्यादि सार्धत्रयम्), Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) joins the mountain setting directly to the city’s dimensions, while Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Śivasahāya explain द्वितीयः in 7.5.21d as signifying a second peak equal in grandeur to Trikūṭa (द्वितीयस्तत्सदृश इत्यर्थः), namely Suvela—the very summit from which Rāma will later survey Laṅkā in Book VI.

7.5.22 (The Chisel-Sheared Summit of Trikūṭa)

Commenting on the central peak (मध्यमे शिखरे), Govindarāja glosses अम्बुदसन्निभे as ‘resembling a rain-cloud’ (मेघसमानरूपे). Both Govindarāja and Rāma Varmā highlight the logical link between the two halves of line 2: the compound टङ्कच्छिन्नचतुर्दिशि supplies the exact reason (हेतुः) why the summit is unapproachable even by birds (शकुनैरपि दुष्प्रापे). Śivasahāya cites the Medinī lexicon to unpack the polysemy of ṭaṅka—stone-cutter’s chisel (शिलाविदारकखनित्रविशेषः), sword, or precipice—explaining that all four flanks drop away sheer as if carved by a blade, depriving even winged creatures of a perch.

7.5.23–24 (Laṅkā’s Dimensions and Imperial Architecture)

Śivasahāya connects the four half-verses of 7.5.22–24a (अर्धचतुष्टयमेकान्वयि) to reveal the grand climax of Viśvakarman’s speech: thirty yojanas wide and a hundred long (त्रिंशद्योजनविस्तीर्णा शतयोजनमायता), girdled by golden ramparts (स्वर्णप्राकारसंवीता आच्छादिता), the city of Laṅkā was already constructed by him at Indra’s command (शक्राज्ञप्तेन निर्मिता). By offering this vacant celestial fortress rather than building a new northern stronghold on Himavat or Meru, the divine architect fulfills their request while keeping the aggressive Rākṣasas isolated beyond the southern sea.

7.5.25 (Amarāvatī’s Counterpart and Hypermetric License)

Bringing Viśvakarman’s counsel to a persuasive close, Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) paraphrases his assurance that once ensconced inside Laṅkā’s mountain stronghold amid myriad Rākṣasa retainers, the brothers will remain utterly unassailable by every adversary (लङ्कां समासाद्य शत्रूणां दुराधर्षा भविष्यथ). Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) pause to examine the metrical irregularity of the opening nine-syllable pāda अमरावतीं समासाद्य, which renders line 1 hypermetric at seventeen syllables: Rāma Varmā explicitly classifies it as archaic epic license (नवाक्षरपादतार्षी), whereas Govindarāja attributes the extra syllable directly to the prosodic exigency of the proper noun Amarāvatī (पदवशादक्षराधिक्यम्).

7.5.26–27 (Settlement of the Golden Fortress)

Turning to the Rākṣasas’ triumphant occupation of Laṅkā, both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja explain सहस्रानुचराः not as a narrow numerical count but as an indefinite multitude—‘attended by thousands, that is, countless followers’ (सहस्रमनेकेऽनुचरा येषां ते सहस्रानुचराः). Śivasahāya observes that 7.5.27 restates their joyous settlement with heightened architectural vividness (तदेव भङ्ग्यन्तरेणाह), analyzing दृढप्राकारपरिखाम् as a bahuvrīhi compound (‘possessing formidable ramparts and deep moats’, दृढाः प्राकाराः परिखाश्च यस्याः) and glossing हैमैः as ‘fashioned entirely of gold’ (हेममयैः), evoking the golden metropolis familiar from the Sundarakāṇḍa.

7.5.28–30 (The Gandharvī Narmadā and Her Three Daughters)

Introducing the matrimonial alliance that will populate Laṅkā with its fateful next generation, all three commentators pause on the striking negative epithet अराक्षसी (‘non-Rākṣasī’) in 7.5.29d. Rāma Varmā and Śivasahāya gloss it as ‘belonging to a race distinct from the Rākṣasas’ (राक्षसजातिभिन्ना), while Govindarāja likewise renders it as ‘outside the Rākṣasa lineage’ (राक्षसजातिव्यतिरिक्ता). Just as Sukeśa earlier wed the Gandharva maiden Devavatī, his three sons now receive from the Gandharvī Narmadā three full-moon-faced brides radiant as Intelligence, Fortune, and Fame, bestowed scrupulously in order of seniority (ज्येष्ठक्रमेण).

7.5.31 (The Auspicious Asterism Uttaraphālgunī)

Commenting on the marriage rite in 7.5.31, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) clarifies दत्ता मात्रा as signifying that the three brides were bestowed freely by their mother Narmadā’s own willing consent (मात्रा नर्मदयेच्छया दत्ता इत्यर्थः). Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) identify the lunar asterism presided over by the deity Bhaga (नक्षत्रे भगदैवते) specifically as Uttaraphālgunī (भगदैवतं नक्षत्रमुत्तराफाल्गुनी)—the very auspicious star under which Rāma and Sītā themselves were wed in the Bālakāṇḍa. Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) likewise emphasizes the solemn maternal bestowal of the Gandharva maidens under this favored nuptial constellation.

7.5.32–34 (Sundarī and the Lineage of Mālyavān)

Once wed (कृतदाराः), Śivasahāya observes that Sukeśa’s three sons reveled happily with their consorts like immortals with celestial nymphs (भार्याभिः सह चिक्रीडुः). Unpacking the charming wordplay (yamaka) in सुन्दरी नाम सुन्दरी (7.5.32d), Śivasahāya distinguishes the proper name from the descriptive epithet—‘Sundarī by name, distinguished by exquisite beauty’ (सुन्दरी सौन्दर्यविशिष्टा सुन्दरी नाम माल्यवतो भार्या). Construing 7.5.33–34 as a single enumerative sequence (सार्धश्लोक एकान्वयी), he records Mālyavān’s eight children: seven fierce warriors—Vajramuṣṭi, Virūpākṣa, Durmukha, Suptaghna, Yajñakopa, Matta, and Unmatta—together with one fair daughter, Analā.

7.5.35 (Ketumatī, Beloved Consort of Sumālī)

Turning from the eldest brother to the second, Agastya introduces Sumālī’s consort Ketumatī, whose full-moon radiance and supreme place in her husband’s heart (प्राणेभ्योऽपि गरीयसी) prepare the ground for the pivotal genealogy of the next spread. Remarkably, almost every warrior enumerated in Mālyavān’s brood—Vajramuṣṭi, Virūpākṣa, Durmukha, Matta, and Unmatta—bears a name famous among Rāvaṇa’s front-line generals in the Yuddhakāṇḍa, illustrating how Vālmīki’s Uttarakāṇḍa consciously anchors the ancestral house of Sukeśa within the epic war Rāma has just brought to a close.

7.5.36–38 (The Fourteen Offspring of Sumālī)

Construing 7.5.37–38 together as a single enumerative sentence (सार्धश्लोकद्वयमेकान्वयि), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) catalogues Sumālī’s fourteen children by Ketumatī—ten formidable sons from Prahasta through Bhāsakarṇa, followed by four daughters: Rākā, Puṣpotkaṭā, Kaikasī, and Kumbhīnasī. Śivasahāya offers an astute grammatical observation on the genitive सुमालेः in 7.5.38f: although the name is inflected as an -in-stem (Sumālin) in the nominative (सुमाली), Vālmīki here treats it as a simple -i-stem (इकारान्तोऽपि सुमालिशब्दः). Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) records the variant reading ऋका for the eldest daughter राका, while confirming singular शुचिस्मिता for Kaikasī.

7.5.37–38 (Epic Echoes and the Mother of Rāvaṇa)

Narratively, this genealogical register holds the crucial link to the Rāmāyaṇa’s central conflict. Among Sumālī’s ten sons stand Rāvaṇa’s maternal uncles who later commanded Laṅkā’s armies against Rāma in Book VI—Prahasta, Akampana, Dhūmrākṣa, Supārśva, and Praghasa—while among his four daughters stands the pure-smiling Kaikasī (कैकसी च शुचिस्मिता), whose union with the sage Viśravas in Sarga 9 will bring forth Rāvaṇa, Kumbhakarṇa, Śūrpaṇakhā, and Vibhīṣaṇa. Thus the prolific house of Sukeśa directly bridges the primeval Rākṣasa clans of Laṅkā and the Paulastya dynasty overthrown by Rāma.

7.5.39 (Vasudhā, Consort of Mālin)

Introducing the third brother’s consort, Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) analyzes the closing simile यक्षीवरोपमा as ‘comparable to the foremost among Yakṣī nymphs’ (यक्षीवरो यक्षीश्रेष्ठा तयोपमीयते सा यक्षीवरोपमा), while Śivasahāya confirms the variant name वसुदा for Mālin’s Gandharva bride (यक्षीवरोपमा वसुदा नाम गन्धर्वी मालेर्भार्यासीत्). The layered visual epithets—रूपशालिनी (‘endowed with grace’), पद्मपत्राक्षी (‘lotus-petal-eyed’), and स्वक्षी (‘fair-eyed’)—underscore the celestial Gandharva refinement repeatedly blended into the Rākṣasa bloodline, setting a poised contrast just before Agastya names Mālin’s four upright sons who later serve Vibhīṣaṇa.

7.5.40–41 (Mālin’s Four Sons, Ministers of Vibhīṣaṇa)

Identifying सुमालेरनुजः as Mālin (सुमालेरनुजो माली), Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) notes that his four sons—Anila, Anala, Hara, and Sampāti—became the faithful counselors of Vibhīṣaṇa (अनलादयो मालेया विभीषणामात्या अभवन्), the very four loyal attendants who later flew across the ocean with Vibhīṣaṇa to seek Rāma’s shelter in the Yuddhakāṇḍa. Both Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) and Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) derive the patronymic मालेयाः rigorously via Pāṇini’s rule इतश्चानिञः (4.1.122), affixing the suffix ḍhak (-eya) to the -i-stem māli.

7.5.42 (The Threefold Tyranny Over the Worlds)

Shifting from anuṣṭubh into the stately twelve-syllable Vaṃśastha meter to signal the sarga’s solemn conclusion, Vālmīki expands his canvas from domestic genealogy to cosmic crisis. Śivasahāya observes how the three Rākṣasa chieftains, reinforced by hundreds of fierce offspring and intoxicated by pride in their vast might (बहुवीर्यदर्पिताः), systematically tormented the gods led by Indra alongside sages, Nāgas, and Yakṣas (सहेन्द्रान् सुरान् ऋषिनागयक्षान् बबाधिरे), precipitating the cosmic emergency that drives the afflicted celestials to approach Śiva and Viṣṇu in Sarga 6.

7.5.43 (Destroyers of Sacred Sacrifice)

Completing his portrait of Rākṣasa arrogance, Śivasahāya glosses प्रशमङ्कराः as ‘perpetual wreckers or defilers of Vedic sacrificial rites’ (क्रतुक्रियाणां सदा प्रशमङ्करा दूषका इत्यर्थः), driven to reckless excess by pride in Brahmā’s boon (वरप्रदानाद्भृशं गर्विताः). Both Rāma Varmā and Govindarāja independently single out the archaic grammar of प्रशमङ्कराः, noting that its nasalized compound form exhibits an ārṣa application of the Pāṇinian suffix khac (प्रशमङ्करा इत्यार्षः खच्), fittingly concluding a sarga rich in ancient epic linguistic forms.

वज्रमुष्टिर्विरूपाक्षो दुर्मुखश्चैव राक्षसः ॥ ३६ ॥

अनला चाभवत् कन्या सुन्दर्यां राम सुन्दरी ॥ ३७ ॥

वज्रमुष्टिर्विरूपाक्षो दुर्मुखश्चैव राक्षसः ॥ सुप्तघ्नो यज्ञ कोपश्च मत्तोन्मत्तौ तथैव च ।
अनला चाभवत्कन्या सुन्दर्यां नाम सुन्दरी ॥

वज्रमुष्टि, विरूपाक्ष, राक्षस दुर्मुख, सुप्तघ्न, यज्ञकोप, मत्त और उन्मत्त—ये सात पुत्र थे । श्रीराम! इनके अतिरिक्त सुन्दरीके गर्भसे अनला नामवाली एक सुन्दरी कन्या भी उत्पन्न हुई थी ॥ ३६-३७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

वज्रमुष्टि, विरूपाक्ष, दुर्मुख, सुप्तघ्न, यज्ञ कोप मत्तु उन्मत्त ऎम्ब एळु पुत्ररिद्दरु. श्रीरामा! इवरल्लदॆ सुन्दरिय गर्भदिन्द अनला ऎम्ब ओर्व सुन्दर कन्यॆयु हुट्टिद्दळु.॥36-37॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

36: Hear now in due order (nibodhānupūrvaśaḥ), O mighty king (mahārāja), what offspring (yad apatyam) that night-roamer Sumālī begot upon Ketumatī (ketumatyām):

37: Prahasta and Akampana, Vikaṭa and Kālakārmukha, Dhūmrākṣa and Daṇḍa, and the mighty Supārśva (supārśvaś ca mahābalaḥ),

सुमालीकी पत्नी भी बड़ी सुन्दरी थी । उसका मुख पूर्णचन्द्रमाके समान मनोहर और नाम केतुमती था । सुमालीको वह प्राणोंसे भी अधिक प्रिय थी ॥ ३८ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

सुमालिय पत्नी केतुमतिय मुख पूर्णचन्द्रनन्तॆ मनोहरवागित्तु. सुमालिगॆ अवळु प्राणक्किन्त हॆच्चु प्रियळागिद्दळु.॥38॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Saṃhrādi, Praghasa, and the Rākṣasa Bhāsakarṇa; and Rākā, Puṣpotkaṭā, the pure-smiling Kaikasī (kaikasī ca śucismitā), and Kumbhīnasī—these are remembered as the offspring of Sumālī (sumāleḥ prasavāḥ smṛtāḥ).

सुमाली जनयामास यदपत्यं निशाचरः ।
केतुमत्यां महाराज तन्निबोधानुपूर्वशः ॥ ३९ ॥

केतुमत्यां महाराज तन्निबोधानुपूर्वशः ॥ ३९ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

महाराज! निशाचर सुमालीने केतुमतीके गर्भसे जो संतानें उत्पन्न की थीं, उनका भी क्रमशः परिचय दिया जा रहा है, सुनिये ॥ ३९ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

महाराजा! निशाचर सुमालियु केतुमतियिन्द पडॆद सन्तान गळ परिचय कॊडुवॆनु, केळु.॥39॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

And Mālin's wife was a Gandharvī of exquisite beauty named Vasudhā (vasudhā nāma gandharvī rūpa-śālinī), lotus-petal-eyed (padma-patrākṣī), lovely-eyed (svakṣī), and peer to the noblest of Yakṣīs (yakṣī-varopamā).

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Agastya names Mālin’s four sons—who later become Vibhīṣaṇa’s loyal ministers—and concludes with stately Vaṃśastha verses depicting the boon-emboldened Rākṣasa hosts roaming the world like wind and wrecking sacred sacrifices.

प्रहस्तोऽकम्पनश्चैव विकटः कालिकामुखः ।
धूम्राक्षश्चैव दण्डश्च सुपार्श्वश्च महाबलः ॥ ४० ॥

कुम्भीनसी च इत्येते सुमालेः प्रसवाः स्मृताः ॥ ४२ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

प्रहस्त, अकम्पन, विकट, कालिकामुख, धूम्राक्ष, दण्ड, महाबली सुपार्श्व, संह्रादि, प्रघस तथा राक्षस भासकर्ण—ये सुमालीके पुत्र थे और राका, पुष्पोत्कटा, कैकसी और कुम्भीनसी—ये चार पवित्र मुस्कानवाली उसकी कन्याएँ थीं । ये सब सुमालीकी संतानें बतायी गयी हैं ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

प्रहस्त, अकम्पन, विकट, कालिकामुख, धूम्राक्ष, दण्ड, महाबलिसुपार्श्व, संह्रादि, प्रघस हागू राक्षस भासकर्ण इवरु सुमालिय पुत्ररागिद्दरु मत्तु राका, पुष्पोत्कटा, कैकसी मत्तु कुम्भीनसी हीगॆ नाल्वरु मन्दस्मितियराद कन्यॆयरिद्दरु. इवरॆल्लरू सुमालिय सन्तानवॆन्दु हेळुत्तारॆ.॥40-42॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

40: Hear now from me (kathyamānaṃ mayā śṛṇu), O Lord (prabho), O Rāghava, what offspring (yad apatyam) the younger brother of Sumālī (sumāler anujaḥ) begot upon her:

41: Anila, Anala, Hara, and Sampāti as well—these four sons of Mālin (māleyāḥ), those night-roamers (niśācarāḥ), became the counselors of Vibhīṣaṇa (vibhīṣaṇāmātyāḥ).

42: Then those three bulls among Rākṣasas (rākṣasa-puṅgavās trayaḥ), surrounded by hundreds of night-roaming sons (niśācaraiḥ putra-śataiś ca saṃvṛtāḥ) and swollen with pride in their vast valor (bahu-vīrya-darpitāḥ), tormented the gods together with Indra, as well as sages, Nāgas, and Yakṣas (surān sahendrān ṛṣi-nāga-yakṣān babādhire).

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

मालीकी पत्नी गन्धर्वकन्या वसुदा थी, जो अपने रूप-सौन्दर्यसे सुशोभित होती थी । उसके नेत्र प्रफुल्ल कमलके समान विशाल एवं सुन्दर थे । वह श्रेष्ठ यक्षपत्नियोंके समान सुन्दरी थी ॥ ४३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मालिय पत्नी गन्धर्वकन्यॆ रूप-सौन्दर्य सुशोभित वसुदा आगिद्दळु. आकॆय नेत्रगळु अरळिद कमलदन्तॆ विशालवागि सुन्दरवागिद्दवु. अवळु श्रेष्ठ यक्षपत्नियरन्तॆ सुन्दरळागिद्दळु.॥43॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Wandering the world (jagad bhramantaḥ), unapproachable as the wind (anilavad durāsadāḥ), rivaling Death himself in fiery might in battle (raṇeṣu mṛtyu-pratimāna-tejasaḥ), and exceedingly arrogant through the grant of their boon (vara-pradānād atigarvitāḥ), they ever proved the destroyers of sacrificial rites (kratu-kriyāṇāṃ praśamaṅkarāḥ sadā).

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

प्रभो! रघुनन्दन! सुमालीके छोटे भाई मालीने वसुदाके गर्भसे जो संतति उत्पन्न की थी, उसका मैं वर्णन कर रहा हूँ; आप सुनिये ॥ ४४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

प्रभु रघुनन्दन! सुमालिय तम्म मालियु वसुदाळिन्द पडॆद सन्तानगळन्नु वर्णिसुवॆनु, केळु.॥44॥

अनल, अनिल, हर और सम्पाति—ये चार निशाचर मालीके ही पुत्र थे, जो इस समय विभीषणके मन्त्री हैं ॥ ४५ ॥

अनल, अनिल, हर मत्तु सम्पाति हीगॆ नाल्वरु निशाचर मालिय पुत्ररागिद्दरु. इवरु ईग विभीषणन मन्त्रिगळागिद्दारॆ.॥45॥

माल्यवान् आदि तीनों श्रेष्ठ राक्षस अपने सैकड़ों पुत्रों तथा अन्यान्य निशाचरोंके साथ रहकर अपने बाहुबलके अभिमानसे युक्त हो इन्द्र आदि देवताओं, ऋषियों, नागों तथा यक्षोंको पीड़ा देने लगे ॥ ४६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

माल्यवन्त मॊदलाद मूवरू श्रेष्ठ राक्षसरु तम्म नूरारु पुत्ररॊन्दिगॆ हागू इतर निशाचररॊन्दिगॆ इद्दु तम्म बाहुबलद अभिमानदिन्द इन्द्रादि देवतॆगळिगॆ, ऋषिगळिगॆ, नागगळिगॆ, यक्षरिगॆ पीडिसतॊडगिदरु.॥46॥

वे वायुकी भाँति सारे संसारमें विचरनेवाले थे । युद्धमें उन्हें जीतना बहुत ही कठिन था । वे मृत्युके तुल्य तेजस्वी थे । वरदान मिल जानेसे भी उनका घमंड बहुत बढ़ गया था; अतः वे यज्ञादि क्रियाओंका सदा अत्यन्त विनाश किया करते थे ॥ ४७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अवरु गाळियन्तॆ जगत्तिनल्लि विचरिसुत्तिद्दरु. युद्धदल्लि अवरन्नु गॆल्लुवुदु कष्टवागित्तु. मृत्युविनन्तॆ तेजस्विगळाद अवरु वरबलदिन्द गर्वितरागि, यज्ञादि क्रियॆगळन्नु सदा विनाश माडुत्ता इद्दरु.॥47॥

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये उत्तरकाण्डे पञ्चमः सर्गः ॥ ५ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके उत्तरकाण्डमें पाँचवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ ५ ॥
