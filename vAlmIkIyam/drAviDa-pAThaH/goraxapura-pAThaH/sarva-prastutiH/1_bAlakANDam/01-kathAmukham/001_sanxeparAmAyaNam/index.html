Desiraju Hanumanta Rao - Intro

Book I : Bala Kanda - Book Of Youthful Majesties Chapter [Sarga] 1

Divine sage Narada arrives at the hermitage of Sage Valmiki in order to enlighten him and keep him informed of his duty to author the epic poem Ramayana. In the dialogue between these two sages, Valmiki elicits from Narada about most virtuous person on earth, namely Rama. In this opening chapter, while eulogizing Rama Narada gives an outline of Ramayana, truly highlighting those aspects that are the keynotes in this epic, like virtuosity, generosity, morality, chastity and the like.

Valmiki Ramayana is said to have been composed basing on each of the twenty-four letters of Gayatri Hymn, and a thousand verses are arranged into one book under the caption of each letter. Though that classification, or dividing verses into thousand chapters is unavailable now, the twenty-four verses identified with the 24 letters of Gayatri hymn, called as Gayatri Ramayana, is available and it is given in the endnote of this page. In tune with the scheme of Gayatri, Ramayana starts the first verse with letter ta an auspicious letter.

भागसूचना

एवं स्थिते महर्षिणा वाल्मीकिना परम-पुरुषस्य पुण्य-श्लोकस्य चरित-वर्णनं देवर्षि-नारदोपदेशेन ब्रह्मणो वर-प्रदानेन च धर्म-प्राधान्येन वेदोपबृंहकत्वेन परम-पुरुषार्थ-साधकत्वेन च कृतं स्मृति-तुल्यम् इत्य् अविवादम् । तादृश-चरित-वर्णनाय लोकोत्तर-गुणं महा-पुरुषं पृच्छति - तपः-स्वाध्याय-निरतम् इत्य्-आदिना ॥

त्र्यम्बकः - धर्माकूतम्

अत्र नारदस्य चत्वारि विशेषणानि । तत्र तपो-निरत इत्य्-अनेन विशेषणेन सर्व-सामर्थ्यं प्रतिपादितम् । तथा च मनुः -

यद् दुस्तरं यद् दुरापं यद् दुर्गं यच् च दुष्करम् ।
तत् सर्वं तपसा साध्यं तपो हि दुरतिक्रमम् ॥

इति । स्वाध्याय-निरत इत्य्-अनेन यत् किंचिद् वदति तद् वेदोक्त-धर्मानुगुणतयैव वदतीति सूचितम् । वाग्-विदां वरम् इत्य्-अनेन वक्तृत्वं प्रतिपादितम् । मुनि-पुंगवम् इत्य्-अनेन अतीन्द्रिय-अभिज्ञत्वं सूचितम् । अग्रे त्रिलोकज्ञ इत्य्-अनेन विशेषणेन लोक-त्रय-संचारेण प्रत्यक्षतया सद्-असद्-वस्त्व्-अभिज्ञत्वं प्रदर्शितम् । एतैर् विशेषणैः सर्व-गुण-समष्टिमत्वं सर्वज्ञत्वं सर्व-जन-मान्यत्वं सर्वोत्कृष्ट-माहात्म्यं च सूचितम् । एतादृशं नारदं भगवान् वाल्मीकिः सर्व-गुण-समष्टि-रूपं पुरुषम्,

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Vālmīki, an ascetic established in austere penance (tapasvī), reverently questioned Nārada—preeminent among contemplative sages (munipuṅgavam), foremost among masters of speech (vāgvidāṃ varam), and ever absorbed in spiritual austerity and Vedic self-study (tapaḥ-svādhyāya-niratam):

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1.1 (tapaḥ-svādhyāya-niratam)

Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) observes that opening the epic with tapaḥ fulfills the precept of auspicious commencement (maṅgalācaraṇa), since tapaḥ denotes Parabrahman and the initial syllable ta destroys obstacles (takāro vighnanāśakaḥ); moreover, tapas signifies Upaniṣadic inquiry into Brahman (tapasā brahma vijijñāsasva, Taittirīya Up. 3.2), while the pairing of tapaḥsvādhyāyaniratam (Nārada) and tapasvī (Vālmīki) establishes the canonical ācārya-śiṣya-lakṣaṇa. Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) explains tapaḥ as brahmajñāna ripened through contemplation (nididhyāsana) and munipuṅgavam as preeminence in reflection (manana).

धर्मज्ञश्च कृतज्ञश्च सत्यवाक्यो दृढव्रतः ॥ २ ॥

मूलम्

धर्मज्ञश्च कृतज्ञश्च सत्यवाक्यो दृढव्रतः ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

नारद मुनिगळे! ईग लोकदल्लि सकल कल्याणगुणगळिन्द कूडिरुव, वीर्यवन्तनू, धर्मज्ञनू, कृतज्ञनू, सत्यभाषियू, दृढव्रतनिष्ठॆ उळ्ळवनू यारु इरुवनु?॥2॥

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

2. asmin loke = in this, world; saampratam = presently; guNavaan = virtuous person; kaH nu = who is, really; viiryavaan ca = vigorous, also; kaH = who is; dharmaj~naH ca = conscientious, also; kR^itaj~naH ca = one who is mindful of good deeds done to him also ; satyavaakyaH = speaker of truth; dR^iDhavrataH = determined in his deed.

अनुवादः

“Who really is that person in this present world, who is virtuous and vigorous, a conscientious one, one who is mindful of good deeds done to him, and also a speaker of truth and who is determined in his deed… [1-1-2]

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Who indeed in this world at the present time (sāmpratam) is supremely endowed with auspicious virtues (guṇavān), and who possesses heroic valor (vīryavān)? Who is a knower of righteousness (dharmajñaḥ), mindful of even the smallest kindness done to him (kṛtajñaḥ), unfailing in truthful speech (satyavākyaḥ), and unshakeable in his sacred vows (dṛḍhavrataḥ)?

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1.2–4 (The Sixteen Virtues)

Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explicitly points out that Vālmīki’s inquiry across vv. 2–4 enumerates sixteen distinct virtues (pṛṣṭa-ṣoḍaśa-saṅkhyāka-guṇānām): (1) guṇavān, (2) vīryavān, (3) dharmajñaḥ, (4) kṛtajñaḥ, (5) satyavākyaḥ, (6) dṛḍhavrataḥ, (7) cāritreṇa yuktaḥ, (8) sarvabhūteṣu hitaḥ, (9) vidvān, (10) samarthaḥ, (11) ekapriyadarśanaḥ, (12) ātmavān, (13) jitakrodhaḥ, (14) dyutimān, (15) anasūyakaḥ, and (16) jātaroṣasya saṃyuge devāś ca bibhyati—which Nārada answers in v. 8 with sixteen corresponding meanings and epithets of Śrī Rāma.

1.2–4 (kṛtajñaḥ, ekapriyadarśanaḥ, & anasūyakaḥ)

Govindarāja defines kṛtajñaḥ as He who prizes even a slight, incidental service as immense (kṛtam upakāraṃ svalpaṃ prāsaṅgikam api bahutayā jānātīti kṛtajñaḥ) while ignoring a hundred offenses, as both Govindarāja and Śiromaṇi illustrate by citing Ayodhyākāṇḍa 2.1.11 (कथंचिदुपकारेण कृतेनैकेन तुष्यति). On ekapriyadarśanaḥ (v. 3), Tilaka glosses it as uniquely peerless and pleasing to behold, while Govindarāja and Śiromaṇi define asūyā (v. 4) via the Amarakośa as imputing faults even to others’ virtues (असूया तु दोषारोपो गुणेष्वपि).

सदाचारदिन्द युक्तनू, सकल प्राणिगळ हितसाधकनू, सकल विद्यापारङ्गतनाद विद्वांसनू, सर्वकार्य समर्थनू, एकमात्र प्रियदर्शन (मनोहर) पुरुषनु यारु इरुवनु?॥3॥

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

  1. kaH chaaritreNa ca yuktaH = appropriate in disposition; kaH sarvabhuuteSu hitaH = who is interested in welfare of all beings; kaH vidvaan = who is an adept one; samarthaH ca = an able one also; kaH ca ekapriyadarshanaH = who is also uniquely pleasant to look at.

अनुवादः

“Who is appropriate in disposition… who is interested in welfare of all beings… who is adept and also an able one… also uniquely pleasant to look at… [1-1-3]

टिप्पनी

The adeptness of that person is in his knowing all the knowable aspects in this world, and he must be able to retain that knowledge to translate into his deeds, not just to sit back with his bookish knowledge, but with utmost practicality. His conduct-wise character shall be acceptable i.e., by his lineage he shall be noble, by his education he must be well-read, by his actions they must be conducive to norms laid down in Veda-s, and thus given any area, he should conduct himself properly. And he must be benign not only to higher-ups but to lowly subjects, like Guha, Shabari et al., and he should deal with wrongdoers and right-doers conscientiously. Further, he in his mien he shall be pleasant, but not an unsightly one. The word eka also means ‘unique’ and thus his complexion, facial, physical structures etc., shall be unique and shall differ from ordinary beings. kShaNe kShaNe yat navataam upaiti tadeva ruupam ramaNiiyataayaaH ‘which / what / who gets newness moment by moment, that alone is pleasant…’

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Who is adorned with spotless hereditary and personal conduct (cāritreṇa yuktaḥ), and who is the active benefactor of all living beings (sarvabhūteṣu hitaḥ)? Who is omniscient in sacred lore (vidvān), who is capable of accomplishing what none other can achieve (samarthaḥ), and who is uniquely delightful to behold at every moment (ekapriyadarśanaḥ)?

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

कस्य बिभ्यति देवाश्च जातरोषस्य संयुगे ॥ ४ ॥

मूलम्

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘मनपर अधिकार रखनेवाला, क्रोधको जीतनेवाला, कान्तिमान् और किसीकी भी निन्दा नहीं करनेवाला कौन है? तथा संग्राममें कुपित होनेपर किससे देवता भी डरते हैं? ॥ ४ ॥

पदच्छेदः

4. aatmavaan = self-composed; kaH = who is; jitakrodhaH = one with controlled ire; dyutimaan = brilliant one; anasuuyakaH = not jealous; kaH = who is; jaataroSasya = with anger born; kasya = of whom; * saMyuge* = in war; devaaH ca = gods even; bibhyati = are afraid.

अनुवादः

“Who is that self-composed one, who controlled his ire, who is brilliant, non-jealous and whom do even the gods fear, when provoked to war… [1-1-4]

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Who is the master of his inner self (ātmavān), who has conquered wrath (jitakrodhaḥ), who radiates transcendent splendor (dyutimān), and who is wholly free from envy or fault-finding (anasūyakaḥ)? And who is that warrior before whose aroused fury on the field of battle even the celestials tremble in awe?

‘महर्षे! मैं यह सुनना चाहता हूँ, इसके लिये मुझे बड़ी उत्सुकता है और आप ऐसे पुरुषको जाननेमें समर्थ हैं’ ॥ ५ ॥

पदच्छेदः

  1. etat aham shrotum icChaami = all this I wish to listen; me kautuuhalam param hi = my inquisitiveness is immense indeed; maharShe= Oh! Great Sage - Narada; tvam = you; samarthaH asi = are competent; j~naatum = to know; evamvidham naram = [about] this kind of man.

अनुवादः

“All this I wish to listen from you, oh! Great Sage, you are competent to know this kind of man, and indeed my inquisitiveness is immense…” Thus Valmiki enquired with Narada. [1-1-5]

टिप्पनी

Valmiki wanted to know about that man - a man with godly qualities. Valmiki is thinking aloud, about the qualities of his prospective hero of this epic and these are the very opening questions put to an Omniscient Sage Narada. Both the sages know of Rama and his deeds. Even then Valmiki asks Narada, “who is that man with godly qualities?’ If Narada tells that Rama is Vishnu Himself, there is nothing left for Valmiki to compose his epic, because there are numerous mythologies, puraaNa-s, that have already adored God Vishnu. If Narada tells that Rama is so-and-so king, again Valmiki need not attempt to author about some king, however great that king might be.

टिप्पनी

The attributes of the hero of Ramayana, as required by Valmiki, are 16; sixteen in number.*1

  • guNavaan 2 - viryavaan 3 - dharmajnaH 4 - kR^itajnaH 5 - satyavaakyaH 6 - dhR^iDhavrataH 7 - caaritravaan 8 - sarvabhuuteShu hitaH 9 - vidvaan 10 - samarthaH - 11 - priyadarshanaH 12 - aatmavaan 13 - jitakrodhaH 14 - dyutimaan 15 - anasuuyakaH 16 - bibhyatidevaaH* These sixteen attributes are attributed to the sixteen phases of the Full Moon, and Valmiki is about to picture Rama to be as pleasant as a full-moon.
त्र्यम्बकः - धर्माकूतम्

इति पृष्टवान् । तस्य सर्व-गुण-समष्टि-रूपः परम-पुरुषः

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“This I earnestly desire to hear, for a profound eagerness fills my heart. O Great Seer (maharṣe)! You alone are capable of knowing such an extraordinary mortal man (evaṃvidhaṃ naram).”

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1.5–6 (sāmprataṃ loke … naram & trilokajñaḥ)

Govindarāja explains that asmin loke (“in this earthly realm”), sāmpratam (“at the present time”), and naram (“a mortal man”) exclude Lord Viṣṇu in Vaikuṇṭha as well as bygone incarnations such as Nṛsiṃha: Vālmīki seeks the Supreme Person walking the earth today in human form. In v. 6, Govindarāja and Tilaka explain trilokajñaḥ as Nārada’s direct knowledge of all three worlds (bhūr-bhuvaḥ-suvar)—or, as Govindarāja adds, the three classes of sentient souls (baddha, mukta, nityamukta).

Why do the Southern manuscripts and commentators retain both dharmasya parirakṣitā (v. 13d) and rakṣitā svasya dharmasya (v. 14a)? Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) gives a fourfold exegesis: (1) rakṣitā jīvalokasya—He protects all sentient beings from peril; (2) dharmasya parirakṣitā—He safeguards universal Vedic dharma on every side (or prompts others to practice it); (3) rakṣitā svasya dharmasya—He upholds His own personal kṣatriya-dharma and especially His supreme vow of śaraṇāgata-rakṣaṇa (protection of those who seek refuge); and (4) svajanasya ca rakṣitā—He cherishes His intimate kinsmen and devotees.

At times Rama is said to be ’the son of Kausalya…’ than the son of Dasharatha, because the word Kausalya does not signify his mother alone, the daughter of King of Kosala, but it has meanings like kShema, kushala, saamarthya, puNya, nipuNatva ‘safeness, soundness, capability, merit, expertise…’ and this also signifies Queen Kausalya’s worshipping of Vishnu. When dynasty, valour, braveness etc., are said, Rama’s name will be attached with Dasharatha. An ocean is not a bottomless one but an unfathomable one. So also Rama’s heart has a reachable bottom, where pearls, gems, and other treasures of virtues are abounding, and it can be reached with a heartily approach. For a hard-hearted one, Rama’s heart becomes an unfathomable abyss and he will get lost in it. girayoH varShadhaaraabhirharyamaaNaa na vivyadhuH | abhibhuuyamaanaa vyasanaiH yathaa adhokShajacetasaa || ’true devotees do not get hassled though subjected to many problems, for they place their faith in the Supreme. Equally, mountains are unshaken though battered by storms or lightings…’ So, like a mountain he is unalterable and constant for a true devotee. An icy-mountain is meltable by scorching sun, thus Rama also melts down when his adherents are scorched with their problems.

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1.17–18 (viṣṇunā sadṛśo vīrye & Rhetorical Figures)

How can Rāma, who is Viṣṇu Himself, be compared to Viṣṇu? Tilaka, Govindarāja, and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explain two complementary perspectives: (a) from the standpoint of Rāma’s assumed human form (mānuṣopādhibhedāt), Nārada compares the incarnate prince to the all-pervading Viṣṇu; or (b) it is an ananvaya-alaṅkāra (self-comparison, since none other equals Him), woven into a mālopamā with varied case endings (gāmbhīrye, dhairyeṇa, vīrye, krodhe, kṣamayā).

पदच्छेदः

  1. mahaabaahuH = Great armed; mahaabalaH = [and] very powerful [Rama]; prekShya = after seeing; asthi = the bone; *utsmayitvaa

अनुवादः

Great armed and very powerful Rama after seeing the bones of Dundubhi smiled and by the big toe of his foot threw it wholly for ten yojana-s length. [1-1-65]

टिप्पनी

Vali was able to throw that body, with whole of his foot, only up to two hundred bow-lengths, where the length of bow is said as six to seven feet. But Rama could kick that heap to ten yojana lengths, roughly ninety miles, only with a flip of his foot-toe’s tip. But Sugreeva continued his grumbling, ‘in those days this skeleton was with flesh and blood, now it became weight-less, thus show me more of your show of strength…’ One Yojana is an ancient measure for distance, where that distance is covered in one yoking. Chambers dictionary puts it as five miles, and it is not agreeable for it was British-Indian revenue measure. Traditionally it is four krosha-s and thus each yojana is nine to ten miles.

टिप्पनी

Govindaraja cleaves the compound utsmayitvaa to ut smayitvaa and takes ut ’to up…’ and fixes it to cikShepa to mean ut cikShepa ‘up-heaved and hurled…’ and smayitva comes to mean ‘smiling self-assuredly, or smiling self-composedly…’ And Maheshvara Tiirtha says that ‘when all-wise-knowers are not able to know what I am, there is no surprise if a monkey in a remote forest doubts my capability… let him see a speck of it…’ thus Rama smiled in aplomb…’

टिप्पनी

And the often repeated epithet to Rama mahaabaahuH means not just ‘yards and yards of lengthy arms…dangling up to knees..’ but ‘one who does unimaginable deeds…’ and his arms are the unusual instruments to perform such unusual tasks, hence ambidextrous, or omni-dextrous…

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Smiling gently (utsmayitvā) and glancing at that skeleton, the mighty-armed hero of boundless strength flicked it with His great toe (pādāṅguṣṭhena cikṣepa) a full ten yojanas away.

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1.65 (utsmayitvā … pādāṅguṣṭhena cikṣepa)

Govindarāja and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) savor the force of utsmayitvā (“smiling gently”): whereas Vālī had flung Dundubhi’s carcass with his whole foot, Rāma—smiling at the thought “What a trifle is this!” (कियन्मात्रमेतदित्यनादृत्य स्मित्वा)—effortlessly flicked the skeleton a full ten yojanas with the mere tip of His big toe (pādāṅguṣṭhena).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1.66 (tālān saptaikena maheṣuṇā giriṃ rasātalaṃ caiva)

Why was a second test needed after hurling Dundubhi’s skeleton? Govindarāja explains Sugrīva’s lingering doubt: Vālī had hurled the heavy, wet corpse when exhausted by long combat, whereas Rāma flung a dried skeleton while fresh (चिरयुद्धपरिश्रान्तेन वालिना आर्द्रं शरीरं प्रक्षिप्तम्, त्वया तु स्वस्थेन शुष्कम्)! To remove all doubt, Rāma pierced seven trees, the mountain, and the sixth netherworld Rasātala with a single arrow—a feat Śiromaṇi hails as an ananvaya-alaṅkāra (only Rāma’s arrow equals Rāma’s arrow).

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

69: “After reassuring Queen Tārā [who sought to dissuade him] (anumānya tadā tārām), Vālī closed in combat with Sugrīva; and right there Rāghava struck him down with a single arrow (śareṇaikena).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1.68–69 (anumānya tadā tārām & śareṇaikena)

Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) and Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) explain anumānya tadā tārām: when Sugrīva roared outside Kiṣkindhā, Queen Tārā tried to restrain Vālī by warning him of Rāma’s alliance, but Vālī respectfully pacified and dismissed her (anumānya) before coming out to fight. Note also why the Southern recension retains v. 69: without it, the finite clause nijaghāna … śareṇaikena (“struck him down with a single shaft”) is missing before the reprise hatvā in v. 70!

1.69–70 (sugrīvavacanād dhatvā vālinam)

Why does v. 70a state that Rāma slew Vālī “at Sugrīva’s word” (sugrīvavacanāt) when Rāma had already pledged Vālī’s death in v. 62c? Tilaka and Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explain that sugrīvavacanāt (citing Sugrīva’s supplication वालिनं जहि काकुत्स्थ मया बद्धोऽयमञ्जलिः) shows that Rāma acted solely out of compassion to redeem His pledge to a surrendered refugee (śaraṇāgata-vatsalatvam) and not from personal hostility, while Govindarāja notes that Rāma waited for Sugrīva’s distress signal in battle before releasing His shaft.

94b, 95a. tathaa = like-wise; mahaayashaaH = highly illustrious one [Rama]; iSTvaa = having performed; ashvamedhashataiH = hundreds of Horse Rituals; dattvaa = [and] having donated; bahusuvarNakaiH = plentiful gold; gavaam koTyayutam = [and] crores of cows; dhanam = [and] wealth; asankhyeyam = [that] cannot be counted; braahmaNebhyaH = to Brahmanas; vidvadbhyaH = [and] to scholars; vidhipuurvakam = according to custom.

अनुवादः

“Like-wise the highly illustrious Rama having performed hundreds of horse rituals and having donated plentiful gold and crores of cows and wealth that cannot be counted to Brahmanas and to scholars according to custom… [1-1-94b, 95a]

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

96. raaghavaH = Raghava; chaaturvarNyam niyokShyati = will direct the four caste-system; sve sve dharme = to their respective duties; sthaapayiShyati ca = and will establish; raajavamshaan = royal dynasties; asmin loke = in this world; shataguNaan = in hundred folds.

अनुवादः

“In this world Raghava will establish kingly dynasties in hundredfold and he will be maintaining the four-caste system positing each in his own duty, may it be caste-bound or provincial-kingdom-bound duty, in order to achieve a perfect social harmony… [1-1-96]

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

94: “All people rejoice perpetually, just as in the golden age of Kṛtayuga (yathā kṛtayuge tathā). Having worshiped the Lord of sacrifices with hundreds of horse-sacrifices (aśvamedhaśatair iṣṭvā) and manifold rites abounding in gold (bahusuvarṇakaiḥ)—

95: “—having duly bestowed ten thousand crores of cows upon learned scholars according to scriptural injunction (gavāṃ koṭyayutaṃ dattvā vidvadbhyo vidhipūrvakam), † and having given immeasurable wealth to the brāhmaṇas, that monarch of supreme renown—

96: “—Rāghava shall establish royal dynasties multiplied a hundredfold (rājavaṃśāñ śataguṇān sthāpayiṣyati), and in this world He shall establish the fourfold social order (cāturvarṇyam), each in its own righteous vocation (sve sve dharme niyokṣyati).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1.94 (aśvamedhaśatair iṣṭvā tathā bahusuvarṇakaiḥ)

Govindarāja, Tilaka, and Śiromaṇi explain that śataiḥ denotes numerous Aśvamedha sacrifices, while bahusuvarṇakaiḥ denotes soma-sacrifices in which abundant gold is distributed as ritual dakṣiṇā (सुबहूनि सुवर्णानि यत्रोपकरणत्वतः । विन्दन्ति स क्रतुः सद्भिः स्मृतो बहुसुवर्णकः).

1.95 (gavāṃ koṭyayutaṃ dattvā vidvadbhyo)

Govindarāja and Śiromaṇi note the scale of Rāma’s Vedic munificence: koṭyayutam is ten thousand koṭis (daśasahasrakoṭiparimitāḥ gāḥ) of cows bestowed upon learned scholars (vidvadbhyaḥ) according to śāstric ordinance (vidhipūrvakam), followed by immeasurable wealth (asaṅkhyeyaṃ dhanam) given to other brāhmaṇas.

1.96 (rājavaṃśāñ śataguṇān sthāpayiṣyati)

Govindarāja, Tilaka, and Śiromaṇi explain that Rāma shall multiply the royal dynasties a hundredfold in prosperity and continuity by bestowing enduring kingdoms across the earth, while establishing all four social orders (cāturvarṇyam) in their respective Vedic svadharma.

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Eleven-thousand-year reign of Śrī Rāma; His return to Brahmaloka; and the Phalaśruti (sacred fruits of reciting the Veda-equal Rāmāyaṇa).

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘फिर ग्यारह हजार वर्षोंतक राज्य करनेके अनन्तर श्रीरामचन्द्रजी अपने परमधामको पधारेंगे ॥ ९७ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

मत्तॆ हन्नॊन्दु साविर वर्षगळवरॆगॆ राज्यवाळिद बळिक श्रीरामचन्द्रनु तन्न परमधामक्कॆ तॆरळुवनु.॥97॥

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

97. raamaraajyam upaasitvaa = Rama having served the kingdom dashavarSasahasraaNi = for ten thousand years; dashavarShashataani ca = and also for thousand years; brahmalokam gamiShyati = will go to the abode of Brahma.

अनुवादः

“Having served the kingdom for ten thousand years and another one thousand years, i.e. for a total of eleven thousand years, Rama will go to the abode of Brahma… [1-1-97]

टिप्पनी

In raamaraajyam upaasitvaa… the word used is upaasana is not ruling by sceptre but it is ‘reverentially serving the kingdom…’ as one would regard or treat his personal god with reverence. Rama thus served his kingdom as a devotee of his subjects and this is the concept of raamaraajya. The brahma loka is not the abode of four-faced Brahma, but still higher abode, rather VaikunTha itself.

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Having tended the kingdom for ten thousand years and a thousand years more [eleven thousand years in all] (daśavarṣasahasrāṇi daśavarṣaśatāni ca rāmo rājyam upāsitvā), Rāma shall depart for the supreme realm of Brahman (brahmalokaṃ prayāsyati).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1.97 (rājyam upāsitvā & brahmalokaṃ prayāsyati)

Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) and Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) draw attention to the verb upāsitvā (“having tended/reverently served the kingdom”): Govindarāja notes that Rāma ruled for eleven thousand years by gently conciliating His subjects without selfish attachment (rājyapālane vyāsaṅgābhāvo darśitaḥ), and then departed for brahmalokam—His own transcendent abode of Vaikuṇṭha (ब्रह्मणः स्वस्य लोकं अप्राकृतस्थानं परमपदं वैकुण्ठम्), or as Śivasahāya (Śiromaṇi) explains, the realm non-different from Brahman.

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘वेदोंके समान पवित्र, पापनाशक और पुण्यमय इस रामचरितको जो पढ़ेगा, वह सब पापोंसे मुक्त हो जायगा ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

वेदगळिगॆ समानवाद, पवित्र, पापनाशक मत्तु पुण्यमय ई रामचरितवन्नु ओदुववनु ऎल्ल पापगळिन्द मुक्तनागि होगुवनु.॥98॥

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

98. idam = this; raamacharitam = Rama’s, legend; pavitram = is holy; paapaghnam = sin-eradicating; puNyam = merit-endowing; vedaiH sammitam ca = and consisting of [teachings of] Vedas; yaH = whoever; paThet = studies [this Ramayana]; sarvapaapaiH pramuchyate = is truly liberated of all sins.

अनुवादः

“This Ramayana is holy, sin-eradicating, merit-endowing, and conformable with the teachings of Vedas. Whoever reads this Legend of Rama, will be verily liberated of all sins… [1-1-98]

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“This sacred narrative of Rāma’s deeds (rāmacaritam)—all-purifying (pavitram), destroyer of sins (pāpaghnam), auspiciously merit-bestowing (puṇyam), and equal in authority to the Vedas themselves (vedaiś ca sammitam)—whoever recites it is liberated from all sins (sarvapāpaiḥ pramucyate).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1.98 (pavitraṃ pāpaghnaṃ puṇyaṃ vedaiś ca sammitam)

Govindarāja and Tilaka distinguish the three adjectives: (1) pavitram = instrument of inner purification (pariśuddhisādhanam / cittaśodhanam); (2) pāpaghnam = destroyer of sins; and (3) puṇyam = generator of positive merit (puṇyasādhanam), which unlike ordinary expiations (prāyaścitta) both removes sin and bestows supreme merit because it is equal to all the Vedas (vedaiś ca sammitam, citing the Rāmopaniṣad).

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

99. etat = this; raamaayaNam = Rama’s, peregrination; aayuSyam = is long-life giving; aakhyaanam = a narrative; paThan = [by] reading [which]; naraH = a human; saputrapautraH = [will be] with sons and grandsons; on enjoying worldly comforts; sagaNaH = with attendants; pretya = [and] after demise; svarge mahiiyate = will be adored in heaven.

अनुवादः

“This Ramayana is long-life giving. A narrative by reading which a human will be blessed with sons and grandsons, and after enjoying worldly comforts with attendants, will be adored in heaven after demise… [1-1-99]

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“The mortal who recites this life-bestowing narrative of the Rāmāyaṇa (etad ākhyānam āyuṣyaṃ paṭhan rāmāyaṇaṃ naraḥ) is exalted in the highest heaven after death, together with his sons, grandsons, and attendants (saputrapautraḥ sagaṇaḥ pretya svarge mahīyate).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

1.99–100 (Universal Adhikāra, Gāyatrī Seal, & Upajāti Finale)

Govindarāja, Tilaka, and Śiromaṇi emphasize the universal grace of vv. 99–100 across all four varṇas (Tilaka supplying śṛṇvan “hearing” for the fourth order in v. 100d). Remarkably, Tilaka observes that to signify how the Rāmāyaṇa expounds the meaning of the Gāyatrī, Vālmīki opens Sarga 1 with the first syllable of the Gāyatrī (त in तपःस्वाध्यायनिरतं) and closes verse 100 with its final syllable (यात् in महत्त्वमीयात्: गायत्र्यर्थप्रतिपादकत्वध्वननाय गायत्र्यादिमाक्षरेणोपक्रम्य यादिति गायत्र्यन्तिमाक्षरेण समापितवान्), sealing the Sarga in 11-syllable Upajāti meter.

  1. paThan = By reading [this Ramayana]; janaH = [that] person; dvijaH syaat = if a Brahmana; vaagR^iShabhatvam iiyaat = obtains excellency in speech; kShatriyaH [syaat] = if Kshatriya; bhuumipatitvam iiyaat = obtains land-lordship; vaNigjanaH = if Vysya; paNyaphalatvam iiyaat = attains profit in trade; shuudraH api ca = if even a Sudra; mahatvam iiyaat = attains excellence.

“Reciting this epic, a twice-born brāhmaṇa attains preeminence in sacred eloquence (vāgṛṣabhatvam īyāt); if he be a kṣatriya, he attains sovereignty over the earth (bhūmipatitvam īyāt); a vaiśya merchant attains the fruitfulness of his enterprises (paṇyaphalatvam īyāt); and a śūdra too attains spiritual greatness (mahattvam īyāt)!”
