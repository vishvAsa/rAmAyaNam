<details><summary>Desiraju Hanumanta Rao - Intro</summary>

**Chapter [Sarga] 58**

Trishanku seeks shelter of Vishvamitra when the sons of Vashishta rebuffed him, and even cursed him
to become a profaner. Narrating his sad tale and his idealistic idea of going to heaven with mortal
body, Trishanku earnestly appeals to Vishvamitra.
</details>

<details><summary>Desiraju Hanumanta Rao</summary>

**पदच्छेदः**

*1. raama* = oh Rama; *trishankoH vacanam shrutvaa* = on hearing Trishanku's sentence;
*tataH* = then; *R^iSiputrashatam* = hundred sons of sage; *krodhasamanvitam*
= infuriated; *raajaanam idam abraviit* = said this to king.

**अनुवादः**

"On hearing the sentence of Trishanku, oh, Rama, the hundred sons of sage Vashishta infuriately said
this to that king." Thus, sage Shataananda continued his narration to Rama and others. [1-58-1]
</details>

<details><summary>Desiraju Hanumanta Rao</summary>

**पदच्छेदः**

*2. durbuddhe* = oh slow witted one [king]; *satyavaadinaa guruNaa pratyaakhyaataH asi*
= you are denounced by mentor [Vashishta] the advocator of truth; *tam* = him;
*samatikramya* = verily over stepping - transgressing; *shaakhaantaram* = another branch
; *katham* = how; *upeyivaan* = come nigh of - approaching.

**अनुवादः**

'You slow-witted king, your veracious mentor has denounced you. But transgressing him how do you approach
another branch of the same tree of wisdom, when that very tree rejected you?' So the sons of Vashishta
have started to denounce Trishanku. [1-58-2]
</details>


<details><summary>English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**1:** Then, O Rāma, upon hearing the words of Triśaṅku, the hundred sons of the sage (_ṛṣiputraśatam_), filled with wrath (_krodhasamanvitam_), spoke thus to the king:

**2:** “You have been refused, O foolish-minded one (_durmedho_), by your truth-speaking preceptor (_guruṇā satyavādinā_)! How is it that, transgressing him (_taṃ samatikramya_), you have resorted to another branch (_śākhāntaram upeyivān_)?
</details>


<details><summary>टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**58.1–2 (krodhasamanvitam & śākhāntaram)**

**Rāma Varmā (_Tilaka_)**, **Govindarāja (_Bhūṣaṇa_)**, and **Śiromaṇi** construe _krodhasamanvitam_ as qualifying _ṛṣiputraśatam_ (the hundred sons filled with anger at Triśaṅku’s request), while **Govindarāja** notes that this Sarga shows the bitter fruit suffered by those who forsake their guru (_gurutyāgināṃ phalaṃ darśayitum_). In v. 2, **Tilaka** glosses _śākhāntaram_ as another protector through sacrificial officiating (_yājanādinā rakṣakāntaram_), and **Govindarāja** and **Śiromaṇi** gloss it as another refuge (_āśrayāntaram_)—likening one who leaves his root preceptor for his sons to a man forsaking a tree’s trunk for a side branch.
</details>
</details>

**अनुवादः**

<details><summary>English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

<details><summary>टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**58.3–4 (purodhāḥ paramā gatiḥ & sovāca)**

Showing the impropriety of bypassing Vasiṣṭha (_tadatikramasyānaucityam_, **Śiromaṇi**), the sons declare that for all Ikṣvākus the royal chaplain is the supreme refuge (_paramā gatiḥ_, SR/**Tilaka**; _paramo guruḥ_, **Śiromaṇi**). On _sovāca_ in v. 4a, **Tilaka** defends the _visarga_-elision via Pāṇini 6.1.134 (_‘so ’ci lope cet’ iti sādhu_).
</details>
</details>

<details><summary>Desiraju Hanumanta Rao</summary>

**पदच्छेदः**

*5, 6a. narashreSTha* = the best for men [here, king of subjects]; *tvam baalishaH*
= you are, childish; *punaH svapuram gamyataam* = again, get back [to your] own city; *
paarthiva* = oh king; *bhagavaan* = godlike [Vashishta is]; *trailokyasya api* =
by [kings] of three worlds; *yaajane shaktaH* = is capable in orchestration of ritual; *[tat*
= therefore]; *tasya* = his [to reputation]; *avamaanam* = not reputing [to disrepute];
*kartum* = to do; *vayam katham shakShaayaamahe* = how we are capable of.

**अनुवादः**

'Oh king of subjects, better get back to your own city again for you are childish. That godlike Vashishta
alone is capable to orchestrate any ritual by any king in all the three worlds. Oh, king, how then we
are capable to cause a disrepute to his reputation.' Thus, the sons of Vashishta refuted Trishanku.
[1-58-5, 6a]
</details>


<details><summary>English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

“You are childishly ignorant (_bāliśas tvam_), O best of men! Return again to your own city (_gamyatāṃ svapuraṃ punaḥ_). The revered sage, O lord of earth, is capable of officiating at sacrifices even for all three worlds (_yājane bhagavān śaktas trailokyasyāpi_).”
</details>


<details><summary>टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**58.5 (bāliśaḥ & trailokyasyāpi yājane)**

**Tilaka** glosses _bāliśaḥ_ as ignorant (_ajñaḥ_), **Govindarāja** glosses it as _mūrkhaḥ_ citing _Amarakośa_ (_‘mūḍhajālmabāliśāḥ’ ity amaraḥ_), and **Śiromaṇi** explains it as lacking sound judgment (_samīcīnavicārayogyamatyabhāvavān_). Since Vasiṣṭha can officiate even for the three worlds (_trailokyasyāpi siddhyarthaṃ yājane_, **Tilaka**), his refusal proves the rite is intrinsically impossible.
</details>
</details>

**पदच्छेदः**

**अनुवादः**

[1-58-8b, 9a]

**टिप्पनी**

This expression *caNDaal* has its own impact. The nomenclature of *caNDaal* is not the
present day 'untouchable' 'outcaste' or a 'pariah' etc., because he is also a member and an important
functionary in Hindu system of castes. In Aranya Kanda Seetha admonishes Ravana with the same wording
at 3-56-18. The analogy she uses there is to differentiate between functionaries like the Vedic-scholar
and the caste-bound Profaner [meaning: one who is not initiated into religious rites or any esoteric
knowledge... but socially designated as a Cremator] and between the Altar of Fire and Fire of Pyre,
and between the Vedic Ritual and Funeral Rites, both involving Vedic-hymns. A Vedic scholar, or a true
Brahmin may perhaps conduct a *yaj~na* but he is no authority to conduct *antyakriya*
'funerals' till its end. At best, a priest's function is until the lighting of the pyre, and even perhaps
until *kapaalamoksha* 'breakage of cranium...' and from then on it is the function of this
*caNDaal* to take care of the corpse consigned to fire and for its complete cremation, since
leaving half burnt corpse to vultures and wolves is sinning as well as for unsanitary situations. Thus,
if a Vedic scholar conducts *jaataadi kriyaaH* 'rites from birth onwards...' as an initiator,
then a *caNDaal* conducts *antya, uttarakriyaaH* 'rites of death afterwards...' as a
terminator. This verbiage is to demark the relative values of functionaries, according to the maxim
'a donkey is to bear and a dog is to bark...' and if their functions are reversed, reversed are our
results.
</details>

<details><summary>टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**58.20 (kṛcchreṣv api gataḥ & kṣatradharmeṇa te śape)**

Even in dire calamities (_āpadgato ’pi_), Triśaṅku vows never to speak falsely, swearing with his Kṣatriya code as witness (_kṣatradharmeṇa tatsākṣitayā_, **Tilaka**, **Govindarāja**).

_____________

**58.21–22 (daivam eva param vs. pauruṣaṃ tu nirarthakam)**

Though Triśaṅku has gratified his elders with noble conduct (_śīlaṃ sadguṇaḥ vṛttam uttamācāraḥ_, **Tilaka**; _sadācāraḥ_, **Govindarāja**), his preceptors were not pleased (_na gacchanti na gacchanti sma_, past sense, **Tilaka**). Hence he concludes that _daivam_—unseen fructifying past karma (_prārabdhaphalam adṛṣṭam_, **Tilaka**, **Govindarāja**)—is supreme in bringing weal and woe, whereas _pauruṣam_—present righteous exertion (_idānīntanadharmānuṣṭhānam_)—is powerless to override past destiny (_prārabdhakarmatiraskārānarham_).
</details>

<details><summary>टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**58.24 (daivaṃ puruṣakāreṇa nivartayitum arhasi)**

Why does Triśaṅku call _pauruṣam_ futile in v. 22 yet ask Viśvāmitra to reverse _daivam_ through _puruṣakāra_ here? While ordinary human effort cannot overcome adverse fate, Viśvāmitra’s sovereign ascetic heroism (_svapauruṣeṇa_, **Śiromaṇi**; _svaprasādarūpānukūlyakaraṇena_, **Govindarāja**) can turn back destiny itself.
</details>
