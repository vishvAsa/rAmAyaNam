Book I : Bala Kanda - The Youthful Majesties

Vishvamitra narrates the legend of Ahalya to Rama and Lakshmana when they arrive at the precincts of Mithila, bidding farewell to the King of Vishaala. Indra is infatuated with Ahalya desires a union with her, for which she complies. However, Gautama catching them unawares curses both Indra and Ahalya.

भागसूचना
  1. राजा सुमतिसे सत्कृत हो एक रात विशालामें रहकर मुनियोंसहित श्रीरामका मिथिलापुरीमें पहुँचना और वहाँ सूने आश्रमके विषयमें पूछनेपर विश्वामित्रजीका उनसे अहल्याको शाप प्राप्त होनेकी कथा सुनाना
सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Marveling at the divine beauty and martial bearing of the two young princes, King Sumati asks Viśvāmitra whose sons they are and why they have traveled on foot along this rugged path.

पृष्ट्वा तु कुशलं तत्र परस्परसमागमे ।
कथान्ते सुमतिर्वाक्यं व्याजहार महामुनिम् ॥ १ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

पृष्ट्वा तु कुशलं तत्र परस्परसमागमे ।
कथान्ते सुमतिर्वाक्यं व्याजहार महामुनिम् ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

वहाँ परस्पर समागमके समय एक-दूसरेका कुशल-मंगल पूछकर बातचीतके अन्तमें राजा सुमतिने महामुनि विश्वामित्रसे कहा— ॥ १ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

अल्लि परस्पर समागमदल्लि ऒब्बरु मत्तॊब्बर क्षेम-समाचार केळि मातुकतॆयाडि, कॊनॆयल्लि सुमतियु महामुनि विश्वामित्ररल्लि हेळिदनु.॥1॥

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

1. tatra = there; parasparasamaagame = on meeting one with the other Sumati with Vishvamitra; sumatiH kushalam pR^iSTvaa = king Sumati, having asked well-being; kathaante = end of episode [at end of the exchanging greetings]; mahaamunim vaakyam vyaajahaara = with great-saint, words, started - raised the topic of Rama and Lakshmana.

अनुवादः

When those two, Vishvamitra and Sumati, have met one another there at that place, king Sumati raised the topic of Rama and Lakshmana, after exchanging greetings and enquiring about the well-being of the great-saint Vishvamitra. [1-48-1]

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Having inquired after one another’s welfare there at their mutual meeting (pṛṣṭvā tu kuśalaṃ tatra parasparasamāgame), at the close of their converse King Sumati addressed these words to the great sage (kathānte sumatir vākyaṃ vyājahāra mahāmunim):

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

48.1–2 (bhadraṃ te & gajasiṃhagatī śārdūlavṛṣabhopamau)

Why does Sumati interject bhadraṃ te while praising the princes? Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explains that it wards off the evil eye (dṛṣṭidoṣo mā bhūd ity āha) and discerns four distinct gaits (caturgatitvam): majestic like an elephant (gāmbhīryagamane gajatulyau), irresistible in attack like a lion (parābhibhavanābhigamane siṃhatulyau), terrifying to foes like a tiger (bhayaṅkaragamane śārdūlatulyau), and proud like a bull (sagarvagamane vṛṣabhasadṛśau). Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) alternatively distributes them: Rāma has the elephant’s gait and tiger’s frame, Lakṣmaṇa the lion’s gait and bull’s build.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

इमौ कुमारौ भद्रं ते देवतुल्यपराक्रमौ ।
गजसिंहगती वीरौ शार्दूलवृषभोपमौ ॥ २ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

इमौ कुमारौ भद्रं ते देवतुल्यपराक्रमौ ।
गजसिंहगती वीरौ शार्दूलवृषभोपमौ ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘ब्रह्मन्! आपका कल्याण हो । ये दोनों कुमार देवताओंके तुल्य पराक्रमी जान पड़ते हैं । इनकी चाल-ढाल हाथी और सिंहकी गतिके समान है । ये दोनों वीर सिंह और साँड़के समान प्रतीत होते हैं ॥ २ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

‘ब्रह्मर्षिगळे! निमगॆ मङ्गळवागलि’. इवरिब्बरु राजकुमाररु देवतॆगळन्तॆ पराक्रमिगळु ऎन्दु तोरुत्तारॆ. इवर नडिगॆ गज मत्तु सिंहदन्तॆ गम्भीरवागिदॆ. इवरीर्वरु वीररु सिंह मत्तु गूळियन्तॆ कण्डु बरुत्तिरुवरु.॥2॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“These two young princes—may auspiciousness attend you (imau kumārau bhadraṃ te)—equal the gods in valor (devatulyaparākramau); heroic, moving with the gait of an elephant and a lion, they resemble a tiger and a bull (gajasiṃhagatī vīrau śārdūlavṛṣabhopamau).

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

पद्मपत्रविशालाक्षौ खड्गतूणधनुर्धरौ ।
अश्विनाविव रूपेण समुपस्थितयौवनौ ॥ ३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

पद्मपत्रविशालाक्षौ खड्गतूणधनुर्धरौ ।
अश्विनाविव रूपेण समुपस्थितयौवनौ ॥ ३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

इनके बड़े-बड़े नेत्र विकसित कमलदलके समान शोभा पाते हैं । ये दोनों तलवार, तरकस और धनुषधारण किये हुए हैं । अपने सुन्दर रूपके द्वारा दोनों अश्विनीकुमारोंको लज्जित करते हैं तथा युवावस्थाके निकट आ पहुँचे हैं ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

इवर विशालकण्णुगळु कमलदळगळन्तॆ शोभिसुत्तिवॆ. इब्बरू खड्ग, धनुष्य, बत्तळिकॆगळन्नु धरिसिरुवरु. तम्म रूप सौन्दर्यदिन्द अश्विनिदेवतॆगळन्नु नाचिसुत्ता, यौवनद हॊस्तिलिनल्लि अडियन्निट्टिरुवरु.॥3॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“With eyes wide as lotus petals, bearing swords, quivers, and bows (padmapatraviśālākṣau khaḍgatūṇidhanurdharau), they are like the twin Aśvins in beauty and have just reached the bloom of youth (aśvināv iva rūpeṇa samupasthitayauvanau).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

48.3–4 (padmapatraviśālākṣau & kathaṃ padbhyām iha prāptau)

Govindarāja remarks that large lotus-petal eyes mark the pinnacle of beauty (nayanasaudaryaṃ hi lāvaṇyaparamakāṣṭhā), while Śiromaṇi derives dhanurdharau with the pacādyac suffix (Pāṇini 3.1.134) rather than karmaṇy aṇ. Seeing their tender feet—soft as atasī blossoms (Govindarāja)—Sumati wonders how two youths resembling the twin Aśvins descended from heaven (devalokād ivāmarau) could have walked here on foot.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

यदृच्छयैव गां प्राप्तौ देवलोकादिवामरौ ।
कथं पद‍्भ्यामिह प्राप्तौ किमर्थं कस्य वा मुने ॥ ४ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

यदृच्छयैव गां प्राप्तौ देवलोकादिवामरौ ।
कथं पद‍्भ्यामिह प्राप्तौ किमर्थं कस्य वा मुने ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘इन्हें देखकर ऐसा जान पड़ता है, मानो दो देवकुमार दैवेच्छावश देवलोकसे पृथ्वीपर आ गये हों । मुने! ये दोनों किसके पुत्र हैं और कैसे, किसलिये यहाँ पैदल ही आये हैं? ॥ ४ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

इवरन्नु नोडिदरॆ ई इब्बरु देवकुमाररु वेषमरॆसि, देवलोकदिन्द पृथ्विगॆ बन्दन्तॆ अनिसुत्तदॆ. मुनिये! इवरिब्बरु यार पुत्ररु? यावुदक्कागि इल्लिगॆ काल्नडिगॆयल्लि बन्दिद्दारॆ.॥4॥

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

  1. mune = oh saint; te bhadram = you, be safe; devatulyaparaakramau = matching with god Vishnu in valour; gajasimhagatii = with striding [like audacious] elephant, [like arrogant] lion; viirau = two valrous ones; shaarduulavR^iSabhopamau= in similitude with [adventurous] tiger, [adamantine] Holy Bull; padmapatravishaalaakSau = broad eyed like lotus petal ; khaDgatuuNiidhanurdharau = wielding sword, quiver and bow; ruupeNa ashvinau iva = like the Twin-god brothers Ashwin by physique; samupasthitayauvanau = in the offing youthfulness; devalokaat = from gods’ abode; yadR^icChayaa iva = as if at their pleasure; gaam praaptau = chanced on earth; amarau = not dying ones [deathless, immortals]; iva = as if like; imau kumaarau = these, youngsters; iha = here, to this place; padbhyaam = with two feet - by footslogging; katham = how; kim artham = what, for; praaptau = how, they chanced; kasya vaa = whose [scions,] are they.

अनुवादः

“These two youngsters, oh, sage, let safety betide you, striding like audacious elephants and arrogant lions, bearing a similitude to adventurous tigers and adamantine bulls, and wielding quivers, swords, and bows, are valorous with their valour matching that of Vishnu, and with their eyes broad like lotus-petals and with youthfulness in the offing they look like the exceptionally beautiful twin-gods, Ashwin-brothers, by their physique. How these two have footslogged and chanced here as though immortals have chanced on earth from the abode of gods at their pleasure? What for they have come here, and whose scions are they? [1-48-2, 3, 4]

टिप्पनी

The word deva can easily be said as a ‘god’ but, this is said to be Vishnu, as implied at 4-43-56 of Kishkindha ‘There the Cosmic-Souled God, namely Vishnu…’ etc. Now the enquirer himself is no less than a godly king, hence he identified the boys as non-terrestrial. No fatherly person accepts youngsters to footslog miles and miles, and so far this is an unobserved and an uncared for aspect by other sages and saints, because all of those sages and saints are footsloggers. This may be one of the reasons for Dasharatha in refusing to send Rama with Vishvamitra. However, King Sumati, being a glorious and fatherly king brought up this topic. Vishvamitra is giving a ‘rehearsal’ for these brothers for their real forest trekking later in the legend. Further, if a divine deed is to be done and a benefit therefrom is to be acquired, one has to footslog. Now Rama is going to perform a divine feat called ‘bending the bow of Shiva’ and thereby winning the hand of an unusual princess Seetha, in marriage. Hence, he footslogged this much distance for siitaakalyaaNaartham, lokakalyaaNaartham Seetha’s marriage, which is for the ‘universal goodness.’

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Like two immortals descended to earth from the world of the gods of their own accord (yadṛcchayaiva gāṃ prāptau devalokād ivāmarau), how have they come here on foot, for what purpose, and whose sons are they, O Sage (kathaṃ padbhyām iha prāptau kimarthaṃ kasya vā mune)?

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

भूषयन्ताविमं देशं चन्द्रसूर्याविवाम्बरम् ।
परस्परेण सदृशौ प्रमाणेङ्गितचेष्टितैः ॥ ५ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

भूषयन्ताविमं देशं चन्द्रसूर्याविवाम्बरम् ।
परस्परेण सदृशौ प्रमाणेङ्गितचेष्टितैः ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘जैसे चन्द्रमा और सूर्य आकाशकी शोभा बढ़ाते हैं, उसी प्रकार ये दोनों कुमार इस देशको सुशोभित कर रहे हैं । शरीरकी ऊँचाई, मनोभावसूचक संकेत तथा चेष्टा (बोलचाल) में ये दोनों एक-दूसरेके समान हैं ॥ ५ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

चन्द्र-सूर्यरु आकाशद शोभॆयन्नु हॆच्चिसुत्तिरुवन्तॆ, ई इब्बरु कुमाररु ई देशवन्नु सुशोभित गॊळिसुत्तिद्दारॆ. देहद ऎत्तर, मनोभाव सूचक सङ्केत हागू नडॆनुडिगळल्लि ऒब्बरु मत्तॊब्बरिगॆ समवागिद्दारॆ.॥5॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Adorning this land as the moon and the sun adorn the firmament (bhūṣayantāv imaṃ deśaṃ candrasūryāv ivāmbaram), they resemble one another in stature, inner expression, and outward demeanor (paraspareṇa sadṛśau pramāṇeṅgitaceṣṭitaiḥ).

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

48.5–6 (paraspareṇa sadṛśau — Mutual Incomparability)

Why are Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa compared to each other (paraspareṇa sadṛśau) in stature (pramāṇa), inner expression (iṅgita), and outward demeanor (ceṣṭita)? All three commentators observe that Vālmīki thereby indicates upamānāntarābhāva (tādṛśapuruṣāntarābhāvāt parasparasadṛśau, Tilaka, Govindarāja, Śiromaṇi)—nothing in creation can serve as a standard of comparison for either brother except the other.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

किमर्थं च नरश्रेष्ठौ सम्प्राप्तौ दुर्गमे पथि ।
वरायुधधरौ वीरौ श्रोतुमिच्छामि तत्त्वतः ॥ ६ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

किमर्थं च नरश्रेष्ठौ सम्प्राप्तौ दुर्गमे पथि ।
वरायुधधरौ वीरौ श्रोतुमिच्छामि तत्त्वतः ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘श्रेष्ठ आयुध धारण करनेवाले ये दोनों नरश्रेष्ठ वीर इस दुर्गम मार्गमें किसलिये आये हैं? यह मैं यथार्थरूपसे सुनना चाहता हूँ’ ॥ ६ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

श्रेष्ठ आयुधधारियाद इवरिब्बरु नरश्रेष्ठ वीररु ई दुर्गम मार्गदिन्द एकॆ बन्दिरुवरु? इदन्नु नानु यथार्थवागि तिळियलु बयसुत्तिरुवॆनु.॥6॥

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

  1. narashreSThau = two best ones among men - Rama, Lakshmana; pramaaNengitaceSTitaiH = by bodily proportions, [and] facial-language, [and] gestures; paraspareNa sadR^ishau = identical to one another; candrasuuryau ambaram iva = Moon, Sun, [decorate the] firmament, as with; imam desham bhuuSayantau = they are refurbishing this province; varaayudhadharau = wielders of best weapons; such a; viirau = valiant ones; durgame pathi = on an non passable route - an arduous path; * kim artham sampraaptau* = for what, reason, they have chanced here; tattvataH shrotum icChaami = I wish to listen - know in truth.

अनुवादः

“These two best ones among men are identical to one another by their bodily proportion, facial-language, and by bodily gestures, and with their presence they refurbish this province like the Moon and Sun brightening the firmament. In truth, I would like to know for what reason these valiant ones who are wielding best weapons have arrived here travelling on an arduous path.” Thus, king Sumati asked Vishvamitra. [1-48-5, 6]

टिप्पनी

The word facial-language translated for ingita may be an extended expression for the body language. This word is for the voice muted facial expressions, given through smiles, eyebrows, lip movement etc. A person could be analysed by these very expressions as we presently call this as face reading. This is what Sugreeva says when sending Hanuma to Rama and Lakshmana for the first time, ‘because you are an expert in reading faces, know them by their facial expressions…’

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“And for what purpose have these two best of men, heroic bearers ofexcellent weapons, arrived along this rugged path (kimarthaṃ ca naraśreṣṭhau samprāptau durgame pathi varāyudhadharau vīrau)? I wish to hear this in truth (śrotum icchāmi tattvataḥ).”

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

Honored by King Sumati after Viśvāmitra recounts their deeds at Siddhāśrama, the princes spend the night at Viśālā and reach the outskirts of Mithilā, where Rāma beholds a deserted ancient hermitage.

विश्वास-प्रस्तुतिः

तस्य तद् वचनं श्रुत्वा यथावृत्तं न्यवेदयत् ।
सिद्धाश्रमनिवासं च राक्षसानां वधं यथा ।
विश्वामित्रवचः श्रुत्वा राजा परमविस्मितः ॥ ७ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

तस्य तद् वचनं श्रुत्वा यथावृत्तं न्यवेदयत् ।
सिद्धाश्रमनिवासं च राक्षसानां वधं यथा ।
विश्वामित्रवचः श्रुत्वा राजा परमविस्मितः ॥ ७ ॥

मूलम् - VR

तस्य तद्वचनं श्रुत्वा यथावृत्तं न्यवेदयत् । सिद्धाश्रमनिवासं च राक्षसानां वधं तथा । १-४८-७

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

इह वर्षसहस्राणि बहूनि निवसिष्यसि ॥ २९ ॥
वातभक्षा निराहारा तप्यन्ती भस्मशायिनी ।
अदृश्या सर्वभूतानामाश्रमेऽस्मिन् वसिष्यसि ॥ ३० ॥
यदा त्वेतद् वनं घोरं रामो दशरथात्मजः ।
आगमिष्यति दुर्धर्षस्तदा पूता भविष्यसि ॥ ३१ ॥
तस्यातिथ्येन दुर्वृत्ते लोभमोहविवर्जिता ।

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

इन्द्रको इस प्रकार शाप देकर गौतमने अपनी पत्नीको भी शाप दिया—‘दुराचारिणी! तू भी यहाँ कई हजार वर्षोंतक केवल हवा पीकर या उपवास करके कष्ट उठाती हुई राखमें पड़ी रहेगी । समस्त प्राणियोंसे अदृश्य रहकर इस आश्रममें निवास करेगी । जब दुर्धर्ष दशरथ-कुमार राम इस घोर वनमें पदार्पण करेंगे, उस समय तू पवित्र होगी । उनका आतिथ्य-सत्कार करनेसे तेरे लोभ-मोह आदि दोष दूर हो जायँगे और तू प्रसन्नतापूर्वक मेरे पास पहुँचकर अपना पूर्व शरीर धारण कर लेगी’ ॥ २९—३२ ॥

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

29. shakram tathaa shaptvaa = Indra, thus, on cursing; bhaaryaam api ca shaptavaan = even, also, he cursed wife; iha = here; bahuuni varSasahasraaNi = many, thousands of years; nivasiSyasi = you will live - tarry on; vaayubhakSaa niraahaaraa = air consuming, without food; bhasmashaayinii = recumbent on ashes - dust; tapyantii = searing [contritely]; sarvabhuutaanaam = for all beings; adR^ishyaa = un seen; asmin aashrame nivatsyasi = you live in this hermitage.

अनुवादः

On cursing Indra thus the sage cursed even his wife saying, ‘you shall tarry here for many thousands of years to come without food and consuming air alone, and unseen by all beings you shall live on in this hermitage while contritely recumbent in dust.’ [1-48-29, 30]

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

इन्द्रनिगॆ हीगॆ शापकॊट्टु गौतमरु तन्न पत्नियन्नू शपिसिदरु-दुराचारिणिये! नीनू इल्ले अनेक साविर वर्षगळवरॆगॆ केवल गाळियन्नु सेविसिकॊण्डु, उपवासविद्दु कष्टपडुत्ता बूदियल्लि बिद्दुकॊण्डिरु. समस्त प्राणिगळिन्द अदृश्यळागि ई आश्रमदल्लि वासिसुवॆ. दुर्धर्ष दशरथनन्दन श्रीरामनु ई घोर वनक्कॆ पदार्पण माडुवनु, आग नीनु पवित्रळागुवॆ. अवन आतिथ्य सत्कार माडुवुदरिन्द निन्न लोभ-मोह मॊदलाद दोषगळु दूरवागुवुवु. मत्तॆ नीनु सन्तोषदिन्द नन्न बळिगॆ बन्दु हिन्दिन शरीरवन्नु पडॆयुवॆ.॥29-32॥

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

31. dasharatha aatmajaH = Dasharatha’s, soul-born son; durdharSaH = unassailable one; raamaH = Rama; yadaa = when; ghoram etat vanam = to this sqalid forest; aagamiSyati = arrives; tadaa puutaa bhaviSyasi = then, you will be purified.

अनुवादः

When that unassailable son of Dasharatha, namely Rama, arrives at this squalid forest, for it will be henceforth rendered so along with you, then you will be purified. [1-48-31]

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

32. durvR^itte = oh ill-behaved woman; tasya = to him - to Rama; aatithyena = by giving warm welcome; lobhamohavivarjitaa = divested of greed, craze; mudaa yuktaa = with rejoice; mat sakaashe = in my, proximity; svam vapuH dhaarayiSyasi = you wear your own body [assume.]

अनुवादः

‘On your welcoming Rama, oh, ill-behaved woman, you will be divested of your greed and craze in which you lingered so far, and then you will assume your own body and then you can be in my proximity, rejoicingly.’ Thus, Sage Gautama cursed his wife Ahalya. [1-48-32]

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

29: “Having thus cursed Śakra, he cursed his own wife as well (tathā śaptvā ca vai śakraṃ bhāryām api ca śaptavān): ‘Here for many thousands of years you shall dwell (iha varṣasahasrāṇi bahūni nivasiṣyasi),

30: “‘feeding on wind alone, fasting from all other food, performing penance, and lying in ashes (vātabhakṣā nirāhārā tapyantī bhasmaśāyinī); unseen by all beings, you shall dwell in this hermitage (adṛśyā sarvabhūtānām āśrame ’smin vasiṣyasi).

31: “‘When the unassailable Rāma, son of Daśaratha, shall come to this dreadful forest (yadā tv etad vanaṃ ghoraṃ rāmo daśarathātmajaḥ āgamiṣyati durdharṣaḥ), then shall you be purified (tadā pūtā bhaviṣyasi).

32: “‘By offering hospitality to him, O wayward woman, freed from greed and delusion (tasyātithyena durvṛtte lobhamohavivarjitā), filled with joy in my presence, you shall resume your own visible form (matsakāśaṃ mudā yuktā svaṃ vapur dhārayiṣyasi).’

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

48.29–30 (vātabhakṣā … bhasmaśāyinī adṛśyā vs. Purāṇic Petrifaction)

How does Vālmīki’s account of Ahalyā subsisting on wind (vātabhakṣā), performing penance (tapyantī), lying in ashes (bhasmaśāyinī), and remaining invisible (adṛśyā sarvabhūtānām) relate to the popular Purāṇic story of her becoming a stone (śilābhāva)? Govindarāja states clearly that in Vālmīki’s text Gautama prescribes severe ascetic expiation under the guise of a curse (vidhīyamānaṃ prāyaścittaṃ śāpāpadeśenocyate), whereas the Purāṇic stone-curse belongs to a different kalpa (śilā bhaveti śāpo … purāṇakathā kalpāntaram anusṛtyeti bodhyam). Tilaka notes both this kalpāntara view and a harmonizing view: invisible in her own form (svarūpeṇādṛśyā), she appeared like a stone image (śilāpratimārūpeṇa) while retaining inner consciousness to fast on wind and lie in ashes.

48.31–32 (yadā tv etad vanaṃ … tasyātithyena — Refuting Kataka’s Athetesis)

Govindarāja and Tilaka record that Kataka claimed vv. 31–32 were interpolated, and Govindarāja refutes him directly: in the next sarga (49.17, smarantī gautamavacaḥ), Ahalyā offers Rāma hospitality explicitly “remembering Gautama’s words,” which would be impossible without vv. 31–32 (smarantī gautamavaca ityādy uttarasargavacanavirodhāt)! Govindarāja adds that even millennia of penance cannot equal a fraction of the grace of Rāma’s foot-dust (bhagavatpādasparśasya kalāṃ nārhanti).

इममाश्रममुत्सृज्य सिद्धचारणसेविते ।
हिमवच्छिखरे रम्ये तपस्तेपे महातपाः ॥ ३३ ॥

सर्वाष् टीकाः
मूलम्

एवमुक्त्वा महातेजा गौतमो दुष्टचारिणीम् ।
इममाश्रममुत्सृज्य सिद्धचारणसेविते ।
हिमवच्छिखरे रम्ये तपस्तेपे महातपाः ॥ ३३ ॥

अनुवाद (हिन्दी)

‘अपनी दुराचारिणी पत्नीसे ऐसा कहकर महातेजस्वी, महातपस्वी गौतम इस आश्रमको छोड़कर चले गये और सिद्धों तथा चारणोंसे सेवित हिमालयके रमणीय शिखरपर रहकर तपस्या करने लगे’ ॥ ३३ ॥

अनुवाद (कन्नड)

तन्न दुराचारिणी पत्नियल्लि हीगॆ हेळि महा तेजस्वि, महातपस्वी, गौतमरु ई आश्रमवन्नु बिट्टु, सिद्धरू, चारणरू सेविसुत्तिद्द हिमालयद रमणीय शिखरदल्लि तपस्सु माडतॊडगिदरु.॥33॥

Desiraju Hanumanta Rao

पदच्छेदः

  1. mahaatejaaH = great-resplendent one; mahaatapaaH = great-ascetic; gautamaH = Gautama; duSTacaariNiim = to bad behaved one - to immodest Ahalya; evam uktvaa = thus, on saying [cursing]; imam aashramam utsR^ijya = shedding this hermitage; siddhacaaraNasevite = adored by siddha-s, caarana-s; ramye himavat shikhare = pleasant ones, on Himalayas, peaks; tapaH tepe= practised asceticism.

अनुवादः

“On cursing immodest Ahalya thus, that great-resplendent Sage Gautama shed this hermitage which was once adored by celestials like siddha-sand caarana-s, and that great-ascetic Gautama practised his asceticism on the pleasant peaks of Himalayas.” Thus, Vishvamitra continued his narration about the legend of Ahalya. [1-48-33]

समाप्तिः

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये बालकाण्डेऽष्टचत्वारिंशः सर्गः ॥ ४८ ॥
इस प्रकार श्रीवाल्मीकिनिर्मित आर्षरामायण आदिकाव्यके बालकाण्डमें अड़तालीसवाँ सर्ग पूरा हुआ ॥ ४८ ॥

अनुवाद (समाप्तिः) (कन्नड)

वाल्मीकि विरचित आर्ष रामायण आदिकाव्यद बालकाण्डदल्लि नलवत्तॆण्टनॆय सर्ग पूर्णवायितु. ॥48॥

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

“Having spoken thus to that erring woman, the resplendent Gautama of great austerity (evam uktvā mahātejā gautamo duṣṭacāriṇīm mahātapāḥ) abandoned this hermitage and practiced penance on a lovely peak of Himavat frequented by Siddhas and Cāraṇas (imam āśramam utsṛjya siddhacāraṇasevite himavacchikhare ramye tapas tepe).”

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

48.33 (himavacchikhare ramye tapas tepe)

Having pronounced both curse and redemption—without even being petitioned, showing his underlying compassion (prārthanām antareṇānugrahavacanoktyā muner dayālutvaṃ vyaktam, Śiromaṇi)—Gautama departs for Himavat to replenish the ascetic energy expended in his curse (tacchāpakṣayitatapaḥprāptyartham, Govindarāja).
