**Book
I : Bala Kanda - The Youthful Majesties**

The visitant Parashu Rama narrates the legends of bows of Vishnu and Shiva to Rama. Unheedful of the request of Dasharatha
to spare his sons, Parashu Rama addresses Dasharatha Rama directly and asks him to take an aim with the longbow of Vishnu,
and if Dasharatha Rama is capable to do so, Parashu Rama says that he will give a duel to him.
</details>

<details><summary>Desiraju Hanumanta Rao</summary>

**पदच्छेदः**

*1. daasharathe raama* = oh Dasharatha's, Rama; *viira* = oh valiant one; *te viiryam adbhutam shruuyate*
= your, valour is being heard [being bruited about] as sensational; *dhanuSaH bhedanam ca eva* = bow's [of Shiva,]
smashing, also thus - other things [about your eliminating Tataka]; *nikhilena mayaa shrutam* = thoroughly heard by me.

**अनुवादः**

Oh, valiant Rama of Dasharatha, your valour is bruited as a sensational valour, and sensational is your smashing of Shiva's
bow, also thus I have thoroughly heard about your others deeds like elimination of Tataka et cetera... [1-75-1]
</details>


<details><summary>English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

“O Rāma, son of Daśaratha! O hero! Your wondrous valor is heard of everywhere (_vīryaṃ te śrūyate ’dbhutam_), and the breaking of the bow (_dhanuṣo bhedanaṃ caiva_) has been heard by me in full detail (_nikhilena mayā śrutam_).
</details>


<details><summary>टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**75.1–2 (dhanuṣo bhedanaṃ caiva & acintyam)**

Opening his commentary on Sarga 75, **Govindarāja** notes that after showing Viṣṇu’s supremacy as cause of the universe, Vālmīki now demonstrates Rudra’s subordination through the history of the two bows (_prayatnaśaktikatvena tasyāparatvam āha_). Both **Rāma Varmā (_Tilaka_)** and **Govindarāja** observe that _caiva_ in v. 1 gathers in Rāma’s earlier feats such as slaying Tāṭakā (_cakāreṇa tāṭakāvadhādi samuccīyate_), while _acintyam_ (v. 2) means inconceivable even by the mind of another (_anyena manasāpy aśakyam_).
</details>
</details>

*3. ghorasamkaasham* = catastrophic, in its aspect - bow; *jaamadagnyam* = [received through Sage] Jamadagni;
*tat* = that; *idam* = this alone; *mahat* = great [fateful]; *dhanuH* = bow; *shareNa eva
puurayasva* = that way, flex [bowstring up to ear] it with an arrow; *svabalam darshayasva* = show
yourself your own capability.

**अनुवादः**

This alone is that catastrophic bow received through Sage Jamadagni... flex it with an arrow on bowstring stretching up

</details>


<details><summary>टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**75.3–4 (jāmadagnyaṃ mahad dhanuḥ & dvandvayuddham)**

**Śiromaṇi** remarks that Paraśurāma’s demand to string the Vaiṣṇava bow betrays his disbelief that Śiva’s bow was broken by sheer strength (_paraśurāmasya viśvāsābhāvaḥ sūcitaḥ_) and reveals how the Supreme Lord’s will deludes even the wise (_īśvarecchāyāḥ śiṣṭabuddhimohakatvaṃ vyaktam_). Only if Rāma strings this bow will Paraśurāma deem him worthy of a duel that redounds to the praise of his valor (_vīryaślāghyam_, **Śiromaṇi**).
</details>
</details>

<details><summary>Desiraju Hanumanta Rao</summary>

**पदच्छेदः**

*4. tat* = thereby; *aham* = I will; *asya dhanuSaH prapuuraNe* = with taking aim of this bow; *
te balam dR^iSTvaa* = on seeing [on examining] your strength; *viiryashlaaghyam* = [if your] valour is deserving;
*aham tava* = I will, to you; *dvandvayuddham pradaasyaami* = I give combative duel.

**अनुवादः**

"Thereby, on examining your strength in your taking aim with this bow, and should you be deservedly valorous, I will give
you a combative duel..." So said Parashu Rama to Dasharatha Rama. [1-75-4]

**टिप्पनी**

Pt. Satya Vrat cites this in his book 'The Ramayana - A Linguistic Study... 'As in English, we speak of *yuddhadaanam*
- giving a fight to the enemy - in Sanskrit, too...' it is giving a duel.

<details><summary>टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**75.5–6 (kṣatraroṣāt praśāntaḥ & abhayaṃ dātum arhasi)**

Why does Daśaratha tremble despite knowing Rāma’s valor against Tāṭakā and Mārīca? **Govindarāja** explains that paternal love overwhelms the king (_snehabharākrānto rāmavaibhavaṃ ... jānann api_), prompting him to appeal to Paraśurāma’s Brāhmaṇa nature and renunciation of wrath against Kṣatriyas (_kṣatraroṣāt kṣatravadhāt_, **Tilaka**).
</details>
</details>

<details><summary>Desiraju Hanumanta Rao</summary>

**पदच्छेदः**

*6. kSatraroSaat prashaantaH* = from Kshatriya-s animosity, appeased you are; *braahmaNaH* = Brahman;
*mahaatapaaH* = one with high [inviolable] ascesis; such as you are; *tvam* = you; *baalaanaam mama
putraaNaam* = youngsters, my, sons; *abhayam daatum arhasi* = apt of you to award no fear [aegis] .

**अनुवादः**

Aren't you a Brahman with inviolable ascesis, and whose rancour on Kshatriya-s has calmed down long back. Why this hostility
again. It'll be apt of you to award aegis to my sons, for they are yet youngsters... [1-75-6]

**टिप्पनी**

Vividly: 'peace is primary for Brahman-s... though that was once disturbed in you, you redeemed it after your eradicating
the then ruthless Kshatriya-s... thus your rancour was appeased then... and you too peacefully retired for inviolable ascesis
and acquired still higher bliss by them... do you now wish to violate your own intrinsic nature of peacefulness being a
blissful one, being an all-knower, being an elderly Brahman, that too on mere boys...
</details>

<details><summary>Desiraju Hanumanta Rao</summary>

**पदच्छेदः**

<details><summary>Desiraju Hanumanta Rao</summary>

**पदच्छेदः**

*26b, 27. raama* = oh Rama; *tat* = that; *dhanuSaH bhedam* = longbow's, breakage; *shrutvaa*
= on hearing; *aham tataH drutam aagataH* = therefore I quickly, came; *kSatradharmam* = fealty to Kshatriya-hood
; *puraskR^itya* = keeping [in view] afore; *evam* = likewise [like the wielding of Shiva's bow];
*pitR^ipaitaamaham* = forefathers [passed on] of father; *mahat* = supernatural; *uttamam* = superlative
one; *vaiSNavam dhanuH gR^ihNiiSva* = you take-you handle longbow of Vishnu.

**अनुवादः**

On hearing about the breakage of Shiva's longbow, then I promptly came here. Thereby, oh, Rama, wield this supernatural
and superlative longbow of Vishnu, which is passed on to me from my forefathers and my father. Keep your fealty to Kshatriya-hood
in view, and wield this as you have wielded Shiva's longbow... [1-75-26b, 27]
</details>

<details><summary>Desiraju Hanumanta Rao</summary>

**पदच्छेदः**

*27. kaakutstha* = oh Kakutstha; *dhanuHshreSThe* = with longbow supramundane one; *parapuramjayam
sharam* = enemy citadels conquering, arrow; *yojayasva* = you join; *shaktaH asi yadi* = if you are capable
; *tataH* = thereafter; *dvandvam daasyaami te* = I give a duel to you.

**अनुवादः**

Take an aim with an arrow that conquers enemy's citadels fixing it on this supramundane longbow... and oh, Kakutstha, should
you be capable of it, thereafter I will give you a duel... [1-75-27]
</details>

<details><summary>English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**27:** “Therefore, O Rāma, honoring the duty of a Kṣatriya (_kṣatradharmaṃ puraskṛtya_), take up this supreme, mighty bow of Viṣṇu (_tad evaṃ vaiṣṇavaṃ rāma ... gṛhṇīṣva dhanur uttamam_), handed down from my father and grandfather (_pitṛpaitāmaham_)!

**28:** “Fit an arrow, the conqueror of hostile citadels, to this foremost of bows (_yojayasva dhanuḥśreṣṭhe śaraṃ parapuraṃjayam_); if you are able to do so, O Kākutstha (_yadi śakto ’si kākutstha_), I shall then grant you single combat (_dvandvaṃ dāsyāmi te tataḥ_)!”
</details>


<details><summary>टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)</summary>

**75.27 (pitṛpaitāmaham & kṣatradharmaṃ puraskṛtya)**

Both **Tilaka** and **Govindarāja** note the Ārṣa _vṛddhi_ of the second member in _pitṛpaitāmaham_ (“handed down from father and grandfather”). Citing **Kataka**, they explain _kṣatradharmaṃ puraskṛtya_: “Do not adopt a Brāhmaṇa’s quietism and say ‘I am unable’—take up the bow as a Kṣatriya!” (_brāhmaṇavac chāntim āsthāya nāhaṃ śakta iti mā brūhi_).

_____________

**75.28 (yadi śakto ’si ... dvandvaṃ dāsyāmi)**

**Tilaka** and **Govindarāja** point out the haughty challenge behind Paraśurāma’s closing words: “Even if you manage to string this bow and fit the arrow, I shall not let you go unchallenged, but will fight you in single combat—or else surrender to me!” (_tāvatāpi na tvāṃ tyakṣyāmi, api tu dvandvaṃ dāsyāmi, anyathā māṃ śaraṇaṃ vrajeti tātparyam_).
</details>
