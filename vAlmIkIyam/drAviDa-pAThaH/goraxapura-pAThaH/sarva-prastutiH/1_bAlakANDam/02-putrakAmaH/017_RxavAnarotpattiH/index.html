Book I : Bala Kanda - The Youthful Majesties

The gods become progenitors Vanara heroes at the dictate of Brahma to come to the aid of Rama in annihilating Ravana. The monkey-like Vanara race is procreated by numerous celestials endowing it with peculiar mighty physique and particular power to come to the help of Rama.

Now when Lord Viṣṇu had entered upon filial status (putratvaṃ tu gate viṣṇau) as the son of that great-souled monarch Daśaratha, the self-existent Lord Brahmā (svayambhūr bhagavān) spoke these words to all the gods:

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

17.1 (putratvaṃ tu gate viṣṇau — Narrative Sequence)

Govindarāja (Bhūṣaṇa) explains the narrative transition from Sarga 16: after Viṣṇu vanished from the celestial assembly (1.16.10), two events ensued—the rise of the fiery Prājāpatya Puruṣa with the pāyasa in Ayodhyā and Brahmā’s command to the gods in heaven. Having completed the pāyasa episode first for narrative continuity, Vālmīki now turns to Brahmā’s decree. Both Tilaka and Govindarāja gloss the past participle gate in an inceptive sense: “when Viṣṇu had set out to assume filial status.”

अनुवादः

“Let mighty and guise changing helpmates be procreated to that truth abiding and valorous Vishnu who is the well-wisher of all of us. [1-17-2]

English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

2: “Create mighty, shape-shifting companions (sahāyān balinaḥ kāmarūpiṇaḥ) for Lord Viṣṇu, who is true to His promise (satyasandhasya), heroic, and ever devoted to the welfare of us all (sarveṣāṃ no hitaiṣiṇaḥ)—

3: Conversant with magic and illusion (māyāvidaḥ), valiant, matching the speed of the wind in swiftness (vāyuvegasamān jave), skilled in policy (nayajñān), endowed with high intelligence, and equal to Viṣṇu Himself in valor (viṣṇutulyaparākramān);

4: Invincible (asaṃhāryān), knowing all strategic expedients (upāyajñān), endowed with celestial frames (divyasaṃhananānvitān), accomplished in the virtues and powers of all weapons (sarvāstraguṇasampannān), and like unto the immortals who partake of nectar (amṛtaprāśanān iva)—

टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

17.2 (satyasandhasya & kāmarūpiṇaḥ)

Why does Brahmā call Viṣṇu satyasandhaḥ (“true to His promise”) here? Rāma Varmā (Tilaka) notes that it preempts any doubt among the gods as to whether the Supreme Lord would actually descend to earth: having pledged His word, He will unfailingly incarnate (nanu kadācid bhagavān pratijñāyāpi nāvatared ata uktam). Govindarāja explains kāmarūpiṇaḥ (“assuming any form at will”) as essential for combating deceitful rākṣasas who fight with illusion (kapaṭa-rākṣasa-yuddha-kṣama-dharmadhāriṇaḥ).

17.3–4 (māyāvidaḥ & sarvāstraguṇasampannān)

Tilaka glosses māyāvidaḥ as “possessing divine counter-powers capable of dispelling demoniac illusions” (asurādi-māyā-saṃhāra-samartha-devamāyā-śaktimataḥ), and offers two explanations of sarvāstraguṇasampannān: either endowed with the power to neutralize all enemy missiles (sarvāstra-nivāraṇa-sāmarthya), or conversant with the projection and withdrawal (prayogopasaṃhārādayaḥ) of all divine weapons.

सर्वाष् टीकाः - अवतारिका
English (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

टिप्पनी

Gandharva-s (Southey’s Glendoveers) are celestial musicians inhabiting Indra’s heaven and forming the orchestra at all the banquets of the principal deities. Yaksha-s, demigods attendant especially on Kubera, and employed by him in the care of his garden and treasures. Kimpurusha-s, demigods attached also to the service of Kubera, celestial musicians, represented like centaurs reversed with human figures and horses’ heads. Siddha-s, are demigods or spirits of undefined attributes, occupying with the Vidyaadharaa-s the middle air or region between the earth and the sun. Griffith.

On v. 16, all three commentators celebrate Hanumān’s diamond-hard frame (vajravad abhedya-śarīrayuktaḥ), Garuḍa-like speed, and supreme wisdom and strength (17ab). टीका-notes (Gemini 4, itihasa.io)

17.32cd–33 (King, Crown Prince, and Ministers)

How did all the troop-leaders attend upon Vālin and Sugrīva as well as upon Nala, Nīla, and Hanumān? Govindarāja explains the political hierarchy of the Vānara realm concealed in this one-and-a-half-verse sentence: they served Vālin as King (rājānam), Sugrīva as Crown Prince (yuvarājam), and Nala, Nīla, and Hanumān as the high Ministers of State (nalādīn mantriṇaḥ), approaching the royal brothers either directly or through their ministers (kecit sākṣāt kecin nalādidvārā, Tilaka).

17.34–36 (ardayan, Vālin’s Rule, & nānāvyañjanalakṣaṇaiḥ)

On v. 34, Tilaka notes the epic omission of the past augment (aḍ-abhāva ārṣaḥ) in ardayan for ārdayan (“they harassed”). Why did Vālin rule and protect all the bears, langurs, and monkeys (v. 35)? Tilaka observes: devarājāvatāratvād iti bhāvaḥ—because Vālin was the incarnation of Indra, the king of the gods! On v. 36, Tilaka and Govindarāja distinguish vividhasaṃsthānaiḥ (diverse bodily statures and builds) from nānāvyañjanalakṣaṇaiḥ (distinctive marks and traits that differentiated each warrior from the others).

17.37 (The Chronological Problem of Vālin, Sugrīva, and Mainda-Dvivida)

At the close of Sarga 17, Govindarāja raises a celebrated chronological question: how can Vālin, Sugrīva, Mainda, Dvivida, and Krodhana be described as born after Viṣṇu’s entry into the queens’ wombs, when Uttarakāṇḍa shows that Vālin defeated Rāvaṇa during the era of Kārtavīrya Arjuna (long before Rāma’s birth), Mainda and Dvivida drank amṛta at the churning of the ocean, and Krodhana fought in the ancient Devāsura war? Calling Vālin a contemporary in age with Rāma (kārtavīryārjunakāliko vālī kathaṃ rāmasamavayaskaḥ syāt) seems to contradict the rest of the epic!

17.37 (The Preceptor’s Resolution — Atrāsmad-gurucaraṇāḥ)

Govindarāja cites his revered teacher’s resolution (atrāsmad-gurucaraṇāḥ): while Brahmā issued his general decree at the time of Rāma’s incarnation—and thousands of gods who had not previously sired sons then begot Vānaras “at that time” (tatra = tatkāle in v. 22d)—verses 10–16 (like v. 7 on Jāmbavān) restate the pre-existing creations of Indra, Sūrya, the Aśvins, and Vāyu, which is why v. 10 says simply janayām āsa without tadā! Alternatively (yadvā), Sargas 15–17 recount the ancient celestial council that preceded the Avatar as an introductory flashback.
